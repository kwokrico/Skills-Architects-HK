# Foreign design handbooks — Hong Kong applicability gate

These three books are foreign design handbooks. They are not Hong Kong statutory instruments. Do not cite them for a Buildings Department, Fire Services Department, or Planning Department limit, and do not quote their dimensions, costs, or code clauses into a recommendation.

Hong Kong instruments already in this suite win: Buildings Ordinance Cap. 123 and its regulations, practice notes, the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings, the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008 and the later universal-access manual, and the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines. Lease conditions, outline zoning plan Notes, and the project brief come next. Existing skill tables come after those. These books come last, and only for the uses named below.

| Order | Source | Use for a number |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hong Kong statute and codes | Submission limit |
| 2 | Lease, outline zoning plan Notes, project brief | Site cap and client requirement |
| 3 | Existing skill tables | Comfort or stock size already recorded here |
| 4 | The three books below | Method or room adjacency only. No imported millimetres |

Do not transcribe tables from the archived PDFs. A copied figure is easy to mistake for a statutory minimum.

## 1. Human Dimension and Interior Space

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Authors | Julius Panero and Martin Zelnik |
| Edition | 1979, Whitney Library of Design, United States |
| Units | Inch-first, with metric conversions |
| Archive | `hk_s_reference/SS/Human Dimension and Interior Space A Source Book of Design Reference Standards.pdf` |

**Allowed use.** Method only, and only where Hong Kong has not set the dimension:

- Design for a range of bodies. An average body fits nobody in particular.
- Keep three questions apart: what a person can reach, what clearance a body needs to pass, and what the user can adjust.
- Size for movement, not only for a person standing still.

**Hard exclusions.** Do not use this book for accessible routes, doors, ramps, stairs, guards, or turning space. The sample is a 1970s United States civilian and military population, not a Hong Kong population study. Its wheelchair figures must not replace the Design Manual. Comfort millimetres stay in [hk-human-scale-dimensions.md](hk-human-scale-dimensions.md). Where that table and the Design Manual differ, the Design Manual is the submission figure and the table is a comfort check. Example: a planning ramp of 1:20 to 1:15 and a 1,500 mm turning circle are comfort figures; the Design Manual ramp and accessible-route widths govern the submission.

## 2. Architect's Data

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Author | Ernst Neufert |
| Edition | Second (International) English Edition, 1980, Blackwell. General editor Vincent Jones |
| Units | Metric |
| Archive | `hk_s_reference/SS/03. Architect_s Data.pdf` |

**Allowed use.** Room relationships only: which rooms belong together in a house, flat, kitchen, bedroom, office, school, or hotel, and only where this suite has no Hong Kong area figure for that relationship. Do not read sizes off those pages.

**Hard exclusions.** Do not use the chapters on standards and regulations, fire precautions, parking, stairs, daylight, structure, or services. Those record European and United Kingdom practice of 1980. Area benchmarks stay in [hk-building-programming.md](../subskills/hk-building-programming/hk-building-programming.md) (Housing Authority schedules, Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines, Education Bureau, Food and Environmental Hygiene Department licensing). Stair, exit, parking, daylight, and structural figures stay in the Hong Kong topic files.

## 3. The Architect's Portable Handbook

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Author | Pat Guthrie |
| Edition | Third edition, 2003, McGraw-Hill |
| Units | United States customary, with some metric |
| Archive | `hk_s_reference/SS/12. The Architect_s Portable Handbook.pdf` |

**Allowed use.** None for a Hong Kong number. The book's own preface ties the third edition to the 2000 International Building Code and NFPA 5000. Treat it as a reminder that preliminary design needs a checklist, then use the Hong Kong topic file for each item on that list.

**Hard exclusions.** Do not use its building laws, occupant load, occupancy classification, allowable area or height, construction type, hourly ratings, exiting, Americans with Disabilities Act sizes, structural loads, span-to-depth ratios, energy code, plumbing fixture counts, heating and cooling sizing, elevator rules, Construction Specifications Institute specification format, or construction costs. Costs in the book are United States figures from about 2003. Structural sizes stay in [hk-structural-systems.md](../subskills/hk-structural-systems/hk-structural-systems.md). Stock sizes stay in [hk-common-material-dimensions.md](hk-common-material-dimensions.md). Accessible sizes stay in [hk-accessibility-design.md](../subskills/hk-accessibility-design/hk-accessibility-design.md).
