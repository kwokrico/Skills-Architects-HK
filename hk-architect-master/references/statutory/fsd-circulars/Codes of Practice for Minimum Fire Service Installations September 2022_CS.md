# Codes of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2022 (this revision; first issue February 1964) | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: This PDF contains two codes. The Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment (Parts I to V) is the schedule the Director of Fire Services uses for the certificate under section 16(1)(b) of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123). The Code of Practice for Inspection, Testing and Maintenance of Installations and Equipment, bound in the same volume, governs acceptance testing and upkeep after installation. Neither code is the Buildings Department Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings, and neither sizes means of escape, compartmentation or emergency vehicular access except where it expressly points to that code or to the Building (Planning) Regulations.

## Regulatory Overview

The minimum-installations code classifies premises and then lists the systems, coverage and specifications for each class, with a high-rise threshold at 30 m to the floor of the uppermost storey above staircase discharge and a recurring basement split at 230 m² total floor area.

Where a premises type is not detailed, or the building is a special design or hazard, the Director determines the requirements and may vary this code or accept a fire-engineering approach that is not inferior to the prescriptive level.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Certificate, discretion and standards (paras 1.3, 1.4, 1.9; Part IV General)

| Rule | Constraint |
|---|---|
| para. 1.4 | The Building Authority may refuse plans under section 16(1)(b) of the Buildings Ordinance unless the Director certifies that no fire service installation is necessary, or that the plans show the minimum installations necessary for the stated purpose in accordance with this code |
| para. 1.3 | The Director may, for a particular building, add, substitute or relax any requirement so that the result is still the minimum necessary for the intended purpose |
| para. 1.3 | A fire-engineering approach may be accepted case by case for special design or hazard, if the safety level is not inferior to the prescriptive requirements |
| para. 1.9 | Standards quoted in this code are the current version at the time of building-plans submission |
| Part IV General | Where requirements are not detailed for particular premises, the Director determines them |

Where a premises paragraph states the additional lining rules, acoustic and thermal insulation linings in ductings and concealed locations, and acoustic, thermal and decorative linings within protected means of escape, are Class 1 or 2 rate of surface spread of flame to British Standard 476: Part 7, or an approved fire-retardant product bringing them to that standard. Intended storage or use of dangerous goods as defined in Chapter 295 is to be notified to the Director.

**SD takeaway:** Lock the intended purpose on the fire-services certificate before plate freeze; a later change of use reopens the minimum-installation set, and the standards are those current at plans submission.

### 2. Classification locks (paras 1.2, 3.1, 3.2)

| Term | Threshold |
|---|---|
| High-rise building | Floor of the uppermost storey **exceeds 30 m** above the point of staircase discharge at ground floor |
| Low-rise building | That floor **does not exceed 30 m** above the same point |
| Basement storey | Any storey below the ground storey from which all required exit routes are upward to the ground storey |
| Cubical extent | Space within the external surfaces of the walls and roof and the upper surface of the floor of the lowest storey, excluding a roof enclosure used only for a water tank, lift gear or other services; an open side is deemed enclosed by a wall from the outer edge of the roof |
| Fire load | (Calorific value of contents in MJ/kg × weight of contents in kg) / floor area in square metres |
| Sleeping risk | Occupants asleep, or transient in a strange building and unable to identify the means of escape, including the old, the infirm and children |
| Commercial | A building, or part, constructed or intended for business, trade or entertainment |
| Domestic | Constructed or intended for habitation |
| Hotel | Used wholly or in part primarily for accommodation on a commercial basis |
| Composite | Any two or more of domestic, commercial, institutional and hotel; a separate paragraph of this code applies to each purpose |
| Industrial | Making, altering, repairing, ornamenting, finishing, cleaning, washing, breaking up, demolition, or adapting for sale, by way of trade or for gain |
| Godown | A warehouse, or any building used wholly or in part for storage of goods or raw material |
| Institutional | Includes government, institution and community facilities; clubs; educational establishments; hostels; hospitals, mental institutions and clinics; prisons and similar institutions; sanatoria |
| Car port | Covered parking open for its entire length or width on at least two sides. Includes driveways; excludes a loading/unloading area (para. 1.2) |
| Garage | Covered parking enclosed by walls, with or without windows, on more than two sides |
| Cold storage | A unit of specific volume entirely given over to storage below 10 °C |
| Protected means of escape | Protected corridors, protected lobbies (including lobbies protecting fireman’s lifts) and protected staircases as defined in the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings |
| Natural venting of staircases | An alternative to staircase pressurization by venting smoke from a staircase adjacent to an external wall to Section 2 of British Standard 5588: Part 5: 1991. A vent opening into a light well, atrium or similar is unacceptable. Clauses 8.4(a), (b) and (h) of that standard are specifically flagged |

Group I is a designated area of special hazard normally inside a building (audio/visual production, battery rooms and electrical charging, boiler rooms, bowling alleys, cold storage, dangerous-goods stores, consumer electrical equipment needing a separate installation, kitchens other than domestic, lift motor rooms, lifts not discharging to a protected means of escape, mechanical plant rooms, and telephone, computer and similar installations). Group II is a building, group or complex of special hazard (including curtain-walled buildings, bulk fuel, petro-chemical complexes, road tunnels and substations). Audio/visual production facilities, cold storage, dangerous-goods stores and mechanical plant rooms appear in both groups.

**SD takeaway:** Measure high-rise from staircase discharge to the floor of the uppermost storey, not to roof plant; at 30 m the building is still low-rise, and above 30 m the high-rise paragraph applies.

### 3. Street hydrants, and items this code requires but does not call fire service installations (paras 1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 5.16, 5.25)

| Item | Rule in this code | Treated as a fire service installation under para. 1.2? |
|---|---|---|
| Street fire hydrant | Accessible street hydrant not greater than **100 m** from the building, in addition to installations inside the building (para. 1.5) | The street system is a listed system in Part II; the 100 m rule is an Authorized Person design duty in para. 1.5 |
| Street hydrant performance (para. 5.25) | Install to Water Supplies Department standard mainlaying practice. Spacing **100 m**, staggered on alternative sides of the roadway where applicable. Wherever possible, at least **two** street hydrants within the site, fixed **not less than 6 m** from the building. One 65 mm outlet: not less than **2 000 litres/min** (33.3 litres/s) at not less than **170 kPa**. Two 65 mm outlets together: not less than **4 000 litres/min**. Minimum delivery time **60 minutes**. If the main cannot do this, augment (for example sump tank and pumps) according to size, nature and available supply | Specification for the street system |
| Fireman’s lift, firefighting and rescue stairway, emergency vehicular access | Provide to every building in accordance with regulations 41B, 41C and 41D of the Building (Planning) Regulations (para. 1.6). Design and construction of the lift and the stairway: Code of Practice on the Design and Construction of Lifts and Escalators, and the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings (para. 5.16) | **No** (para. 1.6) |
| Closed-circuit television, emergency power points, fireman’s communication system, fireman’s emergency switch, pedestrian cross-over | Included where a premises paragraph requires them | **No** (para. 1.7) |

**SD takeaway:** Place at least two street hydrants inside the site where possible, each at least 6 m off the building and none more than 100 m from it, and keep the fireman’s lift and emergency vehicular access on the Building (Planning) Regulations track rather than inside the fire-service-installation plant room.

### 4. Power, alarm points and hose geometry shared by hydrant buildings (paras 1.8, 5.9, 5.10, 5.11, 5.13, 5.14)

| Item | Hard rule |
|---|---|
| Primary and secondary supply | Where an electrical installation must comply with this code, both sources are required. Primary supply comes from the load side of the building’s main isolating device and is independent of non-fire-service circuits from the main supply switchboard (para. 1.8) |
| Hydrant location | In an approach lobby to a required staircase or required staircase enclosure, **not** at the central core of the staircase enclosure. Must not obstruct any door opening or the required width of an exit route, and must not be concealed by an adjacent door leaf when that door is open. Outlet-coupling centre **800 mm to 1 200 mm** above finished floor |
| Hose reel | Tubing **not exceeding 30 m**. Every part of the premises (or the building, as the premises paragraph states) reachable by that length, except strong rooms and safe deposit vaults where the basement paragraphs say so. Internal bore not less than **19 mm**. Nozzle orifice not less than **4.5 mm**, jet not less than **6 m**. Supply pressure at the reel not exceeding **1 200 kPa**. Control valve and nozzle not more than **1 350 mm** above finished floor; if recessed, not more than **500 mm** from the wall face. Rising main for hose reels not less than **40 mm** nominal bore; pipe to an individual reel not less than **25 mm** |
| Manual call point | **0.9 m to 1.2 m** above finished floor (para. 5.11). Also, centre of call points near hose reels **900 mm to 1 200 mm** above finished floor (para. 5.14). At each hose reel, and adjacent to all storey exits (or the entrance lobby in lieu if that lobby leads only to the storey exits) within **not more than 2 m**, and adjacent to all staircase exits to open air on the ground storey or ultimate place of safety. One actuating point and one audio warning device at each hose reel, with fire-pump start, unless the premises paragraph says otherwise. Visual alarms as required by the current Design Manual: Barrier Free Access |
| Visual fire alarm spacing (British Standard 5839 form) | Minimum rating **15 cd**. At least one point in each compartment. Maximum distance between two points **60 m**. A broad guide is one point near every hose reel and alarm point |
| Direct line | Where a fire detection system is provided, the manual system is linked to it and to the Fire Services Communication Centre by direct line (para. 5.11). A detection system has a direct line to that Centre or to other premises agreed with the Director (para. 5.13) |
| Emergency lighting performance | British Standard 5266: Part 1 and BS EN 1838, except exit signs follow para. 5.10. Backed by emergency generator **or** self-contained secondary battery. Any accommodation, regardless of size, horizontal illuminance not less than **2 lx** at floor level. Accommodation exceeding **8 m²** gross: at least **two** emergency luminaires. On power failure, activate within **5 seconds** for bowling alleys, commercial buildings, hotels and institutional buildings |
| Exit signs | Positioned **2 m to 2.5 m** above floor, measured to the base, and directly above the exit doorway where practicable. Character height not less than **125 mm**. New buildings: a single type of exit sign throughout the same development. If there is no emergency generator, secondary battery to British Standard 5266: Part 1 |

**SD takeaway:** Set hose-reel reach at 30 m and put every hydrant in the staircase approach lobby, clear of the stair core and of the exit width, before the floor plate is frozen.

### 5. Main building packages (paras 4.14, 4.15, 4.21–4.23, 4.29–4.32)

Hydrant and hose-reel extent, where required, is the shared rule in topic 4: hydrants in approach lobbies to required staircases, and hose reels so that every part can be reached by not more than 30 m of tubing. Fire detection, where the paragraph says “areas not covered by automatic fixed installations”, covers the gaps left by sprinklers or other automatic water systems. “As required by the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings” is the extent given for fireman’s lifts and, where stated, firefighting and rescue stairways. Audio/visual advisory systems, where listed, are required only where one single occupancy on one floor exceeds **2 000 m²** and the occupants are transient shoppers, audience or guests (institutional: guests or visitors) who need the extra advice.

| Package | Sprinklers | Fire control centre | Emergency generator | Emergency lighting | Other locks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic, up to and including 3 storeys (para. 4.21) | Not required by this paragraph | Not required | Not required | Not required by this paragraph | Portable hand-operated approved appliance: **one per floor**, plus additional for car ports |
| Domestic, low-rise over 3 storeys (para. 4.22) | Not required by this paragraph | Not required | Not required | Not required by this paragraph | Manual fire alarm and hydrant/hose reel |
| Domestic, high-rise (para. 4.23) | Not required by this paragraph | Not required | Yes, sized for the fire service installations and fireman’s lifts it must supply | Staircases, passages, plant rooms, and public areas **including lift lobbies on all floors and refuge areas**. Exit signs for routes from public areas to staircases | Fireman’s lift as required by the fire-safety code. Manual alarm and hydrant/hose reel |
| Commercial, low-rise (para. 4.14) | Total floor area **exceeding 230 m²**, all parts including staircases, common corridors and toilets | Not in the requirements list | Yes | Entire building and all exit routes to ground level | Fireman’s lift as required by the fire-safety code. Smoke extraction: see topic 10. No staircase-pressurization item |
| Commercial, high-rise (para. 4.15) | **All parts**, including staircases, common corridors and toilets | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Entire building and all exit routes to ground level | Pressurization and smoke extraction: see topic 10. Fireman’s lift as required by the fire-safety code |
| Industrial/godown, low-rise (para. 4.29) | Total floor area **exceeding 230 m²**, all parts including staircases, common corridors and toilets | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Entire building and all exit routes to ground level | Fireman’s lift **or** firefighting and rescue stairway, as required by the fire-safety code |
| Industrial/godown, high-rise (para. 4.30) | **All parts**, including staircases, common corridors and toilets | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Entire building and all exit routes to ground level | **Firefighting and rescue stairway** (not the “or fireman’s lift” wording), as required by the fire-safety code. Pressurization: see topic 10 |
| Institutional, low-rise (para. 4.31) | All parts including staircases, common corridors, toilets and bathrooms, where total floor area **exceeds 230 m²** | Not in the requirements list | Hospitals, sanatoria, prisons, or as required by the risk | Entire building and all exit routes to ground level | Sleeping-floor detection: see topic 6. No pressurization item |
| Institutional, high-rise (para. 4.32) | **All parts**, including staircases, common corridors, toilets and bathrooms | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Entire building and all exit routes to ground level | Pressurization only for hospitals and sanatoria: see topic 10 |

Hospital and sanatorium lifts designated for evacuation shall satisfy every condition for a fireman’s lift except the internal floor area of the car and the minimum rated load (paras 4.31 and 4.32, additional requirements).

An industrial or godown building, at either height, has a rising main for each staircase (para. 5.14(f)).

Automatic fixed installations other than water are required, where listed, in areas where water is undesirable for the occupancy or trade. Portable appliances are “as required by occupancy” unless a paragraph gives a number.

**SD takeaway:** A domestic building does not pick up sprinklers or a fire control centre from these paragraphs; a commercial or industrial building above 230 m² total floor area does pick up sprinklers through stairs, common corridors and toilets, and an industrial building picks up a fire control centre even when it is low-rise.

### 6. Hotels and other sleeping floors (paras 1.2, 2.2, 4.27, 4.28, 4.31, 4.32)

Hotels, low-rise and high-rise, carry the same core package: audio/visual advisory system (only on the 2 000 m² transient test), emergency generator, emergency lighting throughout and on all exit routes to ground level, manual alarm, fire detection, hydrant/hose reel, fireman’s lift as required by the fire-safety code, a fire control centre (minimum of one), staircase pressurization where topic 10 is met, sprinklers, static or dynamic smoke extraction, and ventilation/air-conditioning control.

| Hotel rule | Constraint |
|---|---|
| Sprinklers (both heights) | **All parts**, including staircases, common corridors, toilets and bathrooms |
| Sleeping-floor detection | If any part of a floor is used for sleeping accommodation: smoke detectors or multi-sensor detectors for the **entire floor**, excluding toilets, bathrooms, staircases, and open-sided corridors/balconies which are covered by sprinklers. Heat detectors are acceptable in electrical/mechanical rooms and kitchens |
| Guestroom sounders | Every smoke or multi-sensor detector in a guestroom is integrated with a proprietary sounder base |
| Open-sided corridor or balcony | Open side above safe parapet height on at least **50%** of the wall/side facing external air, if that space is to be excluded from the sleeping-floor detectors |
| Smoke extraction on the guest route | **All internal means of escape serving all guest rooms**, irrespective of cubical extent or compartment volume. That route is from outside the guest rooms to a pressurized or naturally ventilated staircase, a protected lobby, or open air, unless the route itself has openable windows to open air whose aggregate area **exceeds 6.25%** of the floor area of that route |
| Other smoke extraction | Atrium compartment exceeding **28 000 m³**, or any basement level or floor forming part of that compartment which exceeds **7 000 m³**; or any fire compartment exceeding **7 000 m³** with openable windows not exceeding **6.25%** of its floor area and a designed fire load likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²** |

The same sleeping-floor detector rule, including the 50% open-side test, applies to institutional buildings. In student hostels the bedroom smoke or multi-sensor detectors are integrated with proprietary sounder bases (paras 4.31 and 4.32). The Part II pressurization table treats hotels, hospitals and sanatoria as their own cubical-extent band (topic 10).

**SD takeaway:** Hotel guest-room floors are sprinklered through the bathrooms, and the corridor from the guest-room door to a vented or pressurized stair needs smoke extraction unless that corridor’s own openable windows exceed 6.25% of the corridor floor area.

### 7. Basements (paras 4.3, 4.4)

| Item | Total floor area not exceeding 230 m² | Total floor area exceeding 230 m² |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinklers | Not in the requirements list | **All parts**, fast-response heads, except areas where water is undesirable and except strong rooms and safe deposit vaults |
| Detection | Entire basement, except car parking areas, strong rooms and safe deposit vaults | Same exceptions |
| Hydrant/hose reel | Hose reels need not reach strong rooms and safe deposit vaults | Same |
| Emergency lighting and exit signs | Entire basement and all exit routes to ground level | Same |
| Fireman’s lift or firefighting and rescue stairway | As required by the fire-safety code | **Firefighting and rescue stairway** for basements of **three or more levels**, or as required by the fire-safety code |
| Staircase pressurization | Not in the requirements list | Basements of three or more levels where there is **no open-air access route for firemen**, cubical extent **exceeds 7 000 m³**, and designed fire load is likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²**. Number from the basement band in topic 10, not exceeding the stair count in the fire-safety code |
| Smoke extraction | Not in the requirements list | Any fire compartment exceeding **7 000 m³** with designed fire load likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²**; or an industrial basement; or basements of **three or more levels** except areas used solely for car parking |
| Audio/visual advisory | Not in the requirements list | Same 2 000 m² single-occupancy transient test as commercial floors |
| Automatic fixed installation other than water | Areas of special risk | Areas where water is undesirable |

Hot-smoke test may be stipulated, where the Director considers it necessary, for compartments with headroom of **12 m or more**, or with irregular geometry or extraordinary size. The same hot-smoke note appears on the above-ground smoke-extraction paragraphs.

**SD takeaway:** A basement over 230 m² is a fast-response sprinkler compartment including the stairs, and a basement of three or more levels needs a firefighting and rescue stairway plus smoke extraction except in areas used only for car parking.

### 8. Composite buildings, curtain walls and refuge floors (paras 4.16, 4.18, 4.19, 4.41, 5.6)

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Composite (para. 4.16) | Each use follows its own paragraph. Where the non-domestic portion is sprinklered, sprinklers in a staircase that communicates with the domestic portion extend **two further floors** above or below the non-domestic portion, and those heads are **fast-response** |
| Curtain wall below six storeys (para. 4.18) | Normal requirements according to occupancy |
| Curtain wall of and above six storeys (para. 4.19) | Normal requirements according to occupancy. Where sprinklers are required, the system is **one grade above** the accepted risk category, unless the building is constructed to the current Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings. Example in the code: light hazard becomes ordinary hazard group 1; ordinary hazard group 4 becomes high hazard |
| Refuge floor (para. 4.41) | The building’s required installations extend to the refuge floor as appropriate. An **external drencher** with an **independent water supply** protects **all external wall openings**, operated automatically by a quick-opening valve or deluge valve from approved heat detectors or sprinklers in the same areas, plus manual control |
| Open roof used as a refuge floor | Sprinkler or drencher is **not** required |
| Drencher performance (para. 5.6) | Water curtain not less than **10 litres/min per m²** of opening. Drencher heads not more than **2.4 m** apart. Discharge pressure at every head not less than **0.35 bar**. Water for **30 minutes**. One independent tank per block: if there are two refuge floors, size the tank on the floor with the larger external-wall opening area; three or more refuge floors are considered case by case. Elevated tank may feed by static pressure if flow and pressure are enough. Duty and standby pumps. Intermediate booster where the height between the topmost drencher head and the lowest drencher inlet exceeds **60 m**, with fire-engine boost at **800 kPa** upstream of the inlet and a bypass if the pump fails |
| Drencher inlet | Prominent position on the exterior of that block. Coupling centre **600 mm to 1 000 mm** above ground. All-round clearance not less than **100 mm** inside the enclosure |
| Drencher for compartmentation | Same specification as the refuge-floor drencher, except the water supply lasts for the required fire-resistance period |

**SD takeaway:** On a composite building, run the non-domestic staircase sprinklers two storeys into the domestic stairs with fast-response heads, and give every enclosed refuge floor its own drencher tank sized for 30 minutes on the floor with the larger wall openings.

| Battery room and electrical charging (para. 4.5) | Automatic fixed installation other than water where water is undesirable. Gas extraction to below the lower explosive limit. Manual alarm at **all exit doorways**, not at hose reels |

**SD takeaway:** A basement garage in a three-storey house still takes the electric-vehicle switch and extra extinguishers, and any lift that does not open into a protected lobby needs a smoke detector within 1.5 m of the door unless the building is sprinklered or is a domestic building of not more than three storeys.

Pressurization plant (para. 5.21): designed, and each drawing and calculation certified, by a registered professional engineer under Cap. 409 in building services, fire or mechanical engineering. Basis is British Standard 5588: Part 4: 1998. One system per pressurized staircase. Overall height under **11 m**: single or multiple injection. Overall height **11 m or more**: multiple injection only, air supplies not more than **12 m** apart. With refuge floors, measure that height from the lower refuge floor to the next refuge floor. Enclosure of the plant at least the fire resistance of the staircase served, or of the serviced or containing compartment, whichever is higher. Generally no fire or smoke dampers in the ductwork or shafts. Air-release points and actuators at a common area or a non-fireman’s-lift lobby. A low-resistance air path at every floor served, so the design flow through the door can vent to open air. Supply and exhaust air to the generator room is a separate rule (topic 11); pressurization intake follows clause 6.2 of BS 5588: Part 4.

| Smoke-extraction trigger above ground | Test |
|---|---|
| Commercial, audio/visual, and the atrium clause | Atrium compartment exceeding **28 000 m³**, or any basement level or floor of that compartment exceeding **7 000 m³**; or any fire compartment exceeding **7 000 m³** whose openable windows do not exceed **6.25%** of its floor area **and** whose designed fire load is likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²** |
| Industrial/godown | Fire compartment exceeding **7 000 m³**, with the same 6.25% window test and the same 1 135 MJ/m² fire load. The atrium sentence is not repeated in paras 4.29 and 4.30 |
| Hotels | Guest-room internal means of escape regardless of volume (topic 6), plus the atrium and 7 000 m³ tests |

Dynamic smoke-extraction geometry (para. 5.23): smoke travel not more than **30 m** to the nearest extract inlet, and at least one extract point in each **500 m²** of floor area (atria and tunnels agreed per project; hotels follow the internal-means-of-escape rules). Make-up air at least **80%** of the extract rate. Free-area velocities: non-mechanical make-up **3 m/s**; mechanical make-up **6 m/s**; extract **6 m/s**. Basements and commercial compartments: minimum **8 air changes per hour**. Any compartment of 7 000 m³ or less is designed as **7 000 m³**. Each basement compartment has its own system, and each such system has at least **two** independent plants and ductwork. Fire-service-installation rooms are not connected to another compartment’s extract. Hotel internal means of escape: not less than **10 air changes per hour**, and the distance between any supply point and any extract point along the corridor does not exceed **10 m**. One hotel corridor system may serve up to ten above-ground compartments but must be able to run **any two** at once. A commercial or industrial above-ground multi-zone system may serve up to ten compartments but fully operates **any one** zone. Smoke shafts contain **no other services**. Discharge at least **5 m** in any direction from air inlets or other openings, not into a means of escape or a free-air fireman’s staircase, and the bottom of the outlet at least **3 m** above the surrounding horizontal surface. Equipment handling smoke suitable for continuous operation at **250 °C** for not less than **1 hour**. If a fire-engineering smoke reservoir is used, its area is not larger than **2 000 m²**, and the method needs the Director’s approval. Flat oval ducts wider than **1 m** are not used. Aluminium sheet is not used.

**SD takeaway:** A sealed high-rise commercial or industrial building over 28 000 m³ with a fire load above 1 135 MJ/m² needs pressurized stairs if it has no natural venting; a hotel needs that pressurization from the window test alone, and its guest corridors are extracted at 10 air changes per hour with supply and extract not more than 10 m apart.

### 12. Sprinklers, ventilation shutdown and water mist (paras 5.23, 5.24, 5.27, 5.28, 5.29)

| System | Rule that affects the plan |
|---|---|
| Sprinklers (para. 5.24) | Design and install to the Loss Prevention Council Rules for Automatic Sprinkler Installations incorporating BS EN 12845, or another standard acceptable to the Director |
| Smoke system actuation (para. 5.23) | Smoke detectors for the area, preferably cross-zoned. Where sprinklers exist, a flow switch on the main sprinkler feed to the area also starts smoke extraction. Manual fire alarm does **not** start it. Remote on/off at the fire control panel |
| Ventilation/air-conditioning control (para. 5.27) | Stop mechanically induced air movement in the fire compartment. Exempt: staircase pressurization and smoke extraction; individual self-contained or split direct-expansion units not connected to ductwork; a minor system whose ducts stay inside one compartment **and** whose airflow does not exceed **1 000 litres/s**; kitchen, fume and toxic or explosive extract; systems that supply at low level and/or extract at high level without recirculation; an individual plant room or toilet ventilated directly outside and serving no other area |
| Shutdown method | Any one of: (A) smoke detection or sprinklers shut down fans serving that compartment; (B) duct smoke detectors in exhaust and/or recirculation; (C) the building fire alarm shuts down all fans in the building; (D) one smoke detector per **250 m²** of net floor area under slab or false ceiling. Plus manual override at the central fire control panel for every method |
| Water spray (para. 5.29) | Independent tank for at least **30 minutes** |
| Water mist (para. 5.28) | Prior approval, listed system, endorsed by a registered professional engineer under Cap. 409 in building services, fire or mechanical engineering, or a person acceptable to the Director. Acceptance is project by project. Initially limited to **mechanical plant rooms** |

**SD takeaway:** Do not use a water-mist system to delete sprinklers outside mechanical plant rooms; mist needs a project-by-project approval, and sprinklers follow the Loss Prevention Council Rules from the point the pipe enters the building.

### 13. What the inspection code in the same PDF adds (Inspection Code, Part I and Appendix 8)

The inspection code does not lay down hard-and-fast design rules. Special factors may vary any building, and the Director may require extra tests. Initial inspection is by a Fire Services inspecting officer, arranged with the Authorized Person and the registered fire service installation contractor, and only after the contractor has certified the installation in efficient working order. That signed form is a certificate under regulation 9 of the Fire Service (Installations and Equipment) Regulations (Cap. 95 sub. leg. B).

Appendix 8 sets minimum fire-resisting cables. Cables for automatic actuating devices, fixed installations, hydrant/hose-reel systems, drencher, foam, staircase pressurization, smoke extraction, street hydrants, water mist, water spray, fire-service water supply, and the cable from the emergency generator to the main essential switchboard, include British Standard 6387 Category CWZ or BS EN 50200 PH120 with a 120-minute survival time, among other listed options. Audio/visual advisory and emergency-lighting circuits distinguish standard (30-minute) and enhanced (120-minute) grades.

**SD takeaway:** Size and place the plant so the Part V clearances can be commissioned; the inspection checklists do not add a second coverage schedule.
