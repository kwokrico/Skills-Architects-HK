# FSD Circular Letter No. 3-2022 Annual Inspection Checklist for Fire Alarm Systems
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
26 September 2022 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: Introduces the annual inspection checklist for fire alarm systems, effective 1 December 2022. It states minimum inspection items against the minimum-FSI Code version relevant to the building and does not publish a separate alarm-design code.

## Regulatory Overview
The checklist is read with the completion and responsibility principles in FSD Circular Letter No. 4/2019, and conformity is to the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment relevant to the building and to the Code of Practice for Inspection, Testing and Maintenance of Installations and Equipment. It takes effect on 1 December 2022 and is to be reviewed after 12 months.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Panel arrangement (Annex item 1; Table I)
| Arrangement | Where it is inspected |
|---|---|
| F.S. control and indicating panel | Appendix I |
| Repeater panel | Appendix II |
| Mimic panel | Appendix III |
| External charger and battery | Appendix IV |
| No F.S. control and indicating panel; system controlled by the fixed fire pump control panel | Annual Inspection Checklist for Fire Hydrant/Hose Reel Systems |
| Direct telephone link, where provided | "Power On" amber indicator and "Normal" green indicator at the DTL fire signal box are lit, with no "Fire Alarm" indication; on manual-call-point activation the "Fire Alarm" red indicator lights and the signal is transmitted to the Service Provider |
| Drawings and log | As-built schematic at the pump room or enclosure where provided, and adjacent to the F.S. panel; as-built zoning schedule at the F.S. panel; log book in the fire control centre or F.S. control room, or near a status panel at the main entrance or caretaker's counter when there is no such room |

**SD takeaway:** Place the main panel, any repeater or mimic, the direct-telephone-link box and the log book in the fire control room or, where there is none, at the main entrance or caretaker's counter, with wall space for the schematic and zoning schedule.

### 2. Call points, sounders and visual alarms (items 2, 3 and 5)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Manual actuating point mounting | Surface mounted or semi-recessed, front face proud of the mounting surface, free from obstruction |
| Height | Installed at the appropriate level above finished floor; this circular states no numeric height |
| Marking | Manual call point or break-glass unit marked to BS EN 54-11, or another standard acceptable to the Director of Fire Services; push-button or manual-switch type labelled |
| Zone response | Activation operates all fire alarm devices in the corresponding alarm zone |
| Delay | Any delay between activation and operation of devices in the corresponding zone is within 3 seconds |
| Sound | Audio devices produce the required sound pressure level at the designated locations |
| Visual fire alarm | Flashing red light, labelled, positioned so attention is gained by direct view or by illumination of the surrounding area, and free from obstruction to that view |
| Flash rate | 30 to 120 flashes per minute (0.5 Hz to 2 Hz) |
| Open area | Visual alarms within the same field of view in any open communication area are synchronised |
| Place of public entertainment | Where licensing requires an emergency alert system, music and video sound and images are suppressed and visible and audible warnings are produced when a manual actuating point operates |
| No F.S. panel | Devices in the zone operate continuously until the activated manual actuating point is reset |
| Test sounding | Each test sounding normally lasts not more than 5 seconds and ceases for not less than 5 seconds; a real-fire sounding is normally continuous |

**SD takeaway:** Keep call-point faces proud of the wall, design each zone to sound within 3 seconds, and synchronise red visual alarms in open areas at 30 to 120 flashes per minute.

### 3. Cables and the effect of a short circuit (item 4; Appendix I, A1.3)
| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| BS 5839-1:1988 and relevant circular letters, where prolonged operation is required | Mineral-insulated copper-sheathed cable, or cable to BS 6387 AWX/SWX, or another fire resisting cable of the required rating, for fire alarm devices, F.S. panels, repeater panels, mimic panels and/or power supply |
| Same 1988 case | Cables protected by embedding in plaster, concrete or soil and/or by enclosure in a fire resistant or underground cable duct |
| BS 5839-1:2002+A2:2008 or BS 5839-1:2017 and relevant circular letters | Fire resisting cables of the required rating, including supports, for (i) critical signal paths between initiation points and fire alarm devices, (ii) extra-low-voltage supply from an external power supply unit, (iii) the final circuit providing low-voltage mains to the system, and (iv) the power supply to fire alarm devices |
| Mechanical protection | Cables other than mineral-insulated copper-sheathed cables and steel-wire-armoured cables protected against mechanical damage and rodent attack |
| Hazardous area | Devices, components and wiring of an explosion-protected type for the area classification, apparatus group and temperature class |
| Short circuit, BS 5839-1:2002+A2:2008 | Loss of protection limited to not more than one floor, plus a maximum of five devices (manual actuating points and/or fire alarm devices) on the floor immediately above and five devices on the floor immediately below |
| Short circuit, BS 5839-1:2017 | Loss of protection limited to not more than one floor |
| 2002+A2:2008 or 2017 | The audio device at the F.S. panel, or on the external wall where that is the applicable location, stays in full working order when a short circuit or an open circuit affects other audio devices |

**SD takeaway:** Match cable rating and loop layout to the BS 5839 edition the building must meet: the 2017 edition limits a short-circuit loss to one floor, and the 2002 edition with amendment 2 also allows five devices on the floors immediately above and below.

### 4. Batteries, charger and panel logic (Appendices I and IV)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Battery life | Marked with the month and year of installation; if the nominal design life is unknown it is deemed 4 years; a battery past that life is replaced with a secondary battery of nominal design life not less than 4 years |
| Unsealed batteries | Terminals covered with protective gel; plates submerged; low cells topped with distilled or de-ionized water; low-density electrolyte replaced |
| Charger on alarm load | With the battery disconnected and maximum alarm load on, charger output is not less than 95% of nominal voltage; a dummy load may be used instead of a full alarm-load test |
| Float voltage | Steady-state float charge, with charger supply and quiescent load connected and with no fire signal, within the battery manufacturer's range |
| Indication | Manual actuating point gives an audio alarm and a visual zone indication at the F.S. panel and, where provided, at repeater and mimic panels; the zone indication stays lit until the point is reset and the Reset button is pressed |
| Silence | Alarm mute/silence stops audio devices in the building; a later manual actuating point in a new zone re-operates the devices for that zone |
| Evacuate | Where an Evacuate button is provided, it actuates every fire alarm device on the system |
| Other systems | A fire alarm signal is transmitted to the control panels of other fire service installations, and to other installations, where the interface applies |

**SD takeaway:** Specify secondary batteries with a nominal design life of at least 4 years and a charger whose output under alarm load is at least 95% of nominal voltage.
