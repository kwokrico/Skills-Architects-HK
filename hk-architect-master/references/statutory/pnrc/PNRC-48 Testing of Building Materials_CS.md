# PNRC-48 Testing of Building Materials
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue October 2001 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs whose tests the Building Authority will rely on for building materials and proprietary fire-resisting products. It is not a Building Authority certificate or test report for a product, and the Buildings Ordinance contains no provision for the Building Authority to issue one.

## Regulatory Overview
The Building Authority relies on authorized persons, registered structural engineers and independent laboratories to test and certify materials and products, and on completion the certification route is Practice Note for Registered Contractors 25. Laboratories are those accredited by the Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme (HOKLAS), or by other accreditation bodies that have mutual recognition agreements or arrangements with HOKLAS, and the test must be inside the laboratory’s scope of accreditation.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Materials normally tested by an accredited laboratory (paras 3–4)

The following list is not exhaustive:

| Material or product |
|---|
| Concrete cube and core |
| Steel reinforcement |
| Reinforcement splices and couplers |
| Curtain wall systems |
| Fire resisting products |

**SD takeaway:** Concrete, reinforcement, couplers, curtain wall and fire-resisting products are specified for test by a HOKLAS laboratory, or a laboratory under a HOKLAS mutual-recognition arrangement, and only for tests inside that laboratory’s scope.

### 2. Proprietary fire-resisting products (paras 5–6)

Acceptability is judged against the performance standards in Building (Construction) Regulation 90 and the current Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction, in particular for compartment walls, protective cover to structural steel, fire-resisting doors and other materials used in fire-resisting construction. Compliance may rest on a test or assessment report from an accredited laboratory as in paragraph 3. The AP or RSE ensures that the report was prepared by an accredited laboratory and is still within its validity period.

**SD takeaway:** Fire doors, compartment walls and structural-steel fire protection need an in-date accredited test or assessment report against Building (Construction) Regulation 90 and the Fire Resisting Construction code; the note does not state a validity length.

### 3. Three named assessing organisations (paras 7–8)

A letter of 25 July 1994 listed three assessing organisations which are not accredited laboratories. With this practice note that list ceases to have effect. Assessment reports on fire-resisting products prepared by the three organisations remain acceptable until further notice:

| Organisation | Limit stated in the note |
|---|---|
| Building Research Establishment (UK) | Assessment reports |
| International Fire Consultants Ltd. (UK) | Limited to assessment reports endorsed by Mr Peter E Jackman |
| Warrington Fire Research Consultants (UK) | Assessment reports |

**SD takeaway:** New fire-product evidence should come from an accredited laboratory; older assessment reports from those three organisations can still be used until Buildings Department says otherwise, and International Fire Consultants reports only if endorsed by Mr Peter E Jackman.
