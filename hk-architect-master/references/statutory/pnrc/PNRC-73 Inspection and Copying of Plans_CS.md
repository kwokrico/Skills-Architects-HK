# PNRC-73 Inspection and Copying of Plans
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Previously known as PNAP 110. First issue February 1985 / Last revision December 2010 / This revision July 2016 (AD/CS) (Paras. 7 to 11 & 14 amended) | Buildings Department

> Scope note: This note is how the public may inspect and copy building records the Building Authority holds for the purposes of the Buildings Ordinance. It does not authorise inspection of a project that is not yet complete, and it does not transfer copyright when the authorized person, registered structural engineer, or registered geotechnical engineer changes.

## Regulatory Overview
Plans and other documents submitted to the Building Authority for the purposes of the Buildings Ordinance are public records, and, where the copyright owners do not specifically deny access and subject to paragraphs 2 to 13, the Building Authority may under section 36G, on request and payment of the prescribed fee, permit inspection and issue copies. Under the obligation to confidence, inspection and copies are not permitted until the subject building or works have been completed, and the records that may then be inspected or copied are confined to the two classes in the table below.

## Critical main topics and subtopics
### 1. Only completed projects, and only for the declared purpose
The Building Authority takes into account the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486), the Copyright Ordinance (Cap. 528), the Code on Access to Information, and the obligation to confidence.

Records that may be inspected or copied, after completion:

- (i) the latest approved plans, structural calculations, and related documents of completed building and minor works projects; and
- (ii) the latest submitted plans and details of Class I and Class II minor works, the latest submitted plans or description of Class III minor works, photos, and related documents of completed minor works under the simplified requirements.

The Building Authority may permit inspection or copies only to facilitate ascertaining:

- (a) matters relating to the construction of any building or the carrying out of any building works (including minor works) or street works;
- (b) whether a building, building works (including minor works), or street works have been completed or carried out in compliance with the Buildings Ordinance or any other enactment; and
- (c) other matters the Building Authority considers appropriate, in the interest of the public.

The applicant declares the matter. An application under (c) is considered only if justified with relevant special circumstances, or if accompanied by an authorization from the copyright owner with a positive statement that he is the owner of the copyright.

**SD takeaway:** Existing-building schematic work can be based on the latest approved plans and structural calculations, and on the completed minor-works records in (ii), only after those works are complete; an unfinished submission is not available for inspection or copy under this note.

### 2. Building Information Centre, BRAVO, occupation permit, and delivery
Fees are those prescribed in regulation 42 of the Building (Administration) Regulations.

**SD takeaway:** An occupation-permit copy for schematic existing-building work is Form BIC4 with the correct lot number and address; a copy of plans without a prior visit is Form BIC-3, with the written fee reply coming within 10 days.

### 3. A new authorized person does not inherit copyright in the resigned authorized person's plans
Plans of buildings are protected as copyright artistic works under the Copyright Ordinance. On a change of authorized person, registered structural engineer, or registered geotechnical engineer, the new one who wishes to use the approved plans of building works, or the submitted plans and details of minor works, of the resigned one, to build or to amend, must have obtained the copyright owner's consent. Without that consent the new appointee may infringe the copyright. The note says these points are not an exhaustive account of the Copyright Ordinance, and that a legal advisor should be enlisted as necessary.

**SD takeaway:** A schematic redesign that continues another consultant's approved plans, or another consultant's submitted minor-works plans, waits on written copyright consent from the copyright owner.
