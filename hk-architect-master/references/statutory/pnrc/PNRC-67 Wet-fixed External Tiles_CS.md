# PNRC-67 Wet-fixed External Tiles
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue July 2008; last revision October 2020; this revision July 2021 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs design and construction of ceramic or mosaic tiles wet-fixed on cement-sand rendering as an external finish, aimed at a minimum safety standard against detachment. It is not mandatory to the exclusion of other systems: alternative products may be used with professional judgement, and certain external rendering, external wall tiles and roof finishes on an existing building may be minor works under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNRC 71.

## Regulatory Overview
Wet-fixed tiles are normally adhered to rendering with proprietary adhesive or cement-sand slurry, and the rendering is bonded to the external wall with spatterdash and/or a bonding agent. Authorized persons, registered structural engineers and contractors are advised to follow these guidelines where that system is used, and Appendix B lists the standards relevant to external rendering and tiling.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Joints, render thickness and substrate (App. A paras 2–13)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Joints between tiles | Usually 8 mm to 10 mm, and in any case not less than 3 mm, so that grout can be inserted |
| Movement joints | Formed to the full depth of the rendering at the time of rendering, and sealed; between panels at regular intervals; where tiling abuts restraining surfaces; over construction joints, control joints or movement joints in the substrate |
| Cold joints | Expanded metal lathing fixed over cold joints before rendering |
| Concrete drying before render | Six weeks recommended |
| Further coats | Each intermediate layer normally several days, or the manufacturer’s period for a proprietary product; final coat cured and left to dry for at least two weeks |
| Each render coat | Not less than 8 mm and not greater than 16 mm |
| Total render thickness | Not more than 20 mm; a proprietary product follows the manufacturer’s thickness |
| Spatterdash | Preferably within 24 hours after striking formwork; if later, remedial measures including a bonding agent, agreed with the AP and RSE as applicable |
| Substrate | Rigid and sound; honeycombs, loose particles, dirt, oil, grease, dust, paint and efflorescence removed; dampened but not too wet |
| Sequence | Work downwards, from higher points to lower points, where practicable; tap and rectify debonded rendering before tiles are fixed |
| Mechanical keys | Spatterdash, or scratching or bush hammering; projected horizontal fins to subdivide tiles into panels of reasonable size are encouraged wherever practicable |
| Uneven substrate | Reinforcement or expanded metal lathing anchored to the concrete, or a proprietary adhesion system, where render would otherwise be uneven or too thick |

**SD takeaway:** Panel the elevation with full-depth movement joints and optional horizontal fins, keep render coats between 8 mm and 16 mm and the total at not more than 20 mm, and allow six weeks after the concrete before rendering.

### 2. Tiles, adhesives, grout and mesh (App. A paras 14–23)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Tile area | Tiles larger than 0.1 m² secured by mechanical means |
| Weight | Controlled and considered in the design; size and thickness selected with the adhesive system |
| Poor water absorption | Not recommended externally; if used, special polymer-modified adhesive to the manufacturer’s recommendations; compatibility tests if in doubt, before work starts |
| Water absorption, external ceramic tiles | Not more than 0.5% up to first-floor height; not more than 3% above first-floor height |
| Ceramic tiles with cement slurry | Soak tiles in water for a minimum of 30 minutes, then stack to drain surface water immediately before tiling; dampen the rendering |
| Proprietary adhesive | Tiles and rendering not too wet |
| Paper-faced mosaic | Adhesive covers the entire area of each tessera |
| Bonding agent | Usually mixed with cement and sand before application, and also used as an admixture in the mortar; follow the manufacturer |
| Grout | Preferably type CG2 to BS EN 13888:2009; low shrinkage and good adhesion; start as soon as the bedding has hardened sufficiently |
| Sealant | Must take the anticipated movement without losing adhesion, and resist water and ultraviolet light |
| Reinforcement | Austenitic stainless steel wire, preferably 2.5 mm diameter, welded into a mesh of approximately 50 mm × 50 mm; fixings also austenitic stainless steel |
| Galvanised reinforcement | Only for limited areas in sheltered locations; not recommended externally or where constantly exposed to moisture |
| Manufactured sand | May replace river sand; for private building projects follow Technical Circular (Works) No. 8/2018 |

**SD takeaway:** Keep external tiles at or below 0.1 m² unless they are mechanically fixed, use 0.5% absorption up to first floor and 3% above, and where mesh is used specify austenitic stainless steel about 2.5 mm at about 50 mm centres.

### 3. Pull-off test and maintenance (paras 6 and App. A paras 25–30)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Pull-off test | On-site, to confirm the tiles will perform; proposal, acceptance criteria and non-compliance plan developed with the AP/RSE before tiling starts |
| Sampling | At least three samples of each type of wall tile for every ten typical floors or part thereof, for tiles on rendering over concrete, tiles direct on concrete, or slurry on rendering over concrete |
| Sample location | Selected by the AP/RSE; not taken from tiles installed over waterproofing layers |
| Laboratory | Accredited under HOKLAS for the particular test; results on a HOKLAS endorsed certificate |
| Infra-red scanning | May be used by a specialist, as appropriate, to locate cavities, as a supplement |
| Workers | Trade-tested; for proprietary products, trained by the manufacturer |
| In use | Regular inspection of tile surfaces and joints; owners of new buildings informed to arrange debonding surveys at periodic intervals, by hammer tapping supplemented as appropriate with infra-red thermography and/or other void detection |

The note does not state a numeric pull-off stress. Appendix B names, among others, BS 5385-2:2015, BS EN 12004-1:2017, BS EN 12004-2:2017, BS EN 13888:2009, BS EN 13914-1:2016, BS EN 14411:2016, BS EN 197-1:2011, BS 4483:2005, BS EN 13139:2013 and BS 6213:2000+A1:2010.

**SD takeaway:** Allow at least three pull-off samples of each tile type for every ten typical floors or part thereof, chosen by the AP/RSE, tested by a HOKLAS laboratory, and not taken from tiles over a waterproofing layer.
