# PNRC-36 Lift Maintenance and Replacement
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued July 1997 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Fire-resisting construction rules while lift landing doors are opened or removed for maintenance or replacement. It does **not** size lift cars, shafts or machine rooms.

## Regulatory Overview
Paragraph 11.2 of the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction 1996 requires a door at a lift landing that gives access to the car to have a fire resistance period of not less than 1 hour with regard to integrity, and that door should normally stay closed unless a lift car stops at that landing. This note sets the rules for maintenance and replacement so that open or removed landing doors do not let fire spread between floor compartments through the lift shaft and its openings.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Landing doors kept open
All landing doors should remain closed during the works, except as follows.

| Situation | Limit |
|---|---|
| Normal case | No more than one landing door in a lift shaft open at any one time |
| Door open where the car is not level with that landing | Attended by lift workers |
| More than one door open in the same shaft | Maximum **three** landing doors open at the same time, and **no hot works or welding** |

**SD takeaway:** Lift-maintenance method statements have to keep a shaft to one open landing door, or to three only if hot works and welding are excluded.

### 2. Landing doors taken down
Removal of more than one landing door in a lift shaft at any one time should be avoided. If more than one is removed at the same time, the openings are protected by temporary hoarding with a fire resistance period of not less than **one hour**.

**SD takeaway:** Replacing more than one landing door at once means a one-hour temporary hoarding over those openings, not an open shaft.

### 3. Openings and access doors in the hoarding
Openings in the hoarding are limited to small ventilation openings and access doors to the lift shaft.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Ventilation opening | Not exceeding **5,500 mm²**, at the upper portion of the hoarding |
| Number | Limited to **two per liftway**, subject to a maximum of **four** in the hoarding |
| Access door | Same fire resistance period as the hoarding; self-closing; locking device against unauthorised access; readily opened from the inside without a key |

**SD takeaway:** Temporary shaft hoardings are one-hour construction with only the vent and access-door allowances above.

### 4. Escape routes, materials in the shaft, and unattended works
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Escape route | Temporary hoarding enclosing a lift shaft should not, as far as reasonably practicable, obstruct or reduce the width of any escape route |
| Works inside the shaft | Scaffoldings, formworks, plankings, struttings and other temporary works in non-combustible materials |
| Lunch breaks and end of the day | Before unfinished works are left unattended, return opened landing doors to the closed position, or enclose the openings with fire-resisting hoarding |

The note asks registered contractors to apply these requirements and to bring them to everyone carrying out lift maintenance or replacement under their supervision. It states that the requirements will be incorporated into the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction in due course.

**SD takeaway:** Lobby and escape width at lift landings must still work when a one-hour hoarding is standing in front of the shaft, and the shaft must be closed or hoarded whenever the works are left unattended.
