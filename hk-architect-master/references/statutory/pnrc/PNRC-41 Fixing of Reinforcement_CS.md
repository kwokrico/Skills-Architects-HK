# PNRC-41 Fixing of Reinforcement
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue March 1998; this revision July 2003 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs good practice in fixing bar and fabric reinforcement so that cover and position survive construction loads. It is not a substitution for the bar schedules on the approved plans, and it does not authorise fixing a batch before the PNRC 33 tests on that batch are complete.

## Regulatory Overview
Displaced reinforcement has been a major cause of undue corrosion, spalling and, in some cases, collapse. Bar and fabric reinforcement from each batch is not fixed until testing of that batch has been completed, as described in Practice Note for Registered Contractors 33.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Bar condition, spacers and ties (paras 3–6 and 8)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Rust and damage | Remove loose rust, excessive flaky rust or mill-scale by wire-brushing; do not use badly corroded, damaged or scaling steel |
| Spacers, chairs and supports | Enough to hold reinforcement in the location on the approved plans and to maintain the specified minimum cover at all positions |
| Spacing of spacers and chairs | Maximum 1.5 m; for 20 mm diameter or smaller bars, reduce the spacing to avoid sagging |
| Spacer material | Concrete, or a proprietary plastic or concrete type; as small as practicable; able to carry the weight of reinforcement and construction loads without breaking, deforming or overturning |
| Spacer quality | Strength and durability not less than that of the surrounding concrete |
| Ties | Enough intersecting and lapping bars tied with tying wire, tying devices or clips; ends of those ties must not encroach into the cover |
| Laps, joints and connectors | Only at the specified positions; connectors fixed to the manufacturer’s recommendations and with the manufacturer’s equipment |

**SD takeaway:** Cover on the drawings is held by spacers at not more than 1.5 m, closer where bars are 20 mm or smaller, and those spacers must be at least as strong and durable as the surrounding concrete.

### 2. Access, cantilevers and free-standing cages (paras 7 and 9)

Reinforcement must not be contaminated or displaced by people walking on it or by concrete wheelbarrows. Access is by planks, ladders or other methods agreed by the AP/RSE, independently supported and well clear of the completed reinforcement. Subsequent trades, including conduits in floor slabs and concealed building-services conduits, must not displace bars or spacers. On cantilevered projecting structures, extra chairs and ties are required so that completed top reinforcement does not deflect or displace under access or other construction loads. Free-standing reinforcement is secured and braced against wind and other loads, and prefabricated cages are supported and braced before lifting.

**SD takeaway:** Cantilever top steel needs extra chairs and ties on the drawings’ fixing notes, and slab conduits must be routed so they cannot push that steel down.

### 3. Supervision before the pour (paras 10–11)

The registered contractor provides continuous supervision so that fixing follows the approved plans and this practice note, and so that reinforcement is not displaced during placing and compaction. The AP/RSE provides adequate quality supervision. The contractor informs the AP/RSE to inspect all reinforcement after fixing and well before concreting, so that rectification is still possible. For walls, columns and similar members, that inspection is arranged before formwork is fully erected. The contractor then keeps the reinforcement in the approved condition until concreting is complete.

**SD takeaway:** Wall and column reinforcement is inspected before the formwork is closed, and the contractor’s supervision of fixing is continuous through compaction.
