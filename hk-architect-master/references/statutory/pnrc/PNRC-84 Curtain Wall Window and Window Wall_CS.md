# PNRC-84 Curtain Wall Window and Window Wall
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue February 2024 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs performance, tempered-glass quality control, structural sealant, spider fixings, locking devices, curtain-wall safety tests, and spare-glass records for curtain wall, window and window wall. Aluminium-window design and installation are also covered by PNRC 47. A curtain-wall safety test is not required for repair or replacement under the fast-track procedures in PNAP ADM-19 or under minor works item 1.61.

## Regulatory Overview
Curtain wall is designed to sections 29 to 31 of the Building (Construction) Regulation, including wind loads, the horizontal imposed loads in section 9 of and Table 3 in the Schedule where no protective barrier is provided, protection of openings, corrosion protection and material quality. Window and window wall also meet the Building (Construction) Regulation, the lighting and ventilation requirements of the Building (Planning) Regulations, and the relevant codes, and certain repair, replacement, construction or alteration in an existing building may be minor works under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNAP APP-147.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Accepted references (para 3 and App. A)

Standards commonly used, and acceptable to the Building Authority, are in Appendix A and PNAP APP-53. Appendix A is not exhaustive. A chosen design standard is applied consistently, and the Building Regulations take precedence if they conflict with another standard. Appendix A names the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Glass 2018 for glass design, workmanship and testing of curtain wall, window and window wall, and lists stainless and steel tolerance standards: BS EN 10029:2010 (hot-rolled steel plates 3 mm thick or above), BS EN ISO 9445:2006, BS EN ISO 9445-1:2010, BS EN ISO 9445-2:2010, BS EN 10048:1997, BS EN 10051:1991+A1:1997 and BS EN 10095:1999.

**SD takeaway:** Adopt the Glass Code 2018 and one consistent design standard for the whole curtain wall, window or window wall system, with the Building Regulations overriding that standard where they differ.

### 2. Tempered glass: factory, heat soak and supervision (paras 6–7)

Heat-soak processing conforming to BS EN 14179-1:2016 and the Glass Code is carried out to all tempered glass panes used in curtain wall, window and window wall. The following are imposed on approval of plans:

| Requirement | Rule |
|---|---|
| Factory | ISO 9001 quality assurance certification, under item 6 of section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance |
| Quality Assurance Scheme | Submitted at least 14 days before production starts, under regulation 10 of the Building (Administration) Regulations, with the RSE’s statement that the scheme meets the Buildings Ordinance and the approved plans; items are those in clause 9.1.3 of the Glass Code |
| Heat-soak compliance reports | Issued by the manufacturer, endorsed by the RSE, in accordance with clause 9.3 of the Glass Code, before the occupation permit application or Form BA14, as appropriate |
| Quality supervision plan | By the RSE and the registered contractor, at least 14 days before tempered-glass production; standard form in Appendix B |
| RSE quality control supervisor | Grade T3 technically competent person under the RSE’s stream, Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 (2021 edition); supervises at least 30% of the tempered glass panes used in the project |
| Contractor quality control coordinator | Grade T1 under the contractor’s stream; full-time continuous supervision of the heat-soak process of all tempered panes; measures glass surface temperature with the coordinator’s own data logger at one-minute intervals; record kept in the factory |
| Log books | Names, qualifications and heat-soak details kept in the factory |
| Videotelephony, if the RSE’s supervisor uses it | Two-way audio and video; colour; resolution not less than 480p; non-rewritable DVD-ROM; assistant in the factory is not a member of the contractor’s supervisory team; videos and log book submitted within 14 days after the heat-soak process for each batch; RSE certifies each disc with a permanent marker |
| Quality supervision report | By the RSE before the occupation permit application: confirmation that supervision followed the plan, copies of both log books, and a soft copy of the data-logger record |

**SD takeaway:** Tempered glass is an ISO 9001, heat-soaked product, with the quality scheme and supervision plan lodged at least 14 days before production, a T3 supervisor on at least 30% of panes, and a full-time T1 coordinator logging temperature every minute.

### 3. Heat-soak process checks (App. B Annex 1)

| Check | Figure stated in the note |
|---|---|
| Oven calibration | Clause 9.3.2 of the Glass Code and BS EN 14179-1:2016; reports kept in the factory |
| Thermocouples | Calibrated at 6-month intervals as recommended in HOKLAS Supplementary Criteria No. 2 |
| Factory thermocouples | A minimum of 8, installed and monitored to clause 9.3.3 of the Glass Code |
| Coordinator’s thermocouple | One additional thermocouple, read on the coordinator’s own data logger |
| Clearance between panels | A minimum of 20 mm, for heated-air circulation |
| Start of heating | All panels at ambient temperature |
| End of heating | When the surface temperature of the last glass reaches 250°C |
| Upper temperature | Surface temperature not allowed to exceed 290°C; time above 270°C minimised |
| Heating rate | Maximum 3°C per minute |
| Holding phase | Starts when every pane has reached 250°C; duration at least 120 minutes; surface held at 260°C ± 10°C |
| Opening the oven | Doors not opened until the control-element reading has dropped to 70°C |
| After the cycle | Number of broken glasses recorded; fragmentation test carried out |

**SD takeaway:** The heat-soak cycle used for specification and programme is a 20 mm minimum gap, heating at not more than 3°C per minute to 250°C without exceeding 290°C, then a hold of at least 120 minutes at 260°C ± 10°C, and the oven stays shut until 70°C.

### 4. Structural sealant (para 8)

Structural sealant must be compatible with the glazing system and the structural frame it touches. Before the occupation permit application or Form BA14, submit compliance certificates made by the sealant manufacturer — sealant compatibility test report, sealant adhesion test report and print review report in accordance with the Glass Code — together with a deglazing test report. The RSE who prepared the plans appends a statement that the acceptance criteria appropriate to the test have been met.

**SD takeaway:** A structurally sealed façade needs compatibility, adhesion, print-review and deglazing certificates, accepted by the RSE, before occupation permit or BA14.

### 5. Spider fixings — proof load (paras 9–10 and App. C)

Plans show mechanical properties, dimensions, load capacities and the proprietary model or series. The fixing at the glass must accommodate all anticipated movements. Proof load testing follows Appendix C and the Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Steel 2011, after completion of the works. The RSE endorses and submits the report before occupation permit or BA14. Proof load tests are not required if that type of spider fixing is already in the Central Data Bank under PNAP ADM-20 and uploaded to the Buildings Department website.

| Test rule | Figure |
|---|---|
| Sampling | At least 1%, or 3 numbers, whichever is more, of each representative type |
| Setup | Compatible with the fixing details on the approved plans |
| Test load | Clause 16.2.1.2 of the Steel Code 2011; actual dead load present during the test may be taken as the weight of the spider component; remainder of dead load may be taken as the weight of the attachment (for example the glass) |
| Application | Applied and released in at least 5 increments and 5 decrements |
| Readings | Taken only when deformation is completely stable; at least three readings at 5-minute intervals once the test load is reached, until there is no significant increase |
| Creep | None for a period of at least 15 minutes under the test load |
| Plot | Substantial linear behaviour under the test load |
| Residual deformation | On removal of the test load, not exceeding 5% of the maximum deformation recorded |

**SD takeaway:** Specify the spider model on the plans and allow a proof-load sample of at least 1% or 3 pieces, whichever is more, unless that model is already on the Central Data Bank.

### 6. Locking devices and openable sashes (paras 11–15 and App. D)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Materials | Durable and non-combustible |
| Layout | Evenly distributed along the sash or sub-frame |
| Ultimate design strength | Characteristic strength divided by a factor of safety of 1.8 |
| Proof load | Appendix D; report endorsed by the RSE and submitted before occupation permit or BA14 |
| Exemption | Not required if that type is already in the Central Data Bank |
| Sampling | At least 0.1%, or 5 numbers, whichever is more, of each type; setup compatible with the approved fixing details |
| Loading | Test load applied at a constant rate |
| Characteristic strength | Average test load at failure minus K times the standard deviation |
| K | For at least 5 specimens, K may be taken as 3.41; a K-factor for a larger number of specimens may be used if appropriate |
| One handle | A single handle bar should not be connected to more than 8 locking points |
| Top-hung sash | Size not exceeding 2.5 m² |
| Side-hung sash | Width not exceeding 700 mm |
| Hinges | Adequate to hold the weight of the sash or sub-frame |
| Assembly | Registered contractor provides site supervision so that fabrication tolerance does not add moment to the locking devices |

**SD takeaway:** Limit a top-hung sash to 2.5 m² and a side-hung sash to 700 mm width, design locking devices at a factor of safety of 1.8, and do not run one handle to more than 8 locking points.

### 7. Curtain-wall safety test (paras 16–17)

A safety test is required under item 6 of section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance. It is carried out by a laboratory accredited by HOKLAS, or by a body with a mutual recognition agreement or arrangement with HOKLAS, and the test is within that laboratory’s scope. Reports are on a HOKLAS endorsed certificate or an equivalent certificate or report, with the RSE’s statement that the acceptance criteria have been met, and are submitted before occupation permit or BA14. The test is not required for curtain-wall repair or replacement under the fast-track procedures in PNAP ADM-19, or under minor works item 1.61. Test panes are chosen by engineering judgement, with mathematical models where appropriate, and it is desirable to specify the representative panes on the curtain-wall plans. Detailed requirements are in clause 8.3.1 of the Glass Code 2018.

**SD takeaway:** Name the representative safety-test panes on the curtain-wall plans; the HOKLAS test is an occupation-permit item except for ADM-19 fast-track repair and minor works item 1.61.

### 8. Spare glass (paras 18–19 and App. E)

Inspection, maintenance and repair follow Annex D of the Glass Code. At completion of a new building or of alterations and additions, spare panes may be reserved. The original registered general building contractor confirms they were made for that project, puts the approval and consent letters, certificates, test reports, compliance report and quality assurance scheme into the maintenance manual, labels each pane with a unique identification number, and records them on Schedule A. Owners keep a traceable quantity record by type.

For later alterations and additions that need approval, the RSE checks the spare-glass documents, states on the plans that original-project spares will be used and will be inspected before installation, inspects them against Annex D of the Glass Code, and submits Schedule B with Form BA14. If a Schedule A completed and endorsed by the original contractor is submitted with that BA14, copies of the heat-soak quality supervision plan and quality supervision report for those spare tempered panes are not required. Minor-works use of spare panes follows the Technical Guidelines on the Minor Works Control System. Schedule B for minor works is endorsed by the prescribed building professional for Class I, or by the authorized signatory of the prescribed registered contractor for Classes II and III.

**SD takeaway:** If spare tempered panes are to be reused, Schedule A must be in the maintenance manual at completion, because that endorsed schedule is what allows a later BA14 to omit a fresh heat-soak supervision plan and report.
