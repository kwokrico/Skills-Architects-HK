# PNRC-80 Approval and Consent Conditions
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue September 2020; this revision March 2025 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: PNRC 80 describes conditions and requirements the Building Authority may impose under the Buildings Ordinance when approving plans and when granting consent, and it summarises common ones in Appendix A. The approval or consent letter for the project is the binding list. Appendix A states that its items are not exhaustive, and the note points specific material and work-type conditions to the BD website.

## Regulatory Overview

Under section 17 of the Buildings Ordinance, conditions imposed on approval and on consent are case or task specific and generally cover quality supervision, qualified supervision, quality assurance and control, material testing, instrumentation to monitor the effect of the works, and record documentation. They are complied with before the consent application, during the works, and/or at certification of completion of building works or street works.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Quality supervision and how continuous supervision is judged

The AP, RSE and RGE provide periodic supervision. The registered contractor provides continuous supervision, in accordance with the supervision plan. Quality supervision is part of the supervision plan system in the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 and the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans 2009, so that the works follow the Buildings Ordinance and its subsidiary regulations, the approved plans, and the approval and consent conditions. Further guidance on quality supervision is in APP-158.

The manner of continuous supervision varies with the task, its scale and complexity, the stage of the works, and whether a non-conformity can be identified and rectified during and after that sequence. An intermittent absence of a technically competent person must not interrupt continuous supervision of the works, and must not leave a chance of improper performance or a serious consequence. Connecting steel reinforcing bars to type 2 mechanical couplers is supervised by full-time TCP(s) of the registered contractor. Where the quality of a procedure can still be checked at a hold point after that procedure, and non-conformities can be resolved before the work is covered or the next stage starts, the note’s example is laying and fixing of steel reinforcing bars: TCPs of the various streams may use engineering judgement to inspect at and between hold points.

**SD takeaway:** Specify type 2 mechanical coupler splices only with full-time registered-contractor supervision in the quality supervision arrangements; bar fixing can use hold points while the bars are still open to inspection.

### 2. Qualified supervision and the Smart Site Safety alert systems

Under item 6 of section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance, qualified supervision may be imposed for certain stages of construction or for particular operations.

With effect from 1 July 2025, where the estimated total cost of building works exceeds $30 million, conditions requiring qualified supervision by both of the following, under the Smart Site Safety System, are imposed under item 6(e) of section 17(1):

| System | Zone |
|---|---|
| Mobile Plant Alert System | Unsafe acts / dangerous situation alert for the mobile plant operation danger zone |
| Tower Crane Alert System | Unsafe acts / dangerous situation alert for the tower crane lifting zone |

Those conditions are imposed on granting (a) the first approval of plans or a major revision of plans, and/or (b) the first consent to the commencement and carrying out of building works that involve mobile plants and/or tower cranes. The works named are demolition, site formation, excavation and lateral support, foundation, pile cap and superstructure. The same conditions are imposed on the first approval and/or the first consent for an alteration and addition project that involves structural works and meets the $30 million threshold.

For superstructure, the circular letter of 28 March 2024 already required these two systems, with effect from 1 July 2024, on the first approval of superstructure plans or a major revision of superstructure plans, and on the subsequent consent for those works.

The authorized person declares, under regulation 10 of the Building (Administration) Regulations, whether the estimated total cost of building works of the project exceeds $30 million. The declaration covers demolition, site formation, excavation and lateral support, foundation, pile cap, superstructure, and alteration and addition works. It is submitted with the first consent application for any of those works, whichever application is submitted earlier. A later consent application does not require a further declaration. Failure to submit the declaration with that first consent application may lead to refusal of consent under section 16(3)(b) of the Buildings Ordinance. Where the declaration states that the estimated total cost exceeds $30 million, the Building Authority imposes the alert-system conditions on the first consent for all of the work types named above.

**SD takeaway:** If building-works cost will exceed $30 million and the site will use mobile plant or a tower crane, allow the two alert systems in the first approval and first consent, and place the authorized person’s cost declaration on the earliest consent application.

### 3. Materials, monitoring of adjoining property, and records

Section 3 of the Building (Construction) Regulation requires that materials used in building works or street works are of a nature and quality suitable for their intended use, are adequately mixed or prepared, and are applied, used or fixed so as to perform their intended functions. They are adequately tested by recognised tests. Supervision and quality assurance and control run through production and construction. The manufacturer’s recommendations on installation, operation and maintenance are followed. The material is used after the testing requirements of the Buildings Ordinance and its subsidiary regulations are complied with and the acceptance criteria are met. If any material is used before testing, full traceability is required so that material which fails the acceptance criteria can be rectified or replaced.

During construction, the AP, RSE, RGE and registered contractor see that vibration, tilting and movement of adjacent buildings, structures, land, streets or services, and where appropriate the change in groundwater level, are monitored and assessed so the works do not cause undue effect on them.

Under regulation 10 of the Building (Administration) Regulations, the Building Authority may require record plans and reports at stages of the works, for inspection by the Buildings Department and/or for certification of completion. The supervision team is kept aware of the imposed conditions, and a copy of all approval and consent letters is kept on site.

**SD takeaway:** Specify materials that stay traceable to a recognised test until acceptance, and allow monitoring of adjoining buildings, streets, services and groundwater where the works can affect them.

### 4. Appendix A — common conditions and documents

Under item 6 of section 17(1), the following may be imposed at approval and/or consent. The note says the list is not exhaustive, and that the letter for the project is to be studied and observed.

| Condition | What the note specifies |
|---|---|
| (a) Qualified supervision by experienced and competent persons | The RSE assigns a quality control supervisor to supervise the works, sets the inspection frequency at not less than once a week, and devises checklists. Minimum qualification and experience match TCP grade T3 in the RSE’s stream under the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009. The RGBC or registered specialist contractor assigns a quality control co-ordinator for full-time on-site supervision and devises checklists. Minimum qualification and experience match TCP grade T1 in that contractor’s stream. Names, qualifications, date, time, items and results go in an inspection log book kept on site for the Buildings Department. The number of supervisors matches the scale of the works. For Type 2 mechanical couplers splicing steel reinforcing bars, the co-ordinator’s minimum is revised from grade T1 to grade T3. |
| (b) Mobile plant alert and/or tower crane alert under the Smart Site Safety System (4S) | Details are in the Common Conditions and Requirements on the BD website |
| (c) Sampling and testing | Construction materials and standard of workmanship |
| (d) Instrumentation | Checking design assumptions and monitoring the effect of the works |
| (e) Performance review | May be imposed; the criteria are those stated in the approval or consent letter |

Under regulation 10 of the Building (Administration) Regulations, documents and information that may be required when plans are approved include:

| Document | What the note says |
|---|---|
| Quality Supervision Plan | Specifies the quality supervision to be provided. Tempered glass: RSE and RGBC streams supervise the heat-soak process carried out by the manufacturer. Type 2 mechanical couplers: RSE and RGBC or registered specialist contractor streams supervise the splicing. |
| Quality Assurance Scheme of the manufacturer | Quality of production |
| Effect on the surroundings | For example a pre-construction condition survey and monitoring records |
| Certificates | Construction materials |
| Strength / performance test reports | As imposed |
| Audit reports | By the RSE and the authorized signatory of the RGBC or registered specialist contractor |
| Statement signed by the RSE | Submitted documents comply with the stipulated requirements |
| Record plans and reports | Of construction |
| Declaration by the authorized person | Estimated total cost of building works exceeds $30 million |

Common conditions for specific structural materials and components, structural forms and construction methods, and types of works are on www.bd.gov.hk under the “Codes and references” page of “Resources”.

**SD takeaway:** Tempered-glass heat soak and Type 2 coupler splices need a Quality Supervision Plan, an RSE quality control supervisor at T3 inspecting at least weekly, and a contractor quality control co-ordinator who is full-time and, for Type 2 couplers, at T3.

A similar practice note has been issued to AP, RSE and RGE.
