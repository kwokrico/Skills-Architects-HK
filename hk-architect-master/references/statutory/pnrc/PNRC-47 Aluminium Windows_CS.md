# PNRC-47 Aluminium Windows
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue July 2001; last revision March 2006; this revision February 2021 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs design and installation of aluminium windows so that they meet section 3 of the Building (Construction) Regulation. It is not a permission to depart from paragraphs 3 to 9 without separate substantiation, and certain window repair and replacement may instead be minor works under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNAP APP-147.

## Regulatory Overview
Section 3 of the Building (Construction) Regulation requires materials to be suitable, adequately prepared, and fixed so that they perform their intended functions, and those requirements apply to aluminium windows in any building. Compliance with the design and installation requirements in paragraphs 3 to 9 is accepted as meeting section 3; any deviation has to be substantiated separately.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Performance, minor works and accepted standards (paras 2–4 and 9)

Certain repair and replacement of windows may be minor works under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNAP APP-147, instead of prior approval and consent. Where aluminium windows are proposed, the registered contractor provides experienced supervisors and workers and quality assurance procedures. Standards commonly used for design and construction of windows, acceptable to the Building Authority, are in Appendix A to both PNAP APP-37 and PNAP APP-53. Design attention covers horizontal imposed loads, protection of openings, the function of protective barriers, locking devices of openable sashes and sub-frames, corrosion protection, quality control of materials, and protection against spread of fire and smoke between floors. Locking devices must be properly assembled under adequate site supervision, because improper assembly can put additional moment on the locking-device components.

**SD takeaway:** Window replacement on an existing building may sit in the minor-works list in Schedule 1 and PNAP APP-147, but a new aluminium window still has to meet the paragraph 3 to 9 sizes and fixings unless a deviation is substantiated.

### 2. Frames, fixing lugs and sash limits (paras 5–7)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Members, transoms, mullions and glass | Adequate size and strength for location, height and orientation |
| Frames | Securely and rigidly fixed to the openings in the walls |
| Fixing lugs, where used | Stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel; minimum material thickness 1.5 mm; 300 mm centres maximum |
| Spacing greater than 300 mm | AP/RSE must be satisfied that structural stability and waterproofing are not compromised |
| Structural members | Minimum aluminium thickness 2 mm |
| Mullion depth | Not less than 38 mm |
| Hinges and fastenings | Withstand positive and negative design-wind pressures when the window is closed; size and strength commensurate with the window |
| Side-hung sash | Width not exceeding 700 mm |
| Top-hung sash | Size not exceeding 2.5 m² |
| Hinges | Adequate to hold the weight of the sashes or sub-frames |
| Window stay | A suitably designed stay can minimise the effect of strong wind on hinges and casements |

**SD takeaway:** Lock the module at side-hung width not over 700 mm and top-hung area not over 2.5 m², with 2 mm aluminium, mullions at least 38 mm deep, and fixing lugs at least 1.5 mm thick at not more than 300 mm centres.

### 3. 4-bar hinges (para 8 and App. A)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Material | Stainless steel, with adjustable friction shoe |
| Bar thickness | Minimum 2.5 mm for all stainless steel bars of the hinge |
| Fixings | Stainless steel rivets or screws; aluminium rivets shall not be used |
| Number and size | At least 3 stainless steel rivets of diameter not less than 4.8 mm, or stainless steel screws of diameter not less than 5 mm, to fix each of the bottom and top bars of the hinge to the frame and to the openable sash |
| Dissimilar metals | Measures to prevent contact between stainless steel and aluminium |
| Positive mechanical fixing | Insert a stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel plate, bar or angle not less than 3 mm thick inside the section to provide threads; Annex I illustrates this, including an M5×10 stainless steel screw as a typical screw |
| Alternative | Thicken the window section locally to not less than 5 mm; Annex II illustrates 5 mm local thickening and an M5×16 mm pan-head machine screw, pitch 0.8 mm |
| If neither insert nor 5 mm thickening is used | Substantiate by calculation that the aluminium section can anchor the stainless steel rivet or screw |
| Hinge length | At least 60% of the width of the side-hung casement sash; Annex I marks length ≥ 60% of sash width and sash width ≤ 700 mm |

**SD takeaway:** A 4-bar hinge is stainless steel at least 2.5 mm thick, fixed with at least three stainless steel rivets (≥ 4.8 mm) or screws (≥ 5 mm) to each top and bottom bar, into a 3 mm insert or a 5 mm local thickening, and at least 60% of the side-hung sash width.

### 4. Head drip, cleaning and water test (paras 11–14 and App. B)

Corrosive cleaning agents are not used on external walls and windows at completion unless they are thoroughly washed with clean water immediately after application. For durability, the top member of the window frame should have a built-in projecting fin with a drip nose, as Appendix B, to limit water lying in the frame or casement. A field water-penetration test on the installed aluminium windows is advisable; some international methods are in Annex A of the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Glass 2018. Water leakage is also addressed in the Guidelines on Prevention of Water Seepage in New Buildings enclosed with the Buildings Department circular letter of 7 March 2005.

**SD takeaway:** Draw a projecting head fin with a drip nose, and allow a field water-penetration test to Annex A of the Glass Code 2018 on the installed windows.
