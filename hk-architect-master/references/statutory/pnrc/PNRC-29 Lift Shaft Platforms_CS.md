# PNRC-29 Lift Shaft Platforms
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue October 1995; this revision February 2011 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs temporary wall-supported platforms inside lift shafts during construction — rain shelter, wall formwork deck, mid-shaft protection, debris catcher or crash deck. It is not a permanent lift-shaft or lift-installation design note, and it is not a substitute for the Construction Industry Council guidelines it points to.

## Regulatory Overview

Lift-shaft platforms are temporary works inside shafts of buildings under construction, and collapses have killed and seriously injured workers. The Labour Department may suspend erection, maintenance and dismantling under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance if the design, supervision, risk assessment or fall-arrest rules in this note are missed.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Design, method and supervision before anyone works on the platform

Construction is governed by the Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations. Occupiers and employers also owe the general duty to provide a safe place of work under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) and the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59). A suspension notice under section 10 of Cap. 509 is in view if any one of the following is missed:

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Design | Every platform designed by an authorized person or registered structural engineer employed by the contractor; the design plan contains all details and specifications |
| Method | Construction plans and method statements for erection, alteration, maintenance and dismantling, prepared by an engineer of technically competent person grade T4 or above employed by the contractor, and checked and signed off by that authorized person or registered structural engineer |
| Build | Constructed to the construction plan under the full-time immediate supervision of a competent person with technically competent person grade T1, appointed in writing; on completion, checked by the contractor’s engineer |
| Alteration | Redesigned by the contractor’s authorized person or registered structural engineer; construction plan and method statement prepared by the contractor’s engineer and signed off as above |
| Risk assessment | Before erection, maintenance or dismantling, by a planning team of site managerial and supervisory staff from the main contractor and subcontractors, including at least one person at grade T4 or equivalent; includes a thorough inspection by the T1 competent person of the stability of platforms already erected |
| Upkeep | Maintained to the construction plans; materials on the platforms removed regularly |
| Dismantling | Full-time immediate supervision by the same T1 competent person |
| Dumping | Nothing thrown, tipped or shot onto a platform unless the contractor’s authorized person or registered structural engineer designed it for that loading |
| Falls | Suitable fall-arrest systems provided and used during erection, maintenance and dismantling |

Kept on site for an Occupational Safety Officer: the design plan; the construction plan and method statement; a record of the date of erection, maintenance and dismantling and the name of the competent person who supervised; and the risk assessment report.

**SD takeaway:** Shaft platforms are designed temporary works with a named T4 method and a T1 supervisor, not carpenter-built decks added as the core rises.

### 2. Temporary occupation permit while shaft works continue

If a temporary occupation permit is used, safety of fire, workers and occupants is settled at planning and design. Every condition of the permit, read with the temporary occupation permit boundary plan accepted or approved by the Building Authority, is maintained at all times until the occupation permit, including while lift-shaft works and platform works are in progress. Precautionary measures include fire separation of adequate fire rating between occupied areas and construction areas.

Further reading cited in the note: Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance sections 6, 7 and 10; Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance section 6A; Part VA of the Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations; and the Construction Industry Council’s *Guidelines on Safety of Lift Shaft Works (Volume 1 — During Construction Stage and Before Handing Over to Lift Installation Contractor)*, paragraph 9 (design and construction of the platforms) and paragraph 12 (temporary occupation permit).

**SD takeaway:** A partial-occupation scheme that leaves shafts in construction needs a fire-rated separation on the temporary occupation permit boundary that stays intact while platforms are erected and struck.
