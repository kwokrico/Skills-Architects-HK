# PNRC-65 Run-in and Run-out
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue September 2006; in operation 1 January 2007 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Design, construction and self-certification of a vehicular run-in and run-out on a public road where the lease allows ingress and egress. It names Highways Department standard drawings. It does **not** restate dimensions, gradients or construction details from those drawings (Annexes 1 and 2 are untranscribed in the text).

## Regulatory Overview
Where vehicular ingress and egress are allowed under the lease, the run-in and run-out is designed and built to the standards in this note and Appendix A, which adopt Highways Department specifications and named standard drawings. Completion is certified to the Buildings Department, Highways Department inspects and takes over the works, and a Highways Department objection on defects may be a ground for refusing an occupation permit under section 21(6)(a) of the Buildings Ordinance.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Footpath match and saw-cut
The note provides guidelines for a self-certification system proposed to replace the special lease condition on construction of the run-in and run-out for new leases. It came into operation on **1 January 2007**. A similar practice note was issued to authorized persons and registered structural engineers.

| Adjoining footpath | Run-in and run-out |
|---|---|
| Concrete | Construct in concrete |
| Paver blocks | Use paver blocks, with a visual contrast and/or a change in pattern relative to the adjoining footpath |
| Either | Design and construction appropriate for safety and convenience of vehicular and pedestrian traffic |

On completion, the authorized person ensures the adjoining footpath or pavement is made good. Saw-cut method is required so adjoining pavements are not damaged. Damage outside the run-in and run-out area is reinstated and made good only after a separate Excavation Permit from the Highways Department.

**SD takeaway:** The run-in is the same family of material as the footpath, with a visual or pattern change if it is pavers, and the cut line stays inside the run-in footprint.

### 2. Appendix A standards (drawing numbers only)
These standards are acceptable to the Highways Department. Use the latest versions; updates are posted on the Highways Department homepage. The text of this note does not give dimensions from the drawings.

| Item | What the note names |
|---|---|
| General specification | General Specification for Civil Engineering Works: Section 9 (carriageways: sub-base and bituminous materials), Section 10 (concrete carriageways), Section 11 Parts 5 to 7 (footways and paved areas), plus any corrigendum |
| Concrete run-in | HyD standard drawings **H1113, H1114, H1115, H1116** (Annex 1); GS clauses **6.68, 9.44 and 16.58–16.62** |
| Paver run-in | HyD standard drawings **H1103, H5101, H5102, H5114, H5115, H5116** (Annex 2) |
| Paver evidence | AP obtains laboratory test certificates or equivalent information from the registered contractor |
| Paver colour | Specified by the AP to achieve visual contrast and/or a change in pattern |
| Paver design | Latest HyD requirements, taken from the Publications and Press Release section of the HyD homepage and incorporated on the general building plans at building-plan submission |
| Underground utilities | HyD Technical Circular No. **3/90**, or any updated version, for the minimum ground cover that protects underground facilities. This note does not state that cover depth |

**SD takeaway:** General building plans carry the current HyD drawings and paver requirements; this note is the drawing list and the submission rule, not a dimension schedule.

### 3. Utilities, plans and Excavation Permit
The authorized person checks with the relevant authorities for utility services under the proposed run-in and run-out, coordinates diversion with the utility undertakings, and puts the design details on the general building plans for circulation to the Highways Department for comment. Utilities diversion and the Excavation Permit from the Highways Department should be arranged early.

**SD takeaway:** Run-in geometry is on the general building plans for Highways Department comment, with utility diversion and the Excavation Permit on the programme before construction.

### 4. Certificate of completion and occupation permit
If the run-in and run-out is finished before the occupation-permit application, submit the Certificate of Completion of Vehicular Run-in and Run-out (Appendix B) to the Buildings Department. The authorized person is encouraged to complete and certify it well before that application.

| Certificate part | Who | What is confirmed |
|---|---|---|
| Part A | Authorized person | Completed in accordance with the approved plans and PNAP 300; location on an attached block plan; ready for handing over to the Highways Department (delete if not applicable) |
| Part B | RGBC, or RSC in the stated category | Completed in accordance with the approved plans and PNRC 65 |
| Copy | Highways Department | Certificate copied to HyD with a copy of the block plan |

On receipt of that certificate, or on an occupation-permit application, the Buildings Department notifies the Highways Department, which inspects and takes over the run-in and run-out. Defects are passed back through the Buildings Department for the authorized person to rectify. If the Highways Department objects to accepting the works because of defects, that may be a ground for refusal of the occupation permit under section 21(6)(a) of the Buildings Ordinance.

**SD takeaway:** A defective run-in that the Highways Department will not take over can block the occupation permit, so certification should finish well before the permit application.

### 5. Highways Department constructs the run-in
Where the Highways Department has agreed to construct the run-in and run-out for the owner, the authorized person submits to the Buildings Department the entrustment letter and a copy of the demand-note receipt for the fees. If a temporary run-in and run-out is built so that it does not endanger the public or inconvenience vehicular or pedestrian traffic using it, the Buildings Department may process an occupation-permit application before the permanent run-in is finished. Under the Helping Business Scheme, authorized persons are encouraged to build the permanent run-in with their own resources; the Highways Department has said its resources are fully stretched and may not be able to undertake the works.

**SD takeaway:** An occupation permit can move ahead of a Highways Department permanent run-in only with the entrustment papers and a safe temporary run-in; the note expects the project to build the permanent one itself.
