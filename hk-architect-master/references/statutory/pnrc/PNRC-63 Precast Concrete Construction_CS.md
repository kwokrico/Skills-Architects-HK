# PNRC-63 Precast Concrete Construction
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue August 2006; last revision July 2012; this revision April 2022 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs quality assurance, qualified supervision and quality audit for key precast concrete elements made in a precasting factory. It does not apply to minor elements such as non-structural precast concrete blocks, architectural features and planters.

## Regulatory Overview
The AP and RSE provide periodic supervision under section 4(3)(a) of the Buildings Ordinance and regulation 37 of the Building (Administration) Regulations, and the registered contractor provides continuous supervision under sections 9(5)(a) and 9(6)(a) and regulation 41. Design, construction and quality control also follow the Code of Practice for Precast Concrete Construction 2016.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Approval conditions: factory QA and supervision plan (paras 4–6)

| Condition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Factory | ISO 9001 quality assurance certification, imposed under item 6 of section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance |
| Quality Assurance Scheme | Copy submitted at least 14 days before production starts in the factory, under regulation 10 of the Building (Administration) Regulations, with the RSE’s written confirmation that the scheme will secure compliance with the Buildings Ordinance and the approved plans |
| Scheme contents | Quality-control tests of materials; calibration of laboratory equipment; efficiency and operation of casting-yard equipment; production process; testing procedures and requirements; frequency and extent of inspection by in-house staff and independent parties; frequency and extent of audit by in-house staff and independent parties |
| Independent parties | Not under the direct employment of the factory: a certification body under the Hong Kong Certification Body Accreditation Scheme, or an inspection body under the Hong Kong Inspection Body Accreditation Scheme |
| Qualified supervision | Imposed under item 6 of section 17(1), by the RSE and the registered contractor, for fabrication, erection and examination |
| Precast Concrete Supervision Plan | Submitted at least 14 days before production, under regulation 10; names, qualifications, identification, inspection frequency, confirmation of appointment and contact details of the quality control supervisor and quality control coordinator |
| RSE’s quality control supervisor | Same minimum qualifications and experience as a grade T3 technically competent person under the RSE’s stream in the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 (2021 Edition); frequency not less than once a week; supervision requirements for in-situ reinforced concrete in PNAP APP-158 are the reference for how the RSE sets that supervision |
| Contractor’s quality control coordinator | Continuous supervision in the factory; minimum qualifications and experience as a grade T1 technically competent person under the contractor’s stream |
| Log books | Names and qualifications recorded; production, inspection, auditing and testing recorded by both supervisors; kept at the factory, with a copy at the site office, and produced to Buildings Department when required |

**SD takeaway:** Structural precast is locked to an ISO 9001 factory, with the quality assurance scheme and supervision plan in at least 14 days before casting, a T3 supervisor at least weekly, and a T1 coordinator full time at the factory.

### 2. Quality audit at the factory (paras 7–8 and App. A)

The RSE and the authorized signatory of the registered contractor visit the factory in person to inspect the first batch, then carry out quality audit checks at least once every month. Quarterly checks are inspections in person. The monthly checks in between (the 2nd, 3rd, 5th, 6th months, and so on) may be in person or by videotelephony. Audit reports by the RSE and by the authorized signatory, each duly endorsed, are submitted under regulation 10 within 14 days after each check, including the first visit. The first visit verifies that the quality assurance scheme and procedures are followed and the production line works, and it covers clauses 3.1 to 3.14 and 4.1 of the Precast Concrete Code.

Videotelephony is two-way simultaneous audio and video. The video is in colour, resolution not less than 480p, on a non-rewritable DVD-ROM. The RSE and authorized signatory are assisted at the factory by the quality control supervisor and coordinator respectively. Supervision must not be inferior to a personal visit. Items covered in the videos are recorded contemporaneously in the audit reports and submitted with the discs within 14 days. Each disc is certified with a permanent marker that the RSE or authorized signatory personally used videotelephony.

Minimum factory audit scope (Appendix A):

| Works item | What is checked |
|---|---|
| Formworks | Smoothness, cleanliness and dimensions of the steel mould; mould release agent |
| Reinforcing bars | Size, pattern, fixing and layout; spacers; concrete covers; material testing reports |
| Concrete | Placing and compaction; curing; material testing reports |
| Finished products | Surface and defects; sizes, dimensions and fabrication tolerances; starter bars |
| Inspection records | Quality control supervisor’s log book (RSE); coordinator’s log book (authorized signatory) |
| Others | Any other item the RSE or authorized signatory considers essential |

**SD takeaway:** Programme the first in-person factory visit on the first batch, then a monthly audit with an in-person visit every quarter, and file each report within 14 days.

### 3. Alternative: audit after delivery to site (para 9 and App. B)

Instead of the RSE’s monthly factory audits, the RSE may make the first visit in person and then audit elements after delivery to the building site. That choice needs one-month prior written notification to the Building Authority. The first-visit report is still submitted within 14 days, and each on-site audit report, covering the quality control supervisor’s supervision, is submitted within 14 days of that check. The authorized signatory’s factory audits in paragraph 7 are unchanged by this alternative.

| Sampling | At least 1% of the precast concrete elements, with a minimum of one per every 20 batches of delivery |
|---|---|
| Batch | The same type of element (façade, beam, column, bearing wall, and similar) delivered to the site on the same day |

| Check on each sample | Extent |
|---|---|
| Opening-up | Three locations, each 100 mm × 300 mm, for bar size, pattern, layout and concrete cover |
| Covermeter | Six locations, six readings at each, in an area of 450 mm × 450 mm |
| Concrete strength | Non-destructive test (for example rebound hammer) at three locations, plus material testing reports |
| Finished product | Surface and defects; sizes, dimensions and fabrication tolerances; starter bars |
| Records | Quality control supervisor’s log book |

**SD takeaway:** If factory audits by the RSE are dropped after the first visit, notify the Building Authority one month ahead and open up at least 1% of units, and at least one unit in every 20 delivery batches, at three 100 mm × 300 mm windows.

### 4. Separate RSE for elements that do not stabilise the parent structure (paras 11–14)

For precast elements that do not affect the stability of the parent structure, such as a precast façade or staircase, a separate RSE may design and supervise production and erection. Forms BA4 and BA5, stating the appointment and the scope, are submitted with the plans. The project RSE notifies the Building Authority in writing that the appointment and the demarcation are understood. The separate RSE’s submission includes an assessment of the effect on the parent structure, appended with the project RSE’s statement that the connection details and their effects are understood. The separate RSE supervises production, erection and the connection (ties, anchor plates, cast-in embeds and similar), unless those parts were installed in the parent structure before the appointment, in which case the project RSE shows them on the superstructure plans and supervises their installation. The separate RSE still meets paragraphs 4 to 9 and submits those documents, and on completion certifies the works under regulation 25(3) of the Building (Administration) Regulations.

**SD takeaway:** A precast façade or staircase can have its own RSE, but BA4 and BA5 and the project RSE’s confirmation of the connections go in with the plans.

### 5. Supervision checklist items that affect the specification (App. D)

The standard supervision plan undertakes compliance with PNAP APP-143 and the Precast Concrete Code 2016. Annex 1 lists items for both streams. Items marked in the note as essential at the Code’s clauses 3.2 to 3.14 include moulds, cast-in items, placing and curing, demoulding only after sufficient strength, finished condition, and storage. Steel and concrete quality (delivery records, CS2 sampling and testing of bars, CS1 cube sampling, compression testing and statistics) are checked by both streams. Special testing of finished products, clause 4.3 of the Code, includes watertightness (for example a water-penetration test) and the performance of tiled finishes (for example a pull-off test). In-situ strength may be checked by coring or rebound hammer. A non-conformity with material concern for safety, or one that cannot be verified because the area has been covered up, is recorded in the coordinator’s log book and, where applicable, on Form B in Appendix III of the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 (2021 Edition).

**SD takeaway:** Precast specifications need CS2 bar tests, CS1 concrete cubes, a pull-off test where tiles are cast on, and a water-penetration test where watertightness is required; the note does not state pass values for those special tests.
