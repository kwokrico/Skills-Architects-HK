# PNRC-61 Protection of Natural Streams
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue May 2005 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Planning and construction controls, drawn from Environment, Transport and Works Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 5/2005, for works in or affecting natural streams and rivers, especially Ecologically Important Streams. For this note, natural streams and rivers exclude flood retention ponds, fishponds, and engineered stormwater drainage such as drainage channels, nullahs, and ditches (examples named: Tin Shui Wai Main Nullah, Yuen Long Nullah, Tuen Mun Nullah, engineered sections of Shing Mun River, and concrete U-channels).

## Regulatory Overview

A natural stream or river is a natural channel fed from upper terrain, perennial or flowing only in the wet season, with a natural bed and largely natural banks and riparian vegetation. At planning stage, designers are to avoid impact on natural streams and rivers, particularly Ecologically Important Streams; the updated list is on the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department website cited in the note.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Direct and indirect impacts to design out

Direct impacts are physical changes to the habitat. Examples: training, filling, culverting, narrowing, widening, damming, realignment, diversion, and bank stabilisation such as shotcreting. Indirect impacts come from works that pollute the stream or affect its flow. Examples: site formation, landfilling, discharge of silt and polluted water, dumping of debris nearby, and foundation works involving dewatering, plus geotechnical and demolition works in the proximity of the stream.

Most natural streams are subject, to various extents, to the Land (Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 28), Waterworks Ordinance (Cap. 102), Town Planning Ordinance (Cap. 131), Public Cleansing and Prevention of Nuisances Regulation (Cap. 132BK), Country Parks Ordinance (Cap. 208), Waste Disposal Ordinance (Cap. 354), and Water Pollution Control Ordinance (Cap. 358).

Where impact is unavoidable, measures to minimise or compensate are to take account of advice from the relevant authorities and of any environmental review or study. Mitigation for temporary works and construction activities is written into the contract. Projects with only potential indirect impacts on natural streams other than Ecologically Important Streams are advised to use good site practice and pollution control during construction.

**SD takeaway:** Keep site formation, dewatering, and discharge off the natural stream, and treat culverting, realignment, or shotcrete bank works as impacts to avoid at planning stage.

### 2. Order of channel works — least impact first

Appendix A ranks engineering options for modifying a natural river or stream in ascending order of environmental and ecological impact:

| Order | Option |
|---|---|
| 1 | Protect or stabilise the bank at erosion points with natural materials such as rock |
| 2 | Remove fallen objects and obstructions, and clear vegetation, selectively |
| 3 | Enlarge the channel by modifying one bank only |
| 4 | Enlarge by modifying or widening both banks |
| 5 | Enlarge by deepening |
| 6 | Realign by creating a new channel |
| 7 | Construct a new channel with artificial non-vegetative smooth lining, such as concrete |

Concrete lining is the last resort, used only when more environmentally friendly designs are proved impracticable. Works are preferably in the dry season, when flow is low. Temporary access is located to limit disturbance of the streambed and riparian vegetation. Smaller plant may be specified. A temporary sewerage system is to collect wastewater. Storage of equipment, fill, chemicals, fuel, debris, and spoil is identified well away from the stream before works start. Landscape and visual impact is assessed early.

**SD takeaway:** If the stream must be touched, adopt the earliest option on this list that works, and do not adopt a smooth concrete channel until the earlier options are shown to be impracticable.

### 3. Ecologically Important Streams — extra design rules

In addition to section 2, Appendix A asks for:

| Topic | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Banks and bed | Diversified trees, shrubs, herbaceous and wetland plants, and grass to form an ecological riparian zone. Leave the natural bottom intact where practicable. Limit work to bank stabilisation or bank widening using soft but adequately robust techniques |
| Linings | Avoid or minimise concrete. Unless site constraints are severe, use geotextile-reinforced grassed lining, bio-engineering, natural stone wall, or gabions on the embankment, and random rubble or stones on the bottom |
| Special flora and fauna | Alternative design to avoid disturbance. If unavoidable: translocation, temporary migratory pathways, recolonisation, or a compensation plan. After the works, consider substrates of similar size and composition to the original bed |
| Phasing | Do not work the whole width at once if the stream is wide enough. Leave adequate width intact, keep flow, and keep a free passage so water does not stagnate |
| Dams | If upper reaches are fish breeding grounds and the works include a dam, provide a specially designed fish ladder |
| Before start | Consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department on rare species. Inspect for pools of considerable size and preserve them as far as possible |

**SD takeaway:** On an Ecologically Important Stream, keep the bed, specify a soft bank treatment, phase the works so part of the width and the flow remain, and consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department before commencement.

### 4. Construction-stage controls

Appendix B measures for works inside or near natural rivers and streams:

| Control | What the note asks |
|---|---|
| Isolation | Temporarily isolate the works, for example with sandbags or silt curtains with the lead edge at the bottom and properly supported props. Prevent pollution or siltation of the water gathering grounds |
| Bed and crossings | Preserve the natural bottom and existing flow. Keep any temporary access track on the riverbed to the minimum width and length. Support temporary river crossings on stilts above the riverbed |
| Materials | Cover stockpiles and locate them away from the stream. Cover and dispose of debris and spoil promptly so rain does not wash them into the stream |
| Wastewater | Order of approach: minimise generation, then reuse and recycle, then treat. Place discharge outlets of treatment facilities well away from the stream |
| Vegetation | Avoid or minimise removal of bank vegetation. Hydroseed or plant disturbed areas to blend with the surroundings |
| Support | Lateral support where needed so soil or mud does not slip into the stream, without unduly impeding flow during heavy rain |
| Supervision | Supervisory staff stationed on site to supervise and monitor the works |

**SD takeaway:** The construction plan isolates the stream with silt control, keeps crossings on stilts, and discharges treated water well away from the channel.
