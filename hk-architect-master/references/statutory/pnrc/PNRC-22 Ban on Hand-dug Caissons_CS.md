# PNRC-22 Ban on Hand-dug Caissons
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued May 1993; this revision February 1995 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs the statutory ban on hand-dug caissons and the only personal exemptions under Buildings Ordinance section 16(1A). It is not a foundation design guide, and it is not a general consent to dig a shaft with a person inside it.

## Regulatory Overview
The Buildings (Amendment) Ordinance 1995, enacted on 19 January 1995, bans hand-dug caissons, defined in Buildings Ordinance section 2(1) as any foundation or earth-retaining structure, or part of one, whose construction includes excavation of a shaft by digging carried out by any person inside the shaft, with or without machine tools. Section 16(1A) empowers the Building Authority to refuse approval of plans that include a hand-dug caisson, with exemption only in the circumstances in paragraph 3, demonstrated to the satisfaction of the Building Authority personally.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. The only exemptions (para 3)

| Route | What must be shown |
|---|---|
| (a) Size limit | Depth does not exceed 3 metres, and the diameter of the inscribed circle is not less than 1.5 metres |
| (b) No alternative | For that site, a hand-dug caisson is the only practical construction method, or there is no other safe engineering alternative |

Examples given for (b) are narrow or steeply sloping sites where difficult access or insufficient working space makes machine tools impossible or unsafe.

**SD takeaway:** Foundation concepts should assume machine methods; a hand-dug shaft is an exemption case, and the dimensional route is only a shaft not deeper than 3 m whose inscribed circle is at least 1.5 m.

### 2. If an exemption is sought (paras 4–6)

The AP/RSE must specify stringent safety requirements in the method statement and ensure the registered contractor complies with them, including the Construction Site (Safety) Regulations administered by the Commissioner for Labour. The note also points to the HKIE booklet “Guidance Notes on Hand-dug Caissons” and the Occupational Safety & Health Council “Code of Safe Working Practices for Hand-dug Caissons”. A 12-month grace period applied after the revision; with effect from 1 February 1996 the Building Authority will not approve plans involving hand-dug caissons unless the section 16(1A) circumstances exist.

**SD takeaway:** From 1 February 1996, a hand-dug caisson does not enter an approval set unless the section 16(1A) case is made personally to the Building Authority, with a safety method statement.
