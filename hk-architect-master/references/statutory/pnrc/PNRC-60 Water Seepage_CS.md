# PNRC-60 Water Seepage
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue March 2005; this revision December 2010 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Reminds the design and construction team of the moisture-protection performance in the Building (Construction) Regulations and of the Buildings Department *Guidelines on Prevention of Water Seepage in New Buildings*. Detailing methods are in those Guidelines. This note names the regulations and the parts of the building that have repeatedly seeped.

## Regulatory Overview

Regulation 3 of the Building (Construction) Regulations requires materials to be of a suitable nature and quality, adequately mixed or prepared, and applied, used, or fixed so as to perform the functions for which they are designed. Regulations 34, 38, 41, and 48 require external walls, roofs, and kitchen and bathroom walls and floors to be protected against penetration of moisture or water, and the works are to be structurally safe.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Performance the design must meet

Authorized persons, registered structural engineers, registered general building contractors, and registered minor works contractors are to meet those performance requirements. Prevention at source depends on choice of material, design and specification, workmanship, and supervision.

| Regulation | Duty stated in this note |
|---|---|
| 3 | Suitable materials, properly prepared, and fixed so they perform |
| 34, 38, 41, and 48 | External walls, roofs, kitchen walls and floors, and bathroom walls and floors protected against moisture or water |

**SD takeaway:** External walls, roofs, and kitchen and bathroom walls and floors are specified for moisture protection under regulations 34, 38, 41, and 48, with materials that satisfy regulation 3.

### 2. Locations that have seeped, and the Guidelines

Past records cited in the note identify roof, external wall, window, bathroom, kitchen, plumbing and drainage pipes, car park floor, and basement as areas susceptible to water seepage if design and construction are weak. Contractors are advised to apply the specified waterproofing to these areas and to supervise that work closely.

The Buildings Department has issued the *Guidelines on Prevention of Water Seepage in New Buildings* for general reference.

**SD takeaway:** Detail waterproofing for the roof, external wall, windows, bathroom, kitchen, plumbing and drainage pipes, car park floor, and basement, following the Buildings Department Guidelines.
