# PNRC-78 Pay for Safety Scheme
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue September 2017 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs the contractual practice of pricing construction-site safety as its own paid items under the Pay for Safety Scheme, in place since 1996. It is not a relaxation of the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans or the Code of Practice for Site Supervision, and it does not set new statutory safety dimensions.

## Regulatory Overview

Contractors must still meet site-safety monitoring under the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans and the Code of Practice for Site Supervision, and must price the cost of continuous supervision in the tender. The Pay for Safety Scheme keeps that cost as identified, pre-priced items in the bills so payment follows certified completion of the safety work, including training.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Safety as a priced and certified item

Construction involves risky activities. Contractors adopt safe practices and precautionary measures; authorized persons, registered structural engineers and registered geotechnical engineers must see that works under their supervision are carried out safely. If safety cost is not identified for pricing, valuation and payment, the resources for those measures can be undermined.

Under the scheme, introduced in **1996**, the employer pays the contractor when safety-related items, including training, are satisfactorily completed, as vetted and certified by the employer’s representative, at the pre-priced rates in the Site Safety section of the Bills of Quantities or the Schedule of Rates.

The note points to: the Development Bureau Construction Site Safety Manual; the Factories and Industrial Undertakings (Safety Management) Regulation (Cap. 59AF); the Real Estate Developers Association of Hong Kong and Hong Kong Construction Association Safety Partnering Programme; the Construction Industry Council’s *Guidelines on the Adoption of the Pay for Safety Scheme* and Chapter 7 of its *Reference Materials on Construction Site Welfare, Health and Safety Measures* (March 2017); and safety training courses of the Labour Department’s Occupational Safety and Health Training Centre and of the Occupational Safety and Health Council.

Authorized persons are encouraged to recommend the scheme to their clients. Contractors are asked to initiate the same provisions.

**SD takeaway:** When the client’s procurement route is set, site safety — supervision, precautionary measures and training — is a separate priced section of the bills, not a lump absorbed in the preliminaries.
