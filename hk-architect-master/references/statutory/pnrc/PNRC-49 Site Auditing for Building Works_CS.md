# PNRC-49 Site Auditing for Building Works
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue April 2001; last revision February 2016; this revision April 2018 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs the Buildings Department’s strategy for auditing building works on site under section 22 of the Buildings Ordinance, including foundation and superstructure items in Appendix I and the fast-track audit for certain two-storey warehouses. It is not day-to-day supervision by the authorized person or the contractor, and it is not the Site Monitoring Section programme in PNRC 31.

## Regulatory Overview

The Building Authority enters sites under section 22 to check compliance, using surprise audits focused on structural safety, with frequency that follows site performance. Foundation and superstructure are audited in detail, minor works are sampled, and qualifying two-storey warehouses follow a shorter inspection track under PNAP ADM-19.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. How audit frequency is set

The strategy expands audits to detailed checking of foundation and superstructure, uses a performance-based frequency, removes predictability of timing, separates submission-checking from auditing, rotates auditing staff, and concentrates investigation on structural safety and integrity.

On the first visit the officer rates site conditions, complexity, the contractor’s on-site organisation, attendance of qualified supervisory personnel of the authorized person, registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer and contractor, completeness of documents on site, and quality of work to date. That rating sets the initial frequency. Later visits are timed from the standard found last time. Particular difficulties, anomalies, or substandard quality bring the next audit forward. The officer decides what proportion of the works to inspect; there is no pre-determined percentage of structural units. Sites in progress and completed sites are liable. There is generally no pre-arrangement of date and time. The auditor is neither the officer who processes the documentary submissions for that site nor the officer who carried out the previous inspection.

Appendix II (April 2001) sets the initial visit at **1 month** after commencement of piling and **3 months** after commencement of superstructure works. The same chart carries later interval labels of **1 month** and **2 months** for foundation and **2 months** and **4 months** for superstructure, and it branches to less frequent or more frequent auditing according to whether the audited works are acceptable, until all structural components are complete. Anomalies are examined with emphasis on those that affect structural safety and integrity or that recur, including whether the anomaly was inadvertent. Properly executed work is not to be interrupted; the audit is not a project-management intervention.

**SD takeaway:** Expect an unannounced structural audit from about one month into piling and three months into superstructure, sooner if early work is weak, so founding records and supervision attendance have to be on site from the start.

### 2. Foundation and superstructure items that are opened up (Appendix I)

| Work | What is audited |
|---|---|
| Driven steel H-pile | Mill certificates for proof yield stress |
| Precast prestressed spun concrete pile | Origin (recognised type) and physical dimensions against approved plans |
| Welding electrode | Grade, for splice-joint strength |
| Supervision | Presence of qualified registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer and registered specialist contractor staff against the site supervision plan |
| Final driving (diesel, hydraulic or drop hammer) | Sufficient driving energy and final set; piles to a suitable founding stratum per approved plans |
| Ground investigation and piling record | Pile-tip founding material able to sustain the design load; final set and founding level consistent with the borehole log |
| Bored pile | Concrete grade and reinforcement mill certificates; depth, diameter and bell-out; actual founding material and pre-drill records |
| Mini-pile | Grout grade, reinforcement mill certificate, coupler; depth and dip angle of raking piles |
| Socket H-pile | Grout grade and steel-section mill certificate; depth and rake as for mini-piles |
| Raft or spread footing | Bearing stratum and physical dimensions |
| Reinforced concrete | Hammer test of structural elements; selected reinforcement bars for tensile test |
| In-situ concrete | Coring tests for strength |
| Transfer plate | Document of the registered structural engineer’s acceptance of falsework design by the registered general building contractor; concreting sequence, for falsework stability |
| Prestressing member | Prestressing profile against approved plans; grout vent so that air is not entrapped |
| Cantilever canopy | Location of construction joint and cover |
| Curtain wall and glass wall | Member size, shape and grade; cast-in anchors |

Selected minor works under the simplified requirements (submitted plans rather than approved plans) receive audit site-safety inspections instead of the systematic programme. Minor works can still include Appendix I items such as spread footing, reinforced concrete, in-situ concrete and cantilever canopy, and those audit aspects then apply.

**SD takeaway:** Transfer-plate falsework acceptance, cantilever-canopy joint and cover, prestress profile and vent, and curtain-wall cast-in anchors are audit items, so they are explicit on the structural set rather than left to site discretion.

### 3. Two-storey warehouses on the PNAP ADM-19 fast track

For a two-storey warehouse that meets the fast-track criteria in PNAP ADM-19, the Buildings Department inspects the foundation bearing stratum within **one working day** (within **8 working hours**) of notification. Scheduled quality inspection of structural elements is combined with the comprehensive inspection at superstructure stage into one joint visit. For a small warehouse with gross floor area not exceeding **1 500 m²**, only one unscheduled audit is carried out unless that inspection reveals irregularities that warrant further unscheduled audits.

**SD takeaway:** A fast-track two-storey warehouse at or under 1 500 m² gross floor area still needs a same-day bearing-stratum call-out, and one clean unscheduled audit is the baseline only while no irregularity is found.
