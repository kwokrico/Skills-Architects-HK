# LACO Circular Memorandum No. 15
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
7 November 1995 | Legal Advisory and Conveyancing Office — Lands Department  
Ref. LACO/HQ/127 II | Immediate effect

> Scope note: Copies consent **progress correspondence to the developer**, and bars **post-consent substantive ASP amendments** (closing a CM No. 7B workaround). Process / ethics for solicitors — architect impact is programme certainty: freeze ASP substance **before** consent if sales launch depends on it.

---

## Regulatory Overview

CM No. 15 applies immediately to all consent applications (residential and non-residential), including those being processed. LACO will copy progress/rejection/clarification correspondence to the developer, and generally will **not** consider substantive ASP amendments after consent is issued.

---

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Developer copied on progress (paras. 1–2)

| Practice | Rule |
|---|---|
| Follow-up correspondence | Copied to developer (including rejection of incomplete apps and requests for clarification) |
| Purpose | Let developer instruct solicitor early; Law Society accepts as within professional conduct |

**SD takeaway:** Expect developer visibility on LACO queries — use that to unblock design/document gaps quickly during consent processing.

---

### 2. No substantive ASP amendments after consent (paras. 3–4)

| Prohibited pattern | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Hold ASP disputes until after consent, then seek substantive amendments while sales proceed | Treated as attempt to circumvent **CM No. 7B**; creates improper pressure on LACO |
| Default rule | Amendments to **substance** of the Agreement generally **not considered after consent** |
| Resolve timing | All ASP content concerns must be settled **during** approval, **before** consent |

**SD takeaway:** Lock ASP (and any design-driven schedules it embeds) before consent — post-consent “fixes” will not be entertained.
