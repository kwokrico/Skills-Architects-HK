# APP-16 Cladding
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision January 2026 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 59; first issue May 1979)

> Scope note: Facing or architectural decoration additional to the structure, such as metal, stone, and glass-fibre reinforced concrete cladding. Performance is under sections 3 and 28 of the Building (Construction) Regulation. Non-combustibility follows section 5 of Part E of the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings 2011: clauses E10.1 and E12.1 for external cladding, and clause E13.1 for internal cladding. Acceptable design standards are listed in Appendix A and are applied consistently. Minor cladding works may use the Minor Works Control System. See PNAP APP-147.

## Regulatory Overview
Cladding has permanent flexible horizontal and vertical joints that take up differential movement between the cladding and the structure, and all anchors, dowels, and fixings are stainless steel or another corrosion-resistant material. Sand and cement bedding, or epoxy bonding alone, is not a suitable permanent fixing for stone.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is shown, and when structural plans are required (paragraphs 4–6)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| General building plans | Location, layout, elevation, and material of cladding on the exterior |
| Structural plans, if any part is above 6 m from the adjoining ground or adjoining floor | Thickness, strength, durability, and type; material of the fixings; layout of the support. Also framing, expansion-joint elevations, anchors and supports, design loads and codes, material specifications, workmanship for welding, corrosion protection, and bi-metallic effects, and calculations checking the parent structure, the cladding system, the elements, and deflection of major members |
| Metal cladding | General notes, typical layout, and typical details may be included in the superstructure plans instead of a separate structural submission. Location, layout, and elevation remain on the general building plans. See the checklist at Appendix B6 and the sample at Appendix B9 of PNAP ADV-33 |
| Separate structural engineer | Forms BA4 and BA5 with the plans, stating the scope. That engineer certifies completion under regulation 25(3) and submits an assessment of the effect on the parent structure, with a statement signed by the project structural engineer. Cast-in anchorage installed before that appointment is shown on the project structural engineer's superstructure plans and supervised by that engineer |

**SD takeaway:** Show all external cladding on the general building plans, and submit structural details whenever any of it is more than 6 m above the adjoining ground or floor.

### 2. Tests before occupation (paragraphs 10–16 and Appendix B)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Drilled-in anchors above 6 m | On-site pull-out tests on a representative number of each type and size, directed by the structural engineer or authorized person. Capacity not less than 1.5 times the manufacturer's recommended tensile load, with no separation, plastic deformation, or deleterious effect |
| Each type of stone | Flexural strength to ASTM C880 or BS EN 12372, or an equivalent, and individual anchorage strength to ASTM C1354, or an equivalent, checked against Appendix B |
| Limestone used outside | Also aged-strength testing for extreme temperature and moisture change. Residual flexural and anchorage capacities not less than 80% of the standard tests in paragraph 13 |
| Specimens | At least 5, selected at random from batches delivered to site, for each of those tests |
| Who tests | An agency independent of the stone supplier. Results certified by the agency and endorsed by the authorized person or structural engineer against the characteristic strengths used in the design. Reports submitted before the occupation-permit application |
| Site check | A grade T3 technically competent person in the structural engineer's stream and a grade T1 person in the contractor's stream, to detect clay-like minerals, iron minerals, or veins |

**SD takeaway:** Do not rely on bedding or glue for stone, prove drilled-in anchors at 1.5 times the recommended load, and for exterior limestone show that aged strength is still at least 80% of the standard tests.
