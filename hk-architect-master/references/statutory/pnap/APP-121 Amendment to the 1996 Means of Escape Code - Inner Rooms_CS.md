# APP-121 Amendment to the 1996 Means of Escape Code — Inner Rooms
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue June 2001 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 257)

> Scope note: A substitute for the vision panel required by paragraph 14.5(e) of the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire 1996. It is not a general means-of-escape design note. Paragraph 14(5) of the code, as cited, does not apply to a habitable room whose escape is through the living or dining room or the kitchen of a domestic flat.

## Regulatory Overview
Paragraph 14.5(e) of the 1996 means-of-escape code requires a vision panel in a room, other than a toilet, whose only exit is through another room. An alternative such as a fire alarm audible in the inner room may replace that vision panel.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Vision panel or alarm (paragraphs 1–2)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Code rule | A vision panel in a room, other than a toilet, from which the only exit route is through another room |
| Alternative | May be substituted by alternative provisions, such as a fire alarm system audible in the room, to alert persons in the inner room of danger from fire or smoke |

**SD takeaway:** An inner room without a vision panel needs an alarm that can be heard inside that room.

### 2. Domestic flats (paragraph 3)
Paragraph 14(5) does not apply to a habitable room with means of escape through the living or dining room or the kitchen in domestic flats.

**SD takeaway:** A bedroom that escapes only through the living, dining, or kitchen of a domestic flat is not caught by this inner-room vision-panel rule.
