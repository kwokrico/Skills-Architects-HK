# APP-28 Qualified Supervision of Site Formation and Related Geotechnical Works
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This update December 1997 | Buildings Department (first issued March 1982; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Buildings Ordinance section 17 qualified supervision for site formation, excavation, foundation works on sloping ground, and ground investigation in the Scheduled Areas. It is the geotechnical counterpart of structural supervision. The Building Authority will normally not impose it where geotechnical problems are minor and the consequences of failure would be insignificant.

## Regulatory Overview
Section 17 allows the Building Authority to require qualified supervision when approving plans or consenting to commencement. For works with significant geotechnical content, supervision is assessed in three categories, which may be combined, and the individuals are submitted for agreement.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. The three categories (paragraphs 2–5)
| Category | Who | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (I) | Periodic inspections by a qualified geotechnical engineer involved in preparing the geotechnical content. Normally a Registered Professional Engineer (Geotechnical), or equivalent, from that firm. Registration is not a prerequisite; each case is considered on its merits | Stability analyses or ground-movement calculations are sensitive to the design assumptions, which must be checked on site under Building (Administration) Regulations 8(1)(bb) and (bc). Critical assumptions often found wrong on site include the geological model and the groundwater regime, such as a perched water table |
| (II) | Periodic inspections by a senior qualified geotechnical engineer who was responsible for that content. Normally a Registered Professional Engineer (Geotechnical) who is a partner, director, or equivalent of that firm | High-sensitivity sites, for example excavation adjoining old buildings with shallow foundations. Personal attention is needed to recognise and communicate concerns to the authorized person and the client |
| (III) | Full-time supervision by a suitably experienced graduate engineer or geologist, or a technical inspector or works supervisor, depending on the works | Day-to-day checks of specification and procedure. Examples: ground anchors, compaction of fill slopes, and excavation and lateral support with complicated procedures |
| Lease | Some sites already have geotechnical supervisory requirements in the lease or land title. The sectional Chief Building Surveyor will advise on application |

**SD takeaway:** On a sensitive excavation or a slope, allow for a geotechnical engineer from the design firm to check the ground model and groundwater as the work proceeds, and for full-time supervision where anchors, fill compaction, or a complex excavation sequence are used.
