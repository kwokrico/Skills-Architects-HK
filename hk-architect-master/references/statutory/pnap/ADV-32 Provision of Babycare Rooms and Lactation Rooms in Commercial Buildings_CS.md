# ADV-32 Provision of Babycare Rooms and Lactation Rooms in Commercial Buildings
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision November 2018 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 305; first issue February 2009)

> Scope note: Encouragement, not a statutory minimum, to provide a babycare room for the public and a lactation room for staff in a private commercial building. Authorized persons are asked to recommend the guidelines to their clients. Sample layouts are Figures 1 to 3; the dimensions below are in the text.

## Regulatory Overview
A babycare room is for breastfeeding or expressing milk, bottle-feeding, and changing nappies, with separate nappy-changing and feeding areas and a private breastfeeding area, and at least one is recommended in each commercial building. For offices in a commercial development, at least one lactation room is recommended in each office building, in the common areas, for mothers to express breastmilk.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Babycare room: place, size, and who may enter (paragraphs 2–8)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Number | Based on the scale of the building and the need of occupiers and visitors. At least one in each building is recommended |
| Location | In the common areas, easily accessible: ground floor with level access, or convenient lift access if on an upper floor. Separate from the toilets. Avoid transmission of foul air |
| Signs | Clear directional signs. The sign shows the breastfeeding and nappy-changing symbols, with clear text such as “Babycare Room” / “育嬰間” |
| Single user | Not less than 5 m², with adequate manoeuvring space for a wheelchair user |
| Multiple users | Not less than 12 m², with separate breastfeeding cubicles, one of which caters for a wheelchair user, plus bottle-feeding areas and nappy-changing areas |
| Access | Both males and females may enter the room. The breastfeeding area is for females only, signed “Breastfeeding Area for Woman only”. A cubicle door and a single-user room door may lock, and must be openable from outside in an emergency, with an “engaged” indicator |
| Finishes | No sharp edges or hard corners. Non-slip floors and easily cleanable finishes. Family-friendly and safe for young children |
| Light and air | Where a sanitary fitment such as a sink is provided, illumination and ventilation follow regulation 36 of the Building (Planning) Regulations (Cap. 123F). A thermostat for users is recommended |

**SD takeaway:** Place at least one babycare room in the common area, off the toilets, at not less than 5 m² for one user or 12 m² if it is shared, with one wheelchair breastfeeding cubicle in the shared room.

### 2. Babycare room fittings (paragraphs 9–10)
| Required | Optional |
| --- | --- |
| Comfortable chairs or benches for breastfeeding, bottle-feeding, and expressing | Disinfectant for the changing mat, such as a sensor alcohol dispenser |
| Nappy-changing table with a raised edge and a wipeable soft mat. If space allows, place it perpendicular to the wall, at a height and with knee and manoeuvring space for a person with a disability | Clothing hooks beside the feeding chairs |
| Sink with a lever tap and soap dispenser. An automatic sensor tap and dispenser are desirable | Safety mirror |
| Hand-drying facility, such as a paper-towel dispenser | Floor drain or water-leakage sensor |
| Waste container with a hinged cover for nappies | Emergency alarm |
| Small side table | |
| Electrical outlet for expressing equipment | |

**SD takeaway:** Provide the changing table, a lever-tap sink, a covered nappy bin, and a power outlet inside the room, not as loose furniture added later.

### 3. Lactation room and operation (paragraphs 11–18)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Number and place | At least one in each office building, in the common areas, for persons authorised by the landlords or tenants and their employees. Separate from the toilets, with no foul air |
| Size | Not less than 7.5 m², for at least two users at a time, with privacy, and at least for one wheelchair user. Separate cubicles with doors |
| Entry | Sign that only females may enter. Cubicle locks openable from outside in an emergency, with an “engaged” indicator |
| Basic fittings | Electrical outlets, small side tables, and comfortable chairs or sofas |
| If space and services allow | Refrigerator, hand drying, covered waste container, safety mirror, clothing hooks, sink with lever tap and soap dispenser, and a floor drain or leak sensor where a sink is provided |
| Milk storage | Expressed milk is stored in a refrigerator. The refrigerator need not be inside the lactation room |
| Operation | Both room types are free of charge during the opening hours of the building. Post the opening hours, the target users if applicable, and the manager’s contact beside the sign. The responsible person checks them regularly |

**SD takeaway:** Provide a lactation room of not less than 7.5 m² in the common area of each office building, with two cubicles including one for a wheelchair user, and keep it out of the toilets.
