# ADV-19 Construction and Demolition Waste
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue June 2000 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009; previously PNAP 243)

> Scope note: Advice on reducing construction and demolition material and on disposing of it through a trip-ticket system. It recommends that private projects follow the public-works practice in Works Bureau Technical Circular No. 5/99. It does not itself prohibit a material.

## Regulatory Overview
More than one quarter of the solid waste disposed of in landfills comes from building construction, so waste is to be reduced at planning and design as well as on site. Public fill, the inert portion, goes to public filling facilities, and construction and demolition waste, the non-inert portion, goes to landfills.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Design measures that cut waste (paragraph 2)
| Stage | Measures named in the note |
| --- | --- |
| Planning and design | Precast and prefabrication, including modular design, precast façade, staircase, and slab, prefabricated external elements, precast bridge decks or footbridges, precast paving blocks, prefabricated kitchen or bathroom accessories, and proprietary doorsets and partition walls; sprayed plaster; balancing cut and fill; pulverised fuel ash in concrete for substructure, superstructure, and streetworks; combined services trenches; purchasers’ choice of finishes and fittings; less timber from non-sustainable sources; system formwork |
| Construction | A waste management plan; non-timber hoarding; metal formwork and metal falsework; on-site sorting; on-site water conservation; recycling concrete as fill or hardcore; recycling aggregate for concrete in non-structural work; identifying recycling facilities; an underground mechanical spray wheel wash |

**SD takeaway:** Balance cut and fill, combine the service trenches, and specify precast or system formwork so the volume that leaves the site is decided before tender.

### 2. Waste management plan (paragraphs 3–4)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Recommendation | Advise the client to require the contractor to submit a waste management plan for the authorized person’s agreement |
| Contents | Types and estimated quantities; when the waste arises; reduction measures; on-site separation; on-site and off-site reuse; storage areas; quantities for off-site disposal; disposal outlets; monitoring and auditing; organisation; a reuse and recycling list with quantities; the trip-ticket system; processing, storage, and disposal of hazardous waste; and packaging |
| Over 300,000 m³ | The Director of Environmental Protection is prepared to advise before the plan is accepted. The Public Fill Committee of the Civil Engineering Department is also prepared to advise on management and beneficial reuse |

**SD takeaway:** If the job will produce more than 300,000 m³ of construction and demolition material, obtain that advice before the waste management plan is accepted.

### 3. Trip-ticket system (paragraphs 5–7 and Appendix A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Split | Public fill (soil, rock, concrete, bricks, bituminous materials, and similar inert material) to a designated public filling facility. Construction and demolition waste (the non-inert portion) to a designated landfill. Public fill should not go to a landfill |
| Private projects | The Building Authority strongly recommends the same system. The project administrator applies through the Civil Engineering Department and the Environmental Protection Department for the designated outlets |
| Application data | Contract number and title, site location, anticipated quantities, disposal programme, any recommendation in an environmental impact assessment or contamination assessment, contaminant test results if any, types of fill and waste, and the project officer’s contacts |
| Each truck | A Disposal Delivery Form issued by the project officer. The driver presents it at the entrance, obtains a receipt at the exit, and the contractor gives the original receipt to the engineer’s or architect’s representative. The project office reconciles that against the monthly transaction report |
| Dumping licence | A dump truck needs a valid Dumping Licence issued by the Civil Engineering Department. A truck without one is rejected |
| Hours and charge | Public filling facilities are normally open 8:30 a.m. to 12:00 noon and 1:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m., and are open to the public free of charge for land delivery of inert material by vehicles |

**SD takeaway:** Specify the designated public filling facility for inert material and the designated landfill for non-inert waste, and require a trip ticket for every truck.
