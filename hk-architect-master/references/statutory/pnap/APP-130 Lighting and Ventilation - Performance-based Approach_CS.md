# APP-130 Lighting and Ventilation — Performance-based Approach
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision December 2016 | Buildings Department (first issue December 2003)

> Scope note: Alternative performance standards the Building Authority will accept in place of the prescriptive rules in regulations 30, 31, and 32 of the Building (Planning) Regulations for natural lighting and ventilation of habitable rooms and domestic kitchens. Office conversions of existing buildings are a separate modification route. It is not a daylight code for non-domestic rooms generally.

## Regulatory Overview
A habitable room may be accepted at a vertical daylight factor of 8% and 1.5 air changes per hour by natural means, and a domestic kitchen at 4% with 1.5 natural air changes plus 5 mechanical air changes. A modification of the prescriptive regulations is accepted if those standards are met, including by the simplified method in Appendix A.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Performance standards (paragraph 1)
Vertical daylight factor is measured at the centre of the window pane. It is the percentage of illuminance on a vertical surface against the instantaneous horizontal illuminance from a complete hemisphere of sky, excluding direct sunlight, and it includes light from the sky and reflected light from buildings and the ground.

| Room | Vertical daylight factor | Air changes per hour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habitable room | 8% | 1.5 by natural means |
| Kitchen | 4% | 1.5 by natural means plus 5 by mechanical means |

A project may mix prescriptive rooms and performance rooms. For regulation 31(2), the base may be measured at an angle of not more than 15 degrees from the external wall.

**SD takeaway:** Size a habitable-room window for 8% vertical daylight factor and 1.5 natural air changes, and a kitchen for 4% plus a mechanical 5 air changes.

### 2. Unobstructed vision area (Appendix A, Part II)
The standards are deemed met if the windows meet the simplified lighting method and the ventilation method. The unobstructed vision area of a window is the unobstructed area inside a cone of 100 degrees, measured to both edges of the glazing, symmetrical and perpendicular to the window plane. The maximum length of the cone equals the height of the facade in which the window is provided. Facade height is measured from the top of the window head at the lowermost storey in which the window is provided to the top of the parapet of the main roof. Drying racks, small projecting air-conditioner platforms or hoods, and window eaves may be disregarded if they are not excessive.

**SD takeaway:** The daylight cone is 100 degrees and no longer than the facade height from the lowest window head to the main-roof parapet.

### 3. Office conversion (paragraph 6)
Where wholesale conversion of an industrial or other building to offices cannot provide the required natural light and ventilation because of the original building, a modification of regulations 30 and 31 will be favourably considered if there is adequate artificial lighting and mechanical ventilation and an energy-efficient design that could achieve 40% in Energy Use and Indoor Environmental Quality under BEAM Plus conferred by the Hong Kong Green Building Council. Prerequisites: the Council’s letter acknowledging project registration, an undertaking to submit the Provisional Assessment result showing that 40% before consent, and the Final Assessment result showing that 40% within 6 months of the Building Authority’s acknowledgement of Form BA14.

**SD takeaway:** An office conversion that cannot daylight the floors needs 40% in Energy Use and Indoor Environmental Quality under BEAM Plus before the modification of regulations 30 and 31.
