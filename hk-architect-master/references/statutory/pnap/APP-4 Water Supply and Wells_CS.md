# APP-4 Water Supply and Wells
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision December 2005 | Buildings Department (first issue January 1975; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Flushing and potable demand, and the tests required before a well or other non-waterworks source is accepted. Apply to the Water Authority for a Certificate Regarding Water Supply Availability before design starts, and submit it with the first building plans. Evaporative air-conditioning is not supplied from fresh or salt mains except for the immediate needs of a specific industrial process.

## Regulatory Overview
Where the Water Authority certifies on Form WWO 1004, or the current equivalent, under Regulation 10A(5) that a waterworks supply is not available, permission to take water from a well or other source may be considered. Potable quality must satisfy the Building Authority, and a Water Authority sample report or other acceptable evidence may be required.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Flushing demand (paragraph 4)
| User | Allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Domestic buildings | 450 litres per required soil fitment per day |
| Offices, factories, department stores, shops, public buildings, and other non-domestic buildings of a like nature | 450 litres per required soil fitment per day |
| Restaurants | 13.5 litres per seat per day |
| Cinemas | 4.5 litres per seat per day |
| Schools | 18 litres per head per day |
| Hotels and boarding houses | 90 litres per room per day |

Potable demand is assessed on the merits of the case under Regulation 10A(4) and 10A(2). Consult the Water Authority if in doubt.

**SD takeaway:** Size the flushing store to 450 litres per required soil fitment per day for domestic and ordinary non-domestic buildings, and use the lower seat or room rates only for restaurants, cinemas, schools, and hotels.

### 2. Wells (paragraphs 7–12 and Appendix A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Where | Normally not on government land. On private land, Building (Construction) Regulation 85 permission only if yield is adequate and abstraction will not adversely affect nearby services, buildings, structures, or land |
| Areas to avoid | Yuen Long Town in Scheduled Area No. 2 and Ma On Shan in Scheduled Area No. 4. Flushing water will normally be supplied there whenever possible, because groundwater abstraction is likely to cause excessive settlement |
| If effects are not already demonstrated | A registered geotechnical engineer submits a site investigation with groundwater levels and permeability tests, a short-term and long-term assessment of groundwater change, movement, and stability, and field tests if needed |
| Yield test, pumping under 12 hours a day | Appendix A, for Regulation 10A(7). Only from 1 December to 30 April. Seven consecutive days. Days 1 to 6 at the proposed daily yield averaged over the proposed pumping period, not more than 12 hours a day so that at least 12 hours of recovery remain. Quantity by meter, not by pump rating. Day 7 at full discharge and maximum drawdown, examined for suspended soil particles. Discharge to a proper drain or watercourse, not back into the well or aquifer |
| Acceptable result | Daily quantity not less than the required daily quantity, adequate recovery after each day's pumping, and clean water on the day-7 examination |
| Longer pumping or doubt about long-term yield | A more sophisticated test may be required. Other methods in BS 6316:1983 may be approved |
| Notice | Notify the Buildings Department and the Geotechnical Engineering Office in writing at least one week before the test. Send one extra copy of the yield-test report to the Geotechnical Information Unit |

**SD takeaway:** Do not rely on a well in Scheduled Areas No. 2 or No. 4, and elsewhere prove yield with a seven-day test between 1 December and 30 April at not more than 12 hours of pumping a day.
