# APP-105 Water Seepage
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision September 2024 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 230; first issue October 1999)

> Scope note: Prevention of water seepage in new buildings, and when water-borne pipes may pass through structural members. Existing seepage is treated as a management and maintenance matter. Enhanced guidelines in Appendix B apply to new general building plans, or a major revision, submitted after 31 March 2025. Pipe ducts that keep pipes off structure may be excluded from gross floor area under PNAP APP-93.

## Regulatory Overview
External walls must be impervious, damp walls and floors of rooms with a water supply must resist moisture, and roofs must be weatherproof, under sections 27, 32, 33, and 34 of the Building (Construction) Regulation, with materials meeting section 3. Water-borne piping is kept out of structural members except the limited penetrations in Appendix A.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Where seepage is designed out (paragraphs 5–8)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Susceptible parts | Roofs, external walls, curtain walls, windows, balconies, utility platforms, bathrooms, kitchens, plumbing and drainage pipes, car-park floors, and basements |
| Design response | Specify waterproofing, supervise construction closely, and test waterproofing performance on completion |
| Routing | Design all water-borne piping off structural elements so it can be repaired and replaced |
| Plans | State on the structural and drainage plans that no water-borne piping will be embedded in structural elements other than paragraph 2 of Appendix A. If any section is embedded, submit the routing with the drainage plans and justify long-term integrity. Omission is a ground for disapproval under section 16(1)(i) of the Buildings Ordinance. Section 14(4)(a) of the Building (Construction) Regulation is relevant |
| Pipe ducts | The Building Authority is prepared to exclude genuine pipe ducts designed to PNAP APP-93 from gross floor area |

**SD takeaway:** Keep water-borne pipes out of columns, slabs, walls, beams, transfer plates, pile caps, and footings, and use a pipe duct if that is how they are routed.

### 2. Permitted penetrations (Appendix A)
Embedment is not permitted in those members except pipes that pierce them, where there is no adverse effect and the pipes are easily accessible. Sleeves cast in are preferred.

| Penetration | Limits treated as no adverse effect |
| --- | --- |
| Vertical pipe through a reinforced concrete floor slab or transfer plate | Hole not greater than 150 mm in diameter or the minimum bar spacing of the slab in either direction, whichever is the less. No main reinforcement severed. Trimming bars not less than the size of the main reinforcement around the hole |
| Horizontal pipe through a reinforced concrete beam | Hole not greater than 150 mm or one-third of the beam depth, whichever is the less, at the neutral axis. Vertical and horizontal trimming bars not less than 16 mm diameter around the hole and at each side. No shear reinforcement severed |
| Horizontal pipe through a structural wall | Hole not greater than 150 mm or the minimum spacing of the vertical reinforcement, whichever is the less. Vertical and horizontal trimming bars not less than the size of the vertical reinforcement, around the hole and on both sides |

**SD takeaway:** A sleeve through a slab or beam stays at or below 150 mm, and a beam hole also stays within one-third of the beam depth at the neutral axis.
