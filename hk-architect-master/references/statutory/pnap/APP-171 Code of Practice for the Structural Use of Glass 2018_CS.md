# APP-171 Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Glass 2018
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue February 2024 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Amendments to the Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Glass 2018 promulgated in Appendix A (July 2020) and Appendix B (February 2024), and uploaded to the Buildings Department website. It is not a substitute for the glass code. Design to the amended clauses.

## Regulatory Overview
The July 2020 amendments change the retaining-device rule for structural-sealant glazing and the glass-fin torsion symbol. The February 2024 amendments delete the 8 mm weep holes from the typical glass-balustrade detail, revise the repeated-pressure test pressure, and revise the failure-load symbols in Annex B.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Retaining devices if the sealant bond fails (Appendix A, clause 7.1.1)
A facade or glass element fixed by structural sealant on four sides, where any point of the pane is at a height of 5 m or more above the finished floor of the accessible area on either side, shall be designed to prevent the pane falling if the sealant bond fails. A retaining device (feature capping, angle, bracket, insert, or similar) is provided at any two opposing edges. The device and the associated panes shall resist 37% of the design wind pressure on the pane, multiplied by a partial load factor of 1.0. Design wind pressure is the wind reference pressure from the Code of Practice on Wind Effects in Hong Kong 2019, without adjustment factors. Self-weight is mechanically supported by setting blocks.

Gaskets are provided on both sides of the glass pane unless it is structurally glazed, and have continuous mechanical engagement (clause 7.5.1). Deglazing tests follow the sealant manufacturer’s suggested percentage (clause 9.4).

**SD takeaway:** Four-side structural silicone at 5 m or more above an accessible floor needs retaining devices on two opposing edges, sized for 37% of the unadjusted 2019 wind reference pressure.

### 2. Glass-fin torsion (Appendix A, Annex C)
For a solid rectangular glass fin, the torsional moment of inertia may be taken as J = d t^3 / 3 times (1 − 0.63 t / d), where d and t are the depth and thickness of the fin. G and E are taken as 28,700 N/mm2 and 70,000 N/mm2.

**SD takeaway:** Use fin thickness t, not breadth b, in the torsion equation, with G of 28,700 N/mm2 and E of 70,000 N/mm2.

### 3. February 2024 test and balustrade changes (Appendix B)
| Item | Amendment |
| --- | --- |
| Figure 6.1 | The 8 mm diameter weep holes for external application in the typical glass balustrade details are deleted |
| Clause 8.3.1(b) | For the repeated positive and negative pressure test, p2 is the net wind pressure P on the mullion of the representative portion in the performance test, from the Code of Practice on Wind Effects in Hong Kong 2019. Not fewer than 5 pressure pulses. If the 2004 wind code is used for the curtain wall, p2 is the product of the total pressure coefficient cp and the design wind pressure qz for that part of the building |
| Annex B, item B1(2) step k and Figure B1.1 | Failure load is recorded as W, and the applied-load expression in the figure is revised |

**SD takeaway:** Do not rely on the deleted 8 mm weep holes in Figure 6.1, and set the repeated pressure test to the 2019 net mullion pressure with at least five pulses.
