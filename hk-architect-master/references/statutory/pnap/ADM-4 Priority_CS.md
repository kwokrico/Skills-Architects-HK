# ADM-4 Priority
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision March 2005 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009; first issue April 1977)

> Scope note: Sets which new-building submissions may be processed ahead of the queue. It does not change planning, lease, or land-transaction priority.

## Regulatory Overview
On application, the Buildings Department may grant priority status for processing submissions for new buildings that fall within a short list of public-interest and specified-agency projects. Priority applies only to matters involving the Buildings Department and has no bearing on land transactions.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Projects that may be granted priority (paragraphs 1–3)
| Criterion | Projects |
| --- | --- |
| (a)(i) | Urgently required public utility and institutional buildings, including schools, electricity sub-stations, and similar buildings where urgency and public interest can be shown |
| (a)(ii) | Hong Kong Housing Society projects |
| (b) | Projects on lands owned by the Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks Corporation (previously the Hong Kong Industrial Estates Corporation) |
| (c) | Any project submitted for or on behalf of the Urban Renewal Authority by a wholly owned subsidiary or a joint-venture consortium of which the Authority is a partner |
| (d) | Projects likely to contribute significantly to the economic growth of Hong Kong; positive support from relevant government departments is required |
| Exceptional | Other high public-interest projects not in the list, only in exceptional circumstances and after consultation with the departments concerned |

Apply with the first submission of plans. Alteration and addition projects are not normally granted priority, except projects designed to combat air, noise, or water pollution, or to eliminate or reduce discharge of existing polluting waste and noxious or toxic effluents.

**SD takeaway:** If the project is a new building in one of the listed categories, ask for priority with the first plan submission; do not rely on it for alteration and addition work unless the pollution exception applies.

### 2. Keeping or losing priority (paragraphs 4–5)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Review | Priority projects are kept under constant review |
| Withdrawal | If a priority project does not proceed expeditiously, priority status may be withdrawn |
| Limit | The grant applies only to Buildings Department matters and has no bearing on land transactions |

**SD takeaway:** Priority is a processing favour that can be withdrawn if the project stalls, and it does not move a land transaction.
