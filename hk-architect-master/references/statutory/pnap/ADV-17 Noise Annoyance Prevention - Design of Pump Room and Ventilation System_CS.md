# ADV-17 Noise Annoyance Prevention — Design of Pump Room and Ventilation System
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue September 1998 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Layout and insulation guidelines so pump rooms and ventilation plant do not annoy residents. The legal noise limits are in the Noise Control Ordinance and its Technical Memorandum, which this note does not reproduce.

## Regulatory Overview
Pump rooms and ventilation systems can cause noise annoyance in the same building or a nearby building if airborne and structure-borne paths are not designed. The note gives insulation and isolation measures, and advises a ventilation-system noise level 5 dB(A) below the acceptable levels in paragraph 2 of the Technical Memorandum for the Assessment of Noise from Places other than Domestic Premises, Public Places or Construction Sites.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Pump-room layout and insulation (paragraph 2)
| Path | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Buffer | Prefer to separate pump rooms from residential flats by non-sensitive space such as a stairwell or switch room |
| Airborne, pumps | Use quieter pumps and motors. If the total sound power level of the pumps exceeds 100 dB(A), consider a higher insulating capacity than the figures below |
| Airborne, fabric | Walls and ceilings or floors adjoining noise-sensitive receivers (residential flats and school premises) with a minimum Sound Transmission Class of 50 |
| Openings | Locate away from sensitive areas, or treat air grilles and fan outlets with an acoustic louvre or silencer |
| Doors | Doors facing sensitive areas with a minimum Sound Transmission Class of 25 |
| Structure-borne | Anti-vibration mounts sized to the load, and flexible joints (Appendix A). Spring or rubber isolators on pipes and hangers. Rubber sleeve and gasket where pipes pass through walls |
| Flow | Size pipes to avoid noise from excessive velocity, especially at valves |

**SD takeaway:** Do not place a pump room against a flat; buffer it, give the party wall Sound Transmission Class 50, and isolate the pumps and the pipes.

### 2. Ventilation plant near dwellings and schools (paragraphs 3–5)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Target | 5 dB(A) below the relevant acceptable noise level in paragraph 2 of that Technical Memorandum |
| All systems | Quieter models of appropriate capacity; isolate rotating or vibrating parts with mounts, flexible connections, damping, and sound-absorbing lagging (Appendix B) |
| Near residential premises or a school | Locate sources away from the receivers; check large fans and air-cooled chillers; add acoustic panels, enclosures, silencers, or louvres where a source faces a sensitive receiver |
| Legal ceiling | The Noise Control Ordinance and the Technical Memorandum issued under it stipulate the maximum allowable noise from pump rooms and from ventilation and air-conditioning systems |

**SD takeaway:** Keep large fans and air-cooled chillers off facades that face flats or schools, and design the system to 5 dB(A) under the Technical Memorandum acceptable level.
