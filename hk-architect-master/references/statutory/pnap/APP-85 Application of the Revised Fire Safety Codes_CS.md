# APP-85 Application of the Revised Fire Safety Codes
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued October 1996 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: How the 1995 means-of-access code, the 1996 fire-resisting-construction code, and the 1996 means-of-escape code apply in situations the Ordinances do not state explicitly. Fire-engineering submissions are promised a separate guideline.

## Regulatory Overview
The three revised codes are the Code of Practice for Means of Access for Firefighting and Rescue 1995, the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction 1996, and the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire 1996. Each contains a fire-engineering approach as an alternative to the prescriptive provisions.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What must follow the revised codes (paragraph 2)
| Situation | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Alterations and additions | Generally only the areas affected, including shared exits, need comply. Unaffected parts need not follow the revised codes |
| New restaurant licence | Only the areas subject to the new licence application need comply |
| Consent for works on plans approved before the effective date | Generally no objection to consent if the application is made within two years of the approval date and is in order in all other respects |
| Population by head count | No objection in principle where alteration works are proposed, or in comparable premises, if the authorized person shows the comparables are suitable. Location, management, patronage, class, type, and age should be similar. For a new building, a prescriptive population leaves margin for later alterations |

**SD takeaway:** On an alteration, upgrade only the affected areas and the shared exits to the revised codes, and apply for consent within two years of an approval that predates those codes.
