# ADV-4 Control of Environmental Nuisance from Construction Sites
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision March 2026 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 144; first issue June 1991)

> Scope note: A checklist of site measures against noise, dust, water, pests, roads, and hoardings, with the ordinances that enforce them. It is advice to authorized persons, registered structural engineers, and registered geotechnical engineers; a similar note goes to registered contractors. It does not replace an environmental permit where the project is a designated project.

## Regulatory Overview
Construction sites are to be planned so that noise, air, water, health, and street nuisances are prevented or minimised, using the practical measures in Appendix A. Where the project is a Schedule 2 designated project under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance, the project proponent obtains an environmental permit before construction and operation and complies with every condition of that permit.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Noise and when a construction noise permit is required (Appendix A, items 1–2)
| Work | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Percussive piling | Between 7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m. on a day that is not a public holiday, a construction noise permit is required. It is generally prohibited at other times |
| Restricted hours | Between 7:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m., or at any time on a general holiday including Sunday, powered mechanical equipment for general construction works, and prescribed construction works in designated (predominantly built-up) areas, also need a construction noise permit |
| Prescribed construction works | Erection or dismantling of formwork or scaffolding; loading, unloading, or handling of rubble, wooden boards, steel bars, wood, or scaffolding material; and hammering |
| Planning measures | Prefer a quieter method, such as non-percussive piling instead of percussive piling, and quieter powered mechanical equipment. Place the source farther from noise-sensitive receivers, screen it, avoid school examination periods, and avoid running noisy operations together |
| Offence | Works without a valid permit, or in breach of permit conditions, are an offence under the Noise Control Ordinance. The maximum fine is level 6 on the first conviction and $200,000 on a subsequent conviction |

**SD takeaway:** If the foundation method is percussive, or if noisy work will run after 7:00 p.m., on a Sunday, or on a general holiday, allow programme time for a construction noise permit.

### 2. Dust, smoke, and notified construction works (Appendix A, items 3–5)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Notify the Environmental Protection Department beforehand | Site formation; reclamation; demolition of a building; tunnel works excluding any part beyond 100 m from the exit to the open air; construction of the foundation; construction of the superstructure; and road construction works. Maximum fine $25,000 on the first conviction and $50,000 on a subsequent conviction |
| Dust reduction | Cover dusty materials and stockpiles, spray water, hard-pave and spray the main haul road, cover loads, control vehicle speed, wash wheels before leaving the site, water the site, and erect hoarding. Failure to adopt appropriate measures: maximum fine $50,000 first conviction, and $100,000 and 3 months’ imprisonment on a subsequent conviction |
| Specified process | A specified process under Schedule 1 of the Air Pollution Control Ordinance, such as concrete batching where a licence is required, needs that licence. Maximum fine $200,000 and six months’ imprisonment, with a daily fine of $20,000 |
| Open burning | Prohibited under the Air Pollution Control (Open Burning) Regulation. Maximum fine $50,000 plus $500 for every 15 minutes the offence continues, on the first conviction |
| Demolition | Fans, catch platforms, and dust screens are required under the Building (Demolition Works) Regulations. Maximum fine $25,000 (level 4) |

**SD takeaway:** Show hoarding, a paved haul road, wheel washing, and covered stockpiles on the site plan, and notify the Environmental Protection Department before site formation, demolition, foundation, or superstructure works.

### 3. Drains, sediment, and site water (Appendix A, items 6–13 and 18)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Before connection to the public system | Temporary drainage with a de-silting facility. Add a sand trap, settling pit, or grease trap, perimeter channels or sand bags, and pH adjustment or a petrol interceptor where needed |
| Discharge | Construction site discharges need a valid licence under the Water Pollution Control Ordinance. Discharge of waste or polluting matter into a water control zone: maximum penalty $200,000 and 6 months’ imprisonment for the first offence |
| Demolition and foundations | Seal sewer and drainage connections before those works start. Failure to seal them in demolition is an offence under the Building (Demolition Works) Regulations, maximum fine $25,000 (level 4) |
| Surface water | Direct it through sand or silt removal before it enters a natural channel or a surface-water drain. Do not connect it illicitly to a foul sewer |
| Toilets | Connect temporary toilets to a proper sewer, or, in an unsewered area, to a septic tank and soakaway, or to a sewage treatment plant for large flows. The same principle applies to kitchens |
| Stockpiles and slopes | Cover open stockpiles and temporarily exposed slopes with tarpaulin or similar fabric, especially in the rainy season |
| Manholes | Do not block access to public manholes or drainage reserves with temporary structures |

**SD takeaway:** Design the temporary drainage with a silt trap before any connection, and seal the existing sewers before demolition or foundation work.

### 4. Streets, hoardings, and waste (Appendix A, items 19–34)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Chemical waste | Register with the Environmental Protection Department as a chemical waste producer, package, label, and store the waste, and use a licensed collector. Maximum fine $200,000 and 6 months’ imprisonment |
| Mud and litter on streets | Wash wheels, flush vehicle bodies, cover loads, and keep a minimum footpath width. Excavation in a road follows the excavation permit and the Excavation Permit Processing Manual |
| Falling objects | Provide fans, catch platforms, and mesh screens, and clear them regularly |
| Hoardings | Keep them to the approved hoarding plan, including the minimum width, sound, and clear for pedestrians. Survey existing traffic signs, taxi ranks, bus stops, queuing areas, and crossings, and agree the hoarding plan with the Transport Department. The note also points to PNAP APP-23 |

**SD takeaway:** Fix the site entrance, wheel-wash, and hoarding width on the hoarding plan so the footpath and traffic signs stay usable.
