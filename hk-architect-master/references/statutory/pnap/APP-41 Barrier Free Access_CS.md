# APP-41 Barrier Free Access
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision June 2025 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 112; first issue April 1985)

> Scope note: Access for persons with a disability under section 84 of the Disability Discrimination Ordinance, Cap. 487, regulation 72 and the Third Schedule of the Building (Planning) Regulations, and the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008. The 2025 Edition on the Buildings Department website incorporates Appendices C to I and Appendix J. Regulation 8(1)(m) of the Building (Administration) Regulations requires the access to be shown on the plans. Sanitary provision is also under regulation 61(3) of the Building (Standards of Sanitary Fitments, Plumbing, Drainage Works and Latrines) Regulations, and regulation 39(3)(e) of the Building (Planning) Regulations.

## Regulatory Overview
Buildings in Part 4 of the Third Schedule have limited barrier-free access, and every other building, except one exempted under regulation 72(4), complies in full. The Building Authority will not approve plans unless reasonable access will be provided, and an exemption or modification is considered only where special circumstances are shown, such as a site where compliance is impracticable or the provision would impose unjustifiable hardship, with advice from the Advisory Committee on Barrier Free Access.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Where full access may be varied (paragraphs 6–8 and Appendix A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| New building, or alteration of an existing building that already should have access | Vary the regulations only with the special circumstances in Appendix A, considered on the merits |
| Alteration of a building that never had access | Take all practical measures. An exemption is considered case by case |
| Acceptable case for not providing access for persons with locomotory disabilities | A ramp would alter common parts, the applicant has no control over that area, consent of the co-owners or the owners' corporation is declined or cannot be obtained, and, where a ground beam is involved, there is a spatial or structural constraint |
| Committee | The Building Authority takes the committee's advice on whether access is practicable within the curtilage and whether it would impose unjustifiable hardship |

**SD takeaway:** Draw the barrier-free route on the general building plans, and do not omit a ramp to the common parts unless control, consent, and any ground-beam constraint are all demonstrated.

### 2. Recommended elderly-friendly sizes (Appendix J, Chapter 6 of the 2025 Edition)
These are recommended, not the obligatory requirements of the Manual. Owners are encouraged to adopt them.

| Item | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Bathroom | Clear turning space not less than 1,200 mm diameter. Doorway not less than 850 mm. Shower not less than 1,500 mm by 900 mm, with a folding seat. Grab bars. Slip-resistant floors. No shiny finishes such as marble or glazed tile |
| Common doors | Opening force not more than 28 N for an external door and 18 N for an internal door where a closer is fitted. Lever handles. Automatic doors as far as practicable |
| Handrails to corridors, steps, and stairs | Top of rail between 810 mm and 900 mm above finished floor. Timber or a plastic-coated surface |
| Lift | Clear depth not less than 1.5 m. Lobby controls or buttons at 1,050 mm above finished floor, easy to operate, with a luminous contrast of at least 30%. Buttons in the car back-lit. A folding seat or a lean rail in the car, except a fireman's lift. Audio indication |
| Routes | Without steps, thresholds, small ramps, or kerbs where possible. Where a level change remains, handrails or grab bars, and luminous contrast of the floor and wall at the change. Handrails along common corridors. Wider treads and lower risers |
| External paving | Avoid open-jointed pavers and aeration blocks with an uneven or very rough surface |

Obligatory changes made between November 2012 and May 2024 are in Appendices C to I and are already in the 2025 Edition. They include, among other items, grab-rail diameter and length, control-button height, a common-area illumination of not less than 120 lux at finished floor level, and the prohibition of open risers. Use the figure and paragraph cited in the relevant appendix rather than an older printing of the 2008 Manual.

**SD takeaway:** For an elderly-friendly flat, keep a 1,200 mm turning circle in the bathroom, an 850 mm door, and a shower of at least 1,500 mm by 900 mm, and set common handrails between 810 mm and 900 mm.
