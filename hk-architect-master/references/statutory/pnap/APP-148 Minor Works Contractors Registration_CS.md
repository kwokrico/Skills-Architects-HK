# APP-148 Minor Works Contractors Registration
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Buildings Department

> Scope note: Who may be registered to carry out minor works under the Building (Minor Works) Regulation, and the difference between an individual and a company. The list of works is Schedule 1, not this note. It is not a design code. Which items are minor works, and the simplified procedures, are in PNAP APP-147.

## Regulatory Overview
Schedule 1 specifies 187 minor works items, of which 61 are class III, and only a contractor registered for the class, type, and item may carry them out. An individual is registered for class III items; a company is registered for types and classes.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Two registers (paragraphs 2–6)
| Register | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Registered Minor Works Contractor (Individual) | Section 10(1)(a): an individual self-employed worker, for items of class III minor works |
| Registered Minor Works Contractor (Company) | Section 10(1)(b): a corporation, sole proprietorship, or partnership, for types and classes |
| Publication | Names, items, types, classes, expiry, and registration number in the Gazette annually, and on the Buildings Department website, including the authorized signatory |
| Application | An individual specifies items. A company specifies types and classes. The register records only what the applicant is qualified for |

**SD takeaway:** Class III items can be done by a registered individual; other classes need a registered company for that type and class.

### 2. Individual and company tests (paragraphs 8–14)
| Applicant | Must satisfy |
| --- | --- |
| Individual, section 11(2) | Appropriate qualifications and experience; ability to personally carry out the class III items; a recognised training course on class III minor works; suitability |
| Company, section 12(5) | Qualifications and experience of key personnel; access to plant and resources; adequate management structure if a corporation; persons appointed to act understand the works and the basic statutory requirements; suitability |
| Authorized signatory | At least one. A sole proprietor is the only person eligible. A partner appointed by the other partners, or a person appointed by the board, may act for a partnership or corporation |
| Technical director | A corporation needs at least one director, authorised to access plant and resources, provide technical and financial support, and supervise the authorized signatory |
| Dual role | One person may be both authorized signatory and technical director of a corporation if both sets of requirements are met |
| Not at the same time | A registered individual may be employed as authorized signatory of a firm, but not also be its sole proprietor, partner, or technical director. An accepted authorized signatory or technical director cannot be key personnel of another contractor firm at the same time |

**SD takeaway:** A company needs an authorized signatory, and a corporation also needs a technical director who is a director, and that person cannot hold the same key role in another firm.
