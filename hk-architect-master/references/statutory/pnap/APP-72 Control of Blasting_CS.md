# APP-72 Control of Blasting
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision April 2007 | Buildings Department (first issue June 1995; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Blasting in private building works under the Buildings Ordinance and the Dangerous Goods Ordinance, Cap. 295. A blasting permit is also required from the Commissioner of Mines, who is the Director of Civil Engineering and Development, under Dangerous Goods (General) Regulation 46. Railway effects are in Practice Notes 77 and 279. Technical standards are also in the General Specification for Civil Engineering Works.

## Regulatory Overview
The geotechnical assessment at general building plan stage should identify whether rock excavation needs blasting and discuss its feasibility. If blasting is part of site formation, excavation, or foundation works, a blasting assessment prepared by a competent person with at least 4 years of relevant design and supervision experience, and signed by the responsible registered geotechnical engineer and/or registered structural engineer, is submitted with those plans.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the assessment and the plans contain (paragraphs 3–4 and Appendix A)
| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| On the plans | Areas of blasting; geology, ground, and groundwater; locations of sensitive receivers; control criteria; instrumentation and monitoring; precautionary measures such as road closure, evacuation, and restricted hours; requirements for regular reports; a note that the plan is read with the blasting assessment |
| Assessment | Site plans of all sensitive receivers, including streets, structures, foundations, railways, utilities, water mains, drains, sewers, gas mains, slopes, retaining walls, boulders, tunnels, and caverns; ground and site-history report; condition of sensitive receivers; demonstration that blasting will not injure persons or damage property; preventive measures; action limits for vibration and air-overpressure, with the source of the limits and evidence of agreement with owners or maintenance agents where appropriate; outline blast design; safety management system and sequence; monitoring stations, performance criteria, and action limits; evacuation, closure, and warnings; delivery of explosives; feasibility of an on-site store if one is proposed |
| Supervision of the assumptions | Generally by that competent person during construction. Curriculum vitae is included in the submission |
| If blasting is identified later | The same assessment and any revised plans are still required |
| Demolition | Normally not used, because surroundings are generally high-density. In an exceptional case, the same 4-year competent person prepares the assessment, signed by the registered structural engineer and registered geotechnical engineer, with the demolition plans, following the Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings and the relevant items of Appendix A |

**SD takeaway:** Identify blasting at general building plan stage, and do not submit formation or foundation plans that rely on explosives without a blasting assessment from a person with at least 4 years' blasting experience.

### 2. Site supervision (paragraphs 5–7 and Appendix B)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Legal basis | Section 17 conditions, in addition to the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans 2005 and the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2005 |
| Competent supervisor | Independent of the registered contractor, at least 4 years' relevant experience, assisting the grade T5 technically competent person in the geotechnical or structural stream. A higher requirement may be imposed for scale, complexity, and risk. One person who holds both qualifications may supervise alone. Curricula vitae are accepted before blasting starts |
| Grade T5 tasks | Check the method statement; for each blast, check the design and precautions against the approved plans and the permit; verify ground conditions against the assessment; confirm preventive measures before blasting starts and protective measures before each blast; review sensitive receivers before and after each blast; report after each phase |
| Grade T3 tasks | Inspect preventive works and the protective measures before each blast; monitor operations against the permit; inspect sensitive receivers before and after each blast; carry out the monitoring |

**SD takeaway:** Appoint a blasting supervisor with at least 4 years' experience, independent of the contractor, and have the grade T5 person check every blast against the approved plans and the Mines permit.
