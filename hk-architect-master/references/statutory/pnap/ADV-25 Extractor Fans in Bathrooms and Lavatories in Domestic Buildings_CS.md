# ADV-25 Extractor Fans in Bathrooms and Lavatories in Domestic Buildings
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue March 2004 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Recommends air relief and a fan volume for domestic bathrooms and lavatories that use an extractor fan. It does not replace the lighting and ventilation regulations.

## Regulatory Overview
An over-capacity extractor fan with insufficient fresh-air supply to a bathroom is identified as one factor thought to have contributed to the spread of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome at Amoy Gardens. Where an extractor fan is the mechanical ventilation, plumbing seals must stay intact, and air intake, flow path, and fan capacity must be considered together.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Fan volume and air relief (paragraphs 2–4)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Seals | Keep plumbing seals intact and operating as designed |
| Optimum volume | The World Health Organisation Environmental Health Team advised 2 cfm per square foot (10.2 L/s per square metre). A larger volume adds little comfort and risks negative pressure |
| Air relief | Provide an opening, such as an undercut to the door or a louvred opening in the door or wall, to limit negative pressure |
| Flow path | Avoid short-circuiting of ingress air and exhaust air |

**SD takeaway:** Size a domestic bathroom extractor to about 10.2 L/s per square metre and give the door an undercut or a louvre so the fan cannot pull the traps dry.
