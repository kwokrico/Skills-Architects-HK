# APP-73 Service Lanes
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision April 2011 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 179; first issued May 1995)

> Scope note: Private service lanes used for lighting, ventilation, escape, and access, and when a lane may be counted in site coverage and plot ratio. Building (Planning) Regulation 28 requires every domestic building to be provided with a suitable service lane at the rear or side, except in the circumstances that regulation prescribes.

## Regulatory Overview
A private or partly private service lane may be used to meet Buildings Ordinance requirements for lighting and ventilation, means of escape, and means of access only if the authorized person demonstrates permanent or pertinent rights-of-way and the integrity of the lane is ensured for all users of the proposed building, and an exit into such a lane is not acceptable unless users have unfettered rights-of-way. A lane, public or private, is not acceptable for escape and access if it is obstructed, incapable of improvement, or obviously decaying.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Area calculations and the target width (paragraphs 6–13)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Abutting private lane owned by the developer, not required for any Buildings Ordinance purpose | The Building Authority will give favourable consideration to including that area in site coverage and plot ratio. If rights-of-way have clearly established the legal status, apply for a modification of Building (Planning) Regulation 23(2)(a) |
| After inclusion | On completion the lane must be free of structures, except structures approved and taken into account in the calculations |
| Diverted lane required only for a neighbourhood pattern, not for the development | Favourable consideration to including the area in site coverage and plot ratio |
| Open space | Except as Building (Planning) Regulation 28(3) provides, exclude service lanes from the open-space requirement under regulation 25, whether or not the lane is in the site coverage and plot ratio |
| Long-term width | Upon full development of abutting sites, a lane would be direct and have an unobstructed width of not less than 3 m |
| Surrender | Where improvement is essential in the public interest but the lane is not specifically required for the scheme, plot ratio and site coverage concessions may be considered for free surrender of a lane acceptable to the Government, provided the area surrendered is not part of an existing lane. Submit a plan and a written undertaking to surrender free of cost, to pay for paving, channelling, sewering, drainage, and lighting where appropriate, and to leave the area free of structures |

**SD takeaway:** Do not discharge escape into a private lane without an unfettered right of way, keep a lane that is counted in plot ratio clear of structures, and aim for a direct unobstructed width of not less than 3 m.
