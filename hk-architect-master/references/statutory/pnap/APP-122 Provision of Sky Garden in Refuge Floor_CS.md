# APP-122 Provision of Sky Garden in Refuge Floor
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision September 2010 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 258; first issued December 2001)

> Scope note: Conditions for putting a communal sky garden on a refuge floor, including in wholesale conversion, and the related lift and fire-service rules. Refuge-floor rules remain those in the means-of-escape and fire-resisting-construction codes except where this note states a special provision. Other sky-garden criteria are in Joint Practice Note No. 1.

## Regulatory Overview
A refuge floor may also be a communal garden if planters, furniture, and equipment are excluded from the refuge area, fixed, non-combustible, and clear of exits and lift openings. Wholesale conversion may use a refuge floor, including the main roof, that misses some code dimensions if enhanced fire service installations and greenery are provided.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Garden on a refuge floor (paragraph 2)
All of the following:

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Codes | Paragraph 21 of the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire and paragraph 18 of the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction, except the special provisions in this note |
| Net refuge area | No account of space occupied by planters, equipment, and furniture when assessing net area for refuge and the minimum dimension of the refuge area |
| Fixing | All garden furniture and equipment firmly fixed |
| Materials | Furniture, equipment, rubbish bins, and other artificial features, including finishes, are non-combustible, do not emit toxic gas in fire, and do not obstruct exit routes and lift openings. Rubbish bins, where provided, have self-closing lids |
| Naked fire | The garden is not used for activities involving naked fire, such as barbecuing. A notice to that effect at prominent locations |
| Joint Practice Note No. 1 | Other criteria applicable to communal sky gardens are complied with |

**SD takeaway:** Keep planters and furniture out of the measured refuge area, fix them, and use only non-combustible materials that do not give off toxic gas.

### 2. Wholesale conversion and a green main roof (paragraphs 3 and 6–8)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| New refuge in a conversion | A communal sky garden is acceptable subject to paragraph 2 |
| Shortfall on paragraphs 21.2 and 21.3 of the means-of-escape code and/or paragraph 18.2 of the fire-resisting-construction code | Acceptable, given the original building, subject to enhanced fire service installations to the satisfaction of the Fire Services Department and suitable greenery in the refuge floor |
| Main roof as refuge | Designed as a green roof with not less than 50% of the total roof area planted with greenery, and complying with paragraphs 2(b) to (d). Short grass turf suitable for foot traffic can be counted towards the net area for refuge |
| Example of enhancement | Drencher water flow rate to cover all external wall openings of the refuge floor might have to be greater than 10 litres per minute per square metre. Where a sprinkler system is required, heads shall be fast-response type |
| Drencher waiver | Where a drencher is required at external wall openings, the Fire Services Department may waive it on the merits of the case |
| Plants | Live plants only for the greenery in paragraph 3. Artificial plants avoided where possible; if used, they and the soil substrate are non-combustible and do not emit toxic gases. Highly flammable live plants (resinous sap, or volatile waxes or oils in leaves, twigs, and stems) used with caution. Larger mature size is encouraged, subject to structural feasibility |
| Manual | A maintenance and management manual for the landscape works for end-users |

**SD takeaway:** If the main roof is the refuge in a conversion, plant not less than 50% of the roof, and expect a drencher above 10 litres per minute per square metre at the openings.

### 3. Lifts onto the refuge floor (paragraph 4)
Relaxation of the restriction on lift door openings onto the floor, subject to both:

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Lobby | Lift landing doors open onto a protected lobby with doors of fire resistance period not less than 1 hour and walls not less than 2 hours |
| Lock on alarm | Landing doors at the refuge floor, other than fireman’s lifts, automatically locked when the fire alarm is on |

**SD takeaway:** A passenger lift may open onto the sky-garden refuge only through a 2-hour lobby with 1-hour doors, and those doors lock when the alarm sounds.
