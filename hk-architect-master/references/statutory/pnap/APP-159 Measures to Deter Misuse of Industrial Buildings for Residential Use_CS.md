# APP-159 Measures to Deter Misuse of Industrial Buildings for Residential Use
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue October 2016 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Design measures applied when processing new industrial-building proposals, including alteration and addition, so the building is not readily convertible to residential use. It is not a change-of-use or licensing note. Unauthorized conversion of industrial premises for residential use contravenes the Buildings Ordinance.

## Regulatory Overview
Industrial buildings are not treated as suitable for residential use because units can be used for industrial activities or storage of dangerous and inflammable goods. New proposals are processed so that servicing, toilets, curtain walls, storey height, and facilities stay consistent with industrial use.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Plans that must read as an industrial building (paragraph 2)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Servicing | Workshop servicing shown on the general building plans, particularly loading and unloading areas and cargo lifts, and their segregation from the communal circulation of occupiers and visitors |
| Toilets | Modification to permit non-provision of natural lighting and ventilation to toilets will not be granted generally, except toilets designed for communal use |
| Toilet ducts | Internal pipe ducts for toilets will not be exempted from gross floor area, except for communal toilets in common parts |
| Curtain wall on small workshops | For an industrial building with small workshop units and curtain walls, the outer face of the curtain wall is the external wall for measuring gross floor area and site coverage. A small workshop unit has usable floor area of less than 80 m2 |
| Storey height | Excessive storey height in small workshop units has to be fully justified. Excessive means storey height exceeding 3.5 m |
| Uncommon facilities | Justification will be demanded for designs and facilities not commonly found in an industrial building or not commensurate with industrial use, failing which the proposal may not be accepted as an industrial development |

**SD takeaway:** Keep small workshop units at 80 m2 or more if a residential reading is a risk, justify any storey over 3.5 m, and do not hide toilet ducts inside exempt gross floor area unless the toilets are communal.
