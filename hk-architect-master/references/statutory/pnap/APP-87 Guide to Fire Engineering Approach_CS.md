# APP-87 Guide to Fire Engineering Approach
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued March 1998; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 204)

> Scope note: An alternative to the prescriptive Codes of Practice for Means of Escape, Means of Access for Firefighting and Rescue, and Fire Resisting Construction, for new buildings and for alteration and addition. It addresses Building (Construction) Regulation 90 and Building (Planning) Regulations 41, 41A, 41B, and 41C. Paragraph 3 of those Codes recognises the approach. It does not set evacuation times or tenability limits; paragraph 15 says those standards still have to be developed.

## Regulatory Overview
The design must give an overall level of safety equivalent to full compliance with the prescriptive codes, and demonstrating that equivalence is required for acceptance. If the departure is not substantial, or conservative assumptions will accommodate it, a like-for-like substitution may be enough and a practical test may be required, while a full fire safety strategy is required where the building is special, large, or complex.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Performance that the strategy must address (paragraphs 4–10 and the Appendix)
| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Fire growth | Given the function of the building, the design does not present an unacceptable risk of a fire developing and spreading |
| Occupants | Time to reach a place of temporary or permanent safety without being dangerously affected by heat or smoke |
| Neighbours and compartment | A fire does not spread to adjacent property, and fire and smoke do not spread beyond the compartment of origin |
| Firefighters | Access and operations without undue risk to their health and safety |
| Structure | Stability, insulation, and integrity for a fire of specified intensity and duration |
| Method | Deterministic prediction of outcomes for the worst likely location and time of ignition, using fire load, occupant numbers and locations, and the protection features. Quantitative risk assessment and large-scale tests are only for special buildings, because local statistics for risk factors are lacking |
| If criteria fail | Change the geometry or the fire protection, and repeat for every scenario |

**SD takeaway:** Agree the fire engineering approach with the Buildings Department and the Fire Services Department before building plans are submitted, and show equivalence for escape, compartmentation, firefighter access, and structural stability.

### 2. What the fire safety strategy report contains (paragraphs 11–13)
| Section | What it locks |
| --- | --- |
| Means of escape | Objectives, and where needed escape times, extended travel distances, behaviour, reaction, and wayfinding |
| Design fires | Fire-load calculation and fire size where smoke extract or the effect of heat on the structure is being assessed |
| Smoke control | Clear-layer height, temperature limits on escape routes, and an indication of gas toxicity |
| Fire spread | Combustibility of linings and furnishings, integrity of structure and compartment walls, compartment size, and external spread |
| Structure | Thermal and structural response at elevated temperature, equivalent fire resistance, and radiation to adjacent buildings near the boundary |
| Systems | Sprinkler hazard class, water supplies, coverage, and standard; alarm type and what it activates; first-aid equipment; firefighter access, stairs, fireman's lift, control centre, smoke clearance, and assembly point; emergency lighting |
| Management | Who maintains and tests every compensatory system |
| Evidence | Calculations, sketches, and diagrams, including smoke-filling, design-fire, and egress calculations where they are used |
| Who leads | The authorized person. There is no accreditation of fire engineers. Other disciplines provide specialist input |

**SD takeaway:** Write the strategy around the deviations from the codes, with a design fire, smoke-layer and egress calculations, and a named management duty for every compensatory system.
