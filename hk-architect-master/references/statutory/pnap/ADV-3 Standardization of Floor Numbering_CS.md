# ADV-3 Standardization of Floor Numbering
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision May 2010 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 128; first issue November 1988)

> Scope note: A code of good practice for floor numbers on plans, occupation permits, and in the building. It does not apply to projects that, before 1 September 2010, had a sales brochure deposited with the Buildings Department by recorded delivery, had sold at least one unit, or had applied for an occupation permit.

## Regulatory Overview
Save for the ground floor and the roof, every floor is numbered in one logical consecutive series, ascending above ground and with a larger basement number meaning a lower basement. Floors with a special use, including shop, car park, clubhouse, podium garden, sky garden, refuge, plant room, and observation deck, stay inside that same series.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. The series and the only permitted omissions (paragraphs 2–6 and Appendix A)
| Rule | What is accepted |
| --- | --- |
| Above ground | Consecutive ascending order, for example G/F, 1/F, 2/F, 3/F |
| Below ground | Consecutive order in which a greater number is a lower basement, for example Basement 1, Basement 2, Basement 3 |
| Special-use floors | Numbered in the same series. A use name may sit with the number, for example 20/F Sky Garden, 7/F Mechanical Plant Floor, or 40/F Refuge Floor |
| Omissions | Only “4”, “13”, and numbers ending in “4”. Any other omission is not allowed |
| Not accepted | A non-numerical name used alone, an alias, “also known as x/F”, or an illogical, non-conventional, or non-consecutive number |
| Appendix A labels | B4/F to B1/F, LG/F, UG/F, G/F, CL/F (cockloft), M/F (mezzanine), then 1/F upward, and R/F for the roof. A car park or podium level uses the same floor number |

**SD takeaway:** Draw the stack as G/F, 1/F, 2/F, 3/F and B1, B2, B3, and drop a number only when it is 4, 13, or ends in 4.

### 2. Plans, display, and records (paragraphs 7–10)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Plans for approval | The number of each floor must be clear and unequivocal. If the Building Authority or another department finds the sequence illogical or confusing, the Buildings Department will ask for an amendment |
| Display | Factual floor numbering in Chinese and English, in a conspicuous position at the main entrance lobby, the lift lobbies, and the walls of protected lobbies and landings of the required staircases on each floor |
| Fireman’s lift | The Code of Practice for Means of Access for Firefighting and Rescue requires notices outside the lift wells stating the floors served. The Fire Services Department checks this before the occupation permit |
| Occupation permit and rates | Floor numbers on the occupation permit follow the approved plans. The Commissioner of Rating and Valuation uses those numbers. Later requests to depart from the approved descriptions are not normally entertained |

**SD takeaway:** Put the final floor numbers on the general building plans, because the occupation permit, the rating records, and the stair and lobby signs all follow those plans.
