# APP-27 Gas Water Heaters
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision September 2019 | Buildings Department (first issue May 1983)

> Scope note: Arrangements generally acceptable for the room-sealed gas water heater aperture required by Regulation 35A of the Building (Planning) Regulations in every bathroom. Other arrangements may be accepted on their merits. Installation requirements are also in the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department Code of Practice GU03 for domestic gas water heaters with rated heat input up to 70 kW. Hot-water pipe length follows regulation 19 of the Waterworks Regulations, Cap. 102A.

## Regulatory Overview
A standard aperture is 320 mm wide by 420 mm high, or 240 mm by 240 mm where the bathroom is large enough only for a shower. A non-standard aperture is allowed only if it suits a specific flue installed before completion and can later be enlarged to the standard size, without cutting reinforcing bars or repositioning services, in a way that is clearly discernible.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Position of the aperture (paragraphs 5–7)
| Item | Dimension |
| --- | --- |
| Wall | Clear face of an external wall. In a building with a permanently open and well ventilated balcony approach, the aperture may vent into that balcony |
| Clear area on the internal face | 50 mm above, 100 mm to each side, 150 mm below |
| Base above floor | Not less than 200 mm |
| From a corner or any other opening into the building | Not less than 300 mm |
| Facing wall, other part of a building, or site boundary | Clear space not less than 1,500 mm. If the boundary abuts a street, an aperture may be in any wall abutting that street |
| Below drying racks | Not directly below fixed clothes-drying racks. Fit a flue terminal guard if the appliance has no overheating protector |
| Light well or other enclosed space | No obstruction to vertical airflow. Ventilated to external air below the lowest aperture. Vent area not less than 0.05 m2 per aperture facing into the space. No vent with any internal dimension less than 200 mm |
| Plan size of that enclosed space | Not less than 3 m if not more than 10 storeys high; not less than 4 m if 11 to 19 storeys; not less than 5 m if 20 or more storeys |
| Re-entrant | Plan width not less than 1.5 m. Any obstruction at the open end, such as a tie beam, must not reduce the vertical open area by more than 20% at any storey. No obstruction to vertical airflow |

**SD takeaway:** Place a 320 mm by 420 mm aperture in a clear external wall, at least 300 mm from other openings and 1,500 mm from a facing wall, or use a re-entrant at least 1.5 m wide.

### 2. Sealing, false ceilings, and exemption (paragraphs 8–12)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Heater in a false-ceiling cavity | Quick-release panel removable without hand tools. Non-adjustable purpose-made louvres that pass air at all times, not where they are easily blocked. Follow the manufacturer's requirements |
| Not fitted at completion | Still provide the standard aperture. It may be sealed with easily removable plain concrete, brickwork, or blockwork, and must be clearly discernible from inside, for example by recessing |
| Exemption | Justify the design constraint. The developer or owner undertakes that gas as defined in the Gas Safety Ordinance, Cap. 51, will not be supplied to the domestic part; that the Deed of Mutual Covenant, or the sale and tenancy documents if there is no deed, restricts gas supply and delivery of liquefied petroleum gas cylinders to domestic units (disposable cartridges for portable cassette cookers bearing the GU mark are excluded); and that electric water heaters and electric or induction cookers are installed in all domestic units before the occupation-permit application |

**SD takeaway:** If gas is to be excluded, install electric water heaters and electric or induction cookers before the occupation permit and write the gas restriction into the deed; otherwise leave a discernible, easily opened aperture in every bathroom.
