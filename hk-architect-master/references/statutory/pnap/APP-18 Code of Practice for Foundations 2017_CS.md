# APP-18 Code of Practice for Foundations 2017
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision February 2026 | Buildings Department (first issue February 1980)

> Scope note: The Code of Practice for Foundations 2017 (2024 Edition), as amended in September 2025 by Appendix A, is the deemed-to-satisfy route for the Building (Construction) Regulation. Other practice notes on foundations are listed in Appendix B. A piling system that is not common local practice, or that does not meet the Code, is agreed with the Building Authority before the foundation plans are submitted. PNAP ADM-19 applies.

## Regulatory Overview
For a shallow foundation on granular soil, the ultimate bearing capacity used in the allowable-pressure equation is limited to 3,000 kPa, or 4,500 kPa where the foundation supports a building with a basement, and the factor of safety is not less than 3. The effective overburden depth used to derive the overburden term is not more than 3 m or the foundation breadth, whichever is smaller, except that for a basement on granular soil it is the minimum overburden around the basement perimeter and is not more than 10 m or the breadth, whichever is smaller.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Bearing, stiffness, and tests (Appendix A, September 2025)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Overburden removed | The overburden term does not include pressure that may be temporarily or permanently removed during the design life. Discount voids allowed for underground utilities |
| Young's modulus, shallow foundation on granular soil, if no better data | 1 times the standard penetration test N-value, in MPa, where the design allowable bearing pressure is not greater than 250 kPa. For a raft on granular soil from in-situ rock weathering, such as saprolite or residual soil, with N greater than 30, 1.5 times the N-value |
| Plate-load test, clause 4.2.2 | A modulus greater than 1 times the N-value, or greater than 1.5 times where that raft rule applies, is not used for the settlement estimate |
| Tension test, clause 8.10 | The maximum test load does not stress the pile or anchor beyond yield. Where design tension capacity is bond or friction taken as not exceeding 50% of the corresponding allowable bond stress in compression, the test load may be 1.5 times the design tension capacity |
| Mass concrete benching | Fill deeper than 1 m under a footing or raft is a structural element and is designed to the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Concrete 2013 |
| Large-diameter bored piles and barrettes | Concrete in good contact with bedrock of the required grade. A pipe of not less than 150 mm diameter may be left in, stopped about 1 m above the interface, to allow proof drilling. A thin sediment layer, segregated concrete, or a weathered seam may be accepted only with justification and further proof drilling, or with remedial works shown on the plans |
| Proof drilling | By a registered specialist contractor in the ground investigation field works category, who declares any relationship with the foundation contractor |

**SD takeaway:** Cap ultimate bearing capacity at 3,000 kPa, or 4,500 kPa under a basement, use a factor of safety of at least 3, and treat mass-concrete fill deeper than 1 m as structure.

### 2. Presumed rock values and two-stage submission (Appendix C)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Presumed values from GEO Technical Guidance Notes 26 and 53, revised 28 April 2025 | If those presumed bearing and bond or friction values are used, for marble under Note 26 or igneous rock under Note 53, approval conditions require a settlement analysis of the as-built lengths, a check that the structure can tolerate it, a report at completion, a monitoring scheme agreed before the pile cap or superstructure starts, and a final performance review before the occupation permit or Form BA14. The permit is not issued, and Form BA14 is not acknowledged, until that review is satisfactory |
| Steel H-piles | Sections on the Buildings Department deemed-to-satisfy list meet section 3 of the Building (Construction) Regulation. Any other section needs the manufacturing, mill, chemical, welding, capacity, and accredited-test information in paragraph 4 of Appendix C |
| Two-stage pile plans | Stage 1 gives physical properties at the maximum vertical compression, tension, and lateral capacity, without a rigorous pile-cap analysis or the wall and pile load schedules. Consent for piling or test piles may follow Stage 1 approval. Stage 2 gives the load path, the schedules, and the pile-cap analysis. Both the Stage 1 amendments and the Stage 2 plans are approved, and consented where building works are involved, before Form BA14. Changing only the number or layout of piles is not a major revision unless it affects the fundamental issues in Appendix D of PNAP ADM-19 |
| Deferred ground investigation | Allowed for foundation plans in non-scheduled areas and in Scheduled Areas No. 3 and No. 5, on the terms in Appendix C. It is not the rule for other scheduled areas |

**SD takeaway:** If presumed rock values from Technical Guidance Notes 26 or 53 are used, do not apply for an occupation permit until the as-built settlement review is accepted, and do not certify the piles until Stage 2 plans are approved.
