# ADV-27 Protection of Natural Streams and Rivers from Adverse Impacts Arising from Construction Works
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue May 2005 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009; previously PNAP 295)

> Scope note: Planning and construction measures, drawn from Environment, Transport and Works Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 5/2005, for works in or near natural streams and rivers, and the stricter measures for Ecologically Important Streams. Flood retention ponds, fishponds, and engineered stormwater systems, including nullahs, ditches, and concrete U-channels, are excluded from the definition.

## Regulatory Overview
A natural stream or river is a natural channel fed from upper terrain, perennial or intermittent, with a natural bed and largely natural vegetated banks, and designers should avoid impacts on it, particularly on an Ecologically Important Stream. Where works are unavoidable, they are planned in the dry season with the least intrusive engineering option, and the mitigation measures for temporary works are written into the contract.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What counts, and the order of impact (paragraphs 2–4 and Appendix A, part A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Included | A natural bed of bedrock, boulders, cobbles, gravel, sand, silt, or clay, and defined banks with riparian vegetation. The list of Ecologically Important Streams is on the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department website cited in the note |
| Excluded | Flood retention ponds, fishponds, and engineered drainage such as Tin Shui Wai Main Nullah, Yuen Long Nullah, Tuen Mun Nullah, engineered sections of Shing Mun River, and concrete U-channels |
| Direct impact | Training, filling, culverting, narrowing, widening, damming, realignment, diversion, and bank stabilisation such as shotcrete on a river bank |
| Indirect impact | Site formation, landfilling, discharge of silt and polluted water, dumping of debris nearby, foundation dewatering, and geotechnical or demolition works in the proximity |
| Options, least impact first | (i) Protect an eroding bank with natural materials such as rock; (ii) selective removal of fallen objects and vegetation; (iii) enlarge by modifying one bank; (iv) enlarge by modifying both banks; (v) enlarge by deepening; (vi) realign by a new channel; (vii) a new channel with an artificial non-vegetative smooth lining such as concrete, and only as the last resort |
| Season | Preferably the dry season, when flow is low |

**SD takeaway:** Keep the stream in its natural bed, and treat a concrete-lined channel as the last option after the six less intrusive methods.

### 2. Ecologically Important Streams (Appendix A, part B)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Banks | Diversified planting of trees, shrubs, herbaceous and wetland plants, and grass, to form an ecological riparian zone |
| Bed | Leave the bottom intact where practicable. Limit the work to bank stabilisation or bank widening |
| Materials | Avoid or minimise concrete. Unless the site is severely constrained, use geotextile-reinforced grass, bio-engineering, natural stone wall, or gabions on the embankment, and random rubble or stones on the bottom |
| Special habitat | If disturbance cannot be avoided, provide translocation, a temporary migratory pathway, recolonisation, or compensation. After the works, place substrates of similar size and composition to the original bed |
| Phasing | Do not work the whole width at once if the stream is wide enough. Keep flow and a free passage so that water does not stagnate |
| Fish | If the upper reaches are breeding grounds and a dam is proposed, provide a fish ladder |
| Rare species | Consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department before works start |
| Pools | Inspect for pools of considerable size and preserve them as far as possible |

**SD takeaway:** On an Ecologically Important Stream, leave the bed, use stone or a soft bank, and phase the work so part of the width and the flow stay open.

### 3. Site controls during construction (paragraphs 7–8 and Appendix B)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Isolate the works | Sandbags or silt curtains with a lead edge at the bottom and supported props, so that silt and pollution do not reach the stream or a water gathering ground |
| Access | Keep any temporary track on the bed to the minimum width and length. Support a temporary crossing on stilts above the bed |
| Storage | Cover stockpiles and keep them, and fuel, chemicals, and spoil, away from the stream. Cover or remove debris promptly so rain does not wash it in |
| Wastewater, in this order | Minimise generation, then reuse and recycle, then treat. Place the discharge outlet well away from the stream |
| Vegetation | Avoid or minimise removal of bank vegetation. Hydroseed or replant disturbed areas on completion |
| Support | Provide lateral support so that soil does not slip into the stream, without unduly blocking flow in heavy rain |
| Indirect impacts only, and not an Ecologically Important Stream | Good site practice and pollution control are still strongly advised |
| Supervision | Station supervisory staff on site |

**SD takeaway:** Cross the stream on stilts, keep the haul track off the bed, and discharge treated site water well away from the channel.
