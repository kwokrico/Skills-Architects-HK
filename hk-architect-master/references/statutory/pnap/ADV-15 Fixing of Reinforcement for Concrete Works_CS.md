# ADV-15 Fixing of Reinforcement for Concrete Works
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision July 2003 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009; first issue March 1998)

> Scope note: Good practice for holding bar reinforcement in the position and cover shown on the approved plans. Test requirements for the steel are in the note then numbered PNAP 122.

## Regulatory Overview
Displaced reinforcement is identified as a major cause of corrosion, spalling, and in some cases collapse. Bars and fabric from a batch are not fixed until testing of that batch is complete, and enough chairs and ties must hold the specified cover, especially in cantilevers.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Cover, chairs, and laps (paragraphs 2–9)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Before fixing | Do not fix a batch until its test is complete (the note cites Practice Note 122) |
| Rust | Remove loose rust, excessive flaky rust, or mill-scale by wire-brushing. Do not use badly corroded, damaged, or scaling steel |
| Supports | Enough spacers, chairs, and other supports to keep reinforcement where the approved plans show it and to keep the specified minimum cover at all positions |
| Spacing | Spacers and chairs at a maximum spacing of 1.5 m. For 20 mm diameter bars or smaller, reduce the spacing so the bars do not sag |
| Spacer material | Concrete, or a proprietary plastic or concrete type, as small as practicable, able to carry the reinforcement and construction loads without breaking, deforming, or overturning. Strength and durability not less than the surrounding concrete |
| Ties | Tie enough intersecting and lapping bars. Ends of tying wire, devices, and clips must not encroach into the cover |
| Access | Do not walk or run barrows on the steel. Use independently supported planks and ladders, clear of the completed reinforcement. On cantilevers, extra chairs and ties so top steel is not displaced by access or other construction loads |
| Laps and connectors | Only at the specified positions. Connectors fixed to the manufacturer's recommendations with the manufacturer's equipment |
| Free-standing and cages | Secure and brace against wind and other loads. Brace prefabricated cages before lifting |

**SD takeaway:** Specify chairs at not more than 1.5 m, closer for 20 mm bars and smaller, and extra chairs on cantilever top steel so site access cannot drop the cover.

### 2. Inspection before the pour (paragraphs 10–11)
The registered contractor gives continuous supervision so steel stays as fixed during placing and compaction. The authorized person or registered structural engineer gives adequate quality supervision. The contractor calls that inspection after fixing and well before concreting, so rectification is possible. For walls and columns, inspect before formwork is fully erected. The contractor keeps the steel in the approved condition until concreting is finished.

**SD takeaway:** Programme a reinforcement inspection of walls and columns before the formwork is closed, not after.
