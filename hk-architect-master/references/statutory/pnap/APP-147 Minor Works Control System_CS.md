# APP-147 Minor Works Control System
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Buildings Department

> Scope note: Which small-scale works may proceed without prior approval and consent, and who must be appointed. The size, location, and criteria of each item are in Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation, not in this note. Signboard distinction and other topic rules continue after paragraph 11. It is not a design code for the works themselves.

## Regulatory Overview
187 items are designated minor works in three classes by scale and risk: Class I needs a prescribed building professional and a prescribed registered contractor, with a notice of commencement and a certificate of completion. Class III is notified by a combined notice and certificate from the contractor.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Classes (paragraph 2)
| Class | Count | Character | Example in the note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 58 | Relatively more complicated | Installation of an internal staircase connecting two floors |
| II | 68 | Less complex | Repair of external walls |
| III | 61 | Small-scale, mostly household | Erection of supporting frames for air-conditioners |

**SD takeaway:** Count 58 Class I, 68 Class II, and 61 Class III items, and take the size limits from Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation.

### 2. Who is appointed and what is submitted (paragraphs 4–11)
| Class | Appointment | Submission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Prescribed building professional to design and supervise, and a prescribed registered contractor to carry out the works | Notice of commencement and certificate of completion. The professional gives the contractor a copy of the prescribed plans, details, and supervision plan for keeping on site, and the contractor keeps the acknowledgement of the commencement notice |
| II | Prescribed registered contractor prepares and submits the plans and documents, supervises, and carries out the works | Notice of commencement and certificate of completion |
| III | Prescribed registered contractor | Notice and certificate of completion together |

A prescribed building professional is an authorized person and, if required, a registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer, or registered inspector. For prescribed repair or associated demolition that is Class I, the registered inspector assumes the authorized person’s role under section 27. A prescribed registered contractor is a registered general building contractor, registered specialist contractor, or registered minor works contractor.

| Other rule | Timing or duty |
| --- | --- |
| Common parts | The person who arranged the works is reminded of the deed of mutual covenant. The professional notifies the management office, the Incorporated Owners, or the related owners |
| Supervision plan, where required | Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans and the Code of Practice for Site Supervision |
| Professional ceases on Class I | Standard form to the Building Authority within 7 days |
| Contractor ceases on Class I | Specified form to the authorized person or registered inspector within 7 days; that person submits it to the Building Authority within 7 days of receipt |

**SD takeaway:** A Class I item, such as a stair between two floors, still needs an authorized person and a commencement notice; a household Class III item is a contractor’s combined notice and certificate.
