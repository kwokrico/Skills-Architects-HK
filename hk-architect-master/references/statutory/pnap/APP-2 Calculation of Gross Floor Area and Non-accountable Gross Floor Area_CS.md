# APP-2 Calculation of Gross Floor Area and Non-accountable Gross Floor Area
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision November 2025 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 13; first issue December 1974)

> Scope note: What is counted as gross floor area under regulation 23(3)(a) of the Building (Planning) Regulations, and what may be disregarded under regulation 23(3)(b). Concessions for items that PNAP APP-151 lists are also subject to the pre-requisites, and where stated the overall cap, in that note. Car-park design standards are in PNAP APP-111. This note does not apply the curtain-wall measurement rule to industrial buildings with small workshop units.

## Regulatory Overview
Gross floor area is the area within the outer surface of the external walls, measured at each floor, and includes portions that are not floored over. A split-level difference of less than 1 m may be treated as one level, and a greater difference is clarified with the Buildings Department before the project proceeds.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Voids, curtain wall, and cladding (paragraphs 2–10 and Appendices A and B)
| Item | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Shopping-arcade voids | Total void area not exceeding 10% of the gross floor area of the arcade |
| Duplex flat void | Usable floor space of the premises at least 150 m2. Void not more than 10% of that usable floor space, headroom not more than 6.5 m, only in the living or dining room or the entrance foyer. Only two levels of voids in each residential tower, and not more than 0.5% of the total domestic gross floor area of the development. The 0.5% cap may be applied with flexibility for a low-rise, low-density flat development similar to houses |
| House void | Usable floor space at least 250 m2. Void not more than 5% of that area, headroom not more than 7.0 m, or to the mean level of a pitched ceiling over the void. Same rooms as a flat. No percentage of total domestic gross floor area |
| Other voids that may be modified | In front of cinema and theatre balconies; banking halls; cocklofts for storage in the ground storey of a single-staircase building; auditoria, sporting halls including squash courts, school halls, and religious institutions; main common entrance lobbies of non-domestic buildings. Items (f) and (g) in paragraph 2 also sit under the APP-151 cap |
| Abuse | Upstand beams, large external glazing, and no adjoining structural wall or beam are examples of measures. The void is designated in the deed of mutual covenant, or in the sale or tenancy documents if there is no deed. The exclusion must not breach the town plan or the lease |
| Curtain wall as the face | Measure gross floor area and site coverage to the outer face of the structure if the wall is not part of the structure, a reinforced-concrete dwarf perimeter wall at least 300 mm high is provided, the projection is not more than 200 mm for a domestic building or 250 mm for a non-domestic building, and the external reflectance of the glass does not exceed 20%. A purpose-built hotel, the hotel part, or a hotel converted from a non-domestic building is non-domestic for the 250 mm limit |
| Curtain wall on an existing facade, wholesale conversion, projecting over a street | Exemption from section 31(1) if the glazing is low-energy absorbent or the materials are energy efficient and the design achieves 40% in Energy Use and Indoor Environmental Quality under BEAM Plus. Provisional assessment before consent. Final assessment within 6 months of acknowledgement of Form BA14. Not accepted on existing balconies over a street, or only on a localised part of the facade |
| Cladding | Nominal finishes, or cladding not more than 90 mm thick, or 75 mm on a non-structural prefabricated external wall, may be disregarded. No finish or cladding projects beyond the site boundary. Projection over a street on a refurbishment needs an exemption from section 31(1) |

**SD takeaway:** Measure to the structural face only if the curtain wall projects not more than 200 mm on a domestic building or 250 mm on a non-domestic building, glass reflectance stays at or below 20%, and a 300 mm dwarf wall is provided.

### 2. Plant, stairs, and car parks (paragraphs 11–21 and Appendix C)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Services that may be disregarded, if justified | Lift, air-conditioning, and heating machinery, and similar services including a fire control centre, water tanks, electrical switch rooms, meter rooms, transformer and generator rooms, pump rooms, telephone equipment rooms, cable riser duct rooms, CO rooms, hose-reel closets, sewage treatment plant rooms, and smoke extraction. Enclosing walls and a protected lobby that serve only that room, normally not thicker than 100 mm, may also be disregarded. Air-conditioning and heating plant also follow PNAP APP-151 |
| Single-family house | No duplicated services, and only the minimum area. Consolidate into multi-purpose or central rooms |
| Also modifiable under regulation 23(3)(a) | Chimney shafts; filtration plant for a hotel pool or a communal water feature; a satellite-master-antenna room not exceeding 1.2 m by 1.2 m to paragraph 33 of PNAP APP-42; genuine pipe ducts and air ducts; plant for rainwater or grey-water recycling and a battery room for solar panels; a refuge floor that complies with the fire safety code. Ducts for non-mandatory features follow the APP-151 cap. Ducts for mandatory features do not |
| Stairs, lift shafts, and vertical ducts | Counted with the floor they pass through, except a refuge floor. They may be discounted where they serve only floors that are themselves not accountable |
| Car parks, paragraph 18, subject to the dates in paragraph 19 | Underground: 100%. Aboveground, not more than two floors: 100%; other floors above: 50%. Aboveground 100% if underground parking is technically infeasible or there is no adverse environmental or visual impact, if required under the lease as Government Accommodation, or if required under the lease for subsidised sale or rental flats accepted by the Government and provided by the Hong Kong Housing Society or the Urban Renewal Authority. Loading and unloading: 100% underground and at ground, 50% above ground, and 100% in the special cases. The 100% and 50% car-park figures require electric-vehicle charging-enabling facilities except where an automated parking system is installed |
| Dates | Underground public car parks: on or after 1 March 2017. Paragraph 18(b): on or after 1 November 2025. Paragraphs 18(d) and 18(e): on or after 1 August 2023. Electric-vehicle requirements in paragraph 4 of Appendix C: new building plans for new developments on or after 1 November 2025. Earlier approvals follow the December 2023 version, subject to section 16(3)(d). Not applicable to existing car parks |
| What counts as underground | Ceiling soffit at or below the mean street level, and that mean is not more than 5 m above the lowest street level, with the multi-street average in Appendix C. For light and air, not more than half the top parking storey, and not more than 2.5 m, may protrude. A semi-sunken floor that follows a sloping site may also be accepted |
| Chargers | A medium charger is not less than 7 kW. A quick or fast charger is 50 kW, 100 kW, or higher. The general building plans show a schedule, and a registered professional engineer in electrical or building services certifies the report to the February 2023 technical guidelines, the November 2025 supplement, and the April 2015 charging guidelines. The report may be deferred to before superstructure consent. The Environmental Protection Department replies within 22 calendar days on the submission and within 10 calendar days on the completion certificate. No objection is lodged with the occupation-permit application. Meter rooms for the chargers are disregarded whether the car park is above or below ground |
| Technical reasons for no basement car park | Major underground utilities, Scheduled Areas No. 1, 2, 3, or 4, or other complex geotechnical constraints, with evidence |
| Public transport terminus | Accountable unless the town plan or a planning approval says otherwise |
| Bicycles | Covered spaces required by the lease and designated as common areas may be exempted from regulation 23(3)(a) |

**SD takeaway:** Count a public transport terminus, discount an underground car park in full only if it is charging-enabled, and treat an aboveground car park above the second floor as half accountable unless a listed exception applies.
