# APP-154 Design Requirements for Columbarium Facilities
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue August 2012 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Buildings Ordinance design and construction requirements for a new columbarium and for conversion of existing premises. Town planning, environmental hygiene, traffic, fire safety, licensing, and the land lease are outside this note and must still be cleared with the relevant departments. Dead and imposed loads are in the Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads 2011.

## Regulatory Overview
A columbarium is designed as other assembly premises for fire resistance, with an occupancy factor of 2 m2 of usable floor area per person, and with sanitary fitments sized to that population. Conversion of existing premises is likely a material change in use under section 25(1) of the Buildings Ordinance and is limited to non-domestic buildings.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Fire and access (paragraphs 2–4)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Means of escape | Part B of the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings 2011. Occupancy factor: Item 5d of Table B1, 2 m2 of usable floor area per person |
| Usable floor area | Except the area for niches and staircases, include the prescribed width of exit routes, including corridors of balcony-approach design, and circulation areas such as lift lobbies |
| Fire resisting construction | Part C of the code. Fire resistance rating and compartment limits as Other Assembly Premises in Table C1. Burner facilities for cremation of offerings are an area of special hazard, separated by fire barriers under Subsection C13 |
| Means of access | Regulations 41A, 41B, 41C, and 41D of the Building (Planning) Regulations and Part D of the code. If emergency vehicular access is omitted or cannot meet the code, apply for exemption or modification on Form BA16. Details are in PNAP APP-136 |

**SD takeaway:** Count population at 2 m2 of usable floor area per person, including exit routes and lift lobbies but not the niche area or staircases, and separate offering burners as a special hazard.

### 2. Sanitary fitments and access for persons with a disability (paragraphs 5–6 and Table 1)
Fitments in separate rooms exclusively for male and female persons.

| Item | Provision |
| --- | --- |
| Population | 2 m2 of usable floor area per person |
| Male to female | 1 : 1.5 |
| Male | 1 watercloset fitment and 1 urinal for every 150 persons or part thereof; 1 lavatory basin for every 300 persons or part thereof |
| Female | 1 watercloset fitment for every 75 persons or part thereof; 1 lavatory basin for every 300 persons or part thereof |
| Barrier-free | Regulation 72 of the Building (Planning) Regulations and the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008. In an alley between rows of niches, allow space in front of each row for worship and offerings, in addition to the minimum clearance for the passageway for persons with a disability and for means of escape |

**SD takeaway:** Provide one female watercloset per 75 persons and one male watercloset plus one urinal per 150 persons, and widen niche aisles beyond the accessible-passage minimum.

### 3. Conversion of existing premises (paragraphs 7–8)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Loads | Design dead load and imposed load in the Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads 2011 |
| Change in use | Likely a material change in use under section 25(1). Comply with paragraphs 2 to 7 |
| Building type | Columbarium facilities only in non-domestic buildings |
| Industrial buildings | Only conversion of the entire building is acceptable to the Fire Services Department. Partial conversion is considered undesirable because it may attract a large number of persons exposed to risks they would neither be aware of nor prepared to face |

**SD takeaway:** Do not put a columbarium in part of an industrial building; convert the whole building, and only in a non-domestic building.
