# APP-145 Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance, Cap. 572
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision October 2010 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 302; first issue October 2007)

> Scope note: Which existing composite and domestic buildings are target buildings under the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance, and which fire-safety construction and installation measures may be directed. It does not apply to buildings constructed in accordance with the Buildings Ordinance (Application to the New Territories) Ordinance (Cap. 121). Detailed construction requirements are in Schedules 1 and 2 of the Ordinance and the 1995–1996 codes named in paragraph 8. It is not a new-building fire code.

## Regulatory Overview
The Ordinance, in operation from 1 July 2007, requires certain composite and domestic buildings to be upgraded to current fire safety standards, covering both fire service installations and fire safety construction. The Director of Buildings enforces the planning, design, and construction measures; the Director of Fire Services enforces the installations and equipment.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which buildings are caught (paragraphs 2–6)
A composite or domestic building if (a) and either (b) or (c):

| Test | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Use | Constructed or intended to be used partly for domestic and partly for non-domestic purposes, or for domestic purposes with more than 3 storeys |
| Plans | Plans of the building works first submitted for approval on or before 1 March 1987, or constructed on or before 1 March 1987 where no plans were submitted on or before that date |
| Domestic building | Includes an ancillary club house, carpark, and recreation facilities for the exclusive use of residents and their invited persons |
| Domestic purposes | Human habitation. Does not include a hotel, guesthouse, home for the elderly, child care centre, or nursery |
| Composite building | Partly domestic and partly non-domestic, excluding a building whose non-domestic part consists wholly of a factory or industrial undertaking, godown, warehouse, or place of bulk storage |
| Excluded | Buildings constructed under the Buildings Ordinance (Application to the New Territories) Ordinance (Cap. 121) |

**SD takeaway:** A domestic building of more than 3 storeys, or a composite building, whose plans went in on or before 1 March 1987, is a target building unless it is a New Territories Ordinance building or a hotel, guesthouse, elderly home, child care centre, or nursery.

### 2. Measures and the codes that set them (paragraphs 7–8)
| Construction (Schedules 1 and 2) | Installations and equipment |
| --- | --- |
| Adequate means of escape | Automatic sprinkler system |
| Adequate means of access for firefighting and rescue | Automatic cut-off devices for a mechanical ventilating system |
| Measures to inhibit spread of fire and to ensure structural integrity | Emergency lighting; manual fire alarms; fire hydrant and hose reel; requirements in the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment 1994 |

Schedule 1 covers the non-domestic parts of a target composite building. Schedule 2 covers a target domestic building or the domestic parts of a target composite building. The detailed construction requirements are in the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire 1996, the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction 1996, and the Code of Practice for Means of Access for Firefighting and Rescue 1995.

**SD takeaway:** Upgrade both escape, access, and fire-resisting construction to the 1995–1996 codes, and the installations list in paragraph 7(b).

### 3. Directions, alternatives, and plan fees (paragraphs 11–16)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Direction | Under section 5(1) or 5(2), the Director of Buildings may serve a fire safety direction to comply with Schedule 1 or 2 |
| Order | After conviction for failure to comply, the Director may apply under section 6(1) for a fire safety compliance order |
| Alternative | If compliance with a requirement would not be reasonable having regard to structural integrity and the technology available, other requirements may be directed. Genuine difficulties may be met by alternative measures based on fire safety engineering principles that equally achieve the primary objectives |
| Advisory committee | Established under section 5(10) to assist on those alternatives. Membership is in Appendix A |
| Plans | Non-exempted works, whether voluntary or to satisfy a direction, are applied for in the usual way. Three sets of fire-service installation plans, instead of two, are required by the Fire Services Department |
| Fee | No fee under Building (Administration) Regulation 42 if the proposals are entirely for improving fire safety measures pursuant to a direction |

**SD takeaway:** Works done only to meet a fire safety direction attract no plan fee under regulation 42, and need three sets of installation plans.
