# APP-141 Division of Responsibilities between Authorized Person, Structural Engineer and Geotechnical Engineer
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision February 2009 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 294; first issue December 2004)

> Scope note: Which geotechnical tasks a registered geotechnical engineer signs as an executive duty, and which are advisory, from 31 December 2005. The task list is Appendix A; the detailed split is Appendix B. It is not a geotechnical design code. Read with the practice notes named in paragraph 4, particularly PNAP 50, 75, 77, 83, 85, 132, 148, 161, 200, 283, and 284.

## Regulatory Overview
From 31 December 2005 a registered geotechnical engineer must be appointed for the geotechnical elements under section 4(1)(c) of the Buildings Ordinance and is statutorily responsible for those elements, while the authorized person remains the coordinator. Who prepares and signs each prescribed plan is set by Building (Administration) Regulation 12.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Executive tasks the geotechnical engineer signs (paragraph 5 and Appendix A)
The registered geotechnical engineer prepares and signs the prescribed geotechnical plans and reports, and certifies that the works were carried out in accordance with the approved plans and are geotechnically safe.

| Task | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| 2A and 2B | Ground investigation in scheduled areas, and in non-scheduled areas |
| 4 | Site formation |
| 7A and 7B | Groundwater drainage works in Scheduled Area 1; water supply and wells |
| 10 | Remedial works to dangerous hillsides |
| 11 | Long-term monitoring after the occupation permit, including reports that justify the functioning of permanent anchors and horizontal drains |

**SD takeaway:** Site formation, scheduled-area ground investigation, and post-occupation anchor monitoring are signed by the registered geotechnical engineer, not only advised on.

### 2. Advisory tasks (paragraph 6 and Appendix A)
The geotechnical report or supporting document is prepared and signed by the registered geotechnical engineer, and that signature is acceptance of responsibility for it. The authorized person or registered structural engineer prepares and signs the plans and the non-geotechnical reports.

| Task | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Demolition affecting slopes and retaining walls |
| 3 | Geotechnical assessment for a general building plan, where required |
| 5A and 5B | Foundations in Scheduled Areas Nos. 1, 2, and 4 and the Designated Area of Northshore Lantau; foundations affecting a slope or retaining wall |
| 6 | Excavation and lateral support |
| 8 | Superstructure report on geotechnical design parameters |
| 12 | Hoardings, covered walkways, and gantries affecting the stability of a nearby slope or retaining wall |

Sanctions for an offence or disciplinary matter are under sections 40 and 7 of the Buildings Ordinance, as for an authorized person or registered structural engineer.

**SD takeaway:** Foundations next to a slope, excavation and lateral support, and a hoarding that can affect a slope still need a signed geotechnical report, while the structural or building plans are signed by the structural engineer or authorized person.
