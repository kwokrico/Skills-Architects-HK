# APP-109 Geotechnical Manual for Slopes — Guidance on Interpretation
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue June 1999 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 234)

> Scope note: How to read the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes, 2nd edition (May 1984), including replacement factors of safety and which chapters are superseded. It is not a new slope-design manual. Later guidance listed in section 10 of Appendix A replaces the named chapters.

## Regulatory Overview
The Manual remains the consensus guidance for investigation, design, construction, and maintenance of slopes and site formation, read with this note. “Risk” in the Manual means consequence, and the old high, low, and negligible labels are now consequence-to-life Categories 1, 2, and 3 and economic Categories A, B, and C.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Factors of safety for new slopes (Appendix A, Table 1)
Minimum factors for a ten-year return period rainfall. A Category 1 slope also needs a factor of at least 1.1 for the predicted worst groundwater conditions. The figures are recommended minima.

| Economic consequence | Consequence-to-life 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1.4 | 1.4 | 1.4 |
| B | 1.4 | 1.2 | 1.2 |
| C | 1.4 | 1.2 | Greater than 1.0 |

Temporary works use the same factors as permanent new works (Table 1), judged on the conditions and consequence category during construction. A less reliable design solution needs a higher minimum factor than a more reliable one, even if the calculated factor would otherwise match.

**SD takeaway:** A new slope that can affect occupied buildings (Category 1) is designed to 1.4 for the ten-year rain and at least 1.1 for the worst groundwater.

### 2. Existing slopes and fill (Appendix A, Tables 2 and 3, and sections 8–9)
Table 2, for stability assessment and remedial or preventive works on an existing slope tied to new works, after rigorous investigation and where loading, groundwater, and form stay substantially the same: Category 1 is 1.2, Category 2 is 1.1, and Category 3 is greater than 1.0, for a ten-year return period rainfall. Back-analysis may assume the existing slope had a minimum factor of 1.0 for the worst known loading and groundwater. A failed or distressed slope must have the cause identified.

| Fill rule | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| New fill, exceptional large platform that will not support structures | Interior may be 90% of maximum dry density if formation-level fill is 95% for at least 1.5 m vertically and peripheral slopes are 95% for at least 3 m. Provide subsurface drainage behind the less permeable edges |
| Reclamation and fill behind retaining structures | Compaction is not generally set by slope stability |
| Existing loose fill, standard treatment | Recompaction of the surface fill to a vertical depth of 3 m, unless the slope is consequence-to-life Category 3, is judged too small to pose a significant hazard, or has a mature vegetation cover that helps stability and there is a reasonable alternative |

Consequence-to-life Category 1 examples include failures affecting occupied buildings (residential, educational, commercial, or industrial), bus shelters, and railway platforms; buildings storing dangerous goods are also Category 1. A bus shelter with a cover is an occupied building; a bus stop without a cover is not that example. Buildings beyond the expected travel distance of debris may be downgraded to Category 2, and beyond the possible extreme limit to Category 3.

**SD takeaway:** An existing Category 1 slope may be assessed at 1.2 only after a rigorous study; a new Category 1 slope stays at 1.4.

### 3. What the Manual no longer governs (Appendix A, section 10)
| Manual text | Superseded by |
| --- | --- |
| Chapter 1 and section 2.3.3 | Geological Survey Maps and Memoirs, and Geoguide 3 |
| Chapter 2 except section 2.3.3, sections 3.5 and 10.2 | Geoguide 2 |
| Section 4.6 | GEO Publication No. 1/93 |
| Chapter 7, except remedial or preventive design of existing gravity walls in section 7.3.3 | Geoguide 1, GCO Publication No. 1/90, and GEO Circular No. 6/96 |
| Chapter 11 | Geoguide 5 |

**SD takeaway:** Use Geoguide 1 for retaining walls and Geoguide 5 for slope maintenance, and keep this note’s tables for the factors of safety.
