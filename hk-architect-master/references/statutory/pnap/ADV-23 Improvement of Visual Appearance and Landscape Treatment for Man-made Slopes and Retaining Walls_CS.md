# ADV-23 Improvement of Visual Appearance and Landscape Treatment for Man-made Slopes and Retaining Walls
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision May 2004 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009; previously PNAP 270; first issue December 2002)

> Scope note: Landscape treatment and bio-engineering for new, upgraded, and maintained man-made slopes and retaining walls. It recommends the guidelines; it does not replace a slope-stability design. Consequence-to-life categories are in PNAP 234. Tree felling also follows Practice Note 267 and Lands Administration Office Practice Note No. 8/2002, which this note cites.

## Regulatory Overview
New and newly upgraded slopes should be landscaped wherever possible, and shotcrete or chunam is only a last resort after other techniques are found impracticable. Large slopes should be avoided or reduced by replanning the layout or by a retaining structure that is not itself massive or visually obtrusive.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Shotcrete and chunam (Appendix A, paragraphs 1–4)
| Rule | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Last resort | Use only after other techniques are not practicable |
| Bottom of the slope | Avoid shotcrete or chunam over the bottom 3 m, the part most visible to passers-by |
| If it is unavoidable | Toe wall planters, planter boxes, tree pits, creeper holes, colour or pigments in the shotcrete, and tree rings large enough for air and water to reach existing tree roots |
| Category 3 soil cut | In general, do not use shotcrete or chunam on a soil cut of Consequence-to-life Category 3 as defined in PNAP 234. On a future upgrade, replace existing shotcrete or chunam on such a slope with vegetation. On other categories, replace it where slope safety allows; failing that, add landscape treatment. Stone pitching is an alternative |
| Rock, Grades I to III | Generally do not cover fresh to moderately decomposed rock where the joints are tight and the average spacing is greater than 60 mm. More closely jointed rock (spacing under 60 mm), open joints, or rock that weathers quickly may warrant a cover, confirmed by a suitably experienced geotechnical engineer or engineering geologist |
| Weepholes | Locate them from a site assessment of discontinuities and seepage, following Figure 5.4 of the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes (2nd Edition) |
| Fractured rock | Prefer wire mesh to shotcrete where restraint is needed. Give large concrete buttresses and dentition a surface finish, and consider masonry facing to match the rock. Break up a large uniform surface |

**SD takeaway:** Keep the bottom 3 m of a new slope vegetated, and do not specify shotcrete on a Category 3 soil cut or on tight Grade I to III rock with joints wider than 60 mm apart.

### 2. Trees, planting, and maintenance (paragraphs 6–9 and Appendix A, paragraphs 5–10)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Existing healthy trees | Preserve them. Do not damage the crown, trunk, or roots. If the slope is covered, provide tree rings of sufficient size. Add structural support where a tree could fall under its own weight or with wind, and keep that support visually unobtrusive |
| Inspection | Arrange a suitably experienced person to inspect the vegetation, especially unhealthy or decaying trees, and carry out remedial work. Submit the inspection report to the Building Authority for record before the certificate of completion of site formation |
| Felling or transplanting | Last resort, and only for a genuine safety concern with no practical way to keep the tree healthy. Prior government approval may be required under the land grant and under legislation |
| Species | Soft landscape should be as maintenance-free as possible. Avoid species that need a lot of trimming or dry-season watering. Consider native species, particularly those with seasonal flowering |
| Hydroseeding | Generally applicable to soil cut slopes and fill slopes. After hydroseeding, provide a biodegradable protective fabric on the slope face irrespective of gradient, to reduce raindrop impact while the grass establishes |
| Manual | Specify the maintenance of the landscape measures in the slope maintenance manual |

**SD takeaway:** Retain healthy trees with rings and support, and submit the vegetation inspection report before the site-formation completion certificate.

### 3. Guidance documents named in the note (paragraphs 10–11 and Appendix A)
| Document | Use stated in the note |
| --- | --- |
| Geotechnical Manual for Slopes (2nd Edition) | General use of grass, shrubs, and trees, including hydroseeding |
| GEO Publication No. 1/2000, Technical Guidelines on Landscape Treatment and Bio-engineering for Man-made Slopes and Retaining Walls | Design process for new slopes, upgrading, maintenance, and improvement |
| GEO Report No. 116, Review of Effective Methods of Integrating Man-made Slopes and Retaining Walls (Particularly for Roadside Slopes) into Their Surroundings | Aesthetic design, particularly of roadside slopes |
| Layman’s Guide to Landscape Treatment of Slopes and Retaining Walls | For the general public, from District Offices |

**SD takeaway:** Design the slope finish to GEO Publication No. 1/2000, and obtain specialist landscape advice where the feature is large or roadside.
