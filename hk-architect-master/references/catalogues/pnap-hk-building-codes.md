| ADV-1 | Asbestos | On a demolition, repair, or clearance of unauthorised works, finish the asbestos investigation and abatement, with 28 days' notice, before other building activities start. Do not assume an exemption; use it only for material on the 6 June 1997 gazette list of low-risk non-friable work. | ADV-3 | Standardization of Floor Numbering | Draw the stack as G/F, 1/F, 2/F, 3/F and B1, B2, B3, and drop a number only when it is 4, 13, or ends in 4. Put the final floor numbers on the general building plans, because the occupation permit, the rating records, and the stair and lobby signs all follow those plans. Printed figures: Above ground: Consecutive ascending order, for example G/F, 1/F, 2/F, 3/F; Below ground: Consecutive order in which a greater number is a lower basement, for example Basement 1, Basement 2, Basement 3; Special-use floors: Numbered in the same series. A use name may sit with the number, for example 20/F Sky Garden, 7/F Mechanical Plant Floor, or 40/F Refuge Floor; Omissions: Only “4”, “13”, and numbers ending in “4”. Any other omission is not allowed; Appendix A labels: B4/F to B1/F, LG/F, UG/F, G/F, CL/F (cockloft), M/F (mezzanine), then 1/F upward, and R/F for the roof. A car park or podium level uses the same floor number |
| ADV-4 | Control of Environmental Nuisance from Construction Sites | If the foundation method is percussive, or if noisy work will run after 7:00 p.m., on a Sunday, or on a general holiday, allow programme time for a construction noise permit. Show hoarding, a paved haul road, wheel washing, and covered stockpiles on the site plan, and notify the Environmental Protection Department before site formation, demolition, foundation, or superstructure works. Design the temporary drainage with a silt trap before any connection, and seal the existing sewers before demolition or foundation work. Fix the site entrance, wheel-wash, and hoarding width on the hoarding plan so the footpath and traffic signs stay usable. Printed figures: Percussive piling: Between 7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m. on a day that is not a public holiday, a construction noise permit is required. It is generally prohibited at other times; Restricted hours: Between 7:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m., or at any time on a general holiday including Sunday, powered mechanical equipment for general construction works, and prescribed construction works in designated (predominantly built-up) areas, also need a construction noise permit; Offence: Works without a valid permit, or in breach of permit conditions, are an offence under the Noise Control Ordinance. The maximum fine is level 6 on the first conviction and $200,000 on a subsequent conviction; Notify the Environmental Protection Department beforehand: Site formation; reclamation; demolition of a building; tunnel works excluding any part beyond 100 m from the exit to the open air; construction of the foundation; construction of the superstructure; and road construction works. Maximum fine $25,000 on the first conviction and $50,000 on a subsequent conviction; Dust reduction: Cover dusty materials and stockpiles, spray water, hard-pave and spray the main haul road, cover loads, control vehicle speed, wash wheels before leaving the site, water the site, and erect hoarding. Failure to adopt appropriate measures: maximum fine $50,000 first conviction, and $100,000 and 3 months’ imprisonment on a subsequent conviction; Specified process: A specified process under Schedule 1 of the Air Pollution Control Ordinance, such as concrete batching where a licence is required, needs that licence. Maximum fine $200,000 and six months’ imprisonment, with a daily fine of $20,000; Open burning: Prohibited under the Air Pollution Control (Open Burning) Regulation. Maximum fine $50,000 plus $500 for every 15 minutes the offence continues, on the first conviction; Demolition: Fans, catch platforms, and dust screens are required under the Building (Demolition Works) Regulations. Maximum fine $25,000 (level 4); Discharge: Construction site discharges need a valid licence under the Water Pollution Control Ordinance. Discharge of waste or polluting matter into a water control zone: maximum penalty $200,000 and 6 months’ imprisonment for the first offence; Demolition and foundations: Seal sewer and drainage connections before those works start. Failure to seal them in demolition is an offence under the Building (Demolition Works) Regulations, maximum fine $25,000 (level 4); Chemical waste: Register with the Environmental Protection Department as a chemical waste producer, package, label, and store the waste, and use a licensed collector. Maximum fine $200,000 and 6 months’ imprisonment; Hoardings: Keep them to the approved hoarding plan, including the minimum width, sound, and clear for pedestrians. Survey existing traffic signs, taxi ranks, bus stops, queuing areas, and crossings, and agree the hoarding plan with the Transport Department. The note also points to PNAP APP-23 |

Printed figures: (d): Public utilities or public services. Exposed supports for electric lines are also subject to regulation 11 of the Electricity Supply Regulations (Cap. 406A) |
| ADV-9 | Submission of Development Progress | Keep a March and September schedule of occupation-permit dates and unit counts, and do not use this saleable-area definition as the gross-floor-area calculation. Printed figures: Progress: Comments such as building plans approved, foundation completed, constructed to 4th floor, external finishing completed; Non-domestic units: If a floor is not yet partitioned, count the floor as one unit. The note's example: 5 units per floor on 1/F to 3/F and unpartitioned floors from 4/F to 10/F gives 22 units |
| ADV-11 | Suspended Working Platforms | Detail the roof suspension so the platform stays off the facade, with wire ropes, a safety rope at each point, and outrigger counterweights of at least three times the balance weight at full load. Mark the safe working load and the maximum number of persons on the platform, and do not put it into use without the competent examiner's load test. Printed figures: Width: Sufficient length and at least 440 mm wide; Guard-rails: Suitable guard-rails to a height between 900 mm and 1,150 mm; Toe boards: Not less than 200 mm high; Gap: Space between any toe board and the lowest guard-rail above it not exceeding 700 mm; Counterweights: On any outrigger, not less than 3 times the weight necessary to balance the load on the projecting part when the platform is fully loaded; Workers: At least 18 years old, with a certificate from training recognised by the Commissioner for Labour or provided by the manufacturer or its local agent |

| ADV-14 | Facilities for External Inspection and Maintenance of Buildings | Reserve a roof track and enclosure within 15 m or 10% of building height, whichever is less, and design the supports for wind on every exposed part of the facade. Even without a gondola, cast in roof anchorages and make the bottom of every pipe lightwell a common area with a way in. Printed figures: Planning: A gondola enclosure may be treated as a rooftop utility structure and exempted from the height restriction if the size is the minimum that meets the function. In general, rooftop utility structures should not exceed 15 m beyond the main roof or 10% of the building height, whichever is the less. Also follow the statutory town plan and ridgeline views; Platform: Follow PNAP ADV-11 for installation, use, and maintenance |

| ADV-18 | Corruption Prevention | Instruct site staff, before works start, that any advantage from a contractor or supplier is refused and reported to the ICAC on 2526 6366. Printed figures: Report a bribe: Immediately to the ICAC, 2526 6366, 24 hours; Not only money: A loan, gift, favour, offer of employment, or any other advantage. The definition is in the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance, Cap. 201; Code of conduct: Advisable to issue a company code and staff instructions. The ICAC Advisory Services Group gives free confidential advice on 2526 6363 |
| ADV-21 | Management Framework for Disposal of Dredged-Excavated Sediment | Design the basement or reclamation to leave the mud in place, and start the removal justification early if marine disposal is still required. One result above the upper level makes the sediment Category H and rules out ordinary open-sea disposal. Allow about 8 months for the Sediment Quality Report, and keep the dredging tender inside the report’s reliability period, which is at least 3 years from the start of sampling. Category L can go to open sea; Category M and Category H need a confined site or special treatment, and the contractor cannot dump until the permit is issued. Printed figures: Exempt from the rationale submission: Category L sediment below 50,000 m³. Also: emergency dredging for safety or to avert an environmental hazard; maintenance or deepening of harbour fairways, berths, anchorages, navigation channels, or approaches; maintenance, but not construction, of watercourses, rivers, stream courses, drainage channels, or outfalls; Cadmium: 1.5; Chromium: 80; Copper: 65; Mercury: 0.5; Lead: 75; Zinc: 200; Arsenic: 12; Nickel: 40; Silver: 1; Low molecular weight PAHs: 550 µg/kg; High molecular weight PAHs: 1,700 µg/kg; Total PCBs: 23 µg/kg; Tributyltin in interstitial water: 0.15 µg TBT/L; Waiver of sampling: Emergency dredging, or small-scale maintenance dredging of less than 5,000 m³ in situ. Submit previous data or the known history; Time: Sampling, testing, and the Sediment Quality Report normally take 8 months; Grid: Low contamination: 200 m by 200 m, surface sample only. High: 100 m by 100 m, vertical profile. Very high, such as near an outfall or nullah: 50 m by 50 m, vertical profile; Vertical profile: Continuous samples. Top of sub-samples at the seabed, 0.9 m, 1.9 m, 2.9 m, then every 3 m to the bottom of the dredged layer; Holding: Keep samples at 4°C in the dark and do not freeze. Recommended maximum holding time is 2 weeks for a chemical test and 8 weeks for a biological test; Tier III: Required for Category M, and for Category H only if one or more contaminants exceed 10 times the lower chemical exceedance level. Category L does not go to Tier III; Category M tests: One set of three tests on each composite of up to 5 continuous samples: a 10-day burrowing amphipod test, a 20-day burrowing polychaete test, and a 48–96 hour larvae test. Failure of any one test is failure of the biological test; Category H dilution: If above 10 times the lower level, mix the sample with 9 portions of reference sediment before the same three tests; Formal report: Where biological screening is required, submit the Sediment Quality Report at least 3 months before the dredging contract is tendered, or at least 2 months before a maintenance-dredging works order. If biological screening is not required, the preliminary report may be deemed the formal report; Reliability: At approval, the Director specifies a reliability period of not less than 3 years from the actual start of sampling. If the lapse between the start of sampling and the start of construction exceeds 3 years, re-testing is required unless waived. The contract must state the expiry date and that extending the report is the contractor’s cost if the dumping-permit application is late; Category L: Type 1, open-sea disposal; Category M that passes biological screening: Type 1, open-sea disposal at dedicated sites; Category M that fails, or Category H that is not above 10 times the lower level, or Category H above that multiple that passes the dilution test: Type 2, confined marine disposal. East Sha Chau mud pits are designated for this; Category H that fails the dilution test: Type 3, special treatment or disposal, agreed with the Director of Environmental Protection. Treatment so that it becomes suitable for confined marine disposal is not ruled out; Small Category L jobs: Allocation of disposal space for less than 50,000 m³ of Category L sediment is delegated to the Director of Environmental Protection; Contract: Include the disposal requirements and Notes (1) to (6). Open-sea sites are multi-user, so the contract must allow diversion from one site to another. Type 2 and Type 3 allocations must state the committee’s and the Director’s conditions |
| ADV-24 | Floor Drains in Kitchens and Bathrooms | If a kitchen or bathroom has a floor drain, route some waste discharge into its U-trap and detail the connection so the trap cannot back-flow onto the floor. |
| ADV-25 | Extractor Fans in Bathrooms and Lavatories in Domestic Buildings | Size a domestic bathroom extractor to about 10.2 L/s per square metre and give the door an undercut or a louvre so the fan cannot pull the traps dry. Printed figures: Optimum volume: The World Health Organisation Environmental Health Team advised 2 cfm per square foot (10.2 L/s per square metre). A larger volume adds little comfort and risks negative pressure |
| ADV-27 | Protection of Natural Streams and Rivers from Adverse Impacts Arising from Construction Works | Keep the stream in its natural bed, and treat a concrete-lined channel as the last option after the six less intrusive methods. On an Ecologically Important Stream, leave the bed, use stone or a soft bank, and phase the work so part of the width and the flow stay open. Cross the stream on stilts, keep the haul track off the bed, and discharge treated site water well away from the channel. |
| ADV-29 | Construction Site Safety - Pay for Safety Scheme | Ask the client to put a Site Safety section in the bills, with pre-priced items including training, so safety is paid only when those items are certified complete. Printed figures: Since: The Pay for Safety Scheme was introduced in 1996 |
| ADV-31 | Building External Finishes - Wet-fixed Tiles | Joint the tiles at not less than 3 mm, mechanically fix any tile larger than 0.1 m², and keep ceramic water absorption to 0.5% up to first-floor height and 3% above it. Keep each render coat between 8 mm and 16 mm and the total at not more than 20 mm, after about six weeks of concrete drying, and do not use galvanised mesh on an exposed wall. Specify a pull-off test of at least three samples of each tile type on every ten typical floors, chosen by the authorized person or structural engineer and kept off the waterproofing. Printed figures: Joints between tiles: Usually 8 mm to 10 mm, and in any case not less than 3 mm, so that grout can be inserted; Grout: Preferably type CG2 to BS EN 13888:2009. Start grouting as soon as the adhesive bed has hardened sufficiently; Large tiles: Control the weight in the design. A tile larger than 0.1 m² is secured by mechanical means; Water absorption of ceramic tiles: Not more than 0.5% up to first-floor height, and not more than 3% above first-floor height; Cement slurry: Soak the tiles in water for a minimum of 30 minutes and stack them to drain surface water immediately before tiling. Dampen the rendering. When a proprietary adhesive is used, neither the tiles nor the rendering should be too wet; Spatter dash: Preferably within 24 hours after striking the formwork. If later, prepare remedial measures, including a bonding agent, with the authorized person and registered structural engineer; Thickness: Each coat not less than 8 mm and not greater than 16 mm. Total thickness not more than 20 mm. A proprietary product follows the manufacturer’s thickness; Metal reinforcement: Austenitic stainless steel wire, preferably 2.5 mm diameter, welded to a mesh of about 50 mm by 50 mm, with austenitic stainless steel fixings. Galvanised reinforcement is only for limited sheltered areas and is not recommended externally or where constantly exposed to moisture; Manufactured sand: May replace river sand. For private projects, follow Technical Circular (Works) No. 8/2018 |
| ADV-32 | Provision of Babycare Rooms and Lactation Rooms in Commercial Buildings | Place at least one babycare room in the common area, off the toilets, at not less than 5 m² for one user or 12 m² if it is shared, with one wheelchair breastfeeding cubicle in the shared room. Provide the changing table, a lever-tap sink, a covered nappy bin, and a power outlet inside the room, not as loose furniture added later. Provide a lactation room of not less than 7.5 m² in the common area of each office building, with two cubicles including one for a wheelchair user, and keep it out of the toilets. Printed figures: Single user: Not less than 5 m², with adequate manoeuvring space for a wheelchair user; Multiple users: Not less than 12 m², with separate breastfeeding cubicles, one of which caters for a wheelchair user, plus bottle-feeding areas and nappy-changing areas; Light and air: Where a sanitary fitment such as a sink is provided, illumination and ventilation follow regulation 36 of the Building (Planning) Regulations (Cap. 123F). A thermostat for users is recommended; Size: Not less than 7.5 m², for at least two users at a time, with privacy, and at least for one wheelchair user. Separate cubicles with doors |
| ADV-35 | Greening in Buildings | A fixed planter is building works and needs approval or a minor-works item; soil and plants in an existing box do not, but the slab and the drains still have to take the saturated weight. Design the roof or podium for the fully saturated greening as dead load plus the imposed load, fall it at not less than 1:80, and keep greenery out of the refuge. Printed figures: Designated minor works: May use the simplified requirements under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNAP APP-147, instead of prior approval and consent; Code: Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads 2011: weight of soil, waterproofing, drainage system, and plants is dead load. Imposed load is additional. Where people may congregate, use the minimum imposed load for that use; Existing building: Check approved records and site condition against the greening load, from a green carpet to a deep water pond. Follow PNAP APP-117; Falls: Surface fall not shallower than 1:80 on the structure and the greenery. Adapt the drains to the greenery system. On an existing building, add drains if the present system is inadequate, to avoid ponding and overload; Barriers: Protective barriers such as balustrades and railings for safe access and to prevent falling, under regulation 8 of the Building (Construction) Regulations, plus access and safety provisions for maintenance |
| ADV-37 | Conduct of Registered Building Professionals under the Buildings Ordinance | Supervise so that the built work does not deviate in any material way from the approved plan, and declare any financial interest in the works to the client. Report an irregularity found during the appointment to the Chief Officer/Technical Services, and treat that report as confidential. Printed figures: Section 7(1A)(b): If a registered building professional has been negligent or has misconducted himself in a professional way, the Building Authority may bring the matter to a disciplinary board appointed under section 5. Before referral, the Authority considers the seriousness of the case, including whether the person deserves censure under section 7(1); Other laws: Repeatedly fails to remind the appointed contractor to observe site safety under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Chapter 59). Uses the Buildings Department logo on a name card, letterhead, or publicity material |
| ADV-38 | Innovative Building Materials and Technologies | Use this route for a building, drainage, or structural product that is not already in a practice note, a code, or the Central Data Bank, and send a geotechnical innovation to the Geotechnical Engineering Office instead. A letter of in-principle acceptance lasts at most 5 years and still needs approval and consent, and a section 42 application where the design departs from the deemed-to-satisfy provisions. Printed figures: Application: Appendix A, signed by the supplier or manufacturer and the appointed professionals; an innovation brief stating use, design principle, job reference if any, and the innovative content; building, drainage, or structural plans showing how the Ordinance is met or that performance is equivalent or not inferior; and a technical specification with catalogue, test or assessment reports from accredited laboratories, and a factory quality-assurance scheme with ISO 9001 or an equivalent certification; Determination: Normally within 45 days of receipt, as a letter of in-principle acceptance or a letter of comments, if the application has sufficient information; Comments: Respond to every comment within 30 days or the application may be treated as abandoned. After the response, a determination follows within 30 days. A refusal requires a new application under paragraph 2; On a project: Pre-acceptance is not an exemption from prior approval and consent. A plan that uses the pre-accepted innovation is accompanied by an application for modification or exemption under section 42 of the Buildings Ordinance where appropriate. The project designers remain responsible for compliance with the Ordinance, the approved plans, and the letter; Validity: A maximum of 5 years. Renewal is an Appendix B application by the authorized person and, as appropriate, the registered structural engineer and registered geotechnical engineer, checked against the latest Ordinance, codes, and practice notes |
| APP-1 | Validity of Approved Plans | Freeze the floor area to within 10% and freeze the use before locking the scheme, because either a larger cut or a use change needs an amending Exclusion Order from the Lands Tribunal. | APP-2 | Calculation of Gross Floor Area and Non-accountable Gross Floor Area | Measure to the structural face only if the curtain wall projects not more than 200 mm on a domestic building or 250 mm on a non-domestic building, glass reflectance stays at or below 20%, and a 300 mm dwarf wall is provided. Count a public transport terminus, discount an underground car park in full only if it is charging-enabled, and treat an aboveground car park above the second floor as half accountable unless a listed exception applies. Printed figures: Shopping-arcade voids: Total void area not exceeding 10% of the gross floor area of the arcade; Duplex flat void: Usable floor space of the premises at least 150 m2. Void not more than 10% of that usable floor space, headroom not more than 6.5 m, only in the living or dining room or the entrance foyer. Only two levels of voids in each residential tower, and not more than 0.5% of the total domestic gross floor area of the development. The 0.5% cap may be applied with flexibility for a low-rise, low-density flat development similar to houses; House void: Usable floor space at least 250 m2. Void not more than 5% of that area, headroom not more than 7.0 m, or to the mean level of a pitched ceiling over the void. Same rooms as a flat. No percentage of total domestic gross floor area; Other voids that may be modified: In front of cinema and theatre balconies; banking halls; cocklofts for storage in the ground storey of a single-staircase building; auditoria, sporting halls including squash courts, school halls, and religious institutions; main common entrance lobbies of non-domestic buildings. Items (f) and (g) in paragraph 2 also sit under the APP-151 cap; Curtain wall as the face: Measure gross floor area and site coverage to the outer face of the structure if the wall is not part of the structure, a reinforced-concrete dwarf perimeter wall at least 300 mm high is provided, the projection is not more than 200 mm for a domestic building or 250 mm for a non-domestic building, and the external reflectance of the glass does not exceed 20%. A purpose-built hotel, the hotel part, or a hotel converted from a non-domestic building is non-domestic for the 250 mm limit; Curtain wall on an existing facade, wholesale conversion, projecting over a street: Exemption from section 31(1) if the glazing is low-energy absorbent or the materials are energy efficient and the design achieves 40% in Energy Use and Indoor Environmental Quality under BEAM Plus. Provisional assessment before consent. Final assessment within 6 months of acknowledgement of Form BA14. Not accepted on existing balconies over a street, or only on a localised part of the facade; Cladding: Nominal finishes, or cladding not more than 90 mm thick, or 75 mm on a non-structural prefabricated external wall, may be disregarded. No finish or cladding projects beyond the site boundary. Projection over a street on a refurbishment needs an exemption from section 31(1); Services that may be disregarded, if justified: Lift, air-conditioning, and heating machinery, and similar services including a fire control centre, water tanks, electrical switch rooms, meter rooms, transformer and generator rooms, pump rooms, telephone equipment rooms, cable riser duct rooms, CO rooms, hose-reel closets, sewage treatment plant rooms, and smoke extraction. Enclosing walls and a protected lobby that serve only that room, normally not thicker than 100 mm, may also be disregarded. Air-conditioning and heating plant also follow PNAP APP-151; Also modifiable under regulation 23(3)(a): Chimney shafts; filtration plant for a hotel pool or a communal water feature; a satellite-master-antenna room not exceeding 1.2 m by 1.2 m to paragraph 33 of PNAP APP-42; genuine pipe ducts and air ducts; plant for rainwater or grey-water recycling and a battery room for solar panels; a refuge floor that complies with the fire safety code. Ducts for non-mandatory features follow the APP-151 cap. Ducts for mandatory features do not; Car parks, paragraph 18, subject to the dates in paragraph 19: Underground: 100%. Aboveground, not more than two floors: 100%; other floors above: 50%. Aboveground 100% if underground parking is technically infeasible or there is no adverse environmental or visual impact, if required under the lease as Government Accommodation, or if required under the lease for subsidised sale or rental flats accepted by the Government and provided by the Hong Kong Housing Society or the Urban Renewal Authority. Loading and unloading: 100% underground and at ground, 50% above ground, and 100% in the special cases. The 100% and 50% car-park figures require electric-vehicle charging-enabling facilities except where an automated parking system is installed; Dates: Underground public car parks: on or after 1 March 2017. Paragraph 18(b): on or after 1 November 2025. Paragraphs 18(d) and 18(e): on or after 1 August 2023. Electric-vehicle requirements in paragraph 4 of Appendix C: new building plans for new developments on or after 1 November 2025. Earlier approvals follow the December 2023 version, subject to section 16(3)(d). Not applicable to existing car parks; What counts as underground: Ceiling soffit at or below the mean street level, and that mean is not more than 5 m above the lowest street level, with the multi-street average in Appendix C. For light and air, not more than half the top parking storey, and not more than 2.5 m, may protrude. A semi-sunken floor that follows a sloping site may also be accepted; Chargers: A medium charger is not less than 7 kW. A quick or fast charger is 50 kW, 100 kW, or higher. The general building plans show a schedule, and a registered professional engineer in electrical or building services certifies the report to the February 2023 technical guidelines, the November 2025 supplement, and the April 2015 charging guidelines. The report may be deferred to before superstructure consent. The Environmental Protection Department replies within 22 calendar days on the submission and within 10 calendar days on the completion certificate. No objection is lodged with the occupation-permit application. Meter rooms for the chargers are disregarded whether the car park is above or below ground; Technical reasons for no basement car park: Major underground utilities, Scheduled Areas No. 1, 2, 3, or 4, or other complex geotechnical constraints, with evidence; Bicycles: Covered spaces required by the lease and designated as common areas may be exempted from regulation 23(3)(a) |
| APP-5 | Height of Storeys | Set domestic storeys at not more than 3.5 m typical and 4 m at the topmost floor (4.5 m for a house), measured structure to structure, and drop to 2 m only at internal edge beams, stairs, and notional doorways. Keep a transfer plate within the thickness and area limits above if it is to stay out of gross floor area, and give the services zone its own maintenance access. Printed figures: Beams projecting internally from the face of external walls: 2 m to the underside is permissible, where 2.3 m is otherwise difficult; Staircases: Minimum clear height 2 m; Flat, topmost floor: Not exceeding 4 m. Where the topmost floor is duplex or triplex, that maximum applies to only one storey of the unit; Flat, typical floor: Not exceeding 3.5 m; House: Not exceeding 4.5 m; Domestic blocks of not more than 40 storeys: Transfer-plate thickness not greater than the typical-floor storey height of the blocks.
