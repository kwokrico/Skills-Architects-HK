## PNRC operative rules

Practice Notes for Registered Contractors. These bind the registered contractor. The authorized person still has to brief and check them, because several notes set hold points the architect relies on (testing, supervision, signboard validation, cease-works).

| PNRC-3 | Cease Works Orders | The schematic site-instruction protocol should treat only a written order signed by one of those posts as authority to stop, demolish, or alter building works, street works, lift works, or escalator works. Brief the contractor that a spoken stop, demolition, or alteration demand is reported to the Building Authority after the Warrant Card is seen, and is not itself the written order. |
| PNRC-4 | Hoardings Covered Walkways and Gantries | The site logistics plan holds a 1.1 m clear pedestrian route and, for urban demolition, a steel-plate hoarding with a lit covered walkway. Programme the hoarding permit and each renewal on the 30-day clocks in PNAP APP-23. Any frontage that takes metered or non-metered bays needs a staged suspension sketch, with a seven-working-day or 30-day Transport Department lead time matched to the duration. Printed figures: Pedestrian clear width: Minimum 1.1 m in every case; width is otherwise related to location; Covered-walkway lighting: Recommended layout for a typical walkway 2 m wide and 2.5 m high: luminaires with 18 W or 20 W, 600 mm tubular fluorescent lamps at 3 m spacing; Permit to erect: Building Authority processes it within 30 days if paragraph 19 of PNAP APP-23 is met; Renewal: Apply at least 30 days before expiry (PNAP APP-23 paragraph 25); Minor amendments: May be carried out without prior agreement of the Building Authority if paragraph 26 of PNAP APP-23 is met; More than three months: Written application with sketches at least 30 days before; longest approval is one year, then a fresh application; removal and reinstatement costs paid in advance to the Highways Department; tell Transport Department and Highways Department at least one week before project completion |
| PNRC-5 | Concrete against Adjoining Walls | Do not draw a new concrete face tight against a neighbour’s wall unless the existing wall has been checked as a loaded shutter, or the detail provides independent formwork. Party-wall and basement details must show either a verified existing wall or a separate shutter, because collapse is read as a section 40 offence. |
| PNRC-7 | Airport Control of Obstructions | Crane height, scaffold height and any skyward temporary structure are airport-obstruction items from the start of site planning, on the same footing as the permanent building. A crane or plant strategy above the prescribed limit is a CAD exemption, arranged before that plant is erected. Printed figures: Height: Restricted to the height prescribed under Cap 301 |
| PNRC-11 | Testing of Drainage Works | Drainage that will be buried must be sequenced so the AP can witness the test and file the certificate within 7 days. Leave drainage trenches open until the four working days allowed for the Building Authority to inspect and test have elapsed. Specify bedding, support, and fall so the line can be tested to BS EN 1610:2015 before cover. On sites near a water control zone or gathering ground, allow BA testing of manholes, petrol interceptors, and septic tanks as well as the drains. Printed figures: Witness: AP or the AP’s representative witnesses the test and endorses the results and records (PNAP APP-58); Certificate: AP prepares a completion certificate for the drainage test and submits the certificate, results, and records to the BA within 7 days of the test, whether or not the BA has inspected or tested the drains |
| PNRC-12 | Overheight Vehicles | Haul routes, temporary openings and any structure the contractor must pass under are checked against a 4.6 m load height before plant is specified. |
| PNRC-13 | Pulverised Fuel Ash in Concrete | Specify the PFA percentage in the structural notes, and keep ordinary work at or below 25% unless the pour is a special application that can carry expert control up to the 35% ceiling. Any specification above 25% PFA has to be justified for striking time, creep and long-term deflection before the structural submission is frozen. Printed figures: PFA as a separate constituent: Only with OPC; comply with BS 3892: Part 1:1982, except the maximum water-requirement criterion may not apply; Blended cement containing PFA: BS 6588:1985; nominal PFA content not exceeding 25%; PFA in the concrete: Not exceed 35% by mass of the cementitious content (OPC plus PFA); 25% replacement: Usually only used in normal construction; Replacement exceeding 25%: Special applications such as marine construction and massive pours; expert advice and stringent site control |
| PNRC-14 | Railway Protection | Treat any lot that touches the 30 m zone, and any larger station zone, as railway-protected from the first massing and foundation sketch. Ground investigation and underground drainage inside Area Number 3 stay on the approval-and-consent path unless the specific minor-works carve-out applies, and that carve-out still needs MTRCL agreement before start. Place exploration holes on plan against the underground railway outline before tender, because holes inside 10 m and 3 m need verticality and depth controls. Keep piles, bores, soil nails, and wells outside the 3 m underground exclusion, keep at-grade piling outside 3 m of the fence or 7 m of the track centreline, and hold underground railway pressure change to 20 kPa unless MTRCL and BD accept an engineering approach. Set façades, vents, greening, crane arcs, and storage so openings and greening stay 5 m from railway vents (2.5 m only for the exhaust-directed opening case) and so anything above track stays outside 6 m unless MTRCL has agreed. Design so predicted movement stays inside Alert (1:2 000, 10 mm, 2.5 mm rail difference) and so vibration stays inside the Alert peak particle velocities, because Action is a suspension trigger. If the empirical limits cannot be met, the monitoring plan must split the works so each stage is between 10% and 50% of total predicted movement and must show Alert, Alarm, and Action for every stage. Printed figures: Ground investigation in Area Number 3: Prior BA approval and consent. Plans under Building (Administration) Regulations regulation 8(1)(l). Proposals follow Appendix B. Concurrent approval and consent: PNAP ADM-16; Underground drainage in Area Number 3: Full Buildings Ordinance approval and consent, except underground drainage that may proceed under the simplified requirements of the Minor Works Control System as in paragraph 16; GI or underground drainage in a railway protection area that is not Area Number 3: Forward the proposal to MTRCL for comment before commencement; Class I and Class II minor works in Area Number 3 that involve spread footing, excavation, or underground drainage: BD copies the submission to MTRCL. The prescribed building professional or prescribed registered contractor obtains MTRCL’s agreement before commencement; Borehole or drillhole within 10 m on plan of any point of an underground railway structure or facility, and sunk to a depth of 3 m from the highest point of those structures and facilities: Method statement for checking verticality; Borehole or drillhole within 3 m on plan of any point of an underground railway structure or facility: Method statement for controlling depth; Pile, foundation, borehole, drillhole, well, soil nail, horizontal drain, rock bolt, dowel, or other geotechnical installation: Not within 3 m of any point of the underground railway structures and facilities; Piling: Not within 3 m on plan of the railway fence or wall; where there is no fence or wall, not within 7 m on plan of the centreline of the nearest track; Anchor: Any part more than 3 m from any part of a railway structure or facility. The centroid of the fixed length more than twice the fixed length away from any railway structure or facility; Vertical or horizontal pressure change on underground railway structures and facilities: Not exceeding 20 kPa, from excavation, filling, ground improvement, dewatering, field testing (plate loading, pressure-meter, packer), and from loads added or removed by foundations, including construction loads; Openable or fixed window, doorway, or building ventilation intake or exhaust, relative to a railway ventilation opening (free-standing or inside a building): Not closer than 5 m. May be reduced to 2.5 m if exhaust air from the railway ventilation facilities is directed away and is not likely to affect the opening by natural convection; Vertical greening: Not closer than 5 m to the ventilation opening of any railway ventilation facilities; Scaffolding, working platforms, advertising signs, or projections at a level above the railway tracks: Not within 6 m on plan of any point of the railway tracks without MTRCL agreement. Inside that limit, protect the railway; works may be required outside railway operation hours; Mobile plant or lifting appliance with any part higher than railway track level: Protective measures agreed with MTRCL when operating within 6 m on plan of railway structures and facilities; Jib swing: Arc not to encroach within 6 m on plan of the railway tracks and/or above-ground structures and facilities, except with MTRCL agreement; Storage of materials, dangerous goods, other inflammable materials, containers, huts: Not within 6 m on plan of railway structures, tracks, and ventilation openings without MTRCL agreement; Tree planting on an unfenced railway section: Not at a distance less than 2 m from the nearest rail. Planting proposal, including estimated height and spread at maturity, to MTRCL so collapse of trunk or branches would not affect railway operation; Road-lamp maintenance plant: Not closer to the tracks than the nearest part of the lamp standard where the lamp is higher than the tracks. If the equipment operates higher than 1 m above the tracks and closer than 6 m, MTRCL-agreed protection is required; works may be outside operation hours; Angular distortion in any plane: 1:2 000; Total movement in any plane: 10 mm; Level difference between rails of a track in the perpendicular plane: 2.5 mm; Peak particle velocity, blasting: 15 mm/s; Peak particle velocity, prolonged vibration: 9 mm/s; Peak particle velocity on overhead power-line mast, or signalling and telecommunication furniture: 6 mm/s; Vibration amplitude on that mast or furniture: 48 µm |
| PNRC-15 | Asbestos | On alteration or demolition, freeze the strip-out sequence on a registered-asbestos investigation and complete abatement, with the 28-day Environmental Protection Department notice, before other building works. Name a registered asbestos consultant in the early consultant team, and specify removal only by a registered asbestos contractor unless the work is on the 1997 low-risk non-friable exemption list. Printed figures: Notice: 28-day advance notice to the Environmental Protection Department before commencement of the works |
| PNRC-17 | Environmental Nuisance from Construction Sites | Choose non-percussive piling and place noisy plant away from noise-sensitive receivers, because percussive piling needs a construction noise permit even in the daytime window and is generally prohibited outside 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. Allow haul-road paving, wheel washing, covered stockpiles, and boundary hoarding on the site plan, and notify the Environmental Protection Department before foundation, superstructure, demolition, or site formation, including tunnel works within 100 metres of the open-air exit. Set the site plan with de-silting before any connection to public drains, roofed wash and servicing bays, sealed abandoned connections before demolition or foundations, and toilets to a sewer or a septic tank rather than to a storm drain. Fix the hoarding plan, including its stipulated width and the pedestrian provisions agreed with the Transport Department, together with a safe run-in and catch platforms, before the site boundary is locked. Provide a labelled chemical-waste store and a licensed collector where the works will generate waste oil or waste batteries. Printed figures: Percussive piling: A construction noise permit is required between 7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m. on any day other than a public holiday, and percussive piling is generally prohibited at other times; Powered mechanical equipment for general construction works, and/or prescribed construction works, in designated areas (predominantly built-up areas): A construction noise permit is required during the restricted hours: 7:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m., or at any time on a general holiday including Sunday |
| PNRC-19 | Change of Address | The business address used for the contractor in the schematic appointment documents is the address that must be updated with Form BA 24 within 14 days if it changes. Printed figures: Time: Within 14 days thereof; Form: BA 24 (see PNRC 2); Paper: Registration Unit, Buildings Department Headquarters, North Tower, West Kowloon Government Offices, 11 Hoi Ting Road, Yau Ma Tei, Kowloon, Hong Kong |
| PNRC-21 | Tropical Hardwood Timber | Where the building is set back from the boundary, test whether a covered walkway is required at all, and specify steel hoardings where a hoarding is required. At structural-system selection, prefer precast, structural steel, or steel formwork and steel props over timber formwork for in-situ reinforced concrete. Specify non-timber frames and painted-joinery alternatives, and where hardwood remains, require separation for reuse or supply from renewable sources. |
| PNRC-23 | Certification of Plant Operators | Any scheme that depends on a site crane, hoist or other lifting appliance needs certificated operators in the programme, with training booked before the plant is required. |
| PNRC-24 | Metal Refuse Chutes | Debris paths at schematic stage should be internal, or otherwise clear of the public edge, rather than a chain of metal barrels on the external wall. |
| PNRC-25 | Certificate of Completion and Materials Schedule | Completion and occupation-permit programme should assume these reply windows, and demolition or A&A fast-track (BA14B / BA14C) needs AP and RSE agreement before the form goes in. A shallow spread footing (excavation not over 4.5 m) under one contractor can close on the same completion certificate as the superstructure. Materials that affect structure or fire safety and are not already on the approved plans, plus cast-iron drainage in APP-133, must be specifiable early enough to appear on the completion schedule or on a PNRC 81 conformity certificate. Damper locations need an RSC(V) inspection certificate on the completion path, whether the RGBC, an RMWC or the RSC(V) installed them. | PNRC-27 | Cantilevered Reinforced Concrete Structures | Projections with a clear span over 1000 mm should be drawn as beam-and-slab where practicable, and the plan extent must already satisfy Building (Planning) Regulations 7 and 10 and PNAP APP-19. An external cantilever beam is at least 300 mm deep at the support and is specified for exposure condition 2 or higher. Size the slab at 110 mm, 125 mm or 150 mm from the 500 mm and 750 mm span breaks, and reinforce both faces both ways with main bars at least 10 mm at not more than 150 mm centres and at least 0.25% steel. An exposed cantilever slab longer than 750 mm is a 35 MPa waterproof, hot-dip-galvanized-bar element with a completion report and BA14, and every external cantilever slab is checked to 0.1 mm crack width or 100 N/mm² bar stress. Cast the cantilever with its support, keep the construction joint off the external edge, fall the protection mortar at not less than 1:75, and space outlets at not more than 5 m where the slab cannot be reached for cleaning. Exposed canopies over 750 mm need an as-built record of falls, outlets, cover and galvanized bars, and a maintenance document handed to the owners at completion. Printed figures: Clear span more than 1000 mm: Use a beam-and-slab arrangement instead of a pure slab cantilever where practicable; Span-to-effective-depth: Comply with clause 7.3.4.2 of the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Concrete 2004 (Second Edition); Projection and headroom: Extent of projection and clear space above ground to comply with Building (Planning) Regulations 7 and 10 and PNAP APP-19 as appropriate; Not cantilever behaviour: Paragraph 6 does not apply to in-fill slabs enclosed by edge beams, or slabs supported so they do not behave like cantilevers; Greater than 750 mm: 150 mm; Main bars: At least 10 mm diameter; spacing not greater than 150 mm; Steel area: Not less than 0.25% of the cross-sectional area of the structural concrete; Anchorage: Clause 9.4.3 of the Concrete Code; curtailment rules apply to top bars extended into the adjacent span; Drop at the supporting end: Top main bars not greater than 16 mm diameter, so anchorage into the supporting beams and internal slab can be developed; details drawn at large scale; All external cantilevered slabs: Maximum crack width at the tension face 0.1 mm under the serviceability limit state, or stress in deformed high yield bars not exceeding 100 N/mm² under working-load flexural tension; Waterproofing and falls: Effective waterproofing; membrane or tanking protected by 1:3 cement-sand mortar laid to a fall of not less than 1:75; Drainage: Drain surface water away where practicable; if water is to cascade from the edge (for example a projecting window), no nuisance under Building (Planning) Regulation 4(b); continuous drip or groove at the bottom edge; Inaccessible cantilevers: Maximum distance between drainage outlets, if provided, not more than 5 m; Pipes in the cantilever: Avoid penetration or embedment, especially in slabs; if unavoidable, allow for local loss of strength; PNAP APP-105 for water-borne pipes inside reinforced concrete beams and slabs |
| PNRC-29 | Lift Shaft Platforms | Shaft platforms are designed temporary works with a named T4 method and a T1 supervisor, not carpenter-built decks added as the core rises. A partial-occupation scheme that leaves shafts in construction needs a fire-rated separation on the temporary occupation permit boundary that stays intact while platforms are erected and struck. Printed figures: Method: Construction plans and method statements for erection, alteration, maintenance and dismantling, prepared by an engineer of technically competent person grade T4 or above employed by the contractor, and checked and signed off by that authorized person or registered structural engineer; Build: Constructed to the construction plan under the full-time immediate supervision of a competent person with technically competent person grade T1, appointed in writing; on completion, checked by the contractor’s engineer; Risk assessment: Before erection, maintenance or dismantling, by a planning team of site managerial and supervisory staff from the main contractor and subcontractors, including at least one person at grade T4 or equivalent; includes a thorough inspection by the T1 competent person of the stability of platforms already erected; Dismantling: Full-time immediate supervision by the same T1 competent person |
| PNRC-30 | Suspended Working Platforms | A façade that will be maintained from a suspended platform needs a clear hanging zone wide enough for a deck of at least 440 mm that does not bear on the face. Outrigger and counterweight space, plus an independent lifeline anchorage, are planned with the suspension points so the 3-times balance weight and the safety rope can actually be installed. Each platform is a numbered, load-marked appliance with a current certificate on it; the maintenance strategy should assume re-test after every re-erection and after severe weather. Printed figures: Counterweight: On any outrigger, not less than 3 times the weight needed to balance the load on the projecting part when the platform is fully loaded; Workers: At least 18 years old, and holding a certificate from training recognised by the Commissioner for Labour or provided by the manufacturer or its local agent |
| PNRC-32 | Display of Site Information | The hoarding elevation reserves a prominent panel for address or lot number, the three names, and a telephone number. Sales graphics and advertising stay off the hoarding and covered walkway; only the basic development facts listed in the note belong there. |
| PNRC-33 | Testing of Reinforcement for Concrete | Specify grade 250 only up to 12 mm diameter, or grade 500B or 500C, and exclude cold-worked bars from a CS2 compliance route. Rebar compliance on a private project is a HOKLAS purchaser’s test report tied to the delivery batch, not a mill certificate alone. Off-site cages and threaded bars are acceptable from a CEDD Approved Yard only with qualified supervision, and the product list plus the RSE’s compliance statement must reach Buildings Department within 21 days of delivery to site. | PNRC-34 | Water Carrying Services Affecting Slopes | Any buried water main, drain, or other water-carrying service in or near a slope is designed with a permanent leakage-control measure and an inspection route, using the Code rather than a site-only repair note. Show buried services and the slope they can affect on the same schematic plan, because a section 27C investigation can be required for services laid in or near that slope. |
| PNRC-36 | Lift Maintenance and Replacement | Lift-maintenance method statements have to keep a shaft to one open landing door, or to three only if hot works and welding are excluded. Replacing more than one landing door at once means a one-hour temporary hoarding over those openings, not an open shaft. Temporary shaft hoardings are one-hour construction with only the vent and access-door allowances above. Lobby and escape width at lift landings must still work when a one-hour hoarding is standing in front of the shaft, and the shaft must be closed or hoarded whenever the works are left unattended. Printed figures: Ventilation opening: Not exceeding 5,500 mm², at the upper portion of the hoarding |
| PNRC-37 | Sale Offices and Show Flats | A show flat or site sales office is a temporary-occupation problem from schematic stage: either a segregated part of the new building with a temporary occupation permit, or a temporary building with both a temporary building permit and a temporary occupation permit. The show-flat plan needs its own street-connected protected escape, a fire separation from live construction, and a works programme that can stop if the permit condition requires it. |
| PNRC-38 | Registration of Building Contractors | Lock the package to an RGBC only where the works are outside the five specialized categories, and name an RSC entered in the matching sub-register where the works are specialized. If any one of the six circumstances is absent, treat the demolition as specialized works and appoint a specialist contractor in the demolition sub-register. Place the contract with a contractor whose accepted AS is appointed for that registration, and, for a corporation, whose Technical Director is a board director authorised to commit plant, money, and supervision. Hold the tender award until the contractor, and any new Authorized Signatory, is on the register with a committee recommendation where section 8B(10) or an addition application requires one. Treat a conviction or disciplinary record on minor works or prescribed window inspection or repair as still requiring a committee interview on renewal or restoration; the course certificate remits 15 PP and leaves that interview in place.
