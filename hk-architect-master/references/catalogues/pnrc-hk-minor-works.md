# pnrc hk minor works

Source topic: `subskills/hk-minor-works/hk-minor-works.md`.

## PNRC operative rules

Practice Notes for Registered Contractors. These bind the registered contractor. The authorized person still has to brief and check them, because several notes set hold points the architect relies on (testing, supervision, signboard validation, cease-works).

| PNRC-47 | Aluminium Windows | Window replacement on an existing building may sit in the minor-works list in Schedule 1 and PNAP APP-147, but a new aluminium window still has to meet the paragraph 3 to 9 sizes and fixings unless a deviation is substantiated. Lock the module at side-hung width not over 700 mm and top-hung area not over 2.5 m², with 2 mm aluminium, mullions at least 38 mm deep, and fixing lugs at least 1.5 mm thick at not more than 300 mm centres. A 4-bar hinge is stainless steel at least 2.5 mm thick, fixed with at least three stainless steel rivets (≥ 4.8 mm) or screws (≥ 5 mm) to each top and bottom bar, into a 3 mm insert or a 5 mm local thickening, and at least 60% of the side-hung sash width. Draw a projecting head fin with a drip nose, and allow a field water-penetration test to Annex A of the Glass Code 2018 on the installed windows. Printed figures: Fixing lugs, where used: Stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel; minimum material thickness 1.5 mm; 300 mm centres maximum; Spacing greater than 300 mm: AP/RSE must be satisfied that structural stability and waterproofing are not compromised; Structural members: Minimum aluminium thickness 2 mm; Mullion depth: Not less than 38 mm; Side-hung sash: Width not exceeding 700 mm; Top-hung sash: Size not exceeding 2.5 m²; Bar thickness: Minimum 2.5 mm for all stainless steel bars of the hinge; Number and size: At least 3 stainless steel rivets of diameter not less than 4.8 mm, or stainless steel screws of diameter not less than 5 mm, to fix each of the bottom and top bars of the hinge to the frame and to the openable sash; Positive mechanical fixing: Insert a stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel plate, bar or angle not less than 3 mm thick inside the section to provide threads; Annex I illustrates this, including an M5×10 stainless steel screw as a typical screw; Alternative: Thicken the window section locally to not less than 5 mm; Annex II illustrates 5 mm local thickening and an M5×16 mm pan-head machine screw, pitch 0.8 mm; If neither insert nor 5 mm thickening is used: Substantiate by calculation that the aluminium section can anchor the stainless steel rivet or screw; Hinge length: At least 60% of the width of the side-hung casement sash; Annex I marks length ≥ 60% of sash width and sash width ≤ 700 mm |

Appoint a company only with at least one accepted Authorized Signatory for the types and classes on the submission, and, for a corporation, a Technical Director who is a board director of that company alone. For a Class I submission, allow time for a committee recommendation before treating the company, the added class, or a new Authorized Signatory as registered for that work. Keep a contractor with a conviction or disciplinary record for minor works or prescribed window inspection or repair on the renewal path that includes a committee interview; the course certificate remits 15 PP and leaves that interview in place. | PNRC-71 | Minor Works Control System | Match the works to a Schedule 1 class before schematic freeze, because Class I needs a prescribed building professional to design and supervise while Class II and Class III are carried through the prescribed registered contractor. A change that increases quantity, adds a minor-works item, or alters the design assumptions needs a new notice of commencement at least 7 days before the work, not a note on the completion certificate. Existing air-conditioner frames, drying racks and small canopies are on the Household Minor Works Validation Scheme only if erected or carried out before 31 December 2010; the further amenity features in footnote 1 use 1 September 2020. In Area Number 3, do not start Class I or Class II spread footing, excavation or underground drainage until the MTR Corporation Limited has agreed, even though the minor-works file itself is not circulated to other departments. Draw minor-works plans at not less than 1:100 and block plans at not less than 1:500, and treat the Appendix F 600 mm, 3 m and 100 kg notes as the sample for item 3.27 rather than as the Schedule 1 text. A rooftop equipment cabinet or an external antenna is a minor-works item only where it is one of the Appendix E item numbers, and the operator’s Office of the Telecommunications Authority, planning and lands approvals remain outside the minor-works submission. Printed figures: Count in the note: 58 items; Example printed in paragraph 2: Internal staircases connecting two floors; Appointment printed in the paragraph 4 table: Prescribed building professional to design and supervise, and prescribed registered contractor to carry out the works; Specified form: Yes; Photographs: Yes; Minor Amenity Feature Validation Scheme: Another 21 items of unauthorised minor amenity features. Footnote 1 lists supporting structures or metal casings for building services installations, supporting structures for radio base stations, supporting frames for air-conditioning units or light fittings, solid fence walls, mesh fences or metal railings, poles, metal gates, canopies, retractable awnings, trellises, and metal ventilation ducts or any associated supporting frames; Signboard Validation Scheme: Certain unauthorised signboards. Details are in PNAP APP-155; Minor Amenity Feature Validation Scheme, prescribed building or building works falling within a corresponding Class I item: An authorized person if it is a specified construction under section 37(4) of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation; an authorized person and a registered structural engineer if it is not |
| PNRC-72 | Authorised Signatory for Minor Works | Name one authorized signatory on the minor works submission and treat the signatory of Form MW01 or MW03 as the person accountable for the contractor's duties on that submission. Use the same authorized signatory on Form MW01 and on the supervision plan, and show that name on every specified form for the submission. For a Class I submission, obtain the authorized person's countersignature and give the Building Authority at least 7 days' notice before the new signatory takes the submission; a one-signatory contractor waits for an addition approved under PNRC 38 or 69. A temporary signatory may sign site inspection records for a total of not more than 30 days, and only if already accepted for the classes and types on that submission; that person does not sign Form MW01 or the supervision plan. Printed figures: Prescribed registered contractor, other than a sole proprietorship, with only one authorized signatory: A proposal to change the authorized signatory of the submission will not be accepted unless an application to add the new authorized signatory to the contractor's registration has been made under PNRC 38 or PNRC 69 and approved by the Building Authority.; Contractor with more than one authorized signatory on its registration: Notify the Building Authority in the standard form at Appendix A, countersigned by the authorized person of the submission if the works involve Class I minor works, not less than 7 days before the change. Otherwise the original authorized signatory is still regarded as responsible for the submission.; Timing: Submitted at least 7 days before the absence, except sudden illness or accident. In that case the contractor or the authorized signatory provides documentary proof within 3 days that the signatory is unable to act because of illness or accident.; Duration: Maximum 14 days. A further extension is considered only on documentary proof of special circumstances, such as illness. The total temporary acting period permitted is a maximum of 30 days. |
| PNRC-75 | Signboard Validation Scheme | Class I validation needs an authorized person and, unless the signboard is a specified construction, a registered structural engineer, and any strengthening of a Class I or Class II signboard is notified at least 7 days before it starts. Size an existing signboard to the Appendix A column for its item number, including the spread-footing cap of display area ≤ 1 m², thickness ≤ 300 mm, height ≤ 3 m and footing excavation ≤ 500 mm. Keep projecting signboards within the diagram’s 4.2 m projection, 2.4 m lateral spacing, 3 m opposite-side spacing, 3.5 m over the footway and 5.8 m over the carriageway, and do not replace Appendix A’s spread-footing limit of ≤ 1 m² with the checklist’s 2 m² and 20 m² boxes. Leave a conspicuous face on the signboard for a 35 mm validation number, and replace that number when a later validation follows a physical change. | PNRC-84 | Curtain Wall Window and Window Wall | Adopt the Glass Code 2018 and one consistent design standard for the whole curtain wall, window or window wall system, with the Building Regulations overriding that standard where they differ. Tempered glass is an ISO 9001, heat-soaked product, with the quality scheme and supervision plan lodged at least 14 days before production, a T3 supervisor on at least 30% of panes, and a full-time T1 coordinator logging temperature every minute. The heat-soak cycle used for specification and programme is a 20 mm minimum gap, heating at not more than 3°C per minute to 250°C without exceeding 290°C, then a hold of at least 120 minutes at 260°C ± 10°C, and the oven stays shut until 70°C. A structurally sealed façade needs compatibility, adhesion, print-review and deglazing certificates, accepted by the RSE, before occupation permit or BA14. Specify the spider model on the plans and allow a proof-load sample of at least 1% or 3 pieces, whichever is more, unless that model is already on the Central Data Bank. Limit a top-hung sash to 2.5 m² and a side-hung sash to 700 mm width, design locking devices at a factor of safety of 1.8, and do not run one handle to more than 8 locking points. Name the representative safety-test panes on the curtain-wall plans; the HOKLAS test is an occupation-permit item except for ADM-19 fast-track repair and minor works item 1.61. If spare tempered panes are to be reused, Schedule A must be in the maintenance manual at completion, because that endorsed schedule is what allows a later BA14 to omit a fresh heat-soak supervision plan and report. Printed figures: Factory: ISO 9001 quality assurance certification, under item 6 of section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance; Quality Assurance Scheme: Submitted at least 14 days before production starts, under regulation 10 of the Building (Administration) Regulations, with the RSE’s statement that the scheme meets the Buildings Ordinance and the approved plans; items are those in clause 9.1.3 of the Glass Code; Heat-soak compliance reports: Issued by the manufacturer, endorsed by the RSE, in accordance with clause 9.3 of the Glass Code, before the occupation permit application or Form BA14, as appropriate; Quality supervision plan: By the RSE and the registered contractor, at least 14 days before tempered-glass production; standard form in Appendix B; RSE quality control supervisor: Grade T3 technically competent person under the RSE’s stream, Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 (2021 edition); supervises at least 30% of the tempered glass panes used in the project; Contractor quality control coordinator: Grade T1 under the contractor’s stream; full-time continuous supervision of the heat-soak process of all tempered panes; measures glass surface temperature with the coordinator’s own data logger at one-minute intervals; record kept in the factory; Videotelephony, if the RSE’s supervisor uses it: Two-way audio and video; colour; resolution not less than 480p; non-rewritable DVD-ROM; assistant in the factory is not a member of the contractor’s supervisory team; videos and log book submitted within 14 days after the heat-soak process for each batch; RSE certifies each disc with a permanent marker; Oven calibration: Clause 9.3.2 of the Glass Code and BS EN 14179-1:2016; reports kept in the factory; Thermocouples: Calibrated at 6-month intervals as recommended in HOKLAS Supplementary Criteria No. 2; Factory thermocouples: A minimum of 8, installed and monitored to clause 9.3.3 of the Glass Code; Clearance between panels: A minimum of 20 mm, for heated-air circulation; End of heating: When the surface temperature of the last glass reaches 250°C; Upper temperature: Surface temperature not allowed to exceed 290°C; time above 270°C minimised; Heating rate: Maximum 3°C per minute; Holding phase: Starts when every pane has reached 250°C; duration at least 120 minutes; surface held at 260°C ± 10°C; Opening the oven: Doors not opened until the control-element reading has dropped to 70°C; Sampling: At least 1%, or 3 numbers, whichever is more, of each representative type; Test load: Clause 16.2.1.2 of the Steel Code 2011; actual dead load present during the test may be taken as the weight of the spider component; remainder of dead load may be taken as the weight of the attachment (for example the glass); Application: Applied and released in at least 5 increments and 5 decrements; Readings: Taken only when deformation is completely stable; at least three readings at 5-minute intervals once the test load is reached, until there is no significant increase; Creep: None for a period of at least 15 minutes under the test load; Residual deformation: On removal of the test load, not exceeding 5% of the maximum deformation recorded; Ultimate design strength: Characteristic strength divided by a factor of safety of 1.8; Proof load: Appendix D; report endorsed by the RSE and submitted before occupation permit or BA14; Sampling: At least 0.1%, or 5 numbers, whichever is more, of each type; setup compatible with the approved fixing details; K: For at least 5 specimens, K may be taken as 3.41; a K-factor for a larger number of specimens may be used if appropriate; One handle: A single handle bar should not be connected to more than 8 locking points; Top-hung sash: Size not exceeding 2.5 m²; Side-hung sash: Width not exceeding 700 mm |
