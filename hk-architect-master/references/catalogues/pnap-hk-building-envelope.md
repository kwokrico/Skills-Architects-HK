## PNAP operative rules for this subject

Distilled from the Practice Notes for Authorized Persons critical summaries. The rule column is the schematic-design takeaway in that summary. Where the summary gives no separate takeaway, the overview or the printed table is used, and the note is listed as thin in the building-codes index.

| PNAP | Subject | Operative rule |

| APP-16 | Cladding | Show all external cladding on the general building plans, and submit structural details whenever any of it is more than 6 m above the adjoining ground or floor. Do not rely on bedding or glue for stone, prove drilled-in anchors at 1.5 times the recommended load, and for exterior limestone show that aged strength is still at least 80% of the standard tests. Printed figures: Structural plans, if any part is above 6 m from the adjoining ground or adjoining floor: Thickness, strength, durability, and type; material of the fixings; layout of the support. Also framing, expansion-joint elevations, anchors and supports, design loads and codes, material specifications, workmanship for welding, corrosion protection, and bi-metallic effects, and calculations checking the parent structure, the cladding system, the elements, and deflection of major members; Metal cladding: General notes, typical layout, and typical details may be included in the superstructure plans instead of a separate structural submission. Location, layout, and elevation remain on the general building plans. See the checklist at Appendix B6 and the sample at Appendix B9 of PNAP ADV-33; Separate structural engineer: Forms BA4 and BA5 with the plans, stating the scope. That engineer certifies completion under regulation 25(3) and submits an assessment of the effect on the parent structure, with a statement signed by the project structural engineer. Cast-in anchorage installed before that appointment is shown on the project structural engineer's superstructure plans and supervised by that engineer; Drilled-in anchors above 6 m: On-site pull-out tests on a representative number of each type and size, directed by the structural engineer or authorized person. Capacity not less than 1.5 times the manufacturer's recommended tensile load, with no separation, plastic deformation, or deleterious effect; Each type of stone: Flexural strength to ASTM C880 or BS EN 12372, or an equivalent, and individual anchorage strength to ASTM C1354, or an equivalent, checked against Appendix B; Limestone used outside: Also aged-strength testing for extreme temperature and moisture change. Residual flexural and anchorage capacities not less than 80% of the standard tests in paragraph 13; Specimens: At least 5, selected at random from batches delivered to site, for each of those tests; Site check: A grade T3 technically competent person in the structural engineer's stream and a grade T1 person in the contractor's stream, to detect clay-like minerals, iron minerals, or veins |
| APP-37 | Curtain Wall, Window and Window Wall | Submit structural plans for a window once an opening exceeds 1.8 m or 6 m2 and the wall is at or above 100 m or 2.86 kPa, and keep a top-hung sash to 2.5 m2 and a side-hung sash to 700 mm wide. Use a heat-soaked tempered glass from an ISO 9001 factory, prove sealant and spider fixings before occupation, and do not hang one handle on more than eight locks. Printed figures: External wall, and a structural opening whose least dimension exceeds 1.8 m or whose area exceeds 6 m2, and either 100 m or more above adjoining ground or a wind reference pressure of 2.86 kPa or more: Submit framing and key details. If the 2004 wind code is used instead, the criterion is the design wind pressure qz. Include horizontal imposed load where the window is a protective barrier, to section 9 and Table 3 of the Schedule, and a deflection check to the glass code; Structural span of a mullion, glass fin, or similar element exceeding 6 m: The same structural information, including a glass shopfront; Locking devices, on structural plans submitted on or after 1 August 2020: Durable and non-combustible, evenly distributed. Ultimate design strength is the characteristic strength divided by a factor of safety of 1.8. Show locations and that strength. One handle bar is not connected to more than 8 locking points; Sash size, in general: A top-hung sash not exceeding 2.5 m2. A side-hung sash not exceeding 700 mm wide; Separate structural engineer: Forms BA4 and BA5, an assessment of the effect on the parent structure signed off by the project structural engineer, and certification under regulation 25(3). Cast-in anchorage installed earlier is on the project superstructure plans; Tempered glass: Factory with ISO 9001 certification. The quality assurance scheme, covering clause 9.1.3 of the glass code, is submitted at least 14 days before production, with the structural engineer's statement. Heat-soak compliance reports, endorsed by the structural engineer, are submitted before the occupation permit or Form BA14; Supervision of heat soak: The conditions imposed on approval include inspection covering at least 30% of the panes. The quality-assurance checklist in the appendix includes a minimum clearance of 20 mm between panels and a limit on time above 270°C; Structural sealant: Compatibility, adhesion, and print-review certificates to the glass code, plus a deglazing test, from the manufacturer and accepted by the structural engineer, before the occupation permit or Form BA14; Spider fixing: Proof-load test to Appendix C after the works, unless that model is already in the Central Data Bank under PNAP ADM-20. The model number, properties, dimensions, and capacities are on the plans. The glass interface allows the anticipated movement; Curtain-wall safety test: Required under section 17(1) item 6, by a laboratory accredited for that test, on a Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme certificate or an equivalent, before the occupation permit or Form BA14. Not required for fast-track repair under PNAP ADM-19 or for minor works item 1.61. Choose representative panes and follow clause 8.3.1 of the glass code |
