## PNAP operative rules for this subject

| APP-31 | Permanent Water Supply to Fire Service Installations | Programme the permanent water connection to the fire service installation before occupation, because a rider on the Fire Services certificate blocks the occupation permit. Printed figures: Supply not required, or already permanently connected: The certificate under Buildings Ordinance section 21(6)(d) is issued in the normal way |
| APP-75 | Means of Access for Firefighting and Rescue in Buildings | Put only fireman's lifts in the firefighting and rescue stairway, run that stair to every floor, and keep the approach to those lifts unobstructed. Printed figures: 41B: Fireman's lifts are designed and located so that, in a fire, firemen have safe and unobstructed access to the lifts and to the floors they serve; 41C: Only fireman's lifts are installed in a firefighting and rescue stairway, and the access stairway in that stairway serves all floors of the building |
| APP-80 | Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction 1996 | Use the 1996 Fire Resisting Construction Code for works whose consent is after 1 August 1996. Printed figures: Consented to commence, or being carried out, on or before 1 August 1996: Not subject to the 1996 Code; Later works: The 1996 Code |
| APP-82 | Code of Practice for Means of Escape 1996 | Use the 1996 means-of-escape code, including Part III for a place of public entertainment from 28 June 1996, unless consent was already given on or before 1 December 1996. Printed figures: Part III (places of public entertainment): Effective on 28 June 1996. PNAP 194 refers; Rest of the revised Code: 1 December 1996; Works being carried out, or consented, on or before 1 December 1996: The earlier edition may be used as the basis for the facilities |
| APP-83 | Amendments to Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction 1996 | For consent on or after 18 August 2011, keep compartment-wall openings to 25% of the wall length if the door is rated for integrity only, and use 25 mm cover for 2-hour solid reinforced-concrete floors and landings. Carry every compartment wall and fire-separation wall down to the structural slab, even where a raised floor is 600 mm or less and itself needs no fire resistance period. Printed figures: Paragraph 7.2, fourth line: Delete "but in no case less than 2 hours"; Table C, page 24: Concrete cover to all reinforcement required to provide 2 hours fire resistance period to continuous floors and landings of solid reinforced concrete construction is 25 mm, not 40 mm; Total width of openings not more than 25% of the length of the compartment walls: Same fire resistance period as the walls for integrity only; Total width more than 25%: Same fire resistance period for integrity and insulation. Insulation may be reduced to 30 minutes if additional sprinkler heads on each side are part of the building sprinkler system, comply with the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment, cover each side in full, and are spaced to the LPC Rules incorporating BS EN 12845:2003; When paragraph 10.1 as revised applies: Consent to commencement on or after 18 August 2011. For a new building that consent is the consent to commence foundation works; for alterations and additions it is the consent to commence those works; Page 32: "fixed light;" is replaced by "fixed light with FRP ≥ ½ FRPe;"; Raised floor not more than 600 mm above the structural floor: Not an element of construction and does not require a fire resistance period |
| APP-85 | Application of the Revised Fire Safety Codes | On an alteration, upgrade only the affected areas and the shared exits to the revised codes, and apply for consent within two years of an approval that predates those codes. |
| APP-87 | Guide to Fire Engineering Approach | Agree the fire engineering approach with the Buildings Department and the Fire Services Department before building plans are submitted, and show equivalence for escape, compartmentation, firefighter access, and structural stability. Write the strategy around the deviations from the codes, with a design fire, smoke-layer and egress calculations, and a named management duty for every compensatory system. |
| APP-92 | Amendments to Code of Practice for Means of Escape 1996 | In a dead-end, keep every point in a room within 18 m, direct distance plus travel distance, of an exit or of a point where two directions of escape become available. Printed figures: From any point in a room to an exit, or to a point from which travel in different directions to two or more exits is available: Not more than 18 m, being the sum of direct distance and travel distance; From that choice point to one of the exits: Not exceeding the direct-distance and travel-distance limitations in paragraph 14 |
| APP-94 | Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Ordinance Cap 502 | If a shop, arcade, or similar use exceeds 230 m2, or the commercial building's plans were first submitted on or before 1 March 1987, design the upgrade to the three 1995–1996 fire codes and allow for sprinklers and a hydrant and hose-reel system. Treat a direction under section 5 as a design brief for the affected owner: the works are those in Schedule 2 or Schedule 5, not a voluntary upgrade. Where the prescriptive construction items cannot be built, offer a fire-engineering alternative, and submit three sets of fire-service plans; a direction-only submission is not charged under Regulation 42. Printed figures: Floor area of prescribed premises: Calculated under sections 3(3) to 3(6). Appendix A illustrates shopping-arcade demarcation; Installations: Automatic sprinkler system; automatic cut-off devices for a mechanical ventilation system; emergency lighting; manual fire alarms; portable fire extinguishers; fire hydrant and hose reel system; and the requirements in the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment 1994; Codes for the construction requirements: Means of escape 1996; fire resisting construction 1996; means of access for firefighting and rescue 1995; Fire safety direction under section 5(1), for prescribed commercial premises: Served on the owner by the Director of Buildings, requiring compliance with Schedule 2; Fire safety improvement direction under section 5(1)(A), for a specified commercial building: Served on the owner, requiring compliance with Schedule 5; After conviction for failure to comply: The Director may apply to a magistrate under section 6(1) for a fire safety compliance order or a fire safety improvement compliance order; Advisory committee: The Advisory Committee for the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance and this Ordinance, established on 1 June 2010, deliberates those alternatives when necessary. Terms of reference are in Appendix A to PNAP APP-145; Fee: No fee under Building (Administration) Regulation 42 if the proposals are entirely for improving fire safety measures pursuant to a fire safety direction or a fire safety improvement direction |
| APP-106 | Fire Resisting Construction - Kitchens in Restaurants | Enclose the restaurant kitchen in 1-hour walls with 1/2-hour doors, and add a protected lobby once the kitchen exceeds 45 m2 and opens onto a dining exit route. A hold-open kitchen door is only acceptable if it meets paragraph 17.1 of the Fire Resisting Construction code. Printed figures: Walls: Fire resistance period of not less than 1 hour; Openings: Defended by a door having a fire resistance period of not less than 1/2 hour; Large kitchen: Also a protected lobby between each kitchen door and the dining area where the kitchen usable floor area exceeds 45 m2 and the door opens onto an exit route of the dining area |
| APP-121 | Amendment to the 1996 Means of Escape Code - Inner Rooms | An inner room without a vision panel needs an alarm that can be heard inside that room. A bedroom that escapes only through the living, dining, or kitchen of a domestic flat is not caught by this inner-room vision-panel rule. |
| APP-122 | Provision of Sky Garden in Refuge Floor | Keep planters and furniture out of the measured refuge area, fix them, and use only non-combustible materials that do not give off toxic gas. If the main roof is the refuge in a conversion, plant not less than 50% of the roof, and expect a drencher above 10 litres per minute per square metre at the openings. A passenger lift may open onto the sky-garden refuge only through a 2-hour lobby with 1-hour doors, and those doors lock when the alarm sounds. Printed figures: Codes: Paragraph 21 of the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire and paragraph 18 of the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction, except the special provisions in this note; Joint Practice Note No. 1: Other criteria applicable to communal sky gardens are complied with; New refuge in a conversion: A communal sky garden is acceptable subject to paragraph 2; Shortfall on paragraphs 21.2 and 21.3 of the means-of-escape code and/or paragraph 18.2 of the fire-resisting-construction code: Acceptable, given the original building, subject to enhanced fire service installations to the satisfaction of the Fire Services Department and suitable greenery in the refuge floor; Main roof as refuge: Designed as a green roof with not less than 50% of the total roof area planted with greenery, and complying with paragraphs 2(b) to (d). Short grass turf suitable for foot traffic can be counted towards the net area for refuge; Example of enhancement: Drencher water flow rate to cover all external wall openings of the refuge floor might have to be greater than 10 litres per minute per square metre. Where a sprinkler system is required, heads shall be fast-response type; Plants: Live plants only for the greenery in paragraph 3. Artificial plants avoided where possible; if used, they and the soil substrate are non-combustible and do not emit toxic gases. Highly flammable live plants (resinous sap, or volatile waxes or oils in leaves, twigs, and stems) used with caution. Larger mature size is encouraged, subject to structural feasibility; Lobby: Lift landing doors open onto a protected lobby with doors of fire resistance period not less than 1 hour and walls not less than 2 hours |
| APP-123 | Alternative Designs for External-Wall Fire Separation | If the facade cannot take a 900 mm spandrel, project a 900 mm horizontal apron instead. A balcony open on at least two sides can omit the 12.3(a) spandrel; the rest of the flat still needs the full fire separation. Printed figures: Code: Spandrel not less than 900 mm in height between the external wall at a floor and the external wall at the floor next below; Alternative: Where 900 mm vertical separation cannot be provided due to design constraints, a horizontal apron with a projected width of 900 mm is accepted |
| APP-145 | Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance Cap 572 | A domestic building of more than 3 storeys, or a composite building, whose plans went in on or before 1 March 1987, is a target building unless it is a New Territories Ordinance building or a hotel, guesthouse, elderly home, child care centre, or nursery. Upgrade both escape, access, and fire-resisting construction to the 1995–1996 codes, and the installations list in paragraph 7(b). Works done only to meet a fire safety direction attract no plan fee under regulation 42, and need three sets of installation plans. Printed figures: Use: Constructed or intended to be used partly for domestic and partly for non-domestic purposes, or for domestic purposes with more than 3 storeys; Plans: Plans of the building works first submitted for approval on or before 1 March 1987, or constructed on or before 1 March 1987 where no plans were submitted on or before that date; Excluded: Buildings constructed under the Buildings Ordinance (Application to the New Territories) Ordinance (Cap. 121); Construction (Schedules 1 and 2): Installations and equipment; Measures to inhibit spread of fire and to ensure structural integrity: Emergency lighting; manual fire alarms; fire hydrant and hose reel; requirements in the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment 1994; Direction: Under section 5(1) or 5(2), the Director of Buildings may serve a fire safety direction to comply with Schedule 1 or 2; Order: After conviction for failure to comply, the Director may apply under section 6(1) for a fire safety compliance order; Advisory committee: Established under section 5(10) to assist on those alternatives. Membership is in Appendix A; Fee: No fee under Building (Administration) Regulation 42 if the proposals are entirely for improving fire safety measures pursuant to a direction |
| APP-153 | Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings 2011 | A new building whose foundation consent is after 1 April 2012 is designed to the 2011 fire safety code as a whole. An alteration is checked only on the affected area and its exit routes, but an occupation permit for a pre-2012 approval still needs the 2011 code before occupation if consent was given under the two-year rule. Printed figures: Works being carried out, or consent already given, on or before 1 April 2012: The 1996 and 2004 codes may continue. For a new building, the relevant consent is the consent to commencement of the foundation works; Plans submitted before 1 April 2012 that elect the 2011 code: The 2011 code must be used in its entirety; Cap. 502 or Cap. 572 improvements: Not this code; Consent for plans approved before 1 April 2012: Generally no objection if the application is within two years of the first approval and is otherwise in order. Amended plans showing compliance with the 2011 code are submitted as necessary before the occupation permit. An occupation permit may be refused under section 21(6) if the works do not comply. Consent may be refused under section 16(3)(d) if the first approval was more than two years earlier |
