## PNRC operative rules

Practice Notes for Registered Contractors. These bind the registered contractor. The authorized person still has to brief and check them, because several notes set hold points the architect relies on (testing, supervision, signboard validation, cease-works).

| PNRC | Subject | Operative rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PNRC-6 | Demolition Works Measures for Public Safety | Demolition consent is not ready until the supervision plan, Form BA20 posting and, for a complex structure, a full-time site engineer are in the brief. The demolition plan shows camera positions, and the consent set includes named, certificated plant operators — a crane-and-hammer method is a public-safety decision, not a contractor detail added later. Seal drainage and set the hoarding line before demolition starts, and hold seven days after completion for the BA14A / BA14B certificate. Pre-demolition strip-out, projection removal and debris openings can ride with the demolition submission on Forms MW01 and MW02 when one project team holds both, which should be decided before the demolition plans are frozen. Printed figures: Continuous supervision: Registered specialist contractor, under Buildings Ordinance section 9(6) and Building (Administration) Regulations regulation 41, in line with the supervision plan and the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans 2009 (Buildings Ordinance section 39A); Lodging the plan: Authorized person lodges it before or with the consent application, except works that meet every criterion in paragraph 11 of that Technical Memorandum; Contractor’s part of the plan: Prepared to the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 (2021 Edition); Personal supervision: Building (Demolition Works) Regulations regulation 8; Form BA20 posted in a prominent position naming the appointed technically competent persons; Demolition Code: Full-time site engineer for complex structures, plus the debris disposal and management system and the other execution rules in the Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings 2004; Fire or explosion: Remove all inflammable goods; store any other flammable goods properly; Building (Demolition Works) Regulations regulation 6; seek departmental help if suspected hazardous materials are found; Drainage: Seal every sewer and drainage connection before demolition starts (regulation 3(2)(b) of the Building (Demolition Works) Regulations); Completion: Within 7 days, the contractor certifies on Form BA14A or Form BA14B; consult the authorized person and registered structural engineer on the fast-track acceptance route before submitting Form BA14B; Commencement: Form MW01 not less than 7 days before the minor works, submitted with the demolition-plan approval application by the same team; Completion: Form MW02 with Form BA14A / BA14B; photographs of the completed minor works are not required if the whole parent building is then demolished; Minor works finished before Form BA10: Submit Form MW02 separately, with the Minor Works Control System documents, to New Buildings Division 1 |
| PNRC-22 | Ban on Hand-dug Caissons | Foundation concepts should assume machine methods; a hand-dug shaft is an exemption case, and the dimensional route is only a shaft not deeper than 3 m whose inscribed circle is at least 1.5 m. From 1 February 1996, a hand-dug caisson does not enter an approval set unless the section 16(1A) case is made personally to the Building Authority, with a safety method statement. Printed figures: (a) Size limit: Depth does not exceed 3 metres, and the diameter of the inscribed circle is not less than 1.5 metres |
| PNRC-26 | Plastic Sheet on Scaffolding | Façade and demolition scaffolds are drawn with screens, fans or catch platforms as the protective system, whether or not a sheet is added. An occupied renovation that needs a sheeted scaffold must keep window ventilation and daylight in the phasing, and the sheet itself is specified flame-retardant. Printed figures: Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations (Cap. 59): Regulation 49(1); Building (Demolition Works) Regulations (Cap. 123): Regulation 3(2)(a); Summary Offences Ordinance (Cap. 228): Section 4B(2) |
| PNRC-31 | Monitoring for Site Safety and Quality | Demolition, deep excavation, percussion piling, steep slopes, high retaining walls, projections over streets and heavy plant are the operations that draw more frequent Site Monitoring Section visits, so those sequences need precautionary measures on the drawings. The site set for both full-consent works and minor works includes the plans, the agreed sequence and the supervision plan, because an inspector can ask for them on arrival. |
| PNRC-64 | Site Safety Enhancement | A contract at or above $100 million, or a site that will field 100 or more workers, carries a six-monthly safety audit; that cost and attendance belong in the procurement assumption. Critical stages on the approved plans should be written so they can be dropped straight into the contractor’s inspection programme and method statements. Printed figures: Contract value of $100 million or more, or an aggregate of 100 or more workers in a day on one or more construction sites: The 8 elements in Part 1 and the 2 elements in Part 2 of Schedule 4, and a safety audit every 6 months; An aggregate of 50 or more but less than 100 workers in a day on a single site, or working on 2 or more construction sites: The 8 elements in Part 1 of Schedule 4, and a safety review every 6 months; All contractors: The 4 elements in Part 3 of Schedule 4 were not in operation when this note was issued; Training: Authorized signatory, representative, and technically competent persons T1 to T3 attend safety training; safety officers and safety supervisors attend technical training in building construction (Construction Industry Training Authority, Institutes of Vocational Education and professional bodies) |
| PNRC-78 | Pay for Safety Scheme | When the client’s procurement route is set, site safety — supervision, precautionary measures and training — is a separate priced section of the bills, not a lump absorbed in the preliminaries. |
| PNRC-83 | Demolition of Buildings Code | Demolition drawings and the contractor’s method are prepared to the 2004 code plus the 2016 and 2023 amendments, not to the 2004 text alone. Type G minor-works demolition stays on the Minor Works Control System guidelines; full demolition with a slope, retaining wall or street projection brings the registered geotechnical engineer onto the safety design. Street frontage during demolition needs a tied scaffold (4 m anchors, 15 m brackets above 15 m), a double net where debris may be large, a fire-retardant tarpaulin, and catch platforms under every projection over the street before demolition starts. A building designed to the LCC design codes of 1952, and any cantilever over a street, needs a propping and platform strategy, with the 7.5 mm/s limit used where mechanical demolition will cause prolonged vibration at a neighbour. The demolition programme includes a debris-management system and a waste plan approved before start, plus a daily clear of catch platforms, because debris left on a floor or against a remaining wall is an overload case. Printed figures: Ties: Structural ties and struts to the building; bamboo tied to sound anchors at intervals of not more than 4 m horizontally and vertically; ties and struts to the cantilevered portion above the topmost floor, and again above the floor being demolished; Tall scaffold: If higher than 15 m, steel brackets or another support at intervals of not more than 15 m; Striking: Scaffold comes down with the demolition; when ties and struts are released, the unsecured length is removed; unbraced sections not higher than 2 m from the nearest anchor; Polyethylene net: Minimum specification in Table 3.3, or an approved equivalent: polyethylene, string diameter 1 mm, 16 ply, mesh opening 20 mm, weight 2130 g/m²; Extra net: Where appropriate, a further heavy-duty nylon net of minimum 3.5 mm diameter and mesh opening 50 mm maximum, in addition to the tarpaulin and the polyethylene net, to catch larger falling debris; Tarpaulin: Lightweight and fire-retardant: Type B performance in BS 5867, or the flame-retardant test for lightweight cloths in the Japan Ministerial Ordinance on protective canvas for construction, or an equivalent; Catchfan: An extra bamboo layer with ties is recommended on top of the 0.5 mm metal sheet so the sheet is not ripped off in strong wind or typhoon; through-bolts as well as anchor bolts are noted as industry practice for fixing catchfan supports; Catch platform: On the covered walkway to clause 3.2, and also under elements that can shed debris, including canopies and balconies and in particular any projection over a street, and where the process requires it under architectural features and prestressed elements; installed before demolition starts and designed for the anticipated load; Propping differing from Table 3.4: Full structural substantiation by the registered structural engineer; LCC-era buildings: Special attention where the building was designed to the LCC design codes of 1952, with lower permissible material stress; clause 3.5.3(C)(4) allows slab capacity to be increased by spreading load through sleepers and base plates; Vibration: Registered specialist contractor (demolition) is advised to monitor; peak particle velocity at any adjoining structure should not exceed 15 mm/s for transient vibration and 7.5 mm/s for prolonged and continuous vibration from mechanical demolition (aligned with PNAP APP-137); Cantilevered structures: The last page, on Appendix G Figure G.5, requires a temporary platform unless the cantilever is demolished by cut-and-lift or a similar technique under paragraph 3.5.1(B) |
| PNRC-85 | Fire Retardant Scaffolding Materials | Façade protection is specified to one of the three named standards, with GB 5725-2025 written in for any net that will still be on the scaffold on or after 1 September 2026. A repair scaffold of three or more storeys on an existing building, or a full-height scaffold in a re-entrant, is in the sampling regime; a single-unit truss-out repair is not. Protective sheet and net are a tested lot, with at least a 1.8 m by 2 m sample and a video of the sampling, before they are relied on as the fire-retardant layer. Budget a seven-day notice when the full scaffold is sheeted, a retest at least every 12 months, and replacement of a whole elevation if one audit sample on that elevation fails. Printed figures: GB 5725-2009 “Safety nets”: Protective nets. GB 5725-2025 “Fall protection — Safety nets” takes effect on 1 September 2026 and supersedes GB 5725-2009; BS 5867-2:2008 Type B: Protective nets, screens, tarpaulins and plastic sheeting; NFPA 701:2023 Test Method 2: Protective nets, screens, tarpaulins and plastic sheeting; Building under construction or demolition: The paragraph 6 sampling rules apply. The contractor may install first and then sample, or sample before installation; 2 to 15: 2; 16 to 25: 3; 26 to 90: 5; 91 to 150: 8; 151 to 280: 13; 281 to 500: 20; 501 to 1 200: 32; 1 201 to 3 200: 50; 3 201 to 10 000: 80 |
