| ADV-10 | Lift Shaft Platforms | Do not treat a lift-shaft platform as a site carpenter's deck; it needs a contractor's AP or RSE design, a T4 method statement, and full-time T1 supervision before anyone works on it. If part of the building is occupied under a temporary occupation permit, keep the fire separation in place while lift-shaft platforms are still in the shafts. Printed figures: Method: Construction plans and method statements for erection, alteration, maintenance, and dismantling, prepared by the contractor's engineer of TCP T4 or above, and checked and signed off by the contractor's AP or RSE; Erection: In accordance with the construction plan, under the full-time immediate supervision of a competent person with TCP T1, appointed in writing by the contractor. On completion, checked by the contractor's engineer; Risk assessment: Before erection, maintenance, or dismantling, by a planning team from the main contractor and subcontractors with at least one person at TCP T4 or equivalent, including a thorough inspection of stability by the TCP T1 person |
| ADV-26 | Ventilation of Common Corridors and Lift Lobbies in Buildings | At schematic design, give common corridors and entrance and lift lobbies a natural-vent path or a mechanical system sized to the anticipated population, with intake and exhaust that cannot short-circuit. |
| ADV-28 | Provision of Sanitary Fitments in Offices, Shopping Arcades, Department Stores, Places of Public Entertainment, Cinemas and Other Public Places | For a shopping floor at basement, ground, first, or second floor, assess population at 3 m² of usable floor area per person, then split that population 1 male to 1.5 female. Size the female water closets on the 1-per-25 then 1-per-80 scale, which is much tighter than the male water-closet scale of 1 per 125. A cinema and a place of public entertainment do not share a table: entertainment uses 1 male water closet per 100 and 2 female water closets per 50, while a cinema uses stepped male numbers and 1 female water closet per 40 at the low end. Draw each water-closet cubicle at not less than 700 mm by 1,200 mm with 700 mm by 600 mm clear in front, and do not count a child’s water closet toward the prescribed number. Printed figures: Shopping arcade or department store: basement, G/F, 1/F, and 2/F: 3 m² of usable floor area per person. Usable floor area includes the arcade and its public circulation; Shopping arcade or department store: 3/F or above: 4.5 m² per person; Assembly halls, auditoria, and stadia without seating or with movable seating: 0.5 m² per person on the areas intended for spectators; Dance halls, on the dancing area, disco, and venues for a dancing party: 0.75 m² per person; Museums, exhibition halls, trademarts, and display areas: 2 m² per person; Religious institutions and funeral parlours: 0.5 m² per person on the worship places; Office: Section 5(5)(a) of the Regulations; 1–50: 1 for every 25 persons, or part; 51–125: 3; 126–1,800: 3 plus 1 for every additional 80 persons, or part, over 125; More than 1,800: 24 plus 1 for every additional 90 persons, or part, over 1,800; Place of public entertainment: Water closets: 1 per 100 up to 400; above 400, 4 plus 1 per additional 250 over 400. Urinals: 1 per 50. Basins: 1 per 100; Cinema: Water closets: 1 up to 200; 2 for 201–500; 3 for 501–1,000; above 1,000, 3 plus 1 per additional 500 over 1,000. Urinals: 1 per 100. Basins follow the same bands as the male water closets; Sports stadium: Water closets 1 per 100; urinals 1 per 100; basins 1 per 100; Religious institution or funeral parlour: Water closets 1 per 150; urinals 1 per 150; basins 1 per 300; Children: Design some urinals, basins, and water closets for children’s size and height in shopping arcades, department stores, and other public places. A child’s water closet is not counted toward the prescribed numbers under sections 4 to 8 of the Regulations; Water-closet cubicle, including a squatting toilet: Internal dimensions not less than 700 mm by 1,200 mm. Clear floor space in front of the water closet, inside the cubicle, not less than 700 mm by 600 mm. Circulation space outside the cubicle not less than 700 mm by 600 mm; Urinal: Clear floor space not less than 500 mm by 500 mm. Centreline at least 250 mm from a side wall. Where more than one, at least 500 mm centre to centre. A urinal in a cubicle: internal dimensions not less than 1,000 mm by 500 mm, with 500 mm by 500 mm clear in front, and circulation outside of not less than 500 mm by 600 mm. Every 500 mm of a trough urinal counts as one urinal; Lavatory basin: Clear floor space not less than 600 mm by 600 mm. Centreline at least 400 mm from a side wall. Basins at least 600 mm centre to centre. Circulation space in front of the clear floor space not less than 600 mm by 600 mm; Persons with a disability: Space and fitments follow the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008. The dimensions above are for other users. Designs above these minima are encouraged |
| ADV-30 | Provision of Mechanical Ventilation under Building (Planning) Regulation 34 | An office that cannot open onto regulation 31 light and air needs a mechanical supply of at least 5 air changes per hour to every part of the room. Printed figures: Trigger: An office room that cannot comply with regulation 31 because of position, level, or unsuitable surroundings; Rate: Not less than 5 changes of air per hour, to all parts of the room |
| APP-4 | Water Supply and Wells | Size the flushing store to 450 litres per required soil fitment per day for domestic and ordinary non-domestic buildings, and use the lower seat or room rates only for restaurants, cinemas, schools, and hotels. Do not rely on a well in Scheduled Areas No. 2 or No. 4, and elsewhere prove yield with a seven-day test between 1 December and 30 April at not more than 12 hours of pumping a day. Printed figures: Domestic buildings: 450 litres per required soil fitment per day; Offices, factories, department stores, shops, public buildings, and other non-domestic buildings of a like nature: 450 litres per required soil fitment per day; Restaurants: 13.5 litres per seat per day; Cinemas: 4.5 litres per seat per day; Schools: 18 litres per head per day; Hotels and boarding houses: 90 litres per room per day; Where: Normally not on government land. On private land, Building (Construction) Regulation 85 permission only if yield is adequate and abstraction will not adversely affect nearby services, buildings, structures, or land; Areas to avoid: Yuen Long Town in Scheduled Area No. 2 and Ma On Shan in Scheduled Area No. 4. Flushing water will normally be supplied there whenever possible, because groundwater abstraction is likely to cause excessive settlement; Yield test, pumping under 12 hours a day: Appendix A, for Regulation 10A(7). Only from 1 December to 30 April. Seven consecutive days. Days 1 to 6 at the proposed daily yield averaged over the proposed pumping period, not more than 12 hours a day so that at least 12 hours of recovery remain. Quantity by meter, not by pump rating. Day 7 at full discharge and maximum drawdown, examined for suspended soil particles. Discharge to a proper drain or watercourse, not back into the well or aquifer; Acceptable result: Daily quantity not less than the required daily quantity, adequate recovery after each day's pumping, and clean water on the day-7 examination; Longer pumping or doubt about long-term yield: A more sophisticated test may be required. Other methods in BS 6316:1983 may be approved |
| APP-8 | Chimneys and Flues | Design the steel chimney to Steel Code Clause 13.2, including wind oscillation, and detail a flashing or weather hood wherever the flue passes through a wall or roof. Show chimneys and flues on the general building plans so they are referred to the Environmental Protection Department before approval. Printed figures: Insulation: Depends on fuel type and rate, materials of nearby parts, and use of surrounding areas. Provide calculations and justification. BS 5854:1980 gives guidelines; Steel chimney: Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Steel 2011, in particular Clause 13.2. Analyse wind-excited oscillations by aerodynamic methods and provide control. Circular chimneys may use the simplified method in Clause 13.2.8; Corrosion and bimetallic action: Clause 13.2.7 of the Steel Code. Protective treatments on interior and exterior surfaces follow the manufacturer's instructions and Annex A of the Steel Code; Construction: Relevant parts of the Steel Code, BS EN 1856-1:2009, and BS 5854:1980; Plans: Under PNAP ADM-2, general building plans that include chimneys or flues are referred to the Environmental Protection Department |
| APP-10 | Oil Storage Installations | Treat any above-ground petroleum tank of 110,000 litres or more as an oil storage installation: obtain approval and consent, a licence, and an internal inspection by year 10 and then every fifth year after that first inspection. Do not start demolition until a contamination assessment and an agreed method are in place, and keep the bund and the oil interceptors intact until the tanks and sludge are gone. Printed figures: Annual renewal: Certificates of external inspection of the tanks under Regulation 8(1)(b) and of inspection of the associated works under Regulation 8(1)(c). The associated-works certificate is for a further twelve months, or states that the works are unfit until listed repairs are completed. Copy the Director of Fire Services; Repair or alteration: Two copies of proposals signed by a registered structural engineer, for written authorization under Regulation 10. Extensive repair, or alteration that is non-exempted building works, also needs section 14(1) approval |
| APP-29 | Lift and Escalator Installations — Building Works Requirements | Vent each well at not less than 1% of its plan area and 0.3 m2, separate adjacent lifts for the full height of the well, and give a pit deeper than 2 m its own access door to a stair. Reach a machine or pulley room by a stair at least 900 mm wide once the level change exceeds 0.6 m, and provide a rated lifting point in both the machine room and the well soffit. Printed figures: Vent at the top of a well: At least 1% of the horizontal cross-sectional area of the well, to the open air directly or through ducting or the machine or pulley room. Not less than 0.3 m2 net free area for each well. A common well with four, five, or six lifts: not less than 0.4 m2, 0.5 m2, or 0.6 m2 respectively. Ferrules over openings above the well project at least 50 mm above the slab or finished floor; Partition between moving parts of different lifts: Adequate strength, for example a solid block wall or wire mesh, from the pit floor through the full height of the well and across its whole depth. If each lift is separated by a wall, each well is ventilated to paragraph 3.3.2. The earlier rule that the partition need only rise 2.5 m, and need be full height only if the car-roof edge is within 300 mm of an adjacent moving part, is replaced by this full-height partition; Pit access door: If the pit is deeper than 2 m, an imperforate door leading to a stair, not opening into the well, with a key-operated lock that can be reclosed without a key and opened from inside without a key. If the pit is deeper than 1.6 m but not more than 2 m, the same door is provided if the layout permits; Fall-arrest anchor: At the lowest landing, adjacent to the landing door, between 1.5 m and 1.8 m above finished floor level, unless a pit access door meeting paragraph 3.8.3 is provided; Inspection and emergency doors (Appendix A): Dual key-operated locks needing two designated keys together to open from outside, openable from inside without a key, and a figure-type warning not less than 100 mm high. A single designated key is acceptable for landing doors used as those doors, or where the sill is 1 m or more above the adjoining floor; Level difference greater than 0.6 m: Access and egress by stairs, not a ladder; Those stairs: Width not less than 900 mm. Treads not less than 225 mm, with an anti-slip surface, and risers not exceeding 175 mm. Not more than 16 steps in a flight without a landing. Handrails on both sides. Protective barriers at the outer edges not less than 1.1 m high; Ladder, only if the difference does not exceed 0.6 m: Permanently fixed, with a platform, railings, and handholds at the top; Notices: Incised or embossed, in English and Chinese, in durable material: not less than 25 mm high on well doors and machine-room doors, and not less than 15 mm high at landings |
| APP-58 | Testing of Drainage Works | Keep drainage on the approved alignment and inverts, or amend the plans before the test, and file the Annex I certificate within 7 days certifying BS 8301:1985. Printed figures: Projects with many tests: Developments requiring more than 100 drain tests are not uncommon; fragmented testing is to be managed by the authorized person attending and certifying; Certificate: Confirms the test was witnessed, encloses endorsed test results and record plans under Building (Administration) Regulation 44, and certifies compliance with BS 8301:1985 and satisfaction with the results. Copy the registered contractor |
| APP-62 | Protection of Sewage and Drainage Tunnels | Keep piles, anchors, wells, and excavations more than 3 m from a sewage tunnel, hold pressure change to 20 kPa in soil or 50 kPa hydrostatic in rock, and do not let calculated movement exceed 20 mm. Printed figures: Pressure change on a structure in soil: Not more than 20 kPa, or 5% of total overburden pressure where the structure is deeper than 20 m, from filling, dewatering, and foundation loads including construction loads; Pressure change on a structure in rock, where ground-pressure change cannot be assessed: Hydrostatic pressure not increased or decreased by more than 50 kPa; Movement: Calculated final diametric distortion not exceeding 0.1% of the internal diameter, and calculated total movement in any plane not exceeding 20 mm; Vibration: Peak particle velocity not exceeding 25 mm/s from blasting, and 15 mm/s from pile driving or withdrawal or any prolonged vibration. The same limits apply to ground investigation shocks and percussion or hammer drilling; Exclusion: No hole or excavation, and no pile, foundation, well, soil nail, horizontal drain, or rock bolt or dowel, within 3 m of any point of a sewage tunnel structure, and no ground anchor within 3 m, without prior approval of the works and the method; Within 10 m: Detailed method statements for construction and for monitoring alignment and depth, before works commence. Monitoring data and interpretation are submitted for the duration of the works; Dewatering and wells within 100 m: Assess leakage of sewage and migration of gas, and submit a monitoring programme; Shaft extent: External diameter in soil; internal diameter plus 5 m in rock. The Ap Lei Chau to Aberdeen section is under soil or shallow rock at both ends; elsewhere cover is generally at least 30 m of rock; Ground investigation in Area No. 5: Submit hole locations relative to the tunnels, depths, and method statements. If a hole is less than 10 m from a tunnel, also a method for checking alignment and depth. No hole within 3 m |
| APP-89 | Provision of Better Lift Service | Size domestic or hotel lift shafts so that the area above 2.5% of that gross floor area, and not more than 3.5%, can be disregarded, and do not count refuge-floor shafts in the concession. Do not claim the concession unless every domestic car is at least 1.82 m2 inside a 4.12 m2 shaft, or every office car is at least 2.1 m2 inside a 4.4 m2 shaft, with the engineer's certificate. Printed figures: Domestic or composite building, including a hotel: Shaft area over and above 2.5% of total domestic or hotel gross floor area, but the exempted area is in no case more than 3.5% of that gross floor area. If the shafts exceed 6%, the maximum exemption is 3.5%; Office, or office/commercial, with office-floor gross floor area less than 10,000 m2: Area over and above 5% of office gross floor area, capped at 3%. If the shafts exceed 8%, the maximum exemption is 3%; Office, or office/commercial, with office-floor gross floor area of 10,000 m2 or more: Area over and above 3.5%, capped at 2.5%. If the shafts exceed 6%, the maximum exemption is 2.5%. Between 10,000 m2 and 12,000 m2, a maximum exempted area of 300 m2 may be allowed; Domestic or composite building, each lift car: 1.82 m2; Domestic or composite building, each lift shaft: 4.12 m2; Office accommodation, each lift car: 2.1 m2; Office accommodation, each lift shaft: 4.4 m2; Certificate: A registered lift engineer under the Lifts and Escalators Ordinance, Cap. 618, or a registered professional engineer in the building-services discipline under the Engineers Registration Ordinance, Cap. 409 |
| APP-91 | Maintenance and Replacement Works of Lift Installations | Keep lift landing doors at not less than 1 hour integrity and normally closed, and if more than one is removed, hoard the openings to 1 hour without narrowing an escape route. Printed figures: Openings in the hoarding: None, except small ventilation openings and access doors. Each ventilation opening not exceeding 5,500 mm2, at the upper portion, limited to two per liftway and a maximum of four in the hoarding |
| APP-93 | Planning and Design of Drainage Works | Put common stacks in a duct with 700 mm clear working space, or in a well at least 1,200 mm by 1,500 mm with access every 21 storeys, and do not run one flat's pipes into the flat below. Keep a bath or shower waste to 750 mm at 1:40 if it must reach a trap without entering the flat below, and leave a sunken-slab void open or give every pipe an access point at floor level. Printed figures: Pipe duct: Access from the common parts. Unobstructed working space not less than 700 mm in front of the pipes. Access door or panel not less than 600 mm wide by 2,000 mm high, to Part C of the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings 2011; Pipe well: Plan size not less than 1,200 mm by 1,500 mm. No opening except inspection and maintenance access from the common parts, at not more than 21 storeys apart. Cat ladder with guard rings for the full height. Grating platforms at not more than 4 storeys apart. Each access opening not less than 600 mm by 2,000 mm, to Part C. Ventilation opening at top and at bottom, each not less than one-tenth of the horizontal area of the well; Bends: Only where a change of direction is unavoidable. At the bottom of a stack, radius not less than four times the radius of the stack measured to the centreline where practicable, and in no case less than 200 mm to the centreline; Bathtub or shower to its trap: A modification of regulation 25(1) may allow a total length up to 750 mm at a minimum gradient of 1:40, so that pipes need not enter the unit below; High-rise: Consider separate pipes in zones at design stage. See PNAP ADV-14 for maintenance of external pipes |
| APP-133 | Cast Iron Pipes for Drainage Works | Specify coated cast iron to the stated British Standards, and anchor stainless-steel or equivalent brackets into solid wall. List the cast iron drainage pipes on the materials schedule when applying for the occupation permit. Printed figures: Standards: Relevant British Standards or equivalent. Examples: BS 416:Part 1 or BS EN 877 for above-ground drains; BS 437 or BS 4622 for underground systems and above-ground drains larger than 150 mm diameter; Coating: Suitably coated internally and externally. Cold-applied bitumen-based coating to comply with BS 3416 or equivalent. External coating should contain anti-corrosion inhibitors normally used on metallic structures |
| APP-164 | Enhanced Design Standards of Aboveground Drainage System | Feed the kitchen, toilet, and pantry floor-drain trap from a basin, bath, or shower, and vent the stack at least every 5 storeys up to 20 storeys and every 3 storeys above that. Keep branch connections 2 500 mm clear of an offset bend, and take the lowest floor, plus the floor above on a building over 20 storeys, off the stack. Terminate the vent at least 3 m from a boundary, window, or fresh-air intake, and size a rainwater pipe of 100 mm or less to the roof area in paragraph 7. Printed figures: Kitchen, toilet, or pantry floor drain: Used water from a lavatory basin, bath, or shower is diverted to replenish the trap seal, without backflow. The trap complies with regulation 25; Connection slope and size: Connecting pipes slope upwards at not more than 67.5 degrees from the stack, internal diameter not less than the main anti-syphonage pipe; Spacing, more than 5 and not more than 20 storeys: Connections at intervals of not more than 5 storeys, from the lowest storey with a branch discharge pipe on the stack; Spacing, over 20 storeys: Intervals of not more than 3 storeys; No connection in the restricted zone: Up to 600 mm above the bend for a building of not more than 5 storeys, and 1 000 mm above the bend for a building over 5 storeys, measured from the invert of the tail of the bend, and within 2 500 mm horizontally from the bend; Not applied to: The separate pipework required by paragraphs 2(d) and 2(e); More than 3 and not more than 20 storeys: Sanitary fitments on the floor where the stack discharges to a manhole are not connected to the stack. Separate pipework discharges directly to a manhole; Over 20 storeys: Fitments on that floor and the floor above are not connected to the stack. The pipework for those two floors can be combined if the drainage regulations and PNAP APP-93 and APP-164 are met; Open end of a ventilating pipe on the roof: Not less than 3 m from a common boundary with an adjacent site, and from any window, opening, or fresh-air intake; Accessible roofs: Advisable to carry ventilation pipes not less than 3 m above the accessible roof, in addition to paragraph 2(f); Rainwater pipes of internal diameter not more than 100 mm: 40 mm serves 9 m2; 65 mm serves 33 m2; 80 mm serves 58 m2; 100 mm serves 106 m2 of horizontal roofed-over surface; Existing repair: Advise clients to consider these requirements, including replacing an open hopper-head system with a closed system and traps under regulations 12(2), 12(3), and 25; Regulation 30(2)(b)(ii): The Building Authority is prepared to allow the anti-syphonage pipe to connect to the branch soil or waste pipe at not more than 750 mm from the trap outlet |
