## 8. FSD circular letters

Every Fire Services Department circular letter in the critical-summary library. Section 7 states the Cap. 502 / 572 / 636 upgrade path. This table is the full set, including submission procedure, sprinklers, detection, construction-site fire precautions, and letters that repeal earlier letters. Where a later letter is named in the takeaway, follow the later letter.

| Circular | Operative rule |
| --- | --- |
| CL 1-1997 Fire Services Requirements for Refuge Floors | Lock refuge floor level(s) and façade openings early — drencher coverage + independent tank/pump are massing and plant-room drivers. Do not rely on the 1/97 “external sprinkler in lieu” option; see 7/97. |
| CL 1-1998 Certification of FSI under Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) (Amendment) Ordinance 1998 | Test a commercial unit against both the use-plus-230 m² prescribed-premises test and the pre-23 March 1973 specified-commercial-building test before locking the FSI and construction scope. For a premises or building inside either test, allow at schematic design for sprinklers, mechanical-ventilation cut-off, emergency lighting, hydrant and hose reel, manual fire alarms, portable extinguishers, means of escape, firefighting access, and fire and structural integrity, because a direction may require all or any of them. Issue FSI plans for these premises and buildings for certification on FSI/314B (Revised 5/98), against the Ordinance and the sprinkler rules and minimum-FSI code named on that form. |
| CL 1-2000 Flammability Standards for PU Foam Mattresses and Upholstered Furniture in Licensed Premises and Public Areas | In licensed premises and public areas, specify PU foam mattresses to BS 7177:1996 medium hazard and PU foam upholstered furniture to BS 7176:1995 medium hazard, including loose items. The circular’s specification line is the medium-hazard column; do not substitute the domestic or low-hazard column for licensed premises or public areas. Loose and fixed PU foam mattresses and upholstered furniture in the fit-out need the medium-hazard label, the invoice, and a sealed accredited test certificate, not a catalogue claim alone. |
| CL 1-2001 Application of Ceiling Flush Recessed and Concealed Sprinklers | Pendant-only ceiling rules no longer apply for approved aesthetic heads — but only to stated hazard groups and with FSD-listed products + manufacturer installation rules. Confirm FRC compliance on building plans early — it directly affects sprinkler hazard grading and cost on full-glazed towers. |
| CL 1-2002 Rules for Fire Detection and Alarm Systems for Buildings | Superseded — use CL 6/2021. |
| CL 1-2004 Checklist for the Inspection of Fire Detection and Alarm Systems | Cap each detection zone at 2,000 m² and each loop at 10,000 m² of detector-covered floor, and keep zones to one storey once the building exceeds 300 m². Place a call point at every hose reel and every storey exit and open-air exit so the walking distance is within 30 m, at about 1.2 m above floor. Design alarm sound to 60 dB(A) in domestic buildings and 65 dB(A) elsewhere, each also 5 dB(A) above persistent background, measured 3 m inside the main entrance with doors shut. Provide a red flashing visual alarm in every compartment that the approved plans mark for visual fire alarm, on one of the three listed supplies. Reserve a ground-floor panel at the FSD entrance, or a fire control room, and do not let any time delay hold a manual call point or a sprinkler flow switch. Size the alarm battery for 24 hours plus 30 minutes of all-zone evacuation, or 6 hours plus 30 minutes where a standby generator backs the system. Route alarm, sounder and visual-alarm cabling as a 30-minute fire-resisting system, separated from other services by a barrier, BS 7629 or MICS, or at least 300 mm. |
| CL 1-2008 Inspection Checklist for Street Fire Hydrant System | Keep 1.5 m clear in front and both sides of each street hydrant, and put the control-valve pit 1.5–3 m away, outside the emergency vehicular access. If the street-hydrant tank bottom sits more than 20 m above the lowest hydrant outlet, add a full-size bypass around the pump set, and keep the pump room off exit routes. Size the street hydrant supply so one 65 mm outlet can deliver at least 2,000 L/min at not less than 170 kPa while a second 65 mm outlet is also open. |
| CL 1-2009 Rules for Fire Detection and Fire Alarm Systems for Buildings | Superseded — use CL 6/2021 for detection/alarm design standards. |
| CL 1-2010 Emergency Exit Devices | On MOE doors specify EED from the current FSD accepted list + relevant product standards; never cite CL 4/96 Part III product models for new work. Coordinate panic hardware with MOE Code door furniture rules and fire-resisting door schedules. |
| CL 1-2011 Delisting of Fire Extinguishers Containing Scheduled Substances | From 1 April 2012 specify only a still-approved substitute, and check its maximum protected volume against the aggregate volume of the space. On an existing halon installation, keep cylinders outside the protected room where possible, and put a manual release outside if the cylinders stay inside. For a new automatic total-flooding system specify FM200, Inergen, CEA-410 or HFC-23 to NFPA 2001, and use a fixed sprayer as the CO2 or BTM alternative only where the aggregate volume is 42.5 m³ or less. |
| CL 1-2014 Use of Pre-insulated Panels for Construction of Air Ducts in Mechanical Ventilating Systems | Pre-insulated duct is a niche single-compartment FCU distribution solution — flag early if duct must cross compartments or serve kitchen/grease extract; default to metal ductwork. |
| CL 1-2017 Automatic Actuation Devices for Lifts Not Discharging to Protected Means of Escape | Direct-landing residential / office lifts without lobby = either sprinkler the building or provide AAD + management annunciation — or redesign to protected discharge. At SD, schedule every lift discharge condition floor-by-floor. Lobby-free lift landings in non-sprinkler towers are a hard FSI + EMSD coordination item. |
| CL 1-2019 Ventilation Air Conditioning VAC Control System | Cross-check kitchen MVAC schematics against updated Annex cases before FSI submission; retire Case 12/4 details. At SD, tabulate every PAU/EAF/FCU capacity against 1,000 l/s thresholds and assign Case numbers from the Annex — V/AC control is a submission deliverable, not a note on the MEP drawing margin. |
| CL 1-2020 Revised Application Procedure for Inspection and Testing of FSI in New Buildings | Programme multiple FSI/501a (by trade package) before AP signs FSI/501 — gaps block FS 172. Engage RPE early for SES/SPS/water mist — AP Part B alone insufficient. Include Class 3 portable-equipment FSI/501a in handover document list — not FS 172 blocker if missing from AP application. Build acceptance dossier tracker (501a refs ↔ systems ↔ RPE reports) at SD — mirrors FSD Practical Guide Part III irregularities list. Supersedes single-signature FSI/501 under CL 4/2008 for new buildings — retain 4/2008 plan-chop practice but update acceptance forms to 2020 split. |
| CL 1-2021 Shutdown of Fire Service Installations for Maintenance Inspection Modification or Repair | In sleeping-risk premises, keep sprinkler isolation independent of both the hose-reel system and the fire detection system so one shutdown does not take those systems offline together. Provide sectional isolation and separate duty and standby pumps so a repair can leave one pump and the unaffected zones in service, and treat a shutdown longer than 14 days as limited to improvement works under Cap. 502, Cap. 572 or Cap. 636. Reserve prominent wall space for an A4 shutdown notice, and allow temporary extinguishers and standalone detectors in sleeping-risk, care, child-care, hotel, place-of-public-entertainment and dangerous-goods areas while an FSI is off. |
| CL 1-2022 Clean Agent Fire Extinguishing Systems Enclosure Integrity | Treat protected rooms as sealed controlled volumes — coordinate cable penetrations, raised floor, demountable partitions with hold-time testing; tenants cannot add openings without re-test and FSD notification path. |
| CL 1-2023 Sprinkler System for Car Ports | Size car parks early — 230 m² is a hard sprinkler + tank/pump driver. Include access stairs within sprinkler extent. Voluntary early compliance encouraged. |
| CL 1-2024 Approval of Portable Equipment and Acceptance of FSIs and Fire Safety Products | Specify accepted types for the components in Appendix II, and treat optical-fibre fire cables as a project-specific acceptance, not a catalogue type. Emergency lighting, exit signs, smoke-stop doors and ordinary sprinkler or hose-reel pipework are specified to the codes above; they are not held for a separate FSD product-acceptance letter. A product certification listing can clear acceptance for non-portable equipment, but the specification must still match current local codes at plan submission, and the listing must still be valid. |
| CL 1-2025 Obsolete FSD Circular Letters | Do not detail a roof fire-service pump to the flat-roof spreader in Circular Letter No. 2/2004 or to No. 1/2012; use No. 6/2020. New railway fire-safety guidelines are No. 3/2016, not Nos. 1/2013 or 3/2013. |
| CL 1-2026 Enhanced Measures on Transparency of Defect and Power Supply Management of FSIs | Keep a clear 15 cm × 8 cm face at each of those FSI positions, and on both the reel and the door where the hose reel is concealed. The FSI main switch needs a clear face for a 30 cm × 8 cm seal, and that supply is not a general isolator switched off in normal operation. Draw the Appendix I notice and Appendix II seal into the FSI and switch locations; other formats are accepted only until 28 February 2026. |
| CL 2-1994 Rules for Automatic Sprinkler Installations | Superseded — use current sprinkler circular / CoP. |
| CL 2-1997 Discharge Tests for Total Gas Flooding System | A clean-agent layout can omit the practical discharge only if it is either an approved engineered design with guide-compliant pipework, or a modular system installed to the manufacturer; otherwise detail a full discharge test. Gas-flooding pipework needs its own structure anchors, a support within 1 m of every nozzle and change of direction, and support spacing within Table 1. Do not hang it from water or air pipes. Cast hanger anchors to the bolt pattern on Drawing 1, and check the 40 mm band against both Table 2 (to 80 mm) and the Drawing 1 clip row (to 90 mm) before the support schedule is issued. Even where the practical discharge is omitted, keep a puff-test connection at the cylinder and a straight-through route to every nozzle; a pneumatic-test waiver exists only for a single change of direction between container and nozzle. |
| CL 2-1998 Recall of Omega Sprinklers | Do not specify Omega-series heads from Central Sprinkler, and treat any already installed on the list below as requiring replacement before that installation is relied on. A head whose deflector carries any mark in this list is inside the recall attached to the circular. On an existing ceiling, identify Omega heads from the deflector mark or the three stacked disks, and remove the cover plate of a concealed head before treating it as a different model. |
| CL 2-2000 Exit and Directional Signs | Multi-owner strata without central management → plan for internally illuminated signs (mains + emergency / secondary battery), not tritium self-luminous. Coordinate exit/directional locations with MOE routes at SD; CL 5/2008 later adds graphic-only options — pick one type per new development. |
| CL 2-2001 Introduction of Revised Certificate of Fire Service Installations and Equipment FS 251 | Certificate format does not change the FSI package; lock the design to the applicable installation code, then use F.S.251 (Rev. 7/2000) for completion records from 1 June 2001. Every system on the schematic FSI schedule needs a location and a type that matches the Appendix IV list, because that is what the completion certificate records. Use this list as the naming set for the FSI schedule so each system can be certified under a single code. Allow a prominent, inspectable display position for the annual F.S.251 in the building (management office, fire control room, or main entrance), separate from the plant rooms. |
| CL 2-2002 Hot Smoke Test on Smoke Extraction System | Atria, halls and large irregular volumes need smoke modelling + test budget in programme — confirm with FSD at GBP whether hot smoke test will be mandated. Size make-up air paths, extract positions and curtain geometry for scouring flow — not just code-minimum exhaust CFM. |
| CL 2-2003 Revised Fire Protection Notice No. 11 Notes on Fire Extinguishers | Specify clean-agent portables in place of BCF, do not allow new inverted chemical-foam extinguishers, and where a licence or FSD certificate requires a fire blanket, use an FSD-approved heavy-duty blanket. |
| CL 2-2006 Pressurization of Staircases to BS 5588 Part 4 | Pressurization design is Class A/B only with 0.75 m/s door velocity criterion — size air release paths, relief openings and plant accordingly; do not design to Class C–E tables in BS 5588-4. |
| CL 2-2007 Certification of FSI under Fire Safety Buildings Ordinance Cap 572 | Treat a composite or domestic building with plans on or before 1 March 1987 as inside the Cap. 572 improvement regime when scoping alteration work. Cap. 572 fire service drawings for these buildings must be certifiable on FSI/314C to the sprinkler rules and the Minimum Fire Service Installations code that the form names. |
| CL 2-2008 Fire Protection Measures in Construction Sites | Construction-stage fire protection is a design deliverable from 30 m height — coordinate temporary riser/pump routes with permanent FH shaft and access staircase positions before superstructure passes 30 m. |
| CL 2-2009 Sounder Bases of Smoke Detectors in Hotels Guesthouses and Student Hostels | Hospitality and student hostel room layouts must schedule sounder-base detectors (not plain detectors) in every guest/bedroom — allow ceiling/device coordination with MEP and confirm dB compliance at layout stage. |
| CL 2-2010 Multi-sensor Detectors in Fire Detection Systems | Superseded for design standard — use CL 6/2021. |
| CL 2-2012 Visual Fire Alarm Systems under Design Manual Barrier Free Access 2008 | Pull DM-BFA Table 2 early; do not omit VFA in ancillary club/carpark of residential towers. Guestroom VFA is accessibility-driven, not “every room.” |
| CL 2-2013 Fire Hydrant Hose Reel System | Lobby planning can be compact — place hydrants in every required stair approach lobby, hose reels on 30 m coverage in corridors/units; never locate hydrants at stair core centre. |
| CL 2-2016 Improvised Hose Reel System Cap 572 | Low-rise composite podium HR upgrades without tank space can propose direct-feed HR — coordinate WSD backflow preventer approval and hydraulic calc with RFSIC at SD for Cap. 572 compliance paths. |
| CL 2-2017 Minimum Fire Resisting Cable Requirements for FSI | Riser and cross-floor FSI power/control routing must be planned for enhanced fire cables unless an exemption applies — affects conduit space, riser size and EL/FA circuit segregation at SD. |
| CL 2-2018 Guidelines on Formulation of Fire Safety Improvement Plans | Cap. 502/572 phasing must show tank/pump-first sequence and zone-limited shutdown windows — programme blue-label periods on drawings so occupants and firefighters are not misled by partial FSI. |
| CL 2-2019 Fire Safety Requirements for Waivers for Buffer Floor and Lower Floors of Existing Industrial Building | Buffer-floor conversions are three-dimensional separation problems — no shared stairs/lifts between industrial upper and converted lower; size data/telecom floors to occupancy factor 30 m²/person at SD. |
| CL 2-2021 Practical Guide for Design Installation Acceptance and Maintenance of FSI | Use Appendix I (new/amended plan checklist) and Appendix IV (SES/SPS/VAC checklist) as internal QA before GBP issue — mirrors CL 4/1996 ¶3.7 but in checklist form. Keep Guide on project drive for team onboarding; never substitute Guide checklists for CoP/FS Code numbers at approval stage. |
| CL 2-2022 Codes of Practice Minimum FSI September 2022 | Fix CoP edition at first FSD/BD submission date after 1 Sep 2022; do not mix 2005/2012/2022 provisions on one project. If your FS Notes cite any absorbed CL, reference September 2022 CoP paragraph instead. Re-baseline Part IV premises table and Part V specs against 2022 edition at SD — do not copy 2012 FS Notes forward blindly. Download September 2022 CoP PDF as project baseline document alongside FS Code and PNAP. |
| CL 2-2023 Fire Safety Requirements for Mechanical Ventilating Systems | Schedule filter type-test certificates, damper FRR matching, and insulation stopping points at every fire-rated duct penetration — Cap. 123J submissions will be checked against Appendix 1 checklist. |
| CL 2-2024 Fire Protection Measures in High-rise Buildings under Construction | CL 2/2024 tightens temporary FH performance (3-storey top outlet rule, 6 hr secondary power, site hoses every 5 floors) — integrate temporary/permanent riser design and RGBC notification milestones into construction programme from SD. |
| CL 2-2025 Fire Safety Requirements for Data Centres | Treat data halls > 230 m² as fully sprinklered special-risk compartments; coordinate generator, FCC, HR coverage and V/AC shutdown zoning at SD — not a standard office FSI package. |
| CL 2-2026 Reporting Chain Effects of Shutdown of FSIs and Revised Measures for Building Notices | Treat fire-alarm and FH/HR pump-start supplies as coupled: isolating one can take the other off, and both then have to be reported. Reserve a prominent A3 face at every entrance and exit, at the lift lobby, on affected floors, and outside the fire control room. New shutdown notices use this A3 notice, not the notice in paragraph 7(b) of Circular Letter No. 1/2021. |
| CL 3-1997 Checklist for Fire Shutter Installation | Fire shutters on shop fronts / atria / vehicle openings need dual-side detection + dual-side manual controls, secondary power, and descent timed to the table — coordinate early with FRC FRP of the wall and MOE clearances when shutter is across an exit path. |
| CL 3-1998 Codes of Practice for Minimum FSI 1998 | Superseded — use current CoP Sep 2022. |
| CL 3-1999 Certification of Fire Service Installations and Equipment | FSI plans that amend the approved building plans need the authorized person’s Part I certificate on FSI/314; keep the FSI drawing identical to the approved building plans that FSD holds. Schematic specifications for sprinklers, alarms, external drenchers, carbon dioxide, clean agent, and water spray must be written to the edition named on the form that will certify them. |
| CL 3-2005 Standard Form for Annual Certification on Ventilating Systems | Provide access to every fire damper, filter and precipitator; the annual certificate cannot be completed for plant that cannot be inspected. Draw a clear boundary between the landlord ventilating system and each tenant system so the annual certificate can name what it covers and what it excludes. |
| CL 3-2006 Specification for Automatic Sprinkler Installations | Superseded — use CL 5/2020. |
| CL 3-2007 FSI Required under Fire Safety Buildings Ordinance Cap 572 | Cap. 572 upgrade surveys should document structural/spatial constraints early — relaxations are case-by-case merits, not automatic; every improvised sprinkler needs FSCC alarm linkage. |
| CL 3-2009 Import Banning of Fire Extinguishers Containing Scheduled Substances | Do not specify a new CFC, halon, HCFC or BCM extinguisher or sprayer unit for sale or supply on a project after 1 January 2010. Size any replacement from an extinguisher that is still approvable; do not reuse an Appendix model's protected volume as the basis for a new scheduled-substance specification. |
| CL 3-2010 Rules for Fire Detection and Fire Alarm Systems for Buildings | Superseded — use CL 6/2021. |
| CL 3-2012 Specification for Automatic Sprinkler Installations | Superseded — use CL 5/2020. |
| CL 3-2016 Guidelines on Fire Safety Requirements for New Railway Infrastructures | Railway FSI is not standard building CoP — download the 3rd-issue Guidelines and map Appendix VII smoke-control annexes to every underground/open station and tunnel segment at masterplan stage. |
| CL 3-2017 Reduced Supply Tank Capacity and Fresh Water Incorporation Cap 572 | Cap. 572 tall-building upgrades may halve FS tank volume to 4,500 L with EVA + nearby hydrant — or pursue FW incorporation pilot; involve WSD early for roof-tank capacity and backflow preventer routing. |
| CL 3-2018 Revised Testing Standards for Static Tanks Category 5 Class 1 and 2 DG | Specify every Category 5 Class 1 and Class 2 bulk static tank for a 70 kPa hydraulic or pneumatic leak test lasting at least one hour, witnessed as a Competent Person test before commissioning. Put wall thickness, internal dimensions, volume and material on the licence drawings for these tanks. Keep emergency-generator and emergency-power fuel tanks that store Category 5 Class 3 at 500 litres or below outside this test; every other initial Category 5 static-tank licence from 1 April 2019 needs the 70 kPa test unless the tank is a Cap. 123 oil storage installation. |
| CL 3-2019 Guidance Notes on FSI in Modular Integrated Construction Building Projects | MiC FSI needs accessible inspection openings, joint-free fire-rated cabling between modules and a documented off-site QA log — flag covered sprinkler heads, HR points and FA devices on module junction drawings at SD. |
| CL 3-2022 Annual Inspection Checklist for Fire Alarm Systems | Place the main panel, any repeater or mimic, the direct-telephone-link box and the log book in the fire control room or, where there is none, at the main entrance or caretaker's counter, with wall space for the schematic and zoning schedule. Keep call-point faces proud of the wall, design each zone to sound within 3 seconds, and synchronise red visual alarms in open areas at 30 to 120 flashes per minute. Match cable rating and loop layout to the BS 5839 edition the building must meet: the 2017 edition limits a short-circuit loss to one floor, and the 2002 edition with amendment 2 also allows five devices on the floors immediately above and below. Specify secondary batteries with a nominal design life of at least 4 years and a charger whose output under alarm load is at least 95% of nominal voltage. |
| CL 3-2023 Specification of Gas Extraction System for Battery Room and Electrical Charging Facilities | Battery rooms are a combined FSI package — gas extraction, monitoring to FCC, and (from 2025) water-based fixed installation for Li-ion > 20 kWh; allow dedicated plant space and independent power at SD. |
| CL 3-2024 Flexible Connectors for Single Sprinklers to Range Pipes | Do not assume flexible drops in concealed/soffit zones — access + prior FSD approval are mandatory. Coordinate ceiling layout and inspection hatches with sprinkler specialist early. |
| CL 3-2025 Fire Safety Requirements for Battery Rooms and Electrical Charging Facilities | Size battery/charging rooms by fire compartment and kWh tally at SD — EV charging hubs, BESS and UPS rooms above 20 kWh Li-ion need water-based suppression in the FSI schedule; pair with gas extraction per CL 3/2023. |
| CL 3-2026 IoT Fire Detection and Portable Extinguishers Alternative Cap 572 Low Rise | Cap. 572 low-rise rehab without HR/FA space can pursue IoT FDS + extinguishers — still need sprinklers/ACD where applicable; confirm FSD published service-provider list and dual-SIM gateway locations at SD. |
| CL 4-1996 Consolidated FSD Circular Letters 1996 | Lock FS Notes to approved GBP before FSI/314; budget messenger collection of vetted plans from FSD HQ. Treat smoke-control package as a separate coloured drawing set at SD; coordinate damper IDs with FS Code / FRC compartment lines early. Three-level basement or industrial use = flag enhanced FSI + rescue stair + dynamic SES at massing. Use ¶7 as a GBP QA list before first FSD submission. Do not design new EVA from this 1996 Part X; use MoA Code Part VI + existing CL 4/2005 summary. Retain ¶3.7 smoke-control rules — still current practice for SCS submissions. |
| CL 4-1997 FSD Circular Letters 1996 Chinese Version | Confirm the first plan-submission date before using the Part II water-supply rules; work submitted on or after 1 April 1995 is an LPC Rules job under Circular Letter No. 2/1994. Do not draw a sprinkler system straight off a town main unless a 24-hour supply is confirmed; otherwise store at least 30 minutes and feed the tank from the main, with an inlet and a direct-line alarm. An improvised system is fed first from the existing hydrant/hose-reel installation; if it replaces dedicated smoke extraction, use fast-response heads at one per 9 m², and add intermediate pumps where the building is more than 60 m above the street. On a basement of three storeys or more, or an industrial basement, lock a firefighting stair and mechanical smoke extraction on every storey that is not dedicated parking, and keep fast-response sprinklers and a separate stair linking the basement levels. Size basement smoke plant as two extract fans at 50% each, and place an extract outlet in the centre of every 500 m²; equipment in the smoke path is specified for 250°C for one hour. Provide a direct line from the detection system to the communication centre, or an owner’s 24-hour attended line with the direct line connected within three months of the FS 172 inspection date. Keep risers and building services out of stairs and smoke lobbies; if a duct must pass, enclose it to the same fire resistance as the protected structure and do not steal exit width. Zone the manual alarm to the table above, and size a limited drencher tank at 10 L/min/m² for 30 minutes; do not rely on a new halon fixed system. Size the emergency generator for the essential fire load, including every fireman’s lift, and use mineral-insulated cable to BS 6207: Part 1 or a Loss Prevention Council accepted type on fire-service circuits. SF6 may go in a basement with its own stair and separated ventilation; Midel, silicone, R-Temp, and dry type go on an external wall or, in a basement, on a dedicated stair to open air, each with a lobby and a 4.5 kg CO2 extinguisher. A ground-floor filling station in an industrial or car-park building needs 4-hour separation, a non-hazardous buffer floor, sprinklers throughout, and either a blank façade for three floors plus a canopy, or a drencher on the canopy. On a new site, keep a 6 m emergency lane where there is no street, 7.3 m where it is a road, 4.5 m headroom, a 22 m turning circle, and a gradient no steeper than 1 in 10 off the level; a redeveloped commercial, hotel, or single-stair domestic block needs a 6 m way to one principal face. Keep industrial processes (other than building services) and Building (Planning) Regulations regulation 49 uses out of domestic and composite buildings; where those buildings also contain a cinema or theatre, a school and a child-care centre are excluded as well, and any bank in an industrial building stays fully fire-separated on the ground or first floor. Before upper floors pass 30 m, plan either a rising temporary hydrant or a fixed pump at 900 L/min with outlet pressure held between 350 kPa and 850 kPa. Carry fire dampers in every duct that passes a fire-resisting floor or wall, keep flexible ducts to 4 m and out of fire separations, and do not use a ceiling void as a duct unless it is non-combustible to BS 476: Part 4 and dampered on a smoke-probe system. Give open batteries, and sealed batteries over 400 Ah, a dedicated extract room held to 1% hydrogen with the charger interlocked to the fan; a sealed Category 5 store needs 5 L/s per m² and explosion-protected electrics to BS 5345. Set call points at or below 1200 mm, hose-reel nozzles at or below 1350 mm, and smoke-detector elements 25 mm to 600 mm below the ceiling, and sprinkler the stage at not more than 3 m × 3 m. Specify FSI, shutters, flame-retardant finishes, and gas detectors only with a test from an organisation listed for that column in Part III, and specify portable equipment from the current gazette list. |
