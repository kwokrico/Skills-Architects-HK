# Travel-distance lookup

Establish use classification, internal corridor versus balcony approach, actual exit layout and measurement endpoints before giving a limit. Measure the route using the Part A travel-distance definition and B11 diagrams, accounting for partitions and obstructions. A nominal second exit is not enough: identify the point of choice and apply B11.7's minimum 30° angle between lines to the alternative exits. Sprinklers alone do not increase the standard B11 travel-distance limits.

For use classifications 1 and 2 on a storey with two or more protected exits, FS Code B11.3(a)(i) limits travel from the flat/guestroom exit to the nearest required staircase to **24 m**, or **45 m along a qualifying balcony approach**. Check B10.6 for balcony conditions, B11.2 for dead ends, and the other B11 provisions for the actual arrangement. Do not apply this flat-door measurement to the whole path from the remotest point inside a room.

Keep **B11.2 dead-end travel** separate from **B11.3 overall travel**. The latter includes any dead-end portion within its applicable measurement endpoints; do not add the dead-end allowance to the overall limit. Both tests must be met. Apply balcony-approach allowances only where B10.6 is satisfied, including protection, landing arrangement and unobstructed width.

For an ordinary office (**Use 4a**) with two or more protected exits and **no balcony approach**, B11.2(c)(i) limits dead-end travel to **18 m** to the protected exit or a qualifying point of choice; B11.3(a)(iii) limits overall travel to the nearest protected exit to **36 m**, including the dead end. Sprinkler provision does not change those limits.

Fictional regression examples for that office arrangement:

| Overall / dead-end travel | Clause-based result |
|---|---|
| 40 m / 10 m, sprinklered | Fails B11.3(a)(iii): 40 m exceeds 36 m, although the dead end is within 18 m |
| 30 m / 20 m, sprinklered | Fails B11.2(c)(i): 20 m exceeds 18 m, although overall travel is within 36 m |

When reporting either comparison, identify what must be checked on the plan: actual measured routes and the B11.7 point of choice, including the minimum 30° angle to alternative exits. Saying only “assuming qualifying exits” leaves that verification unspecified. A failed numerical comparison does not make the route/choice check unnecessary.

For other occupancies or exit arrangements, consult the applicable clause rather than extending these figures. A geometric calculator cannot establish the route's legal classification. For existing buildings or A&A, first check the applicable code basis and approved escape layout; see [the shared applicability guidance](hk-exits-and-required-staircases.md).

Source checked 23 November 2026: [BD Fire Safety Code 2011 (2024 Edition), Part B, B10.6 and B11, printed pp. 37–39](https://www.bd.gov.hk/doc/en/resources/codes-and-references/code-and-design-manuals/fs_code2011.pdf). The supporting regulation is Building (Planning) Regulations regulation 41; the detailed travel rules are in the Code.
