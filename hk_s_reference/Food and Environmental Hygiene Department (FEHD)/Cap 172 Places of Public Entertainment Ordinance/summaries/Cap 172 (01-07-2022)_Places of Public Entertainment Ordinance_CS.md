# Places of Public Entertainment Ordinance (Cap. 172)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 1 July 2022 | Licensing authority means the Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs or a public officer authorized under section 3B

> Scope note: This Ordinance prohibits keeping or using a place of public entertainment without a licence, and it empowers regulations on location, materials, staircases, corridors, gangways, seating, entrances, exits, and fire and sanitary measures. Those regulations are not in this consolidation. The Ordinance is not the licence conditions code, and it is not the Buildings Ordinance.

## Regulatory Overview

A place of public entertainment is so much of any place, building, erection, or structure, whether temporary or permanent, capable of accommodating the public, and any vessel, in or on which a public entertainment is presented or carried on whether on one occasion or more (section 2). Public entertainment is any entertainment within the meaning of this Ordinance to which the general public is admitted with or without payment, and entertainment includes the events in Schedule 1.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What must be licensed (sections 2 and 4, and Schedule 1)

No person shall keep or use any place of public entertainment without a licence granted under this Ordinance (section 4(1)).

Schedule 1 events, activities, and other things, or any part of any of them:

| Item | Entertainment |
|---|---|
| (a) | A concert, opera, ballet, stage performance, or other musical, dramatic, or theatrical entertainment. Stage performance includes a tragedy, melodrama, comedy, farce, pantomime, revue, burlesque, burletta, shadow play, an exhibition of dancing, conjuring or juggling, an acrobatic performance, and any other stage event including an interlude |
| (b) | A cinematograph or laser projection display |
| (c) | A circus |
| (d) | A lecture or story-telling |
| (e) | An exhibition of any one or more of pictures, photographs, books, manuscripts, or other documents or other things |
| (f) | A sporting exhibition or contest |
| (g) | A bazaar |
| (i) | An amusement ride within the meaning of the Amusement Rides (Safety) Ordinance (Cap. 449), or any mechanical device, other than such an amusement ride, which is designed for amusement |
| (j) | A dance party |

A dance party means an event at which music or rhythmic sound of any kind or source is provided, the primary activity is dancing by the persons attending, and either the number of persons attending exceeds 200 on at least one occasion or any part of the event occurs between 2 a.m. and 6 a.m.

The Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs may by Gazette order exempt places of public entertainment of a specified class or description from this Ordinance or particular provisions, or from specified regulations or particular provisions of them (section 3A).

**SD takeaway:** A place capable of accommodating the public at which any Schedule 1 entertainment is presented, including a lecture, an exhibition, a bazaar, or a dance party over 200 persons or between 2 a.m. and 6 a.m., cannot be kept or used without a licence unless an exemption order covers that class.

### 2. What a regulation may lock in the plan (section 7)

The Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs may by regulation provide for the location of a place of public entertainment and the circumstances, conditions, and restrictions on that location; the materials of which it shall be constructed; the construction and arrangement of staircases, corridors, gangways, vestibules, seating accommodation, entrances, exits, doors, and fastenings; any stage, electrical wiring, lighting platform, or electrical or other installation, whether temporary or permanent; measures against overcrowding; control and prevention of fires; and maintenance of sanitary conditions.

The general purpose of regulations under section 7(1)(f), which is the maintenance of peace and good order, shall be to ensure the safety of any persons present should a fire or other emergency occur, and to avoid disorder. Entry and inspection of a place or a proposed place may be by the Commissioner of Police, the Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs, the Building Authority, the Director of Fire Services, the Director of Marine, and public officers they authorize.

**SD takeaway:** Stair, gangway, exit, seating, and temporary stage and wiring are regulation subjects under section 7. This consolidation does not print those dimensions.

### 3. What a licence may fix, and closure (sections 10 and 11)

A licence is in such terms as the licensing authority determines and is subject to the conditions specified in it. Conditions may specify the period, the hours, and the days, weeks, or months during which the place may be used for public entertainment; crowd-control measures; the number of first aid personnel; and the maximum number of persons which may be admitted, including different maxima for different classes of entertainment (section 10).

If a place is kept or used without a licence, or in contravention of this Ordinance or regulations under section 7, a magistrate shall, subject to notice, make a Prohibition Order in Form 1 in Schedule 2. It prohibits the keeping or use for all purposes, or for purposes specified, with effect from the 8th day after copies are served by affixing them to a conspicuous part of the place. If that order has been served but has not been continuously complied with from that 8th day, the magistrate shall, subject to the same notice rules, make a Closure Order. On a Closure Order the licensing authority shall lock or seal all or any of the entrances or exits and may disconnect gas, water, and electricity. No person shall enter or remain except with written permission (section 11).

At least 14 days’ notice of intention to apply for either order must be served in the same way, stating the time and place of hearing.

**SD takeaway:** The licence can cap the number of persons and the hours, and an unlicensed place can be prohibited from the 8th day after the order is fixed to the building and then locked, sealed, and disconnected from gas, water, and electricity.
