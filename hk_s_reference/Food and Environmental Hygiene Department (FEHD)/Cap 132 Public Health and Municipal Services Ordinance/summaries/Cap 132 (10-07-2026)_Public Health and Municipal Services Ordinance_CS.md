# Public Health and Municipal Services Ordinance (Cap. 132)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 10 July 2026 | Authorities are the public officers designated in the Third Schedule (section 3)

> Scope note: This Ordinance is the parent for public sewers, latrine notices, canopies, mosquito water on a building site, offensive trades, licences for the Eleventh Schedule and Schedule 11A, ventilation of scheduled premises, and dangerous advertisement hoardings. It does not set food-business layout, swimming-pool dimensions, or latrine numbers. Those sit in regulations the Authority may make. Nothing in Part VIII (ventilation) prejudices the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) (section 103).

## Regulatory Overview

The Director of Drainage Services is the Authority for public sewers and drains (Third Schedule, sections 4 to 7, 9, and 10), and the Director of Food and Environmental Hygiene is the Authority for canopies, mosquito control, latrine notices, offensive-trade declarations, undertakers, and ventilating systems in scheduled premises (Third Schedule, sections 22A, 27, 30, 32, 48, 92AB, 93, and 94). The Director of Buildings is the Authority for a dangerous advertisement hoarding under section 105.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Public sewers, disused drains, and site wash (sections 6, 7, and 10)

A public sewer includes, for this Ordinance, any part of a sewer from the outlet of any disconnecting trap to its junction with a public sewer or drain which lies outside the lot boundary (section 2).

| Act | Section | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Solid matter, mud, or waste other than that in ordinary house sewage | 6(1)(a)–(c) | Do not place or throw it into a public sewer or drain, or a communicating sewer, drain, or inlet, or over a grate, or knowingly permit that, or place it where it is liable to fall or be carried in |
| Trade waste, chemicals, oils, petroleum or petroleum-spirit, waste steam, or heated liquid | 6(1)(d) | Do not discharge it into a public sewer or drain, or a communicating sewer or drain, if alone or in combination it causes or may cause nuisance or danger to persons or danger to the sewer or drain. Trade waste includes waste building or civil engineering materials |
| Damage or alteration | 6(1)(e) | Do not wilfully, except with the Authority’s written permission, or negligently, damage, alter, disconnect, or otherwise interfere with a public sewer or drain or any connection |
| Soil from adjoining land | 7 | The Authority may require the owner or occupier of land adjoining a street or place in which a public sewer, drain, or drainage works is situated to fence off, channel, or embank the land so soil or waste is not carried in |
| Disused drain | 10 | Forthwith after the owner, or in default the occupier, knows of a disused sewer or drain in, under, or upon the premises, give written notice. A person who intends to cease to use any sewer or drain in, under, or upon the premises shall forthwith give written notice of that intention |

**SD takeaway:** Keep site wash, mud, and waste building materials out of the public sewer, and give written notice before a drain on the lot is abandoned.

### 2. Latrines and sanitary conveniences (sections 29, 30, and 32)

Section 29 lets the Authority make regulations for suitable and sufficient latrine accommodation in connection with any premises, whether constructed before or after the commencement of this Ordinance, for a water supply sufficient for the effective action of water closets and urinals, and for the hygienic construction of rooms or compartments in which latrines may be installed.

Where it appears that any premises are without, or without sufficient, latrine accommodation, or that the accommodation is ineffective or of an unsuitable type, the Authority may require the owner, or in stated cases the occupier, to provide the number or type of latrines, or to do such other thing, as the notice specifies (section 30(1)). Where that requirement involves the carrying out of building works within the meaning of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), no such notice shall be issued except with the written consent of the Building Authority.

If a septic tank, cesspool, trap, siphon, or sanitary convenience is, or has been, so constructed or so situated as to be, or to be likely to be, a nuisance or offensive to public decency, the Authority may require it to be removed, reconstructed, screened, or otherwise altered (section 32). A person aggrieved by a section 30 notice may, within 30 days, appeal to the Municipal Services Appeals Board (section 30(3)).

**SD takeaway:** A latrine notice that is building works cannot be issued without the Building Authority’s written consent, so the sanitary layout is a Cap. 123 submission as well as a Cap. 132 notice.

### 3. Canopies and shopfront articles (sections 2, 22A, and 86F)

A canopy is any shade, shelter, or other structure not carrying a floor load which projects from a wall and is cantilevered or supported by brackets, posts, or other means, or is erected on any building or in or over any open space adjacent to or on a building and is supported by posts or other means (section 2).

If litter or waste on or in a canopy is or may become injurious or dangerous to health, is or may become a danger to any person, constitutes a nuisance, or is unsightly, the Authority may require the occupier, or if the occupier cannot be found or there is no occupier the owner, to remove the canopy (section 22A(2)). The Authority shall not remove it until 14 days from service of the notice, or until an appeal to the Chief Executive is determined. Appeal is within 14 days, and the Chief Executive’s decision is final (section 22A(4), (7), and (8)).

Section 86F: a person commits an offence if, without lawful authority or reasonable excuse, the person places or leaves, or causes, suffers, or permits, any article in a public place for the purpose of, in the course of, or otherwise in connection with, a business in a shop, and the article causes or may cause obstruction, inconvenience, or danger to the public.

**SD takeaway:** A canopy that does not carry a floor load can be ordered removed if waste on it is a danger or a nuisance, and shop goods left in a public place are a section 86F offence if they obstruct, inconvenience, or endanger the public.

### 4. Mosquito water on a building site (section 27)

Where it appears there is, or is likely to be, any accumulation of water likely to contain larvae or pupae of mosquitoes, the Authority may by notice require the person responsible to remove it, to prevent any accumulation of water, or to prevent larvae or pupae. Where the premises consist of a building site, the person responsible is the appointed contractor: the registered contractor appointed under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), or, where the site is owned by the Government, the contractor appointed in respect of the site if he has entered on the site.

**SD takeaway:** On a building site the registered contractor, once appointed, is the person who can be required to stop standing water that can breed mosquitoes.

### 6. Ventilation of scheduled premises (sections 2, 93, 94, and 94A, and the Second Schedule)

Scheduled premises are the classes in the first column of the Second Schedule. A ventilating system is a mechanical or electrical system, or both, for introducing or exhausting air, and also an air-conditioning plant which contains a device for reducing or increasing the temperature of the air below or above the temperature of the external air.

Where, in the Authority’s opinion, scheduled premises do not have adequate natural ventilation, the Authority may require a ventilating system which provides, for each person who may be accommodated, not less than the Second Schedule amount of outside air. The Authority may instead specify a lesser amount if, with the available natural ventilation, it will adequately ventilate the premises (section 93(1) and (2)).

| Class of premises | Outside air, cubic metres per hour for each person who may be accommodated |
|---|---|
| Cinemas | 13 |
| Theatres | 13 |
| Dancing establishments | 17 |
| Factory canteens | 17 |
| Funeral parlours | 17 |
| Restaurants | 17 |

Failure to comply within 6 months, or such further period as the Authority may allow, is an offence, and the licensing authority may revoke the licence temporarily or permanently (section 93(3) and (4)). The licensing authority for this Part is the public officer by whom the scheduled premises are for the time being licensed under any enactment (section 93(5)).

Before any new ventilating system is installed, the licensee shall send the Authority a supplier’s certificate stating the fan capacity in cubic metres per minute, the outlet velocity in metres per minute, the designed operating static pressure in pascals, the speed of the fan pulley in revolutions per minute, the net area of the air intake, the net area of exhaust in square metres except where the premises are air-conditioned with positive pressure, and the type of refrigerant if any (section 94(1)). No alteration shall be made to any ventilating system in scheduled premises save with the Authority’s written permission (section 94(2)).

**SD takeaway:** For a restaurant, cinema, theatre, dancing establishment, factory canteen, or funeral parlour without adequate natural ventilation, size the outside-air system to the Second Schedule rate per person who may be accommodated, and do not alter that system without written permission.

### 7. Dangerous advertisement hoardings (section 105)

Where, in the opinion of the Authority, any hoarding, scaffolding, or other structure erected solely for exhibiting advertisements is dangerous, or is likely to become dangerous, by reason of its construction, wind, rain, dilapidation, its age, or other cause, the Authority shall require the owner, or his servant or agent, within a reasonable period, to remove it or to do such work as is specified to render it safe. The Third Schedule designates the Director of Buildings as the Authority for section 105. If the notice is not complied with, the person is guilty of an offence and the Authority may execute the work and recover the expenses.

**SD takeaway:** An advertisement hoarding, scaffold, or structure erected solely to exhibit advertisements is a section 105 structure for the Director of Buildings if its construction, wind, rain, dilapidation, or age makes it dangerous or likely to become dangerous.
