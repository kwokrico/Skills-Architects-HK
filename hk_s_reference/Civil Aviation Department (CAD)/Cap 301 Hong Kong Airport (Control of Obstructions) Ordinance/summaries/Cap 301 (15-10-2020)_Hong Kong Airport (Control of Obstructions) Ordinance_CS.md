# Hong Kong Airport (Control of Obstructions) Ordinance

**Architect critical summary for schematic design**

15 October 2020 | Civil Aviation Department (CAD)

> Scope note: The extract prints **Last updated date 15.10.2020**. Chapter (Cap.) 301 is the enabling Ordinance (commenced 7 June 1957). It does not print a building height in metres, an obstacle-limitation surface, or a height table. Height limits and prohibited areas exist only in an order under section 3, with a plan deposited at the Land Registry. The current height order made under section 3(1AA) is the Hong Kong Airport (Control of Obstructions) (No. 2) Order 1997 (Cap. 301D). Schematic design (SD) reads that order and the deposited plans for the figure that applies to the site.

---

## Regulatory Overview

Cap. 301 provides for the restriction and, where necessary, the reduction of the heights of buildings in the interest of the safety of aircraft, for the control of lighting, and for the erection, provision, and maintenance of aids to air navigation. The Chief Executive in Council and the Secretary for Development exercise the height control by order under section 3, section 4 prohibits erection contrary to such an order, and sections 5 to 14A cover reduction or demolition, marking and lights, lights exposed to the sky, works by the Director of Buildings, and trees that obstruct lights or beacons.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Definitions that affect design (s.2)

Section 2(1) prints these definitions. Words in quotation marks are the English text as printed.

| Term | Text as printed in section 2 |
|---|---|
| **building** | “includes the whole, or any part, of any building, arch, bridge, chimney, cookhouse, cowshed, dock, factory, garage, hangar, hoarding, latrine, lift, matshed, office, out-house, pier, shelter, shop, stable, stairs, wall, warehouse, wharf, or workshop, and any mast or pole or any pile driver, scaffolding, hoist or crane or other structure projecting skywards” |
| **building works** | “includes any kind of building construction, site formation, drainage works, repairs, maintenance, demolition, alteration, addition and every kind of building operation, and any lighting or marking required to be provided under the provisions of this Ordinance” |
| **Hong Kong Airport** | “means the Hong Kong International Airport at Kowloon peninsula and the airport built or to be built at and in the vicinity of Chek Lap Kok, Lantau, New Territories” |
| **Secretary** | “means the Secretary for Development” |
| **Director-General of Civil Aviation** | Section 2 as printed does not define this title. The Ordinance uses it for the officer who advises on height orders, grants short exemptions, and exercises the marking, lighting, and tree powers below |
| **safety of aircraft** (s.2(2)) | “Any reference in this Ordinance to the safety of aircraft means the safety of aircraft using or which will be using Hong Kong Airport.” |

**SD takeaway:** A mast, pole, pile driver, scaffold, hoist, crane, or other structure projecting skywards is a building for this Ordinance. Test roof plant, signage, and site cranes against the height order, not only the occupied floors. “Safety of aircraft” is tied to the two airports named in the section 2 definition of Hong Kong Airport, in the words printed above.

### 2. Power to make height orders (s.3)

The Chief Executive in Council may act under section 3(1) where, in the opinion of the Chief Executive in Council, the safety of aircraft so requires. The power to set a maximum height in an area sits with the Secretary, on the advice of the Director-General of Civil Aviation (section 3(1AA)). Section 3 does not print a height in metres and does not mention principal datum. The height under section 3(1AA) is “the height specified in the order”.

| Provision | Who | What the order may do |
|---|---|---|
| **s.3(1)(a)** | Chief Executive in Council | Prescribe areas within which all buildings are prohibited |
| **s.3(1)(b)** | — | Repealed. The extract prints no replacement text |
| **s.3(1)(c)** | Chief Executive in Council | Require demolition of a building erected in an area where all buildings are prohibited; or require a building that does not conform with an order under section 3(1AA) to be reduced in height or, where reduction is not feasible, demolished |
| **s.3(1AA)** | Secretary, on the advice of the Director-General of Civil Aviation | Prescribe areas within which no building shall exceed the height specified in the order |
| **s.3(1A)** | In an order under s.3(1)(a) or (1AA) | Provide that, subject to conditions prescribed in the order, it shall not apply to any particular area, or any particular building, within a prescribed area |
| **s.3(2)** | Order under s.3(1)(a) | Publish in the Gazette. Deposit at the Land Registry a copy together with a plan of the prescribed area, signed by the Clerk to the Executive Council |
| **s.3(2A)** | Order under s.3(1AA) | Publish in the Gazette. Deposit at the Land Registry a copy together with a plan of the prescribed area, signed by the Secretary |
| **s.3(3)** | Order under s.3(1)(c) | Serve on the owner of the premises affected. May be registered at the Land Registry by a memorial signed by the Clerk to the Executive Council containing the full terms of the order |
| **s.3(3A) and (3B)** | Secretary, on the advice of the Director-General of Civil Aviation | Grant an exemption from an order under section 3(1AA), on terms and conditions required for or related to the safety of aircraft. Publish in the Gazette and deposit at the Land Registry a copy with a plan of the exempted area, signed by the Secretary |
| **s.3(4)** | Director-General of Civil Aviation, by notice in writing | Grant an exemption from an order under section 3(1AA) for a period not exceeding 2 months, which may be extended for further periods of 2 months, on terms and conditions he imposes |

**SD takeaway:** Freeze massing only after the section 3(1AA) order and the Land Registry plan for the site are in hand. A section 3(1)(a) area prohibits all buildings as defined in section 2, including cranes and scaffolds. A section 3(4) exemption is a short notice (2 months, extendable by further periods of 2 months). It is not a permanent design approval. A section 3(3A) exemption is a separate Secretary’s order, with its own deposited plan.

### 3. No building to be erected contrary to an order (s.4)

Section 4, as printed:

> No building shall be erected in any area in which buildings are prohibited, and, subject to section 3(3A) and (4), no building shall be erected above the prescribed height, contrary to any order under section 3(1)(a) or (1AA).

| Consequence | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Carve-outs named in s.4** | Section 3(3A) (Secretary’s exemption) and section 3(4) (Director-General’s short exemption) |
| **Offence (s.20(a))** | Carrying out building works in contravention of section 4. Fine at level 6 and imprisonment for 12 months, plus a further fine of $5,000 for each day on which the offence has continued |
| **Who pays if the building is unlawful (s.17 proviso)** | Section 17 (Government payment of the cost of required works) does not apply to a building erected in contravention of section 4 |

The extract prints “level 6” and does not print a dollar amount for that level.

**SD takeaway:** Do not issue a scheme, a crane plan, or a scaffold height that exceeds the prescribed height or that places a building inside a prohibited area. The only exceptions section 4 names are the exemptions in section 3(3A) and section 3(4). Building works include demolition, alteration, addition, and the lighting or marking this Ordinance requires.

### 4. Demolition or reduction in height (s.5)

Section 5 applies to a building which is the subject of an order under section 3(1)(c). The Director-General of Civil Aviation, after consultation with the Director of Buildings, may by notice in writing served on the owner specify:

- the date by which the building is to be demolished, or its height reduced, in compliance with that order; and
- the date by which the building works of that demolition or reduction shall be commenced.

| Related power | What it does for a reduction or demolition |
|---|---|
| **s.3(1)(c)** | The order that requires the demolition or the reduction (or demolition where reduction is not feasible) |
| **s.15** | The owner, or the Director of Buildings, may apply to a magistrate for a closure order when those works are about to be undertaken. No person shall occupy a building or part to which a closure order relates while the order continues in force |
| **Part III** | Reduction or demolition required by an order under section 3 can give a compensation claim under Part III |

**SD takeaway:** An existing building that exceeds a later height order can be ordered down, on dates set by the Director-General after consultation with the Director of Buildings. Allow those dates in the programme. Missing them is a ground for the Director of Buildings to carry out the works (section 13(b) below).

### 5. Marking and lights (ss.6–7)

| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| **s.6(1)** | Where the Director-General of Civil Aviation is of the opinion that the safety of aircraft requires marking of a building, or warning or guiding lights on it, he serves notice on the owner. The owner elects in writing, within 14 days, whether the Government provides the marking or lights or the owner does |
| **s.6(2)** | If the owner elects the Government, the Government may provide and thereafter maintain them |
| **s.6(3)** | If the owner elects to provide them, the owner does so within the time and in the manner the Director-General requires by notice, and thereafter maintains them “in such manner as the Director may from time to time by notice in writing require” |
| **s.6(4)** | Reasonable expenses incurred by the owner in providing or maintaining them, including expenses of illumination, shall be paid by the Government |
| **s.7(1)** | Where the Secretary is of the opinion that the safety of aircraft so requires, he may by order authorize the Director of Buildings to provide or erect, and thereafter maintain, in any place or on any building, such marks, warning or guiding lights, and aircraft beacons, as the order specifies |
| **s.7(2)** | That order shall be served upon the owner, if any, of the place or building |

**SD takeaway:** Reserve locations, structure, power, and access for warning or guiding lights, marks, and aircraft beacons on the roof and on tall temporary works. The owner has 14 days to elect who installs section 6 marking or lights. Government pays the owner’s reasonable expenses of providing and maintaining them, including illumination. A section 7 beacon is ordered by the Secretary and erected or maintained by the Director of Buildings.

### 6. Occulting lights and lights exposed to the sky (ss.8–11)

| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| **s.8(1)** | Save with the written authority of the Director-General of Civil Aviation, or an exemption granted by him and published in the Gazette, no person shall, in Kowloon or New Kowloon or any other area prescribed by order of the Chief Executive in Council published in the Gazette, expose to the sky or permit to be exposed to the sky any form of occulting light of any colour |
| **s.8(2)** | **Occulting light** includes any light which is cut off from view at regular or irregular intervals or which is visible suddenly or intermittently |
| **s.8(3)** | Section 8 does not apply to navigational or signal lights, or to any occulting light of an intensity of less than 200 cd (candela) |
| **s.9** | Where the Chief Executive in Council is of the opinion that the safety of aircraft so requires, he may by order prescribe areas within which no person shall expose to the sky, or permit to be exposed to the sky, any type of lighting specified in or by the order |
| **s.10(1)** | Where the Director-General is of that opinion, he may by notice in writing on the owner or occupier prohibit the use of any light exposed to the sky |
| **s.10(2)** | If a light is exposed in contravention of that notice, the Director-General may by notice require a supplier of electricity or gas to discontinue the supply to that light |
| **s.10(3)** | A person aggrieved may appeal by petition to the Chief Executive in Council, and shall comply with the section 10(1) notice pending that decision |
| **s.11** | Even where no section 10(1) notice has been given, the Director-General may require a supplier immediately to discontinue, for a period not exceeding 12 hours, the supply of electricity or gas to a light exposed to the sky. If it is not practicable to cut that light only, the supplier may discontinue supply to other lighting to the extent necessary to extinguish that light |
| **s.12** | A servant or agent of the supplier may enter and, where necessary, in the presence of a police officer, break into the building or place to effect that discontinuance |

Section 20 makes a contravention of section 8, and a failure to comply with a notice under section 10(1), an offence (fine at level 6 and imprisonment for 12 months, with a further daily fine of $5,000 in the cases section 20 states).

**SD takeaway:** In Kowloon, New Kowloon, and any further area prescribed by a Gazette order, do not design façade, roof, or feature lighting that flashes, cuts off, or appears suddenly or intermittently and is exposed to the sky, unless it is a navigational or signal light, under 200 cd, or covered by the Director-General’s written authority or a Gazette exemption. A steady light exposed to the sky can still be prohibited by a section 9 order or a section 10 notice. Provide a way to extinguish that circuit without relying on an appeal, because the notice must be obeyed while a petition is pending.

### 7. Director of Buildings may carry out works (s.13)

The Director of Buildings may, on application by the Director-General of Civil Aviation, carry out such building works as may be necessary for the purposes in the table.

| Paragraph | Works |
|---|---|
| **s.13(a)** | Demolition or reduction in height of any building erected in contravention of section 4 |
| **s.13(b)** | Demolition or reduction in height required by an order under section 3(1)(c), where the works have not been commenced by the date in the section 5 notice, have not been completed by that date, or the Director of Buildings is of opinion that they are unlikely to be completed by that date |
| **s.13(c)** | Marking of a building, provision of a light, or maintenance of that marking or light, where a person has not complied with a notice under section 6(3) |
| **s.14(1)** | The Director of Buildings, or a public officer authorized by him, may during the hours of day-light enter and, where necessary, in the presence of a police officer, break into premises to check compliance or to carry out works under section 7(1) or section 13 |
| **s.14(2)** | The Director-General of Civil Aviation, or a public officer authorized by him, may at any time enter and, where necessary, in the presence of a police officer, break into premises to check compliance on marks, lights, or beacons |
| **s.16** | Where the Director of Buildings carries out works under section 13(a), he may recover the cost, including supervision charges, from the owner. If he registers a notice of intention at the Land Registry before starting, the cost and interest remain a first charge on the land until paid |
| **s.17** | The Director of Buildings may agree that the Government pays the cost of building works a person is required to carry out. The proviso excludes a building erected in contravention of section 4 |

**SD takeaway:** A building put up above the prescribed height can be reduced or demolished by the Director of Buildings on the Director-General’s application, and the owner can be charged for that cost. For a lawful building that must later be reduced under a section 3(1)(c) order, keep the section 5 commencement and completion dates, because delay or a likelihood of delay is itself a ground for the Director of Buildings to carry out the works.

### 8. Trees obstructing lights or beacons (s.14A)

Section 14A was added by 77 of 1978. It covers any tree, shrub, plant, or vegetation.

| Provision | Rule |
|---|---|
| **s.14A(1)** | Where the Director-General of Civil Aviation is of the opinion that vegetation obstructs or affects, or is likely to obstruct or affect, the proper operation of any warning light, guiding light, aircraft beacon, or navigational or approach aids so as to impair the safety of aircraft, the Director shall by notice in writing on the occupier call upon him to fell the tree, or remove the plant, shrub, or vegetation, or deal with it so as to remove the obstruction |
| **s.14A(2)** | Failure to comply within 14 days of service is an offence: a fine at level 1 and imprisonment for 6 months |
| **s.14A(3)** | If the notice is not complied with within 14 days, or if immediate felling, removal, or dealing is required, the Director or an authorized public officer may at any time enter and fell, remove, or deal with the vegetation |
| **s.14A(4)** | Reasonable expenses incurred by an occupier in complying with the notice shall be paid by the Government |

**SD takeaway:** Do not rely on a retained tree, screen planting, or a green roof to sit in front of a warning light, guiding light, aircraft beacon, or navigational or approach aid. The occupier can be required to fell or remove it within 14 days, and the Director may enter and do the work if the notice is missed or if immediate action is required. Government pays the occupier’s reasonable expenses of complying.
