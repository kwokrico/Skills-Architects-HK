# HKIA Code of Professional Conduct
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Revision 18 August 2017 (also printed: copyright September 1989; revisions 1 June 1989, 18 January 1995, and 29 May 2010) | The Hong Kong Institute of Architects

> Scope note: This code governs Members of the Institute, and the code says a Member includes Fellow, Member, Associate, and Graduate. It is not the Architects Registration Ordinance and it does not approve building plans. Rule 3.1 refers to the Standard Form of Agreement between Client and Architect. That agreement is not in this library. The same PDF includes guidelines on invited consultancy submissions, adopted at the special general meeting on 18 August 2017, and guidelines on dissemination of information, endorsed at the 8th Council Meeting on 9 September 2008.

## Regulatory Overview

The object of the code is to promote the standard of professional conduct and self discipline required of every Member in the interests of the public, and it rests on four Principles, with Rules under them and guidance notes under the Rules. A Member is liable to reprimand, suspension, or expulsion if his conduct is found to be in contravention of the code or otherwise inconsistent with his status as a Member, and contravention includes conduct which dishonours any of the Principles, whether or not a specific Rule or Note is contravened.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Carrying out the commission (Principle 1)

A Member shall faithfully carry out the duties which he undertakes, and shall have a proper regard for the interests both of those who commission and of those who may be expected to use or enjoy the product of his work.

| Rule | Lock |
|---|---|
| 1.1 | Be prompt. A Member who becomes unable or unwilling to continue shall give reasonable notice of termination |
| 1.1.1 | Do not undertake or continue if he is unable to arrange that his resources are adequate and properly directed to carry out the work expeditiously |
| 1.1.2 | The work of his office and any branch office is under the proper control of a suitably qualified person |
| 1.1.3 | A Graduate who undertakes a commission shall seek guidance from a Member other than a Graduate Member |
| 1.2 | Do not subcommission work for which he has been commissioned without the prior agreement of his client, nor without defining the responsibilities of those concerned |
| 1.3 | Act impartially in all cases in which he is acting between parties, and interpret the conditions of a building contract with fairness |
| 1.4 | A Member appointed assessor for any competition shall not act in any capacity for the work, except that he may act as arbitrator in any dispute between the promoters and the selected architect |

The code applies not only to a Member acting on his own behalf but also to the conduct of any body corporate or incorporate practising as architects in which a Member has an interest (paragraph 0.4).

**SD takeaway:** A Graduate cannot carry a commission without guidance from a Member who is not a Graduate, and work cannot be subcommissioned without the client’s prior agreement and a definition of responsibilities.

### 2. Conflict, statutory approvals, and who may be a partner (Principle 2)

A Member shall avoid any action or situation inconsistent with his professional obligations or likely to raise doubts about his integrity.

| Rule | Lock |
|---|---|
| 2.1 | If interests conflict so as to risk a breach, withdraw, or remove the source of conflict, or declare it and obtain the agreement of the parties concerned to continuance |
| 2.2 | No financial or personal interest in or connection with any business which would or might breach the Principle, unless fully declared and accepted by his client, contractor, and any other parties likely to be affected |
| 2.2.1 | Where a conflict is likely because the Member has a financial or personal interest in a contract, arrange for an arbitrator to be agreed and appointed at an early stage, whether or not a dispute has arisen |
| 2.2.2 | Avoid any situation in which he would be in a position to influence improperly the granting of planning applications or statutory approvals |
| 2.6 | Do not have as a partner or co-director any person disqualified from membership by expulsion from the Institute or another professional institution, unless the Institute otherwise allows, or any undischarged bankrupt, even if that person engages in occupations separate from the architectural practice |
| 2.7 | Safeguard confidential information and do not disclose it to third parties without the client’s written consent, or receive any advantage for disclosing it, or use it for personal gain |

**SD takeaway:** A financial interest in the contract has to be declared and accepted, and an arbitrator arranged early, and the Member must avoid a situation in which he could improperly influence a planning application or a statutory approval.

### 3. The written appointment, and taking over another Member’s work (Principle 3)

| Rule | Lock |
|---|---|
| 3.1 | State clearly in writing the terms and scope of services. A Member may refer to the Standard Form of Agreement between Client and Architect, which the code says covers the key services normally provided and contains the key requirements of an appointment |
| 3.2 | Review with the client in advance the provision of comprehensive professional services in individual service stages |
| 3.4 | Do not give commissions or inducements for or in recognition of the introduction of clients or work |
| 3.6.2 | Before accepting an appointment to continue work on which another Member is or has been engaged, notify that Member in writing, and ascertain from the client that the previous Member’s agreement has been properly terminated and/or concluded and that the client has the right to use any information, drawings, and design prepared by the previous Member |
| 3.6.3 | A Member asked to give an opinion on the work of another architect shall notify that architect, except where it can be shown that to do so would be prejudicial to prospective litigation |

**SD takeaway:** Do not start on another Member’s drawings until that Member has been notified in writing and the client has the right to use the previous information, drawings, and design. The standard agreement itself is not in this PDF.

### 4. Invited consultancy submissions (guidelines adopted 18 August 2017)

These guidelines apply to members who, as individuals, partners, or directors, participate in an invited submission for an architectural consultancy proposal for a project in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, organized by public or private clients. They also apply where the member participates in association with non-members.

If the conditions request a specific concept design proposal with remuneration, the remuneration should be agreed and recorded clearly. The guidelines include a sample tabulation of total gross floor area bands against remuneration and time allowed, with the remuneration and time columns left blank in this PDF, marked as an example only.

The requirements of a concept design proposal are recommended to include a schematic site layout, schematic layout plans of principal functional levels, schematic sections, and a design statement. Drawings requested should be to a reasonable scale compatible with their schematic nature and with the size and scale of the project. Additional reimbursement may be agreed separately when production of perspectives or a model is required.

**SD takeaway:** An invited concept submission is recommended to stop at schematic site layout, principal floor plans, sections, and a design statement, at a scale compatible with that schematic stage, and the fee for that submission should be agreed and recorded before the work.

### 5. What may be said in public (promotion guidelines, endorsed 9 September 2008)

Promotional information provided to the public shall be factual, accurate, decent, not exaggerated, not misleading, not comparative, and not disparaging. A Member shall not use generalised terms such as “best”, “largest”, or “most” in regard to quality or size of his practice or projects, shall not directly or by implication compare his practice with any other Member’s practice, and shall not publicise fee rates or charges. The guidelines apply only in Hong Kong.

**SD takeaway:** Fee rates are not to be publicised, and the practice is not to be described as the best, the largest, or the most.
