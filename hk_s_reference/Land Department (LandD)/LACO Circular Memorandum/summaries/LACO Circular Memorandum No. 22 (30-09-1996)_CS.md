# LACO Circular Memorandum No. 22
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
30 September 1996 | Legal Advisory and Conveyancing Office — Lands Department  
Ref. LACO/HQ/127 II | Government rent apportionment on S&P

> Scope note: Reminds conveyancers to check **Government rent** is paid up and to **apportion** vendor/purchaser liability (especially ahead of post-1.7.1997 **3% of rateable value** rents). Title/conveyancing practice — **not** a schematic design instrument.

---

## Regulatory Overview

CM No. 22 stresses that unpaid Government rent breaches the lease and risks re-entry under the Crown Rights (Re-entry and Vesting Remedies) Ordinance. Purchaser’s solicitor must ensure arrears are cleared by the vendor before completion and retain an appropriate sum for current rent. From 1.7.1997 many properties face annual rent of **3% of rateable value**.

---

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Good title and arrears (paras. 1–3)

| Duty | Hard rule |
|---|---|
| Before completion | Confirm Government rent paid up to date (Treasury enquiries; Law Society Circular 231/95) |
| If arrears | Vendor clears liability up to assignment date before completion |
| Retention | Purchaser’s solicitor retains appropriate amount from purchase money for current rent when demanded |

**SD takeaway:** No design lock — flag rent/title risk only if advising on acquisition of existing stock for redevelopment.

---

### 2. Post-1.7.1997 3% RV rent (para. 4)

| Change | Implication |
|---|---|
| Many properties: annual rent = **3% of rateable value**, adjusted with RV | Larger sums → proper vendor/purchaser apportionment at conveyancing essential |

**SD takeaway:** For acquisition programmes after mid-1997, expect 3%-of-RV rent apportionment on completion — still conveyancing, not GA design.
