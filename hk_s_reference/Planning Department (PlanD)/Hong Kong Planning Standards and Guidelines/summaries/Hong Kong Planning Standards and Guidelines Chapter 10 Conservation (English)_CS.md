# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 10 Conservation
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
March 2020 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: The printed heading is CHAPTER 10 CONSERVATION, and the contents page is dated (March 2020 Edition). This chapter explains conservation from the planning perspective, covering nature conservation and biodiversity and the conservation of declared monuments, historic buildings, sites of archaeological interest and other heritage items, including zoning on town plans (§1.2, §1.3).

## Regulatory Overview
Conservation is treated here as a land-use matter that can be effected by legislation, administrative measures and zoning, and in conservation zones there is a general presumption against development (§1.2, §3.7.2). The chapter records the designated areas and the consultation duties that apply to development at or near them, and it states no measured buffer distance around a country park, marine park, Site of Special Scientific Interest, monument or historic building (§3.5.1, §4.6.3).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Principles of conservation (§2.1–§2.2)
Four principles are stated for the practical pursuit of conservation in land-use planning (§2.1):

| Principle | Text |
|---|---|
| (i) | Retain significant landscapes, ecological and geological attributes and heritage features as conservation zones |
| (ii) | Restrict uses within conservation zones to those which sustain those landscapes, attributes and features |
| (iii) | Ensure compatibility of adjoining uses so as to minimise adverse impacts on conservation zones and optimise their conservation value |
| (iv) | Create, where possible, new conservation zones in compensation for areas of conservation value which are lost to development |

Items of value are identified in strategic and district planning studies, special surveys by Government and non-government organisations, or from public views. Plans that include conservation use have to take account of the need to provide adequate space for development, territorial growth and sustainable development (§2.2).

**SD takeaway:** Show retained conservation land, compatible neighbours and any compensatory conservation zone in the same planning argument as the development land.

### 2. Designated nature conservation areas (§3.1, §3.4, §3.5.1)
The nature conservation policy is to regulate, protect and manage natural resources that are important for the conservation of biological diversity in a sustainable manner, taking social and economic considerations into account (§3.4.1). In 2016 the Government released the first city-level Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (§3.4.2).

|---|---|
| Land designated for nature conservation | Some 47 percent of the land area, 51 900 ha. The footnote states that this land area includes country parks and special areas, and areas zoned Site of Special Scientific Interest, Conservation Area or Coastal Protection Area on statutory town plans (§3.1, footnote 1) |
| Marine area designated for nature conservation | About 2 percent of the marine area, 3 400 ha (§3.1) |
| Country parks and special areas | 24 country parks (43 467 ha) and 22 special areas, 11 of which (845 ha) are outside country parks. The total area is about 44 312 ha and covers 40% of the land area of Hong Kong (§3.5.1(a)) |

They cover a total marine area of 3 400 ha, the same figure §3.1 gives for the marine area designated for nature conservation (§3.5.1(b)) |
| Ramsar Site | Approximately 1 500 ha of wetland at Mai Po Marshes and Inner Deep Bay, listed on 4 September 1995 (§3.5.1(d)) |
| Hong Kong UNESCO Global Geopark | The Hong Kong United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) Global Geopark has a total land area of over 150 km². It comprises the Sai Kung Volcanic Rock Region and the Northeast New Territories Sedimentary Rock Region, is managed by the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, and is protected under the Country Parks Ordinance and the Marine Parks Ordinance (§3.5.1(g)) |
| Sites of Special Scientific Interest | 67 existing sites, shown on Figure 3 (§3.5.1(h)) |
| Priority Sites | 12 Priority Sites identified in 2004 for enhanced conservation under the New Nature Conservation Policy. Locations and profiles are on the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department website and are not listed in this chapter (§3.5.1(f)) |

The same section records, from the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department as at April 2019, about 2 100 species of native vascular plants, 55 species of terrestrial mammals, over 550 species of birds, 90 species of reptiles, 25 species of amphibians, over 180 species of freshwater fishes, over 240 species of butterflies and 128 species of dragonflies (§3.1, footnote 2). It also records rocks ranging from 400 million to 55 million years old, more than 200 offshore islands, over 2 500 km of natural streams and rivers, and that the Mai Po Inner Deep Bay Ramsar Site supports over 70% of the bird species recorded in Hong Kong (§3.1, §3.2.2, §3.2.3).

| Instrument | Rule stated for development |
|---|---|
| Country parks and special areas | Designated under the Country Parks Ordinance (Cap. 208) and managed by the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department on the advice of the Country and Marine Parks Board. Country parks are for nature conservation, countryside recreation and nature education. Special areas are Government land of special interest by reason of flora, fauna, geological, cultural or archaeological features. Criteria for designation include landscape quality, recreation potential, conservation value, size, land status, land-use compatibility and the practicality of management (§3.5.1(a)) |
| Marine parks and marine reserve | Designated under the Marine Parks Ordinance (Cap. 476). Purposes are conservation, education and recreation (§3.5.1(b)) |
| Restricted Areas | The core of the Mai Po Marshes and the mangroves and inter-tidal mudflat of Inner Deep Bay are scheduled under the Wild Animals Protection Ordinance (Cap. 170). Three Restricted Areas are named: Mai Po Marshes, Yim Tso Ha, and Sham Wan of Lamma Island. The Ordinance restricts access. The Town Planning Board has designated special zones and issued guidelines for planning applications within the Deep Bay area (§3.5.1(c)) |
| Water gathering grounds | Four categories: Lower Direct, Upper Direct, Lower Indirect and Upper Indirect. The Water Supplies Department has specific requirements to control or restrict development and land use. Consult the Water Supplies Department. This section states no separation distance (§3.5.1(e)) |
| Priority Sites | Management Agreement: a non-government organisation may seek Government funding to enter a management agreement with landowners or tenants. Public-Private Partnership: development at an agreed scale may be allowed at the ecologically less sensitive portion if the proponent undertakes to conserve and manage the rest of the site on a long-term basis. The proposal is assessed by the Government and considered by the Advisory Council on the Environment. No scale or site area is printed (§3.5.1(f)) |
| Site of Special Scientific Interest | Land-based or marine sites of special interest because of flora, fauna, geographical or geological features, identified by the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department. The Planning Department maintains the register. Once designated, the zone is shown on statutory town plans and, where appropriate, on non-statutory town plans. Normally no new development will be permitted within the zone unless it is necessary for conservation of the site. Departments should give due consideration when development at or near these sites is proposed. Consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department for any proposed development at or in the proximity of a Site of Special Scientific Interest. No separation distance is stated (§3.5.1(h)) |
| Ecologically Important Streams | Natural streams or rivers with important ecological functions, identified and updated by the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department. For projects that may affect one, consult that department and other relevant authorities first. The text refers to Environment, Transport and Works Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 5/2005 (§3.5.1(i)) |
| Other species controls | The Forests and Countryside Ordinance (Cap. 96) and the Forestry Regulations (Cap. 96A), and the Wild Animals Protection Ordinance (Cap. 170), which prohibits hunting territory-wide and possession of scheduled protected wild animals or hunting appliances. Tree retention, removal and replacement, particularly old and valuable trees on Government land, are dealt with in Chapter 4. Works on Government or private land that affect conservation or tree preservation need prior consent or approval (§3.5.1(k)) |

**SD takeaway:** Use 51 900 ha and 44 312 ha as different printed totals, the first covering conservation zonings as well as country parks, and consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department for any work at or in the proximity of a Site of Special Scientific Interest.

### 3. Conservation zones and the presumption against development (§1.2, §3.6, §3.7)
The Town Planning Ordinance (Cap. 131) empowers the Town Planning Board to prepare plans with zones under section 4(1)(g) for country parks, coastal protection areas, sites of special scientific interest, green belts or other specified uses, to promote conservation or protection of the environment (§3.6.2).

| Zone | Planning intention, as printed |
|---|---|
| “Country Park” | To reflect a country park or special area designated under the Country Parks Ordinance (Cap. 208). All uses and developments require consent from the Country and Marine Parks Authority (§3.6.2(i)) |
| “Coastal Protection Area” | To conserve, protect and retain natural coastlines and the sensitive coastal natural environment, including attractive geological features, physical landform or areas of high landscape, scenic or ecological value, with a minimum of built development, and to cover areas which shelter nearby developments against coastal erosion, with a general presumption against development (§3.6.2(ii)) |
| “Site of Special Scientific Interest” | To conserve and protect the features of special scientific interest and to deter human activities or developments within the Site of Special Scientific Interest, with a general presumption against development (§3.6.2(iii)) |
| “Conservation Area” | To protect and retain the existing natural landscape, ecological or topographical features for conservation, educational and research purposes, and to separate sensitive natural environment such as a Site of Special Scientific Interest or a country park from the adverse effects of development. There is a general presumption against development. The text states no separation distance (§3.6.2(iv)) |
| “Green Belt” | To conserve the existing natural environment amid the built-up areas or at the urban fringe, to safeguard it from encroachment by urban-type development, to define the limits of urban and sub-urban development areas by natural features, to contain urban sprawl, and to provide passive recreational outlets, with a general presumption against development (§3.6.2(v)) |

Development control in the “Country Park” zone is mainly carried out by the Land Authority, the Director of Lands, on advice from the Country and Marine Parks Authority, so as to avoid duplication of statutory authority (§3.6.2).

| Development-control rule | Text |
|---|---|
| Presumption | There is a general presumption against development within areas designated for conservation use. Conservation zones on statutory town plans permit very few uses other than those necessary to manage the resource, and some uses require planning permission from the Town Planning Board (§1.2, §3.7.2) |
| Country parks, special areas, marine parks and the marine reserve | Development will be carefully considered by the Country and Marine Parks Authority and the Land Authority, in consultation with the Country and Marine Parks Authority. For major proposals involving country parks and marine parks, public consultation will be conducted (§3.7.2) |
| Adjoining land | A wildlife Site of Special Scientific Interest may only be sustainable if the wider surroundings remain rural so as to provide feeding grounds, and a wetland may only be sustained if a particular water source is protected. Consider compatibility with the zoning of the site and of adjoining land, and biodiversity in the local area (§3.7.3) |
| Avoidance and exception | Development should normally avoid a declared or potential conservation area. A proposal to amend a conservation zone, or to replace it with a use which permits essential development, may only be considered in exceptional circumstances. It must be clearly shown that alternative sites were considered and rejected for sound reasons, examples given being critical timing, prohibitive cost or technical limitations. Consider salvage and possible off-site compensatory provision, and consult departments so that suitable compensatory sites have been fully explored. Each application is judged on its merits by the relevant advisory body, for example the Advisory Council on the Environment or the Country and Marine Parks Board, and when appropriate by the Town Planning Board (§3.7.6) |
| Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance (Cap. 499) | Designated Projects under Schedules 2 and 3 go through the statutory process unless exempted. Only those listed in Schedule 2 require an environmental permit for construction and operation, if applicable, and decommissioning. The Environmental Protection Department consults the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department on nature conservation and ecological assessment (§3.7.4) |
| Government projects that are not Designated Projects | A Preliminary Environmental Review may be required. The text refers to Environment, Transport and Works Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 13/2003 (§3.7.5) |
| Non-statutory plans | Outline Development Plans and Layout Plans should annotate existing designated conservation zones and potential conservation zones (§3.6.4) |

**SD takeaway:** Treat “Coastal Protection Area”, “Site of Special Scientific Interest”, “Conservation Area” and “Green Belt” as zones with a general presumption against development, and justify any amendment by rejected alternatives plus salvage or off-site compensation.

### 4. Deep Bay wetland zones (§3.6.3)
To regulate development within the wetland area around Mai Po Marshes and Inner Deep Bay near the Ramsar Site, the Outline Zoning Plans introduce these zones. Development within them should also comply with Town Planning Board Guidelines TPB PG-No. 12C, Application for Developments within Deep Bay Area under section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance (§3.6.3). The chapter states no buffer width around the Ramsar Site.

| Zone | Planning intention, as printed |
|---|---|
| “Conservation Area” | To discourage new development unless it is required to support the conservation of the ecological integrity of the wetland ecosystem, or the development is an essential infrastructure project with overriding public interest |
| “Other Specified Uses (Comprehensive Development and Wetland Enhancement Area)” | Conservation and enhancement of the ecological value and functions of the existing fish ponds or wetland, through an application for development or redevelopment under the private-public partnership approach. The “no-net-loss in wetland” principle is adopted for any change of use |
| “Other Specified Uses (Comprehensive Development to include Wetland Restoration Area)” | To provide an incentive to restore degraded wetlands adjoining existing fish ponds through comprehensive residential and/or recreational development that includes a wetland restoration area, and to phase out existing sporadic open storage and port back-up uses on degraded wetlands |
| “Other Specified Uses (Comprehensive Development and Wetland Protection Area)” | Comprehensive low-density residential development or redevelopment may be considered provided that all the existing continuous and contiguous fish ponds within the zone are protected and conserved. The “no-net-loss in wetland” principle is adopted for any change in use. The text states no density figure |

**SD takeaway:** In the two Deep Bay zones that adopt it, demonstrate no net loss of wetland, and follow TPB PG-No. 12C for a section 16 application.

### 5. Declared monuments, historic buildings and archaeology (§4)
The heritage conservation policy is “to protect, conserve and revitalise as appropriate historical and heritage sites and buildings through relevant and sustainable approaches for the benefit and enjoyment of present and future generations”, with due regard to development needs in the public interest, private property rights, budget, cross-sector collaboration and public engagement (§4.2). The concept is to conserve individual items and to respect their wider urban or rural setting (§4.1).

| Control | Text |
|---|---|
| Declaration | Under section 3 of the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance (Cap. 53), the Antiquities Authority, the Secretary for Development, may, after consultation with the Antiquities Advisory Board and with the approval of the Chief Executive, declare a place, building, site or structure of public interest by reason of historical, archaeological or palaeontological significance. “Declared monument” is the legal generic term for all four categories (§4.3) |
| Prohibited acts | No person shall excavate, carry out building or other works, or plant or fell trees, on a declared monument without a permit from the Antiquities Authority, under section 6 (§4.3) |
| Proposed monument | Under sections 2A to 2C, the Antiquities Authority may declare a proposed monument after consultation with the Antiquities Advisory Board. The effective period is 12 months only. Similar statutory controls apply during that period (§4.3, footnote 8) |
| Grading | Grade 1: buildings of outstanding merit, which every effort should be made to preserve if possible. Grade 2: buildings of special merit; efforts should be made to selectively preserve. Grade 3: buildings of some merit; preservation in some form would be desirable, and alternative means should be considered if preservation is not practicable (§4.4.3) |
| Legal effect of grading | The grading system is an administrative mechanism carrying no legal effect. The list of Grade 1 buildings is a pool of highly valuable heritage buildings for the Antiquities Authority to consider actively for declaration. Grade 2, Grade 3 or ungraded buildings may also be suggested for declaration if justifications are sufficient (§4.4.4) |
| Relics | Relics fashioned by man before 1800, and discovered after 1976, belong to the Government. Excavation and search require a licence from the Antiquities Authority (§4.4.5) |
| Statutory plans | The Town Planning Ordinance does not have provisions for the protection of declared monuments, historic buildings, sites of archaeological interest and other heritage items. Plans generally show the wider use in which the item sits. Explanatory Statements should refer to declared and proposed monuments, graded historic buildings and sites of archaeological interest. Prior consultation with the Antiquities and Monuments Office is necessary for any development or rezoning affecting these sites or buildings and their immediate environment. No distance is stated for that environment (§4.5.1) |
| Layout Plans | Show declared monuments, recorded sites of archaeological interest, graded historic buildings and other heritage items (§4.5.2) |
| Setting | A monument, historic building or heritage item is related to the context, setting and visual spectrum within which the site can be experienced. Consideration should take account of visual impact, alteration of the landscape including significant trees, physical intrusion or overshadowing by high buildings, compatibility between uses, air flow, buffer zones, and similar matters. The chapter states no buffer width (§4.6.3) |
| Archaeology | Give preservation full and advance consideration. “Preservation by record”, meaning salvage excavation, will normally only be considered in the most extreme cases, and only when it is proved beyond reasonable doubt that no other alternative exists and that the socio-economic benefits demonstrably and significantly outweigh the cultural significance of the site and its integrity. Conserve the main deposit areas by appropriate zoning, for example passive amenities. If disturbance of a site of archaeological interest or archaeological potential is unavoidable, a detailed Archaeological Impact Assessment by a qualified archaeologist is required. The archaeologist shall apply for a licence under the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance. Submit the assessment proposal to the Antiquities and Monuments Office for agreement before applying for the licence (§4.6.2) |
| Redevelopment | Consult the Antiquities and Monuments Office and the Commissioner for Heritage’s Office. For a declared monument, comply with the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance, and observe the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance on impact assessment. If a historic building falls within a larger redevelopment site, incorporate it into the scheme as far as possible. Seek advice from the Antiquities and Monuments Office and the Architectural Services Department on whether the new design blends with the old in character, scale and visual impact. Where the original use is no longer continued, consider adaptive re-use that conserves the heritage values and significance (§4.6.5) |
| Incentives | The Government recognises the need for economic incentives so that private owners may preserve historic buildings, and will discuss preservation-cum-development options. Incentives are considered case by case, balancing preservation and private property rights (§4.6.1) |
| Capital works | Works agents of new capital works projects are required to check with the Antiquities and Monuments Office whether the project affects heritage sites and thus requires a Heritage Impact Assessment, under Development Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 6/2009 (§4.6.8) |
| Other items | Lamp posts, boundary stones, foundation stones and similar items should be preserved at the early planning stage, in situ where the land-use proposal can blend them in or create a suitable amenities area. Consult the Antiquities and Monuments Office when development affects them (§4.6.4) |

**SD takeaway:** Obtain an Antiquities Authority permit before works on a declared monument, and treat grading as an administrative guide with no legal effect of its own. Consult the Antiquities and Monuments Office on the immediate environment and on buffer zones before massing is fixed. The chapter states no width (§4.5.1, §4.6.3).

### 6. Enforcement (§5.1)
| Authority | What the chapter assigns |
|---|---|
| Country and Marine Parks Authority and/or the Land Authority | Enforcement within country parks, marine parks, the marine reserve and special areas. The Land Authority acts in consultation with the Country and Marine Parks Authority |
| Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department | Forests and Countryside Ordinance and Wild Animals Protection Ordinance |
| Environmental Protection Department | Offences under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance |
| Planning Authority, the Director of Planning | Enforcement and prosecution against unauthorized developments in areas covered by a Development Permission Area plan, or by an Outline Zoning Plan which has replaced that plan. A Stop Notice may be issued against the landowner, occupier or person responsible if the development would constitute a health or safety hazard, adversely affect the environment, or make it impracticable or uneconomical to reinstate the land within a reasonable period |
| Priority | Priority actions would be taken against unauthorized developments within conservation zones, such as “Site of Special Scientific Interest” and “Conservation Area” |
| Antiquities and Monuments Office | Protection of declared monuments under the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance |

**SD takeaway:** Assume unauthorized work in a “Site of Special Scientific Interest” or “Conservation Area” is a priority enforcement case under the Town Planning Ordinance, and route monument control through the Antiquities and Monuments Office.
