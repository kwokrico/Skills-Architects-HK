# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 1 Introduction
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
July 2018 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This chapter explains what the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines (HKPSG) is, why it exists, and how it is applied in planning studies, preparation and revision of town plans, and development control (§1.1, §1.2, §5). It is a planning guideline manual, not the Buildings Ordinance, and it does not itself set the facility standards contained in later chapters.

## Regulatory Overview
The HKPSG is a Government manual of criteria for the scale, location and site requirements of land uses and facilities, used at strategic and district planning and in development statements and planning briefs (§1.1, §5.1). The standards and guidelines are neither statutory nor rigid, and this introductory chapter states no plot ratio, site area or facility threshold of its own (§8.2).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Status of the manual (§1.1, §3.1–§3.5, §8.2)
| Role stated in the chapter | What an architect may use it for |
|---|---|
| Forward planning | Land-use budget from standards and socio-economic forecasts; locational guidelines for uses and facilities (§3.2) |
| Development regulation | Guidance on scale, intensity and site requirements, and on supporting facilities (§3.3) |
| Plan implementation | Yardstick for whether land and facilities are sufficient, and for programming community facilities (§3.4) |
| Quality of life | Guidelines on environmental planning, natural landscape and habitats, and cultural heritage and townscape (§3.5) |
| Legal status | Neither statutory nor rigid. Effectiveness depends on flexible application. Where appropriate, a standard may be written into lease conditions or made a condition of planning permission granted by the Town Planning Board (§8.2) |

**SD takeaway:** Do not lock a schematic to an HKPSG figure until that figure is written into the statutory plan, the lease, or a Town Planning Board permission.

### 2. Standards, guidelines, and what this manual does not cover (§4.1–§4.4, §6.2.8)
| Item | Rule in this chapter |
|---|---|
| Planning standards | Provision of land uses, community facilities and infrastructure according to population size, expressed as threshold requirements. Usually a minimum is stipulated; for some facilities both upper and lower limits are given (§4.1) |
| Planning guidelines | Locational criteria, compatibility between uses, development intensity and design guidelines, for general reference, assuming the least development constraints (§4.2) |
| Chapter coverage | Residential densities; community facilities; recreational facilities, open space and greening; industrial land; retail facilities; utility services; internal transport facilities; environmental planning; conservation; urban design guidelines; and miscellaneous standards including underground rock cavern development, petrol filling stations, potentially hazardous installations, vehicle repair workshops, port back-up and open storage, and land use beneath flyovers and footbridges (§4.3) |
| Excluded uses | No standards or guidelines for uses of territorial significance or unique uses and facilities, such as airports, museums and universities. Provision and location of those are subject to individual investigations and other internationally recognised criteria (§4.4) |
| Document structure | Each chapter is independent, with its own page, part and paragraph numbering (§6.2.8) |

**SD takeaway:** Do not look to the HKPSG for a site standard for an airport, museum or university; those uses need their own investigation.

### 3. How the guidelines are applied (§5.1–§5.5)
| Rule | Text |
|---|---|
| Levels | Strategic planning: total land requirements and territorial distribution. District planning: specific areas and sites and their arrangement. Also used for development statements for special areas and planning briefs for large-scale projects (§5.1) |
| Flexibility | Apply with regard to land-use demand, local conditions, development constraints and resource availability. Do not apply one standard in isolation; cross-refer between standards and guidelines where necessary (§5.2) |
| Trade-offs | Standards compete for scarce land and money. Trade-offs may be needed so that the community benefits most (§5.3) |
| Deviation | The reason for any proposed deviation must be explained in the submission of the town plan or development project. If the approving authority is not satisfied, a review may be required (§5.4) |
| Reservations versus programmes | A site reservation on a town plan according to a planning standard does not automatically justify putting the facility into a development programme. Inclusion and priority need separate justification under the Government resource-allocation system (§5.5) |

**SD takeaway:** If the scheme departs from an HKPSG standard, state the reason in the submission, and do not treat a plan reservation as a funded works item.
