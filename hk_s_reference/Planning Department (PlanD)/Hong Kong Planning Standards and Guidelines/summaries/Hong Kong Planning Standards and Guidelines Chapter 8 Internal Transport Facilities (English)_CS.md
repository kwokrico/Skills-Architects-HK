# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 8 Internal Transport Facilities
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
December 2025 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This chapter sets planning standards for rail, roads, public transport terminals, walkability, cycling, and parking and loading/unloading (§1–§7, Tables 1–11). It is a planning guideline, not the Buildings Ordinance. Public-road widths follow the Transport Planning and Design Manual (TPDM); private streets that are not part of the public road system follow the Building (Private Streets and Access Roads) Regulations (§1.2.2, §3.2.4). The December 2025 revision notes update Section 5 on walkability and Section 7 on the minimum headroom of coach and bus parking spaces in new developments.

## Regulatory Overview
Rail is to form the backbone of passenger public transport, and new population and employment centres are to be planned onto existing and proposed railway lines, with roads planned to complement the rail system (§1.1, §2.1, Appendix 1). The widths, terminal sizes and parking ranges in this chapter are guidelines for land and layout; geometric detail is referred to the TPDM, and the Commissioner for Transport decides the parking provision, including within and, where justified, beyond or below the Table 11 ranges (§1.2, §7.1.4, Table 11 Statement of Intent).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Rail (§2.1–§2.5)
No track width, curvature or station size is printed. Those dimensions are subject to feasibility studies approved by the Transport Department, the Highways Department and the relevant committees (§2.3.2).

| Rule | Figure in this chapter |
|---|---|
| Station or public transport interchange to major housing, employment, shopping, commercial, cultural and other intensive centres | Preferably within a walking distance of 500 m, with a planned pedestrian walkway (§2.4.2) |
| Longer walk, up to 1 000 m | Consider a travellator or moving walkway early in planning (§2.4.2) |
| Route protection | Administrative route protection plans issued by the Railway Development Office of the Highways Department, before gazettal. Refer matters that may affect a protection zone to that office. The intention is not to freeze development (§2.5) |

Noise separation from rail is not given in this chapter. It points to Chapter 9 and the Environmental Protection Department (§2.4.3). Designated projects follow the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance (§1.2.1).

**SD takeaway:** Place intensive uses within 500 m walk of a station, and do not draw a rail corridor width from this chapter.

### 2. Roads: hierarchy, carriageway and reserve widths (§3, Tables 1–7)

The widths below are not absolute maxima or minima and may be adjusted for speed, cost, environment and safety (§3.1.5). No separate hard-shoulder width is printed in this chapter.

### 3. Public transport terminals (§4)
Off-street is the rule for bus termini and public transport interchanges. A service terminal point may be on-street only temporarily if it does not obstruct circulation (§4.1.3).

| Facility | Provision printed in this chapter |
|---|---|
| Bus service terminal point | Usually 1 or 2 routes, and departure bays unlikely to exceed four. Regulator’s kiosk, staff restroom and toilet (§4.1.5) |
| Bus terminus | Usually in large residential developments and local commercial or industrial areas. Normally a minimum of 4 departure bays, one wide enough to overtake a stationary vehicle. Same staff facilities (§4.1.6) |
| Bus bay | On rural roads, feeder roads, primary distributors, and 2-lane two-way single-carriageway district and local distributors. Increase the Table 1 or Table 4 carriageway width by 3.25 m. Length for one route normally 40 m (§4.1.12) |
| Bus stop spacing | Normally 400 m apart in urban areas. A longer spacing may be acceptable in rural areas (§4.1.13) |
| Multi-storey bus depot | Ramps must not exceed 1 in 10. Preferred width at least 80 m. Reasonably efficient regular depot: 8 000 m² to 10 000 m², after dead space for ramps. Provided under section 19 of the Public Bus Services Ordinance (§4.1.15) |
| Green minibus terminus | Preferably off-street. On average a 3-bay terminus, each bay for 3 vehicles. Minimum land about 800 m² (§4.2.3) |
| On-street minibus lay-by | Minimum length 21 m, preferably 6 m wide so a stationary vehicle can be overtaken and a fire appliance can operate (§4.2.4) |
| Taxi stand, off-street | Single or dual bay at heavy demand. About 500 m² for a single bay holding up to 5 vehicles, including access and turnaround. Prefer double bays. Dropped kerb for wheelchair users (§4.3.1, §4.3.2, §4.3.7) |
| Passenger ferry waiting area | Normally 1½ boatloads, assuming 500 to 1 500 passengers per boat depending on vessel type, at a minimum of 0.65 m² of waiting area per person (§4.4.3) |
| Public transport interchange | Normally not less than 8 departure bays. For parallel bays, two bays wide enough to overtake a stationary vehicle. Actual size is determined by the Transport Department (§4.5.4) |

Public transport interchange layout (§4.5.3(a)):

| Layout | Site rule in the text |
|---|---|
| Traditional parallel bays | A small site with only a limited number of bays, for example 3 to 4 |
| Peripheral saw-tooth bay with central stacking | Minimum site width 60 m, otherwise buses cannot manoeuvre. Major pedestrian generator at the same level. Development above needs large column spacing |
| Central island passenger platform | Minimum site width 60 m. Major pedestrian generator at a different level |

For major interchanges on a railway, prefer integration into the station concourse, or otherwise a direct link. Saw-tooth layout is to be pursued as far as the site allows. Waiting areas of new multi-modal interchanges should be considered for air-conditioning. Air pollution in a semi-confined interchange is referred to the Environmental Protection Department Practice Note for Professional Persons ProPECC PN 1/98 (§4.5.3).

Cross-boundary coach termini are preferably off-street inside a public transport interchange, with dedicated loading bays and luggage-related facilities. Size and scale are referred to the TPDM; no bay dimension is printed in this section (§4.6).

**SD takeaway:** Allow a 60 m minimum site width before choosing a saw-tooth interchange, and add 3.25 m to the carriageway wherever a bus bay is required.

### 4. Walkability and footpath widths (§5, Tables 8 and 9)
Walkability is defined as the extent to which the city gives priority to pedestrians and encourages walking, through the four pillars of making walking connected, safe, enjoyable and smart (§5.3.1, §5.4.3). Apply the standards with flexibility for the site and the district (§5.2.3).

| Rule | Figure |
|---|---|
| Walking catchment of a rail station or other key public transport node | 500 m (§5.4.1(a)). The chapter also cites TPDM Volume 6 Chapter 10 Section 10.4.2: 300–500 m, or 10 to 15 minutes, as the appropriate regime for most day-to-day travel, with allowance for slope and a hot wet climate |
| Street block or sub-block length | Preferably less than 150 m (§5.4.1(c), §5.5.35) |
| Through Zone level of service | Upper end of Level-of-Service C, 23 pedestrians per minute per metre, as the minimum acceptable standard for the Through Zone where applicable (§5.4.14) |
| Through Zone clear width, urban land uses | 3 m wherever possible, for two-way flow including wheelchair users (§5.4.21, §5.5.46) |
| Footpath that also serves emergency and service vehicles | Generally increase the footpath width to 6 m, and design for the vehicle loading (§5.4.23) |
| If the three-zone total cannot be achieved | Minimum footpath clear width of 3 m (Table 8, note [3]) |
| Shared-surface street | Operating speed below 25 km/h (§5.5.13) |
| Low speed limit zone | 30 km/h or below. If the mean speed before the zone is above 35 km/h, consider raised crossings and kerb build-outs as well as signs (§5.5.16, §5.5.17) |
| Building canopy | Minimum width 2 m, preferred width 2.5 m, vertical clearance preferably 3.5 m (§5.5.27) |
| Stand-alone covered walkway | Pedestrian flow at least 3 000 pedestrians per hour for not less than one hour on a weekday, except that each public hospital gets one covered walkway to major public transport regardless of flow (§5.5.28) |
| Hillside escalator link | Not recommended where the level difference is less than 6 m, or the gradient is less than 1:8, or land or construction is inadequate (§5.5.33) |
| Utility crossing a Street Furniture and Greening Zone | Minimum depth of 2 m below ground where possible, and at least 2 m from the centre of the nearest tree (§5.4.25) |

Footpaths on public roads use three zones: Through Zone, Street Furniture and Greening Zone, and Building Frontage Zone (§5.4.12). Table 8 prints a full three-zone set only for the commercial group. For the other land uses, only the Through Zone width and the projected volume category extracted; Building Frontage Zone and Street Furniture and Greening Zone cells for those rows are omitted.

Other Table 8 notes that did extract: if a bus shelter exceeds 1 m in width, allow up to an additional 2 m in the Street Furniture and Greening Zone. Street furniture wider than the normal 1 m object, such as a gateway or artwork, requires that zone to be widened. For large trees or understorey planting on a boulevard or main road, increase that zone to the range of 3 m to 4 m. Tree planting follows Development Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 3/2024 and the Guidelines on Soil Volume for Urban Trees.

Table 9 sets objectives for full-time and part-time pedestrian streets, shared-surface streets and traffic-calming streets. It prints tests, not widths. Full-time streets give pedestrians absolute priority, with emergency access preserved. Part-time streets have no on-street parking and use lay-bys for loading. Shared-surface streets flush the footpath with the carriageway. The speed figure for shared surfaces is in §5.5.13, not in Table 9.

**SD takeaway:** Draw the Through Zone at the Table 8 width, and do not drop an urban Through Zone below 3 m clear unless the three-zone total truly cannot be achieved, in which case the chapter still requires 3 m clear.

### 5. Cycling (§6, Table 10)
Cycling is described as essentially recreational, and also as a supplementary short-distance mode in some new development areas where geography and road safety allow (§6.1.1). Segregate cycle traffic from pedestrians by kerb and/or railings where possible; otherwise require cyclists to dismount on the shared section (§6.2.4).

Capacity used to set width: 500 bicycles per metre width per hour for one-way operation, and 400 for two-way operation (§6.3.1).

Table 10. Cycle track widths.

| Operation | Desirable | Minimum |
|---|---|---|
| One-way | 2.8 m | 2.0 m |
| Two-way | 4.0 m | 3.5 m |

|---|---|

| Crossing | At right angles to the kerb (§6.3.2) |
| Gradient at subways and footbridges | Desirable maximum 3%; normal maximum 5%; up to 10% for a short distance in exceptional circumstances (§6.3.3) |
| Horizontal radius | Preferably not less than 5 m; 2 m may be acceptable in difficult conditions (§6.3.4) |
| Forward sight | Preferably 25 m, not less than 15 m (§6.3.5) |
| Rest areas on popular recreational routes | About 1 km intervals, with bicycle parking (§6.3.6) |
| Residential bicycle parking where a proper cycle track connects directly to a rail station, flats smaller than 70 m² | Within a 0.5–2 km radius of a rail station: 1 space per 15 flats. Outside a 2 km radius: 1 space per 30 flats (§6.5.2) |

**SD takeaway:** A two-way cycle track needs 3.5 m minimum, plus at least 1.8 m of verge to the carriageway, and the flat-size parking rates apply only where a proper track connects the housing to a station.

### 6. Parking and loading, including coach and bus headroom (§7, Table 11)
Parking should not unduly attract people from public transport to private vehicles. Developments close to railway stations and large public transport interchanges should adopt the lower end of the standards (§7.1.1). The Authority for parking standards is the Commissioner for Transport; for leases, the Director of Lands; for building plans, the Building Authority; for planning briefs, the District Planning Conference (§7.1.4, Table 11 General Note (a)). The minimum provision agreed by the Authority shall not normally count as gross floor area; any higher provision may count. The agreed standard should, where appropriate, go into the lease (General Note (b)). Different uses on one site are cumulative, with a possible reduction where peaks fall at different times (General Note (e)).

All parking spaces of a new development, including private cars, motorcycles, goods vehicles, light buses and coaches, are encouraged to be electric-vehicle charging-enabling. This does not apply to on-street spaces or temporary outdoor vehicle parks. Gross floor area concession is described for car parks that meet the Environment and Ecology Bureau Technical Guidelines and Practice Note for Authorized Persons, Registered Structural Engineers and Registered Geotechnical Engineers APP-2 (§7.2.7–§7.2.10).
