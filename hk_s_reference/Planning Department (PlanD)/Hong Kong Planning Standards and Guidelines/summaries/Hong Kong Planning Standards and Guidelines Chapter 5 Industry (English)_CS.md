# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 5 Industry
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
March 2007 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This March 2007 chapter sets the industrial land-use typology and the parameters for planning new industrial areas: plot ratio, worker density, lot size, site coverage, setback, fire-appliance access, parking and loading, and open-space and landscape layout (§2.1–§2.2, §8, §12–§17). It is a planning guideline. Centralised public open space is to follow Chapter 4, and general transport design is to follow Chapter 8 (§15.1, §16.1, §17.7). Figures 3.1 to 3.7, 4, 5.1, 5.2, 6 and 7.1 to 7.9 did not yield text and are not described here.

## Regulatory Overview
The March 2007 chapter gives maximum average plot ratios, worker densities, lot sizes, site coverages, setbacks, fire-appliance access, parking and loading standards, and landscape dimensions for general industrial, business and special industrial land (§8, §12–§17, Tables 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7). Those development-parameter guidelines are mainly for new industrial areas and, where possible, for incremental use when sizable existing industrial properties are comprehensively redeveloped (§2.2).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Land-use types this chapter plans for (§5–§7, Figure 1)
“Industrial Use”, as adopted from the Town Planning Board, covers manufacture and related processes, storage and cargo handling, and training, research, design, quality control and packaging related to those processes (§5.1). General Industrial Use covers multi-storey factories, purpose-built godowns and industrial/office buildings. Special Industrial Use covers industrial estates, science parks, rural-based industry and other special industries (§7.2).

| Type | Zone named in the chapter | Use rule that affects a scheme |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial (“I”) | “Industrial” on statutory and non-statutory plans | Multi-storey factories for light manufacturing, and multi-storey warehouses. Office related to industrial use is always permitted. A trading firm that needs frequent loading and storage of not less than 30% of its total usable floor area in the same premises is permitted as of right (§7.2.1–§7.2.3) |
| Industrial/office | Within the “I” zone | Industrial and industrial-related uses. No size limit on an office that is directly related to an industrial operation. Trading firms with a minimum of 30% of usable floor area used for storage are allowed as of right (§7.2.4–§7.2.5) |
| Lower floors of an existing industrial or industrial/office building | “I”, and the same buffer rule is repeated for the “Business” zone | A wide range of commercial uses may be always permitted in the purpose-designed non-industrial portion on the lower floors, except basements, if a buffer floor or floors separates them from industrial uses above and no industrial use is inside that portion (§7.2.6, §7.5) |
| Industrial estate | “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Industrial Estate only” | Industries with new or improved technology that cannot operate in a multi-storey factory or a commercial building (§7.2.7) |
| Science park | “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Science Park only” | Research, new technology and product development. Ancillary manufacturing may be permitted on the merits of the case. Service apartments may be provided for visiting research staff (§7.2.8–§7.2.11) |
| Rural-based industry | “Industrial (Group D)” | Low-rise workshops needing open land. Other than land already in this zone, no new “Industrial (Group D)” land should be encouraged (§7.2.12–§7.2.13) |
| Other special industry | “Other Specified Uses” with the appropriate annotation | Capital-intensive, land-extensive uses that may need deep water, waterfront access, bulk storage, or heavy water use (§7.2.14) |
| Business | “Business”, and §7.6 also says “OU(Business)” | A mix of information technology and telecommunications, non-polluting industrial, office and other commercial uses is always permitted in new business buildings. Polluting industrial uses, including offensive trades, are not permitted. In existing industrial and industrial/office buildings in the zone, only less fire-hazard-prone office use that does not serve customers or goods directly to the public is permitted as of right (§7.3–§7.6) |

The footnote to §7.5 defines non-polluting industrial use as industrial use that is not detrimental to occupants or amenity by noise, waste water, vibration, smell, fume, smoke, soot, ash, dust or grit, and it points to the pollution-control ordinances and to Chapter 9.

Figure 1 prints labels only: general industrial use covers industrial and godown for light manufacturing and storage, and industrial/office and godown for industrial-related activities; special industrial use covers industrial estate (high-tech industry), science park (technology-based service industry), rural-based industrial use (informal industry), and other industrial uses with special requirements (special industry). The drawing is not described.

Appendix 1 lists example businesses for each type. It states no plot ratio or floor area.

Common location tests (§10.1) are flat land, access to port, inland container depot or airport, access to trunk roads, access to urban business centres, utilities, siting to avoid environmental effects on sensitive uses, and siting to avoid country parks, water catchments and other sensitive areas. Industrial and industrial/office uses should not immediately adjoin housing unless a commercial or industrial/office building is the buffer, should preferably be in the western quadrant of residential areas so as to be downwind for most of the year, and should preferably be close to cross-border highway or rail links (§10.3). The same western-quadrant preference is stated for industrial estates and for special industries (§10.4, §10.7).

Preferred minimum site areas in the location section: industrial estate about 100 ha; science park about 20 ha; rural-based industry about 5 ha. Special industry is “as per functional requirements” (§10.4–§10.7). Table 4, below, is the lot and coverage table and also prints minimum site areas.

**SD takeaway:** Keep a trading firm in an industrial or industrial/office building only where storage is at least 30% of that firm’s usable floor area, and separate commercial lower floors from industry above by a buffer floor.

### 2. Plot ratio and worker density (Tables 1 and 2, §8–§9)
Table 1 is the guideline for maximum average plot ratio and the range of permissible plot ratios. The ranges are for reference. An individual plot is set inside the range, with local adjustments for height, density, approach roads and infrastructure (§8.2). Use Table 1 for land-grant or lease-modification conditions and for proposed redevelopment sites. For an already developed site that is not under lease modification, the plot ratio in the Building (Planning) Regulations or the lease applies, subject to other statutory limits (§8.3).

Section 9 converts floor-space forecasts to land with three assumptions: internal floor area to gross floor area, the Table 1 plot ratios, and the Table 3 net-to-gross ratios. Industrial-estate and science-park land continues to be estimated by the Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks Corporation. **SD takeaway:** For a new industrial area, lock the district average to the Table 1 maximum for that area, keep each plot inside the printed range, and estimate workers from Table 2 only until a project-specific density exists.

### 3. Site area, lot size, coverage and setback (Table 4, §10, §12–§13)
Table 4 prints the recommended minimum site area, maximum site coverage, lot size, minimum setback and general building height. Two coverage cells join a note marker to the digits: Industrial Use prints “1001”, and Industrial Estate prints “402 - 60”. Note 1, printed separately, says the 100% site coverage should be restricted to a podium height not exceeding 15 m above ground level under the Building (Planning) Regulations, and that tower coverage above the podium should not exceed the First Schedule of those Regulations. Note 2 says a minimum site coverage is stipulated for industrial estates because of the comparatively low land price. This summary does not rewrite “402 - 60” as a separated percentage range.

| Land use | Minimum site area | Maximum site coverage as printed | Recommended lot size | Minimum setback as printed | Height |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Industrial | 10 ha | 100, cell printed as “1001”, note 1 | 2 700 m² (45 m frontage × 60 m depth) | 4.5 side setback and 7.5 front and rear setback above podium level. The cell does not name the unit | High rise |
| Industrial/office | 5 ha | 100 | 2 700 m² (45 m frontage × 60 m depth) | As per Industrial Use | High rise |
| Industrial estate | 100 ha | Cell printed as “402 - 60”, note 2 | 5 000 m²–40 000 m² (62.5 m frontage × 80 m depth) | 4.6 m on every boundary that abuts another industrial lot, and 7.6 m on all other boundaries | Low rise |
| Science park | 20 ha | 65 | 5 000 m²–20 000 m² (62.5 m frontage × 80 m depth). Note 3: guidance only, and a smaller lot is allowed | 5 m on every boundary that abuts another industrial lot, and 10 m on all other boundaries | Low to medium rise |
| Rural-based industrial use | 5 ha | 80 | 1 000 m². No frontage is printed | Vary according to operational requirements | Low rise, 13 m maximum |
| Other special industry | The whole row says vary according to operational requirements | | | | |

Lot-size objectives are parking and loading with manoeuvring, an efficient building, the density in the guidelines, and room for front, rear and side setbacks where a setback is desirable (§12.2). Coverage and setback objectives are streetscape, breezeways, queuing inside the lot, and landscaping (§13.2).

Dangerous-goods stores in multi-storey factories may be not more than 30 m above the surrounding street level. Quantities are restricted to 2 500 litres for Category 5, Class 3 liquids, or 1 350 litres for other substances; solids 900 kg; gas other than liquefied petroleum gas is considered on the merits of the application (§10.8(a)). Storage above the exempted quantity needs a licence from the Director of Fire Services. Liquefied petroleum gas above the exempted quantity needs the Director of Electrical and Mechanical Services. The exempted quantity itself is not given a separate figure. Bulk dangerous-goods godowns need an island site, or an “island” site inside the lot, with a sterile area 6 m wide from any lot boundary line to the building line (§10.8(e)).

**SD takeaway:** Size a general industrial lot at not less than 2 700 m² with a 45 m frontage and 60 m depth, and keep 100% site coverage to a podium not higher than 15 m above ground.

### 4. Fire-appliance access and roads (Table 5, §14–§15)
Each industrial lot in a new industrial area should have two vehicular access roads meeting Figure 4. Figure 4 is an image page. The prose dimensions are:

| Item | Dimension |
|---|---|
| Dual carriageway with a central median, one-way | Minimum 7.3 m wide |
| Carriageway on a curve of radius less than 150 m | At least 7.9 m (§15.6) |
| Two-lane two-way carriageway without a central divider | Minimum 13.5 m (§14.1(a), §15.7) |
| Landscaped median, general industrial uses | At least 5 m |
| Landscaped median, industrial estates and science parks | At least 2 m |
| Standard emergency vehicular access | 9 m wide. An access narrower than 9 m is only where no purpose-built road can be provided, and it should not be less than 6 m |
| Distance from emergency-vehicular-access or kerb edge to the building facade | Should not exceed 10 m |
| Hard-paved footpath immediately adjacent to the lot boundary | 6 m wide, capable of withstanding 20-tonne loading |
| Emergency crash gates | At least 11 m from the access point. §17.4(a) says, for industrial and industrial/office areas, 11 m from either side of an access point |
| Reserve inside industrial-estate and science-park lots | A designated emergency vehicular access not less than 6 m wide in the setback, to be included in the lease |

Public roads are preferred to a designated emergency vehicular access. Where an emergency vehicular access is used, it is to be formed to Highways Department pavement loadings and managed as a public road (§14.1(b)). Figures 5.1, 5.2 and 6 are image pages. For existing industrial areas the extract points to Figure 6 and says the new-area standards could be used for comprehensive redevelopment. Consult the Fire Services Department before an industrial layout is circulated (§14.2).

Every lot should have double frontage. New industrial areas do not use service roads, because parking and loading stay inside the site. Cul-de-sacs should be avoided if possible (§11.2, §15.4, §15.8). Local roads giving principal access are dual carriageways. Roads giving secondary access, with no site entrance, are single carriageways: minimum 10.3 m for two-way and 7.3 m for one-way (§15.9–§15.10).

Table 5, minimum total width of roads giving principal access:

| Land use | Carriageway | Median | Roadside planters | Footpath through zone | Minimum total width |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Industrial | Two lanes, 7.3 m each | 5 m | 4 m | 4.5 m | 36.6 m |
| Industrial/office | Two lanes, 7.3 m each | 5 m | 4 m | 4.5 m | 36.6 m |
| Industrial estate | Two lanes, 7.3 m each | 2 m | 2 m | 3.5 m | 27.6 m |
| Science park | Two lanes, 7.3 m each | 2 m | 2 m | 3.5 m | 27.6 m |
| Rural-based industrial use | 7.3 m | Not applicable | 1.5 m | 2.5 m | 15.3 m |
| Other special industry | Vary as per functional requirements | Not applicable | 2 m | Not applicable | Not applicable |

§15.12 states the same footpath through-zone widths: 4.5 m for general industrial uses, 3.5 m for special industrial uses, and 2.5 m for rural-based industry, and it refers the definition of through zone to Chapter 8, section 5.8 and Table 9. It also states a planting and street-furniture strip of minimum 4 m for general industrial uses and 2 m for special industrial uses, except rural-based industrial areas. Table 5 prints the rural-based planter as 1.5 m. Additional space for a footpath along the building edge is referred to Table 9 of Chapter 8. Those Chapter 8 widths are not printed here.

**SD takeaway:** Give every new industrial lot two frontages, a 7.3 m carriageway each way, and keep the building facade within 10 m of the kerb, with a 6 m hardstanding at the boundary that can take a 20-tonne load.

### 5. Parking and loading (Table 6, §16)
On-street parking is not required in new industrial and business areas. Parking and loading are inside the site, with barriers or planters against on-street parking (§16.2). Chapter 8 is the general internal-transport reference, and the Transport Planning and Design Manual, Volume 7, is the design reference. Where other supporting facilities are provided, Chapter 8 standards apply (Table 6, note 1). No Chapter 8 ratio is printed in this table.

Design standards in the notes: private-car turning circle 7.5 m outer radius; goods-vehicle turning circle 11.5 m outer radius. A container bay, where required, has a turning circle of 11.6 m outer radius and is for a site not less than 45 m × 40 m. On a smaller site the container bay is case by case with the Transport Department. Gross floor area includes a part of the stated area. Goods-vehicle provision is 65% light goods vehicles and 35% heavy goods vehicles.

General Industrial Use and “OU(B)” business buildings:

| Development | Private cars | Goods vehicles and other bays |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial | 1 per 1 000–1 200 m² gross floor area. The extract splits this range over two lines as “1 per 1 000” and “-1 200m² GFA” | 1 goods-vehicle bay per 700–900 m² gross floor area, 50% of them for parking goods vehicles. One container bay if the site is at least 45 m × 40 m |
| Industrial/office | 1 per 600–750 m² gross floor area | 1 goods-vehicle bay per 1 000–1 200 m² of 50% of the industrial/office gross floor area, and 1 per 2 000–3 000 m² of the remaining 50%. 50% of those bays are for parking goods vehicles. Separately, 1 goods-vehicle bay per 800–1 200 m² of commercial gross floor area, for loading and unloading only. One container bay if the site is at least 45 m × 40 m |
| Business-zone industrial building | 1 per 600–750 m² gross floor area | 1 goods-vehicle bay per 1 000–1 200 m² of 50% of the gross floor area, and 1 per 2 000–3 000 m² of the remaining 50%. 50% of those bays are for parking. One container bay if the site is at least 45 m × 40 m. This row does not repeat the commercial-floor bay |
| Business-zone industrial/office building | 1 per 600–750 m² gross floor area | Same split as the industrial/office row, including 1 per 800–1 200 m² of commercial gross floor area for loading and unloading only, and the container bay |
| Office building in the business table | 1 per 150–200 m² for the first 15 000 m² gross floor area, then 1 per 200–300 m² for the remaining gross floor area | 1 goods-vehicle bay per 2 000–3 000 m² gross floor area. For a site of at least 5 000 m² net site area, 1 taxi and private-car lay-by for every 20 000 m², or part thereof, of gross floor area |
| Business building | 1 per 200–300 m² gross floor area | 1 goods-vehicle bay per 800–1 200 m² gross floor area, 50% for parking goods vehicles. At least one taxi and private-car lay-by where the net site area is at least 5 000 m². One container bay if the site is at least 45 m × 40 m |

Special Industrial Use. The industrial-estate private-car and lorry columns are printed as one sentence, not as two separate ratios.

| Type | Parking as printed |
|---|---|
| Industrial estate | 1 per 900 m² gross floor area, or 1 per 450 m² site area, whichever is the greater. Of the spaces, 50% for private cars and light vans, and 50% for parking and loading of lorries. One container bay if the site is at least 45 m × 40 m. This is a minimum. The Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks Corporation assesses the actual number site by site (note 5) |
| Science park | 1 per 75 m² gross floor area, 75% cars and 25% vans. Lorries: 1 per 5 000 m² gross floor area. Container vehicles: nil. The heavy-goods-vehicle provision does not apply (note 6) |
| Rural-based industrial use | 1 parking space per establishment, or 1 per 900 m² gross floor area of the establishment, whichever is the greater, for lorry and visitor parking. The extract does not split this sentence into private-car, lorry and container columns |
| Other special industry | As per functional needs |

A further note under the special-industrial table says 50% of all the required goods-vehicle bays shall be for parking of goods vehicles (note 2 of that table).

**SD takeaway:** For a new industrial building, provide private cars at 1 per 1 000–1 200 m² and goods-vehicle bays at 1 per 700–900 m² inside the lot, and add a container bay only when the site is at least 45 m by 40 m.

### 6. Open space, landscape, and the factory canteen cap (Tables 7 and 8, §17–§18)
This chapter does not print a square-metre-per-worker open-space standard. Centralised public open space is to be provided in accordance with Chapter 4, with an overall masterplan (§17.7). Other open space is to form a network back to that central space, with tree planting tied to street trees. Open space in an industrial layout should be mainly passive, such as sitting-out areas and parks (§11.3(d)). An overall landscape masterplan is required for every type except rural-based industry (§17.3).

Table 7:

| Land use | Landscape masterplan | Median planting strip | Raised roadside planters | Open space provision | Treatment of individual lots | Estate boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Industrial | Yes | 5 m | 4 m | Yes | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| Industrial/office | Yes | 5 m | 4 m | Yes | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| Industrial estate | Yes | 2 m | 2 m | Yes | 2 m planting strip | Yes |
| Science park | Yes | 2 m | 2 m | Yes | 2 m planting strip | Yes |
| Rural-based industrial use | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable | Yes |
| Other special industry | Yes | Not applicable | 2 m | Yes | Not applicable | Yes |

§17.4 repeats the planter widths measured from the inside face: minimum 4 m for industrial and industrial/office areas, and 2 m for industrial estates and science parks. Median strips are minimum 5 m for industrial and industrial/office, and 2 m for industrial estates and science parks. Raised planters should have an open bottom and decompacted subsoil. A 2 m wide planting strip is required in the setback of industrial-estate and science-park lots (§17.5(c)). Science-park boundaries should use landscape rather than walls. Estate-boundary planting width should be between 6 m and 7 m minimum (§17.6). Figures 7.1 to 7.9 are image pages.

Layout-concept titles in the contents, for Figures 3.1 to 3.7, are text. The drawings are image-only: good streetscape for all types; a single large open space for all types; landscaped podia for industrial and industrial/office uses; interlinking shared open space, and a shared open-space courtyard, for industrial estate, science park and business; an access road of minimum width, and a waiting lay-by along a major distributor, for rural-based industry. One image page prints the words “(4) Acceptable” and nothing further.

Factory canteens (§18.5): they are not permitted on areas the lease sets aside for access, parking, loading or unloading. They need no exclusive direct street access, except for emergency use. The canteen area should not exceed 10% of the total gross floor area of the factory building on the lot. A complying canteen may remain for the life of the existing building. Cooked-food-centre standards are referred to Chapter 6, and no ratio is printed here. Hotels may be considered adjoining or inside “Industrial” and “Business” areas. No hotel plot ratio or site area is stated (§18.7).

Table 8 is a checklist of supporting facilities, marked centralised or dispersed. The star grid is layout-broken, so which star belongs to which land use is not readable as a clean cell. The facility names that are readable are medical clinic, post office, day nursery, canteen, cooked food centre, fast food shop, restaurant, supermarket, convenience store, clothing and footwear, consumer goods, durable goods, bank, automated teller machine, centralised telephone systems, reception, meeting rooms, equipment, secretarial services, bookkeeping, accounting, convention and exhibition centre, fuel, machinery, raw materials, motor vehicle services, staff quarters, service apartments, hotel, indoor sports, outdoor sports, and club. For other special industry the table says provision is as per functional needs. The list is not exhaustive (§18.1).

**SD takeaway:** Take the worker open-space quantity from Chapter 4, and inside this chapter lock the industrial median at 5 m, the industrial-estate and science-park setback planting at 2 m, the estate boundary planting at 6 m to 7 m, and the factory canteen at not more than 10% of the building’s gross floor area.
