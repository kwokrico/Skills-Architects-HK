# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 9 Environment
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
March 2014 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This chapter gives planning guidance on air, noise, water and waste for public and private development, permanent or temporary (§1.2, §1.4). It is a planning guideline, not the Buildings Ordinance. The standards are to be applied with flexibility and not in isolation, but a departure is not to be accepted without considering the long-term effect on living conditions (§1.2). Designated Projects under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance (Cap. 499) follow the statutory environmental impact assessment process and need an environmental permit unless exempted (§1.7). The figures below are those printed in the March 2014 edition.

## Regulatory Overview
Environmental planning is to avoid creating new problems at site selection and design, and to use redevelopment to improve conditions, with higher standards for new development than for areas judged only against the existing urban environment (§1.5, §2.1.1). A land-use plan is acceptable when the uses suit the site, are compatible with each other, and are served by land and access for waste and wastewater; uses that conflict with the environmental constraints should be avoided or relocated, or held to the standards in Sections 3 to 6 (§2.2.1, §2.2.5).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Principles, and what a buffer distance means (§2, Appendix 2.1)
The chapter classifies environmental concern as Type I (some activities may cause or suffer impacts, to be limited by layout and controls), Type II (most activities have that potential, so standards and zoning apply, and a scoped assessment may be needed) and Type III (significant impact or high sensitivity, so site selection must observe the standards and a detailed environmental impact assessment may be needed) (§2.2.4). The matrix in Figure 2.2 did not extract as readable ratings, so those cells are omitted.

A buffer area may contain non-sensitive structures or uses. A buffer distance is the shortest horizontal distance between the lot boundaries of the two incompatible uses, unless a section states otherwise (Appendix 2.1).

Locational principles that carry a direction or a test, without a new metre figure (§2.3):

| Aspect | Rule |
|---|---|
| Air | Do not put large emitters where dispersion is inhibited or pollution is already serious. Site major emitters to the west or southwest of urban areas and new towns, using the prevailing north-easterly winds. Do not put high-rise buildings next to low-rise emitters. Do not add goods-vehicle generators in areas that already have severe air pollution |
| Noise | Keep new sensitive uses out of excessive noise, and new emitters away from existing, committed or planned sensitive uses. If neither is possible, design noise reduction into the emitter, and use acoustic insulation where an acceptable exposure still cannot be reached |
| Water | Developments that would significantly disrupt water circulation should be avoided or tested by water-quality modelling before the site is fixed. Coastal waters shift from estuarine toward oceanic from west to east |
| Waste | Reserve sites for municipal waste reception and transfer |
| Industry next to housing | Chimney, non-chimney, large-scale, noisy and wastewater interfaces are to be treated by buffering, screening or comprehensive redevelopment, using the section references in §2.4.1. Phase the works so that a new residential edge is not left against remaining industry (§2.4.3) |

**SD takeaway:** Measure a buffer as the shortest horizontal distance between lot boundaries unless the relevant section names a kerb, a track edge or a chimney.

### 2. Air-quality buffers and open-space setbacks (§3, Tables 1.3 and 3.1, Appendix 3.1)
Prevailing winds are north-easterly for about 80% of the year (§3.2.1). Further industry in Kwun Tong, Tsuen Wan and Kwai Chung is not advisable, and air-polluting industry should be to the west or southwest of main urban centres and new towns, and preferably outside a topographically confined airshed (§3.2.2). Trunk roads should be aligned at the outskirts, and major trunk roads, ports and terminals to the west, wherever practicable (§3.2.4). Specified Processes, listed in Appendix 3.3 from Schedule 1 of the Air Pollution Control Ordinance, need an air-quality impact assessment at site selection (§3.3.1).

### 3. Noise standards (§4, Tables 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3)
Standards apply to uses that rely on opened windows for ventilation, and are the maximum permissible noise levels at the external facade (Table 4.1, notes 1 and 2). Definitions are in Appendix 4.2: NEF is Noise Exposure Forecast; Lmax is the maximum level in the stated interval; Leq (24 hour) is the equivalent continuous level; L10 (1 hour) is the level exceeded for 10% of the hour, used for peak-hour road noise.

Fixed noise sources are also printed once: (a) 5 dB(A) below the appropriate Acceptable Noise Level in Table 2 of the Technical Memorandum for the Assessment of Noise from Places Other than Domestic Premises, Public Places or Construction Sites, and (b) the prevailing background noise level. An LRT curve radius of less than 70 m should be avoided because of squeal. In a town centre, where the LRT runs more slowly, the 25 m separation might be reduced, provided squeal is controlled (§4.2.12). If speed, frequency or rolling stock differ materially from the systems used to set these distances, consult the Environmental Protection Department. Shielding works only if it breaks the line of sight from the window to the rail source. Decking a depot is described as the most effective depot treatment (§4.2.11).

Other design reductions printed as numbers (§4.3, §4.4): a building in the noise shadow of a closer shielding block can be 5 to 15 dB(A) lower. In a rural area, avoid a noise emitter that introduces a fairly consistent excess of 10 dB(A) above the prevailing background. Acoustic insulation is described as a last resort because a closed window in this climate needs air-conditioning (§4.3.10).

### 4. Water quality (§5)
No territory-wide Water Quality Objective concentrations are printed. Objectives are declared for each Water Control Zone under the Water Pollution Control Ordinance, and discharge limits are set in the Technical Memorandum on effluent standards (§5.1.3, Appendix 5.1). Industrial waste is not to be discharged to a sewer unless that Technical Memorandum is met, and discharge of industrial waste to a stormwater drain is prohibited (§5.2.1).

| Rule | Figure or test |
|---|---|
| No public sewer, and population more than 50 | Provide a sewage treatment plant, communal or individual (§5.2.4) |
| Isolated premises with a small number of occupants, where the ground is suitable | Septic tank and soakaway may be used (§5.2.4, §5.2.8) |
| Small sewage treatment plant | The design note cited is for a plant serving a population below 2 000: the Environmental Protection Department’s Guidelines for the Design of Small Sewage Treatment Plants (§5.2.7) |
| Sewerage Impact Assessment | Required or not on the advice of the Environmental Protection Department, case by case, for a major development. Commission sewerage before occupation (§5.2.5) |
| Gazetted beach | No new storm or foul outlet, and no soakaway pit for effluent, within 100 m of the beach boundary in any direction, including rivers and streams (§5.3.2) |
| Marine fish culture zone | No new effluent within 200 m of the seaward boundary or within 100 m of the landward boundary (§5.3.4) |
| Water gathering grounds | Connect to public sewerage. If that is not available, provide a sewage treatment plant meeting the Group A inland-water standards in the Technical Memorandum under the Water Pollution Control (Amendment) Ordinance 1990. Avoid septic tanks and soakaways (§5.3.13) |
| Livestock-waste soakaway | Not less than 30 m from any reservoir, saline water, spring, watercourse or well used for potable supply (§6.3.6) |
| Treated-effluent discharge | Avoid gazetted beaches, typhoon shelters, marinas, Sites of Special Scientific Interest and marine fish culture zones (§5.2.6) |
| Typhoon shelter, marina or boat park | No new sewage outfall, stormwater outfall or nullah is to drain into it (§5.3.11) |
| Natural stream | Development should preferably be located away from it. No metre distance is printed (§5.3.15) |

Septic-tank siting needs space between houses, a low groundwater table, ample distance from retaining walls, wells, streams, pools, beaches and slopes, and adequate soil percolation. The design reference is Practice Note for Professional Persons ProPECC PN 5/93 (§5.2.8). Reclamation requires a hydrographic and water-quality investigation before it proceeds (§5.2.12).

**SD takeaway:** Do not place a new outfall or a soakaway within 100 m of a gazetted beach, or a new effluent within 200 m seaward or 100 m landward of a marine fish culture zone.

### 5. Waste management (§6, Table 6.1)
The waste order in Appendix 6.1 is avoidance and minimisation, then reuse, recovery and recycling, then bulk reduction and disposal, with landfill as the last resort.

Refuse transfer station (§6.2.2): consider one for 500–2 000 tonnes a day in the Urban Area, described as equivalent to 500 000–2 million population, or 100–1 000 tonnes a day in the New Territories, described as equivalent to 100 000–1 million population. Site area 1–2 hectares, preferably on the waterfront in an industrial or other non-sensitive area, or underground.

Refuse collection point, urban and new town areas (§6.2.6–§6.2.8). One point per 20 000 persons, or for an area within 500 m. Adjust in industrial and commercial areas where private facilities exist. Do not site on a trunk road, a primary distributor or a steep road (§6.2.11).

| Site type | Minimum site |
|---|---|
| Island or corner site, head-in and head-out | Gross site not less than 294 m², minimum width 14 m |
| Hemmed-in or gap site | Gross site 405 m² to 594 m², minimum width 27 m. The smaller end takes conventional refuse-collection vehicles only. The larger end takes conventional vehicles and a tractor-trailer. About 150 m² of a large gap site may be excised for another compatible use |
| Exception, back-in and head-out, on a minor road with low flow | Not less than 220 m², 11 m frontage and 20 m deep |

Village-type points (§6.2.10): 9.4 m by 6.4 m (about 60 m²) to 10.9 m by 6.4 m (about 70 m²), serving one or more adjoining villages up to about 1 000 persons, plus one further point per 1 000 population or part thereof for a cluster of settlements. These urban and village rates do not apply inside public housing estates, which follow a separate Housing Authority standard. Each domestic block there still needs a refuse storage and material recovery chamber sized under the Building (Refuse Storage and Material Recovery Chambers and Refuse Chutes) Regulations, Cap. 123H (§6.2.15).
