# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 2 Residential Densities
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
December 2025 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This chapter sets maximum domestic plot ratios, as general guidance, for public and private housing in the Main Urban Areas, the New Towns and New Development Areas (NDAs), and the Rural Areas (§1.1.7, §3.1). It is a planning guideline, not the Buildings Ordinance. The First Schedule to the Building (Planning) Regulations, any tighter control on a statutory plan or lease, and airport height restrictions still apply (§2.2–§2.5). The December 2025 edition states that the chapter was updated mainly to reflect the latest policy directives.

## Regulatory Overview
Residential density is controlled by domestic plot ratio, defined as the gross floor area of a building divided by the Net Site Area, with gross floor area as defined under the Building (Planning) Regulations (§2.1). The table maxima are general guidance to be tested site by site; they are not an automatic across-the-board specification, and the more stringent of the statutory-plan restriction and the First Schedule restriction prevails unless a modification is granted (§1.1.5, §2.4).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Domestic plot ratio and site area (§2.1, Figure 1)
Plot ratio governs gross floor area. Population follows from flat size and persons per flat (§2.1).

| Term | What the extract includes |
|---|---|
| Gross site area | Public roads; facilities, open space or slopes that are not integrated with the main residential development |
| Net site area (site for domestic plot ratio) | Internal roads; facilities, open space or slopes that are integrated with the main residential development in design, as required or agreed by Government, and demonstrated to be technically feasible |

Figure 1 remarks, as printed:

| Remark | Rule |
|---|---|
| (a) | Figure 1 generally applies to site-specific or project-level development where the planning context is more certain. It is not applicable to planning of a New Town or New Development Area, whose residential site areas are ascertained in separate comprehensive studies |
| (b) | In general, public roads are excluded from the Net Site Area. Internal roads are included subject to agreement of the relevant department, case by case, taking account of building bulk, urban design and infrastructure capacity |
| (c) | Whether something is “integrated” is decided case by case, with regard to layout and design, statutory plans, planning briefs, lease development rights, and other government requirements |

**SD takeaway:** Calculate domestic plot ratio on Net Site Area only: keep public roads out, and include internal roads only with the relevant department’s agreement.

### 2. Which intensity control prevails (§2.2–§2.5)
| Regime | Control stated in this chapter |
|---|---|
| Building | Private projects are subject to the Building (Planning) Regulations under the Buildings Ordinance. The First Schedule sets maximum plot ratios and site coverages for domestic and non-domestic buildings of different heights, by site class, according to the number of specified streets not less than 4.5 m wide that the site abuts. Where a residential building also contains a non-domestic element, the maximum permissible domestic plot ratio may be reduced by the composite-building formula (§2.3). This chapter does not print that formula |
| Planning | Intensity is stipulated in the Notes of statutory plans, or implemented through permission under section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance (§2.2) |
| Lease | Maximum gross floor area is generally imposed in a new or modified lease (§2.2) |
| Plan stricter than the First Schedule | The statutory-plan plot ratio or gross floor area restriction prevails (§2.4) |
| First Schedule stricter than the plan | The Building Authority may, on application, consider a modification under section 42 of the Buildings Ordinance so that the First Schedule plot ratio may be exceeded up to the plan maximum. The chapter refers to Joint Practice Note No. 4, “Development Control Parameters – Plot Ratio / Gross Floor Area” (§2.4) |
| Other limits | Airport height restrictions under the Hong Kong Airport (Control of Obstructions) Ordinance may restrict intensity. Special Control Areas may impose more stringent intensities through statutory plans and/or lease conditions (§2.5). Table 1 states that in some areas the maximum domestic plot ratio may not be achievable because of airport height restrictions |

**SD takeaway:** Apply the more stringent of the statutory-plan plot ratio and the First Schedule, and treat a section 42 increase only as something that must be applied for.

### 3. Locational rules for the density zones (§1.1.5–§1.1.7, §1.2.2, §3.2.1–§3.2.2)
The maxima are the figures to be considered or tested where infrastructure capacity and planning considerations allow an increase. The plot ratio for a site is fixed through studies and assessments and endorsed by the relevant authorities (§1.1.5). Guidelines apply to both public and private housing, flexibly, taking account of site conditions, local circumstances and policy objectives (§1.1.7).

| Principle | Text |
|---|---|
| Rail and interchanges | Higher-density housing should be located near rail stations and major transport interchange hubs wherever possible (§1.2.2(c); Main Urban Area principle in §3.2.1(a)) |
| Gradation | There could be a decreasing gradation of density with distance from rail stations and major transport interchange hubs (§1.2.2(d)) |
| Outside the rail catchment | Higher density outside major transport corridors or a rail-station catchment could be considered where feeder services will be adequate (§1.2.2(e)) |
| Poorly served or congested areas | Limit densities where high-capacity transport is lacking, and, wherever possible, reduce densities in highly congested districts with widespread environmental problems (§3.2.1(b), (c)) |
| Environmentally sensitive areas | Low density is more compatible close to wetland, conservation areas, country parks, coastal protection areas and Sites of Special Scientific Interest (§1.2.2(h)) |
| Visual impact | Limit densities where visual impact is the prime concern, and refer to the urban design guidelines in Chapter 11 (§3.2.1(d)) |

Main Urban Area zone character (§3.2.2), which is also the basis for New Town and NDA zones R1 to R3 (§3.3.1):

| Zone | Where it applies |
|---|---|
| Residential Zone 1 (R1) | Highest density. Districts well served by high-capacity public transport, such as a rail station or other major transport interchange. Buildings often include a significant commercial floorspace component |
| Residential Zone 2 (R2) | Medium density. Locations less well served by high-capacity public transport. Usually no commercial floorspace |
| Residential Zone 3 (R3) | Lowest density of the three. Very limited public transport capacity, special urban-design, traffic or environmental constraints, and/or special local character |
| R4 (New Towns / NDAs only) | Very low density, only where justified by severe geotechnical or infrastructure constraints, or by compatibility with adjacent rural low-density development (§3.3.1) |

Zones named in this chapter are R1, R2, R3, R4, RR1 to RR4, and Village.

**SD takeaway:** Test the highest domestic plot ratio at rail stations and major interchanges, and step the ratio down with distance and toward environmentally sensitive areas.

### 4. Maximum domestic plot ratios — Main Urban Areas (§3.2, Table 1)
The Main Urban Areas are Hong Kong Island, Kowloon, and Tsuen Wan, Kwai Chung and Tsing Yi (§3.2.1).

The 2014 Policy Address raised maximum domestic plot ratios generally by about 20% for housing sites in Density Zones of the Main Urban Areas and New Towns other than the north of Hong Kong Island and Kowloon Peninsula, where appropriate, subject to traffic, infrastructure, local character, existing intensity and other impacts (§1.1.3). Footnote 1 states that Table 1 zones R2 and R3, and Table 2 zones R1 to R4, already incorporate that increase. Do not add a further 20% to the printed figures.

| R1 | Kowloon | 7.5 | (iii) (iv) (v) |
| R1 | New development area and comprehensive development area in Hong Kong Island and Kowloon | 6.5 | (vi) (vii) |
| R1 | Tsuen Wan, Kwai Chung and Tsing Yi | 6 | (ii) (viii) |
| R2 | No separate place name is printed in the Location column | 6 | (ix) (x) |
| R3 | No separate place name is printed in the Location column | 3.6 | (ix) (x) |

| Case | Maximum domestic plot ratio |
|---|---|
| Existing built-up Residential Zone 1, Hong Kong Island | 8 / 9 / 10 depending on site classification, as stipulated in the First Schedule (§3.2.4) |
| Kowloon, in general, implemented through statutory plans | 7.5 (§3.2.4) |
| New development areas in Hong Kong Island and Kowloon, including new reclamations and formed areas such as West Kowloon Reclamation, Kai Tak Development and Anderson Road Quarry Development | Generally 6.5, which is lower than the Building (Planning) Regulations limit (§3.2.5) |
| Comprehensive development areas in Hong Kong Island and Kowloon | Normally 6.5; higher plot ratios may be justified according to local circumstances and where infrastructure capacity permits (§3.2.6) |

Notes that change the number:

| Note | Rule |
|---|---|
| (i) | 8, 9 and 10 are Class A, Class B and Class C sites respectively, as defined under the Building (Planning) Regulations. This chapter does not define those classes further |
| (ii) | If there is non-domestic floorspace, the maximum domestic plot ratio is reduced under the composite-building formula. The formula is not printed here |
| (iii) | The Kowloon maximum follows the statutory plans. Site classification is not relevant |
| (iv) | In Kowloon, if non-domestic floorspace has a plot ratio in excess of 1.5, the maximum domestic plot ratio is reduced by the amount of that excess |
| (v) | A domestic plot ratio higher than 7.5 may be justified for specific areas or sites in Kowloon, subject to a planning study confirming technical feasibility and suitability. Footnote 6 ties the general 7.5 to the Kowloon Density Study Review in 2001 and records the same selective-relaxation point for large-scale urban restructuring in western Kowloon, subject to sewerage, transport and government, institution or community facilities |
| (vi) | Higher maxima may be permitted in comprehensive development areas having regard to local circumstances, such as infrastructure capacity |
| (vii) | For the 6.5 row, any non-domestic plot ratio component may be provided in addition to the domestic plot ratio, up to the maximum permitted by the composite-building formula or the statutory plans |
| (viii) | Tsuen Wan, Kwai Chung and Tsing Yi were generally restricted to 5 and were revised to 6 on the 2014 directive (an increase of 20% from 5). Under exceptional circumstances, or for a district-based urban-renewal approach, a higher domestic plot ratio up to a maximum of 8 may be justified for specific areas or sites, subject to a planning study |
| (ix) | In existing development areas, the R2 and R3 maxima can be imposed only on a lease modification or through permission under section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance, unless they are incorporated in the statutory plans |
| (x) | In Special Control Areas the maximum domestic plot ratio may be further limited |
| General | The table is an indication. A lower domestic plot ratio may be specified for transport, infrastructure, environmental, topographical or geotechnical constraints, heritage and nature conservation, urban design (local character, building-height profile, massing, air ventilation, visual impact, ridgelines), or special design considerations |

**SD takeaway:** Start a Main Urban Area test from the printed Table 1 figures — 8/9/10, 7.5, 6.5, 6 and 3.6 — treating the R2 and R3 figures as already including the 2014 increase.

### 5. Maximum domestic plot ratios — New Towns and New Development Areas (§1.1.4, §3.3, Table 2)
Table 2 applies to New Towns excluding Tsuen Wan, Kwai Chung and Tsing Yi, and to New Development Areas. Those three districts are in Table 1.

The 2022 Policy Address adopted a higher maximum domestic plot ratio of 6.5 as a guideline in the next-generation new towns, including the Northern Metropolis and the Kau Yi Chau Artificial Islands (§1.1.4). Section 3.3.2 states that the maximum domestic plot ratio adopted in Residential Zone 1 in most New Towns and NDAs is 6, and that this could be increased generally to 6.5, where infrastructural capacity and planning considerations permit, to align with that next-generation figure. Table 2 prints 6.5 for R1.

| Density zone | Maximum domestic plot ratio |
|---|---|
| R1 | 6.5 |
| R2 | 5 |
| R3 | 3.6 |
| R4 | 0.8 |

Section 3.3.3 states the same R2, R3 and R4 figures: 5, 3.6 and 0.8.

| Note | Rule |
|---|---|
| (i) | Under exceptional circumstances, a higher domestic plot ratio up to 8 should only be adopted in areas which are comprehensively planned to that development scale and with no infrastructure constraints |
| (ii) | If there is non-domestic floorspace, the maximum domestic plot ratio is reduced under the composite-building formula |
| (iii) | Designate R4 only with special justification, such as severe geotechnical or infrastructural constraints, or compatibility with adjacent rural low-density development |
| General | Same lower-ratio caveat as Table 1, including ridgelines, air ventilation and visual impact |
| 2014 uplift | Footnote 1: the Table 2 figures for R1 to R4 already incorporate the general 20% increase. Do not apply it again |

**SD takeaway:** For these New Towns and NDAs, test R1 at 6.5 and R2/R3/R4 at 5 / 3.6 / 0.8, and use a figure up to 8 only where the area is comprehensively planned and has no infrastructure constraint.

### 6. Maximum domestic plot ratios — Rural Areas (§3.4, Table 3)
Densities are lower than in urban areas because of limited transport, utility and social infrastructure, and to protect sensitive natural resources (§3.4.1). Domestic plot ratio is applied to the Net Site Area (Table 3, note (i); Figure 1).

| Density zone | Maximum domestic plot ratio | Locational criteria in Table 3 and §3.4.2 |
|---|---|---|
| RR1 | 3.6 | Commercial centres of Rural Townships. The text gives Sai Kung Town and Mui Wo as examples. Medium-rise |
| RR2 | 2.1 | Areas within Rural Townships outside the commercial centre, and other significant rural development areas served by medium-capacity public transport, such as light rail. Low-rise |
| RR3 | 0.75 | Peripheral parts of Rural Townships or other rural development areas, or locations away from existing settlements but with adequate infrastructure and no major landscape or environmental constraints. Low-rise terraced housing or flats |
| RR4 | 0.4 | Similar locations to RR3 where infrastructure or landscape constraints restrict intensity. Also house developments permitted as replacements for temporary structures in areas requiring upgrading. Detached or semi-detached houses |
| Village | 3.0 | Within the defined envelope of recognised villages. New Territories Exempted Houses: 3 floors on 100% site coverage on a site area of 65.03 m² (Table 3 note (ii); §3.4.2(e)) |

The general note allows a lower domestic plot ratio for the same classes of constraint as Tables 1 and 2.

**SD takeaway:** In rural areas lock the test ratio to Table 3 — 3.6, 2.1, 0.75, 0.4 or 3.0 — and for a village house use 65.03 m², three storeys and 100% site coverage inside the recognised-village envelope.

### 7. Going below the table, and estimating flats and population (§3.5, §4.1–§4.2)
Section 3.5 repeats that a maximum domestic plot ratio lower than the table target may be applied where development capacity, urban design or special design considerations are significant.

At strategic-planning stage, before a layout exists, residential land is estimated by deducting land that is not suitable or available (steep terrain, environmentally sensitive areas, special natural or heritage value or scientific interest, permitted burial grounds, overhead transmission lines, recognised villages, and similar), then allowing for the development theme and for population-related uses (§4.1). Footnote 9, for the Hong Kong 2030+ land-requirement assessment only, assumes land per person of 6 m² for transport infrastructure, 3.5 m² for government, institution or community facilities, and 3.5 m² for open space.

Once the Net Site Area is known, and before a scheme exists (§4.2.2):

| Estimate | Formula as printed |
|---|---|
| Number of flats | Net Site Area × domestic plot ratio ÷ assumed average flat size |
| Population | Number of flats × average persons per flat |

The chapter does not state an assumed average flat size or an assumed persons-per-flat figure.

**SD takeaway:** Drop below the table maximum where capacity or urban-design constraints apply, then estimate flats only from Net Site Area, the adopted domestic plot ratio and an assumed average flat size.
