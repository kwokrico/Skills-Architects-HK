# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 12 Miscellaneous Planning Standards and Guidelines
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
December 2025 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This chapter gives planning standards and guidelines for land uses and facilities that do not fall within the other chapters. The uses are not related to one another. The chapter may later be expanded or curtailed. The December 2025 edition note states that Section 2 has been updated to align with the cavern development strategy (§1.1, edition note).

## Regulatory Overview
The chapter covers rock cavern development, petrol filling stations, potentially hazardous installations, vehicle repair workshops, port back-up and open storage uses, and the use of land beneath flyovers and footbridges (§2–§7). Several siting intervals, site sizes, separation distances and risk criteria are printed in this chapter; where a buffer or safety distance is left to another document or to a case-by-case study, that distance is not stated here (§2.3.5, §3.5.14, §3.6.2, §4.3, §6.8.2).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Rock cavern development (§2)
| Item | Rule in this chapter |
|---|---|
| What a cavern is | A large man-made space in rock. Detailed planning, design, construction, monitoring and maintenance guidance is in Geoguide 4, Guide to Cavern Engineering, Civil Engineering and Development Department. Further non-statutory material is on that department’s website |
| Why it is planned | Relocate or place suitable government facilities in caverns and release the surface site; house suitable “Not in My Backyard” facilities; accommodate land-extensive public or private uses where surface land is short; accommodate uses that need security and a steady environment, such as research or testing laboratories and data centres |
| Potential uses | Commercial: food and beverage, food or wine storage, retail. Industrial: concrete batching, container storage, data centre, industry, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) bulk storage, oil bulk storage, research or testing laboratory, storage or warehousing. Government, institution, community and other specified uses: animal waste facility, archives, columbarium, mausoleum, mortuary, explosives depot or magazine, maintenance depot, refuse transfer facility, service reservoir, sewage or water treatment plant, slaughterhouse, transport connection and network, underground quarrying, vehicle parking, vehicle including bus depot, wholesale market. Public utilities: power station, public utility installation. The list is not exhaustive. Suitability is case by case |
| Studies | Planning and engineering feasibility, environmental impact assessment and traffic impact assessment, plus public consultation as appropriate |
| Gross floor area | Irrespective of how underground ownership is defined, underground and above-ground facilities and building works all count towards gross floor area for the permissible plot ratio under the Building (Planning) Regulations, unless exempted under the Buildings Ordinance. No plot ratio is printed in this section |
| Land disposal | Land sale, build-operate-transfer, or other means. Development parameters should ideally be in the engineering conditions or the lease |
| Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance | An underground rock cavern with a portal less than 100 m from the nearest boundary of an existing or planned environmentally sensitive area listed under Item Q.2, Part I, Schedule 2 is a designated project, and the statutory process applies where required |
| Country parks | Seek the views and consent of the Country and Marine Parks Authority. Each project must be justified on its own merits |
| Government projects | Development Bureau Technical Circular (Works) on Rock Cavern Development applies to all government projects. First check that a suitable surface site is lacking and that there are specific needs or strong merits. Compare cavern and non-cavern options. If the cavern option is recommended, submit the option-assessments report to the Sub-Committee on Cavern Development under the Committee on Planning and Land Development |

**SD takeaway:** The only distance printed for cavern siting is the 100 m portal test against a Schedule 2 environmentally sensitive area. Cavern floor area still counts toward plot ratio unless the Buildings Ordinance exempts it.

### 2. Petrol filling stations (§3)
A petrol filling station (PFS) provides fuelling or charging, and air and water. Vehicular fuels mean petrol, diesel, oil, LPG and electricity unless otherwise specified. In this chapter, PFS generally means a conventional station, a petrol-cum-LPG station, an LPG filling station, and an electric-vehicle (EV) charging station converted from a PFS. Servicing, toilets and related retail may be provided in approved locations. Need cannot be quantified from a formula; reserve sites with the concerned departments. Absence of one brand is not a need for another station.

| Siting rule | Printed distance or test |
|---|---|
| Expressways, trunk roads, primary distributor roads, Rural Roads A | Specially selected sites with well-designed ingress and egress slip roads. On expressways, preferably part of a service area as indicated in Chapter 6, Volume 2, Transport Planning and Design Manual. On trunk roads, primary distributor roads and Rural Roads A, intervals not less than 5 km |
| Expressways and intersections | At least 2 km from any intersection |
| Trunk roads, primary distributor roads, Rural Roads A | Not within 100 m of any bend, vertical curve or road junction. Minimum sight distance 100 m on the approaches to entrances and exits |
| Roads lower in the hierarchy | Sight distance 50 m. If stations are on either side of a single carriageway, stagger them within visible distance, not less than 100 m apart, with the left-hand station seen first. Same side of the road: at least 300 m apart unless contiguous with a common entrance and exit. On dual carriageways, stations may be opposite one another |
| Fire hydrant | Within 100 m of the PFS. Consult the Fire Services Department and the Water Supplies Department if the site is beyond 100 m from an existing hydrant |
| Fuelling-facility safety distances | Required to conform to “Design, construction, modification, maintenance and decommissioning of filling stations” (Association for Petroleum and Explosives Administration and the Energy Institute). Those distances are not printed in this chapter |
| Visual buffer | Landscape treatment such as amenity stripes, boundary walls and a buffer distance from adjacent developments may be used. No buffer width is printed |
| Transport and police | Consult the Transport Department and the Hong Kong Police Force (Traffic Headquarters) on every case. Flexible application may be warranted |

Site dimensions in the text layer (§3.4.1). Depth includes the footway. The hash note applies to the 750 m² figure: it is not necessarily required for an existing PFS retrofitted with LPG filling facilities. Frontage, depth and access-road width are printed only for the LPG filling station row.

| (b) LPG filling station | 375 m² | 25 m | 15 m | 6 m |
| (c) PFS with LPG facilities | 750 m², subject to the retrofit note | Not in the text layer | Not in the text layer | Not in the text layer |
| (d) EV charging station converted from an existing PFS | 375 m² | Not in the text layer | Not in the text layer | Not in the text layer |

No second footway width is printed | Footway at least 3 m; access at least 8.5 m, with a barrier between the PFS and the carriageway |

| Layout and risk rule | Text |
|---|---|
| Access | One ingress and one egress, one-way operation, unless an extra access on a second frontage improves circulation without disrupting the road or emergency evacuation. Means of access, emergency vehicular access and means of escape shall comply with the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings 2011 |
| Waiting | One space adjacent to each metered filling point, plus a minimum of four waiting spaces between the entrance and the filling points. Where lubrication or other servicing is provided, four additional spaces per service bay. Consider one additional space between each air-pumping point. Paragraphs 3.4.7 to 3.4.10 do not apply to EV charging stations |
| Built-up areas | Preferably in relatively open areas. If surrounded, surrounding buildings should desirably be low-rise. “Low-rise” is not given a storey limit |
| Noise | Meet Table 4.1 of HKPSG Chapter 9. Noise-sensitive uses are defined in Appendix 4.1 of that chapter. Operation-hour restrictions may be required |
| Drainage | Covered-area and open-area drains separated by a 150 mm raised kerb or bump. A small PFS with two to three filling islands (or four to six dispensers) and isolated canopies occupying less than two-thirds of the total area may connect all surface drainage to the storm drains via an interceptor |
| LPG separation, as a general rule, subject to quantitative risk assessment | High-rise residential, educational or hospital: 55 m. Commercial, recreational or industrial: 15 m. Low-density residential or incidental dwelling: 15 m |
| LPG vent pipes | Discharge outlets at least 4.5 m from any opening of a building or any non-flameproof electrical equipment. Vents of pressure-relief valves for underground LPG tanks shall not be obstructed |
| LPG inside a building | Not acceptable. An LPG station or a PFS with LPG facilities should be free-standing. Risk is judged against the Government Risk Guidelines in Section 4.4 |
| PFS without LPG inside a building | Avoid. Not in residential buildings, mixed commercial and residential buildings, or basements. Ground floors of carpark, industrial or commercial buildings may be used only if the listed conditions are met, including four-hour fire-resisting enclosures laterally and vertically; ventilation open on at least one of the longest sides or two adjoining sides; the floor immediately above limited to low fire and life risk acceptable to the Director of Fire Services; on a commercial building, the three floors immediately above not used for high life risk or sleeping risk, and no part of the building used for places of public entertainment or a cinema under the Building (Planning) Regulations; openings on those three levels bricked up unless varied by quantitative risk assessment; Town Planning Board approval where necessary. Headroom must be adequate; no headroom figure is printed. Paragraphs 3.8.1 to 3.8.4 do not apply to an EV charging station |
| EV charging | Do not co-locate EV charging with vehicular fuel filling in the same PFS. Minimum of two waiting spaces. Separate EV charging facilities by at least 6 m from any licensed store under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295). Additional separation may be imposed. Consult the Environmental Protection Department, which will liaise with the controlling authorities. Paragraphs 3.5.1, 3.5.3 to 3.5.6, 3.5.9, 3.5.13 and 3.5.14 do not apply to an EV charging station |

**SD takeaway:** Use the printed road intervals, the 55 m and 15 m LPG separations, the 4.5 m vent clearance and the 6 m EV-to-dangerous-goods separation. Do not fill the blank PFS frontage, depth and access cells from another source.

### 3. Potentially hazardous installations (§4)
A potentially hazardous installation (PHI) stores hazardous materials at or above a threshold quantity, which varies by substance. Thresholds generally follow the UK Notification of Installations Handling Hazardous Substances Regulations 1982, with some quantities amended for local conditions. All explosives factories and Government explosives depots are PHIs.

| Type | Threshold quantity in this chapter |
|---|---|
| LPG storage facilities (oil terminals, bulk stores, substitute natural gas plant, and similar) | 25 tonnes or more |
| Town gas installations | 15 tonnes or more |
| Chlorine stores, mainly at water treatment works | 10 tonnes or more, or any storage in one-tonne drums |
| Petrol or naphtha stores, mainly at oil depots | 10 000 tonnes or more |
| Liquid oxygen storage, mainly at industrial gas facilities | 500 tonnes or more |
| Explosives factories and Government explosives depots | Any quantity |

| Control | Rule printed |
|---|---|
| Consultation Zone | Delineated for every PHI. Proposed development inside it is referred to the Coordinating Committee on Land-use Planning and Control relating to Potentially Hazardous Installations (CCPHI). Extent depends on topography, PHI type and storage capacity. No fixed radius is printed. Proposals that increase the number of persons living or working in the zone go to CCPHI. Sizable developments are normally not approved. “Sizable” is not defined by a population or floor area |
| Safety Zone | For explosives factories and depots, in addition to the Consultation Zone, determine a Safety Zone from the current British Table of Safety Distances (Explosives Acts 1875 and 1923). Those distances are not printed here. No inhabited buildings or congregation of people are allowed. Zone size depends on the quantity stored |
| Individual risk | Maximum off-site individual risk associated with PHIs should not exceed 1 in 100 000 per year, that is 1 × 10⁻⁵ per year. The chapter compares this with an average annual risk of dying in a traffic accident of about 1 in 10 000. Figure 4.1 is an individual-risk contour plan; contour values are not in the text layer. Exposure duration is also taken into account |
| Societal risk | Judged on a frequency–number (FN) graph, Figure 4.2. Two FN lines separate acceptable and unacceptable societal risk. A vertical cut-off at the 1 000-fatality level extends down to a frequency of 1 in a billion years, to avoid disasters of more than 1 000 deaths. The intermediate region is “as low as reasonably practicable” (ALARP). The figure labels the regions Unacceptable, ALARP and Acceptable, with fatality ticks at 10, 100, 1 000 and 10 000 and frequency ticks from 10⁻² to 10⁻⁹ per year. The coordinates of the two sloping FN lines are not printed in the text layer |
| Studies | Hazard assessment, planning study and action plan, endorsed by CCPHI. Interim rule before those studies are complete: do not increase the number of persons living, working or congregating in the Consultation Zone, except where refusing the change would deprive a private party of development rights. Those private-right decisions wait for the studies |
| Future sites | Potential PHI sites are in remote areas. A CCPHI Monitoring Zone is delineated. Development is not automatically precluded. Consult CCPHI on non-PHI proposals that could prejudice reservation of the site |

**SD takeaway:** Do not draw a Consultation Zone or a Safety Zone radius from this chapter. Test a scheme inside an existing zone against the printed individual-risk ceiling of 1 in 100 000 per year and against the societal-risk description, including the 1 000-fatality cut-off.

### 4. Vehicle repair workshops (§5)
Existing workshops are usually small, with fewer than five employees and a net floor area between 50 and 100 m². That description is not the minimum for a new site. Locate workshops away from residential areas and sensitive receivers: in the main urban area and new towns, on the periphery of industrial areas, in purpose-designed buildings or on the lower floors of industrial buildings; in rural areas, as low-density buildings on the periphery of new towns or in urban transition areas designated for rural workshop.

| Parameter | Printed figure |
|---|---|
| Multi-storey purpose-designed building | Plot-ratio guidelines in HKPSG Chapter 5 apply. An average plot ratio of 8 may be considered, taking local conditions and viability into account |
| Rural or open-air type | One to two storeys; maximum plot ratio 0.5; water supply and proper sewage disposal; for open-air development, paving, drainage and fencing |
| High-rise purpose-designed floor plate, vehicle lift | Minimum 729 m² (27 m × 27 m) for an efficiency ratio of 60% (net usable floor area / gross floor area) |
| Single-use multi-storey building | Minimum site area 972 m², printed immediately after the vehicle-lift floor-plate rule |
| Ramp building | Minimum floor plate 1 944 m² (36 m × 54 m) for a similar efficiency ratio |
| Low-rise lorry repair | Minimum 1 575 m² per floor (35 m × 45 m) at an efficiency ratio of 65%. At plot ratio 0.5 and one storey, minimum site area 3 150 m² |
| Unit sizes to accommodate | Small up to 100 m²; medium 100 to 200 m²; large over 200 m² |
| Two-person workshop | Internal area of 10 m × 9 m, including storage and a minor office. The design-parameter table states a minimum workshop size of 90 m² (10 × 9 m) |
| Workspaces | A workspace holds a vehicle and the person working on it. Small workshops: 0.5 to 1 workspace per person. Larger firms: 2 to 5 workspaces per person |
| Parking (§5.4.6) | A minimum of 2 parking spaces for each workshop unit, or 0.75 parking space per workspace, or 150 m² of gross floor area, whichever is the higher. The chapter does not state how many spaces the 150 m² figure represents. The design-parameter table restates 0.75 space per workspace and a minimum of 2 spaces per workshop |
| Car ramp | Gradient 1 in 5; inner radius 3.5 m; outer radius 6.1 m |
| Goods-vehicle ramp | Gradient 1 in 10; inner radius 7.2 m; outer radius 13 m |
| Car lift | Minimum 6.15 m × 3.2 m; at least one |
| Fire stairs | Minimum 5.25 m × 2.1 m; at least two |
| Floor-to-floor height | 5.2 m for cars; 7.2 m for lorries |
| Parking stall | Cars: 5.0 m × 2.4 m, minimum headroom 2.4 m. Goods vehicle: 11.0 m × 3.5 m, minimum headroom 4.1 m |
| Street access | As few access points as possible. No reversing to or from the street. Access point at least 15 m from a street corner |

Goods-vehicle workshops are preferably on the ground floor. Dangerous-goods stores face the street, to the Fire Services Department’s satisfaction. Environmental design follows HKPSG Chapter 9. No separation distance from sensitive receivers is printed in this section.

**SD takeaway:** Size a purpose-designed workshop from the floor plates and the 972 m² or 3 150 m² site minima, and apply plot ratio 8 only as the urban average the chapter says may be considered.

### 5. Port back-up and open storage uses (§6)
Open storage here is activity on a site that is generally more than 50% uncovered, other than container-related uses and other than storage ancillary to industry, workshop, warehousing or commercial use on the same site. Port back-up uses are off-port: container lorry park, empty container storage and repair, container yard, and container freight station. Certain open-storage and port back-up uses sit in Column 2 of the “Open Storage” zone and need Town Planning Board permission. Open storage not specified in Column 2 is in Column 1 and is always permitted.

Section 6.4 prints projections from 1993: port back-up land take grew from under 30 sites covering 28 ha in 1983 to over 237 sites covering 198 ha in 1993; maximum demand could be in the order of 380 ha by 2001 and, by 2011, a corresponding total of around 510 ha (about a 150% increase on 1993); open storage demand of 450 ha to 550 ha by 2001 and around 750 ha by 2011 (about a 100% increase on 1993). These are the historical estimates printed in the chapter. They are not a current site-reservation formula. The chapter says demand and supply should be updated.

Broad areas are in five types: (1) continued use and further intensification; (2) limited intensification; (3) limited intensification with improvements; (4) prevent proliferation; (5) discontinue sites in the long term. The 1994 “Study on Port Back-up Land and Open Storage Requirements” holds the area recommendations. Empty container storage and repair should not be allowed in populated countryside.

| Siting or size rule | Printed figure or test |
|---|---|
| Country parks and sites of special scientific interest | Sites within 100 m should normally not be allowed unless insignificant impact is demonstrated |
| Other sensitive receivers | Do not locate adjacent to residential dwellings, hospitals, schools and other community facilities. See HKPSG Chapter 9. Compatible neighbours named here: industrial, godowns, port uses, other open storage and port back-up, and public utility installations |
| Buffer distance | Encouraged on large and small sites. Distance varies with scale and nature of the use, surroundings, screening, and urban or rural context. Determine it site by site under Table 1.3 of HKPSG Chapter 9. No buffer width is printed in this chapter |
| Rural container storage and repair, visual intrusion | Consider stacking restricted to a maximum of 3 units, described as equivalent to the height of a traditional village house. No metre height is printed. Figure 6.1 illustrates landscape screening by mounding and planting; no mound height or setback depth is in the text layer |
| Operational container stacking | Restricted to a maximum of seven units by operational constraints, and may be further restricted by land-use interface |
| Minimum site area, from 1994 survey medians, for judging whether a planning application meets current standards of provision | Container storage and repair 5 100 m²; container yard 4 900 m²; container lorry park 3 000 m²; container freight station 2 000 m²; open storage 1 000 m². New Territories container freight stations should have a minimum of 2 000 m². That requirement does not apply to facilities at the container port, which are considerably larger |
| Observed container-yard sizes in Annex 2 | Existing yards usually vary between 755 m² and 33 900 m², with a mean of just under 1 ha. This is a description of sites, not the minimum above |
| Reservation for future port back-up | Over 1 ha where the policy is large integral areas near container terminals. A major solution space may exceed 10 ha |
| Reservation for open-storage solution space | Over 0.5 ha where the objective is new or reprovisioned growth |
| Multi-storey container lorry park with other compatible uses | Maximum Metroplan standard for godown, at a plot ratio of 8 |
| Dwell time | Containers stored for more than 3 days may be located away from the new terminals, preferably near the border with strategic-road access |
| Sight lines | Adequate setbacks to meet Transport Department requirements. No setback depth is printed |
| Environmental test | Noise modelling for container storage and repair, container yards and container lorry parks against HKPSG Chapter 9. The chapter cites the environmental impact assessment process in PELB Technical Circular No. 2/92 and Environmental Protection Department Advice Note 2/92 |

Smaller sites may be considered in the short to medium term. Discretion applies. Off-port intensity is left to the operator if transport and environmental conditions are met.

**SD takeaway:** Apply the printed minimum areas and the 100 m test against country parks and sites of special scientific interest. Take the buffer width from Chapter 9, Table 1.3, which this chapter does not reproduce.

### 6. Use of land beneath flyovers and footbridges (§7)
Use these sites only after other suitable sites are exhausted, and only if land use, structure, fire safety, traffic, environment, visual impact and the other tests in §7.1.4 are acceptable. Prefer greening. Consider uses early, including at the Investigation Stage of a new major flyover or footbridge after it enters Category B of the Public Works Programme. Any building must be structurally isolated from columns, decks, supports and soffit. Cranes are prohibited unless the Highways Department agrees and, for private development, the Buildings Department agrees. Leave sufficient clearance for inspection and maintenance. No clearance dimension is printed. Do not block, damage or overload the drainage. Uses with a high fire hazard, such as storage of inflammable goods, should not be permitted. Environmentally sensitive uses with prolonged exposure, and polluting uses next to sensitive uses, should not be permitted. “Proximity” is not given a distance. Maintain air and visual permeability at street level. No permeability percentage is printed.
