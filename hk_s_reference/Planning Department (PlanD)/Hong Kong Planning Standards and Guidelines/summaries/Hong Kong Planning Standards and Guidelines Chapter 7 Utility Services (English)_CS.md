# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 7 Utility Services
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
November 2019 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This chapter gives planners the basic requirements for electricity, gas, telephone, radio telecommunications and broadcasting, water, drainage and district cooling in new development areas (§1.1, §1.2). It is a planning guideline, not the Buildings Ordinance. Figures 1 to 7 are cited as schematic drawings and did not yield further dimensions in the text.

## Regulatory Overview
Utility services are treated as basic infrastructure: installation buildings should be limited to the minimum size that meets the needs of the area served, co-location should be examined where safety standards and other policies allow, and screening and landscaping should be incorporated so that no unacceptable adverse environmental impact, including visual impact, is generated (§1.1, §1.3). Site areas, clearances and separations in this chapter are stated for reference or as general rules, and the text requires early consultation because individual reservations are subject to detailed examination (§2.2.2, §2.2.3(f), §2.3.7, §8.2).

### 3. Overhead lines, working corridors and cables (§2.3–§2.4)
New pylons and overhead transmission lines (OHL) should not dictate the pattern of future land use or sterilize land with good development potential. If the land is required for development, existing lines may have to be relaid underground or re-routed, unless they are part of an approved scheme which is the subject of an order under the Electricity Networks (Statutory Easements) Ordinance. The high cost of diversion should be considered (§2.3.1). They should not be erected, as far as practicable, in existing developed areas, areas of substantial development potential, or public open space. Except in very special circumstances, Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI), conservation areas and country parks should also be avoided (§2.3.2). For extra high voltage lines, for example 400 kV, detailed site search reports with full justifications should be prepared (§2.3.3).

| Route | Avoid, as far as practicable, residential areas and environmentally sensitive areas such as SSSI, conservation areas, country parks, ridgelines, or locations which will seriously affect the view from major residential developments (§2.3.4(a)) |
| Where a sensitive route cannot be avoided | Consult the Director of Agriculture and Fisheries where the development is within a country park, the Director of Planning, the Director of Environmental Protection, and the Director of Home Affairs on public consultation (§2.3.4(d)) |
| Designated Projects | For OHLs which are Designated Projects under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance, the statutory environmental impact assessment process must be followed and environmental permits are required for construction and operation. The footnote states that, under Items H.1 and Q.1 of Schedule 2, transmission lines of 400 kV in any location, or more than 66 kV if within environmentally sensitive areas, are Designated Projects (§2.3.4(e)) |

### 8. Drainage services (§7)
Public drainage, covering foul sewerage, sewage treatment and disposal, and stormwater drainage, is provided by the Drainage Services Department. Public or private drainage shall conform with the environmental planning standards and guidelines in Chapter 9. Those standards are not reprinted in this chapter (§7.1.1, §7.1.2).

| Rule | Text |
|---|---|
| Foul sewers | Collect and convey sewage in enclosed foul sewers, normally underground, designed to minimize odour and septic problems. For septicity control, the text refers to Drainage Services Department Practice Note No. 1/2011 (§7.2.1) |
| Alignment | Sewers may lie under carriageways, footpaths or cycle tracks. Avoid amenity strips unless necessary. If that is not feasible, provide separate drainage reserves. Choose the alignment so that maintenance minimizes disruption to vehicular and pedestrian traffic. Where sewers are under carriageways, locate them within one traffic lane as far as possible. Use gravity sewerage as far as possible (§7.2.2) |
| Stormwater | Enclosed drains or open channels. Section 7.2.2 also applies to enclosed stormwater drains. New channels and systems should adopt environmental and sustainable design as far as practicable. For green river channels and flood mitigation and retention, the text refers to Drainage Services Department Practice Note No. 1/2005 (§7.3.1) |
| Channel alignment | Minimize disruption to adjacent communities and minimize land resumption (§7.3.2) |
| Supporting land | Provide vehicular access for operation and maintenance. Landscaping should be considered. Channel embankments may be permitted as amenities or recreational areas, and access roads as waterfront promenades (§7.3.3) |
| Treatment works and pumping stations | Design to minimize noise, odour and visual problems. Locate them away from residential or other sensitive areas by providing suitable buffer zones, where possible. The chapter states no buffer width. If a buffer is not feasible, include acoustic insulation, odour control and landscaping. A pumping station at a prominent waterfront should have its footprint minimized, with landscaping and enhancement. Rising mains and treatment works should minimize unintended prolonged retention of sewage and septic conditions (§7.4.1) |
| Polder and stormwater pumping schemes | These protect buildings and houses in low-lying areas by a stormwater pumping station, a stormwater storage pond, and a flood protective bund or wall around the buildings, houses and storage pond (§7.5.1) |
| Storage ponds | Locate them at the lowest areas of the scheme. They should either be covered or properly fenced off. Under no circumstances should public access routes be allowed through the areas of the stormwater storage ponds (§7.5.2) |
| Drainage reserves | Unrestricted vehicular access at all times. Structures of any kind should generally not be permitted unless in exceptional circumstances. Planting that involves extensive and deep-root plants needs the Drainage Services Department’s endorsement before implementation (§7.6.1) |

**SD takeaway:** Keep a drainage reserve open to vehicles at all times and generally free of structures. Where a sewage treatment works or pumping station adjoins a sensitive use, the chapter calls for a suitable buffer zone where possible and states no width (§7.4.1, §7.6.1).

### 10. District cooling system (§9)
The guidelines are intended for a district cooling system (DCS) carried out by Government as a public project. Proponents other than Government are to consult the Environment Bureau and the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department at an early planning stage (§9.1.2). It is Government policy to formalise the requirement to consider a DCS at the early stage of planning for large-scale new development areas and redevelopment areas where a larger number of potential consumer buildings can be identified (§9.1.1). A DCS produces and distributes chilled water to consumer buildings through a closed-loop network of underground pipes (§9.1.3). Whether a DCS should be developed rests with the Environment Bureau. The Electrical and Mechanical Services Department provides technical advice. Feasibility should be explored in the planning and engineering study for the area (§9.3).

| Planning figure | Printed value |
|---|---|
| Preferred water source | Seawater, for energy efficiency. The plant room should preferably be close to the seashore so as to minimise seawater pipework or culvert length. Other water supply can be considered subject to technical assessment (§9.2.1) |
| Distance to consumer buildings | The Electrical and Mechanical Services Department has recommended that targeted consumer buildings should normally not be more than 2,000 m from the plant room (§9.2.2) |
| Standard plant-room site | Minimum site area 5,400 m², typical dimension 180 m × 30 m, to serve a cooling demand of about 40,000 refrigeration tons. The exact site depends on the scale served and should be determined with the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department (§9.2.3) |
| Demand threshold | In general, chilled water is provided to non-residential developments, because residential developments normally will not adopt the central air-conditioning that a DCS requires. For planning purposes, a total of 200,000 m² of non-residential air-conditioning floor area from all targeted consumer buildings is recommended to warrant a standard DCS that is financially sustainable (§9.2.4) |
| Residential exception | A footnote states that it may be possible to serve residential buildings that have central air-conditioning, subject to request and spare capacity (§9.2.4, footnote 5) |
| Level | Preferably underground, for example beneath a park or playground, with sufficient openings for operation and maintenance. If cooling towers are used because seawater is not available for heat rejection, an above-ground structure is required, and its bulk and height should be minimized (§9.2.5) |
| Co-location | Avoid a standalone or detached plant room as far as possible, and locate it in an area of minimal development potential as far as possible. Co-location with drainage facilities, pumping facilities and flyovers can be considered, as can uses less sensitive to noise and vibration, for example public open space, a public car park, a Government workshop or store, and a public market. In a joint-user building, the plant room is preferably in the basement or on the ground floor (§9.2.6) |
| Environmental assessment | If the new development area or redevelopment area falls within Schedule 3 major designated projects under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance (Cap. 499), assess the DCS in that environmental impact assessment. Water discharges may affect ecology, fisheries and water quality. Assess nuisance to sensitive receivers and to joint users (§9.3.1–§9.3.3) |
| Waterfront design | Above-ground structures, particularly near the waterfront, should respond to the waterfront setting and avoid a visual and physical barrier to the open water and disruption of the continuous waterfront promenade. The text refers to Chapter 11 (§9.3.4) |
| Connection | Refer to the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department Technical Guidelines for Connection to District Cooling System, and consult that department early (§9.3.5) |

**SD takeaway:** Test a Government district-cooling plant against the printed 5,400 m² site, the 2,000 m consumer distance and the 200,000 m² non-residential air-conditioned floor area, and confirm the site with the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department.
