# Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines — Chapter 3 Community Facilities
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
March 2022 Edition | Planning Department (PlanD) — Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines

> Scope note: This chapter assesses land for community facilities provided by Government or the private sector: education, medical and health, police, magistracies, correctional facilities, fire and ambulance, arts venues, community halls and social welfare, post offices, public mortuaries, and funeral depots and parlours. Other services are left to Chapters 4 to 12. The March 2022 edition adds population-based rehabilitation ratios from the Persons with Disabilities and Rehabilitation Programme Plan (2020), with floor areas, locational factors and sensitive-facility guidance, and revises paragraph 1.4.2(b), Sub-section 10.2 (Part), Table 3 (Part) and Table 4 (Part). These are planning guidelines for site and premises reservation. They are not Buildings Ordinance control, and several standards are stated to be applied flexibly or confirmed with the client department.

## Regulatory Overview
Chapter 3 gives population triggers, reference site areas and net operational floor areas for reserving community facilities, including the March 2022 rehabilitation ratios and the rules for sensitive Group A and Group B facilities. Where a premises standard also sits in another instrument, the chapter points to that instrument (for example the Child Care Services Regulations and the residential care home regulations) rather than replacing it.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Sensitive community facilities (paras 1.4.1–1.4.4)
No separation distance or buffer width is printed.

| Group | What it covers | Locational rule in the text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group A | Territorial facilities whose users do not need frequent attendance: correctional facilities, public mortuaries, funeral depots and parlours | Normally stand-alone. Preferably not directly next to, or at a close distance from, residential development and non-sensitive community facilities. If a close location cannot be avoided, provide buffer areas with physical barriers. |
| Group B | Local or district facilities for specific clients who need frequent services: special medical and health clinics, education facilities, and social welfare services (para 1.4.2(b) as revised) | Integrate with the local community rather than segregate, where possible. Consider joint-user development. If a facility should not be near residential or other development, the client bureau or department must give site-identification criteria. |

Accessibility for staff, users and visitors applies to both groups. Group A may be further from populated areas. Clear signage may be needed so access to some Group B facilities is not disturbed. Paragraph 1.4.4 requires the project proponent to consult the Home Affairs Department and the respective District Office at an early stage, to set out why the facility and the site are needed, and to give the District Council and other relevant local groups a chance to comment. Comments are to be answered, consultees told the result, and a record kept. Extra effort is expected for Group B, stressing community integration. In less densely populated New Territories areas, facilities such as clinics have been provided at a smaller scale than in the urban area, and paragraph 1.3 recommends that this continue. No reduced rural area is quantified.

**SD takeaway:** Treat correctional, mortuary and funeral uses as stand-alone Group A sites with a physical buffer from housing, and plan special clinics, schools and social-welfare uses as integrated Group B accommodation, including joint-user buildings.

### 2. Education facilities (Section 2, Table 1, paras 2.2–2.3)

#### Kindergartens (para 2.2.1, Table 4 item 1 and notes)
Sites reserved only for kindergartens are usually unnecessary. Space is allocated in housing estates and large private developments.

#### Primary schools (paras 2.2.2–2.2.14, Table 4 item 2)
One whole-day classroom for 25.5 persons aged 6–11. Reserve on a district basis. The territory is divided into 18 districts, the same as the 18 District Councils. In each new development area, set aside an additional reserve equal to 10% of the number of primary-school sites calculated from the standards (para 2.2.10). If specific demographic projections exist, that 10% can be applied with flexibility.

| School | Population in the 6–11 age group | Reference site area | Minimum width | Printed plan size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30-classroom | 765 | 6 200 m² | 65 m | 95 m × 65 m |
| 24-classroom | 612 | 4 700 m² | 55 m | 85 m × 55 m |
| 18-classroom | 459 | 3 950 m² | 55 m | 72 m × 55 m |

Prefer a site large enough for a 30-classroom school. Use 24-classroom, then 18-classroom, only where a 30-classroom site cannot be reserved. Sites should also take the building, ball courts, bus lay-bys and car parking. If car parking cannot be inside the curtilage, provide it close to the school. The Secretary for Education may accept a site that misses the reference size or is poorly configured; consult the Secretary for Education and the Director of Architectural Services. Table 4 says these land requirements are for general reference and are subject to those two parties. High-rise schools and joint-development school buildings may be considered case by case if the joint use is compatible with the school.

Comprehensive housing, public or private, should be self-supporting in primary places. A Housing Authority estate provides a site for its own design population unless the district already has a surplus of places or reservations. Private comprehensive developments, including Housing Society estates, should secure the same opportunity; whether the developer builds the school, and who pays, is a policy consultation and is not quantified here.

#### Secondary schools (paras 2.2.15–2.2.20, Table 4 item 3)
One whole-day classroom for 40 persons aged 12–17. One 30-classroom school, operating 30 whole-day classes, for every 1 200 persons aged 12–17. Secondary technical and pre-vocational schools use this secondary standard.

#### Other education land (paras 2.2.27–2.2.35, Table 4 items 4–7)
Technical education and vocational training have no fixed provision standard; site area is case by case, on a territory-wide basis. Special schools are territory-wide, located as widely as possible, with no site area printed. Universities funded by the University Grants Committee are territory-wide, with no site area printed. Post-secondary colleges are territory-wide. As a general guide, reserve 2 000 m² to 7 000 m², suitably configured for the building, ball courts and carparks, with total gross floor area about 6 500 m² to 22 000 m². They may be free-standing at ground level or part of a composite structure, and should be easily accessible by public transport.

#### Location (paras 2.3.1–2.3.9)
Apply Chapter 9 environmental guidelines. Do not locate schools close to air-pollution sources, potentially hazardous installations, or within the consultation zone of landfills. Site them away from significant noise; if that is unavoidable, abatement is agreed with the Director of Environmental Protection and the Secretary for Education. Provide emergency vehicular access to the satisfaction of the Director of Fire Services. As a general rule, schools are free-standing at ground level. A school on a podium may be considered case by case if it is still free-standing and meets the other locational requirements. Locate schools, as far as circumstances permit, near public open space and compatible facilities such as public libraries, community centres and indoor recreation centres, excluding open sports grounds and stadiums. Arrange ingress, egress and loading so that local traffic congestion is minimised. Where possible, orientate the classroom block with its longest axis east-west, and place the open play area and ball courts next to the school building. Technical institutes and industrial training centres should preferably be near industrial areas. Primary and secondary schools should, where feasible, be paired or clustered so facilities can be shared or the sites developed as a school village.

Figures 1 to 4 are the general block layouts for the 30-, 24- and 18-classroom primary schools and the 30-classroom secondary school. The text extract does not contain further dimensions from those drawings.

**SD takeaway:** Lock a 30-classroom primary reservation at 6 200 m² with a 65 m width, plus a 10% site reserve in a new development area, a 30-classroom secondary reservation at 6 950 m² with a 65 m width, and kindergartens within a 0.4 km radius that do not rely on an uncontrolled major-road crossing.

### 3. Medical and health facilities (Section 3, Table 4 items 8–11)

| Facility | Provision | Site standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hospitals, all bed types, public and private | 5.5 beds per 1 000 persons, long-term aim. Bed mix (acute general, extended care, psychiatric) is regional | 80 m² per bed, including supporting services, car parking, loading and unloading, and scope for expansion |
| Extended care hospital (para 3.2.2). Table 4 uses the label convalescent/infirmary hospitals | Included in the 5.5 beds | 60 m² per bed |
| Specialist clinic / polyclinic | One whenever a hospital is built (Table 4: whenever a regional or district hospital is built) | Para 3.3.2: about 4 712 m² (62 m × 76 m). Table 4 prints about 4 700 m² (62 m × 76 m). Allow scope to expand ambulatory and out-patient services |
| General clinic / health centre | One per 100 000 persons, applied with flexibility to district needs. Primary care, including general out-patient and family health services | Para 3.4.2: about 2 220 m² (37 m × 60 m). Table 4 prints about 2 200 m² (37 m × 60 m) |
| Rural clinics | No set standard. Each case on its merits | No area printed |

Hospitals and clinics need good accessibility and a reasonably central location. Prefer an elevated hospital site, insulated from noise and fumes from major roads and industrial uses. Put specialist clinics next to public hospitals. Put general clinics centrally in the district, on public transport, and where possible with other daily community facilities. Eight named hospital clusters are an administrative framework; the chapter does not split the 5.5 beds among them.

**SD takeaway:** Reserve hospital land at 80 m² per bed, or 60 m² per bed for an extended-care hospital, against 5.5 beds per 1 000 persons, and where a stand-alone clinic site is required use about 4 712 m² for a specialist clinic and about 2 220 m² for a general clinic.

### 5. Magistracies (Section 5, Table 4 item 16)
One 8-courtroom magistracy for a population up to 660 000, applied with flexibility to Judiciary policy and district needs. For a standard 8-courtroom magistracy, allow a site of about 4 200 m² (61 m × 69 m). Whether it must be a single-user building is confirmed with the Judiciary Administrator and the Government Property Administrator. Single-user site dimensions should be flexible where possible. Locate on or close to main public-transport routes, in a central position, and where possible next to other main Government offices. Provide adequate on-site loading, unloading and parking, subject to Transport Department advice. Table 4 labels the area served as regional.

**SD takeaway:** Reserve about 4 200 m² (61 m × 69 m) for one 8-courtroom magistracy serving up to 660 000 persons, on a central public-transport site with on-site loading and parking.

### 6. Correctional facilities (Section 6, Table 4 item 17)
Facilities include minimum, medium and maximum institutions, drug addiction treatment centres, psychiatric centres, rehabilitation centres, training centres and halfway houses. No population ratio, bedspace rate or site area is printed. Scale is a long-term assessment. Table 4 says reservation is determined on a regional basis and prints no land area.

Location: reasonably insulated from main urban areas, but with good communications for visits; good access between institutions; enough platform area for outdoor recreational fields, away from slopes and terraced site forms; a reasonable aspect given possible effects on adjoining amenity and development. These are Group A sensitive facilities under paragraph 1.4.2(a). Security design should still allow natural light and fresh air to cells and dormitories. No light, air or setback figure is printed.

**SD takeaway:** Do not apply a population ratio; reserve a stand-alone Group A site with platform area for outdoor fields, off slopes, insulated from the main urban area but still visitable.

### 7. Fire service facilities (Section 7, Table 4 item 18)
Provision and siting follow a graded response time from the fire risk category system under the Policy on Fire Fighting endorsed by the Executive Council in April 1977. Response time is the period from receipt of the call at the Fire Services Communication Centre to arrival at the scene. No response-time minutes, and no population ratio, are printed. Initial attendance follows the fire risk category unless a higher response is warranted.

| Station | Minimum site |
| --- | --- |
| Standard divisional fire station, one per division | 2 960 m², minimum frontage 47 m |
| Standard sub-divisional fire station | 1 800 m², minimum frontage 37 m |
| Joint divisional fire station and ambulance depot | 3 830 m², minimum frontage 80 m, drill yard 1 635 m² behind the divisional fire station building block |
| Joint sub-divisional fire station and ambulance depot | 2 670 m², minimum frontage 70 m, drill yard 1 225 m² behind the sub-divisional fire station building block |
| Non-standard fire station | No set site standard; local needs |

If the run-in to the drill yard is not from the back, widen the frontage. The site area stays the same. Joint sites with ambulance depots should be used wherever practicable. A fire station may occupy the ground and lower levels of a building with other government development above only if it is completely segregated, has separate ground-floor access, keeps windows of the other development overlooking the drill yard to the minimum, and the other use is compatible. Combined development is judged case by case and needs Fire Services Department agreement. Locate with ready access onto both directions of a primary or district distributor, taking account of circulation and interchanges.

**SD takeaway:** Reserve 2 960 m² with a 47 m frontage for a divisional station, or 3 830 m² with an 80 m frontage and a 1 635 m² drill yard when the ambulance depot is on the same site, and keep any upper government use fully segregated with its own ground-floor access.

### 8. Ambulance service facilities (Section 8, Table 4 item 19)
The number of ambulances follows projected population distribution and projected incidence of emergency and removal calls, including social-welfare hospitalisation and specialised clinics. No ambulances-per-population figure is printed. Ambulance stations are non-standard and based on local needs.

| Facility | Minimum site |
| --- | --- |
| Ambulance depot. The standard sub-divisional fire station design is used, except there is no drill tower | 1 160 m², frontage 36 m |
| Joint ambulance depot and divisional fire station | 3 830 m², minimum frontage 80 m, drill yard 1 635 m² behind the divisional fire station building block |
| Joint ambulance depot and sub-divisional fire station | 2 670 m², minimum frontage 70 m, drill yard 1 225 m² behind the sub-divisional fire station building block |

If the run-in to the drill yard is not from the back, widen the frontage; the site area stays the same. The same segregation rules as fire stations apply, using the ambulance service yard instead of the drill yard, and Fire Services Department agreement is required. Emergency coverage is within 10 minutes in urban areas and new towns, and within 20 minutes in rural areas. Locate near major hospitals that generate non-emergency removals, areas with many emergency calls, and clinics including physiotherapy. Ready access is required onto both directions of a primary or district distributor.

**SD takeaway:** Reserve 1 160 m² with a 36 m frontage for a depot, and site it so the 10-minute urban and new-town coverage, or the 20-minute rural coverage, can be met from a distributor-road frontage.

### 9. Arts venues (Section 9, Table 4 item 20)
No population ratio and no site or floor area is printed. Need is assessed by the Secretary for Home Affairs. Arts venues here cover dance, drama, film and media arts, music, performance and visual arts, in forms such as theatres, coliseums, studios, exhibition halls, function rooms and production offices. Commercial cinemas and discos are excluded. Museums and libraries are excluded unless they are arts-related.

Two levels are distinguished. Territory-wide venues should be prominent, easily reached by public transport, and within reasonable commuting distance of residents and visitors; venues with many overseas patrons should be near major hotels, tourist attractions and main shopping areas. Community venues, such as town halls and civic centres, need access to the residential districts they serve. Arts-education facilities should be within walking distance of schools and community facilities. No walking distance is printed.

Follow Chapter 9 on environment. Consider buffering from roads and from incompatible uses such as refuse collection points and markets. Setbacks and landscaped open space are named as buffers; no width is printed. Avoid noise from open-area venues on surrounding sensitive receivers. Plan either an arts district (horizontal mix, including supporting carparks, restaurants and shops, within walking distance) or an arts centre (related artforms under one roof). Territory-wide venues should have landmark external design. Provide loading and unloading for props, sets and exhibits, with turning circles for temporary parking of goods vehicles, and container lifts and handling for large objects. Parking for goods vehicles and private cars should be very close, preferably on site. No bay numbers are printed. Provide ramps with handrails to stages, seats and other facilities, disabled toilets, and carparks reserved for disabled users. Include production offices, rehearsal, practice, workshop and, where appropriate, training space, plus storage and possibly set-building space.

**SD takeaway:** Do not reserve a standard arts site from a population ratio; lock public-transport access, a buffer from refuse collection points and markets, and on-site or immediate loading with turning space for sets and props.

### 10. Community halls (paras 10.1.1–10.1.13, Table 4 item 22)
The former community-centre standard, which paired a hall with a welfare block of fixed floor area, is no longer valid. Welfare floor area is now assessed project by project. Halls are provided on need, advised by the Director of Home Affairs, considering population, local character, location, existing halls and their usage and accessibility, and alternative accommodation. If a full-size hall is not justified, use specially designed multi-purpose function rooms or selected school halls.

| Item | Printed figure | Where printed |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Hall capacity and rooms | Multi-purpose hall for 450 persons, a stage, a stage store, male and female dressing rooms, and a meeting room. If not provided elsewhere: ancillary management office, store room, conference room and toilets | Para 10.1.10 |
| Clear height in a joint-user building | Preferably a minimum clearance height of 7.65 m | Table 4 only |
| Stand-alone site, exceptional circumstances | 2,100 m² (60 m × 35 m) | Table 4 only |

Prefer the hall as part of an integrated GIC development in a joint-users building, or as part of a comprehensive development where no suitable GIC site is available. The extract uses the abbreviation GIC and does not spell it out. Stand-alone halls need agreement of the Property Strategy Group and, as advised by the Government Property Administrator, are approved only in exceptional circumstances. Other uses co-located with the hall should preferably be day-to-day public uses such as government offices, welfare facilities and clinics. Locate the hall centrally for the population served. Table 4 labels the area served as local.

**SD takeaway:** In a joint-user building, lock about 1 260 m² gross floor area for a 450-person hall and, from Table 4, a minimum clearance height of 7.65 m; use a 2,100 m² stand-alone site only in the exceptional case Table 4 describes.

### 11. Social welfare floor-area basis, child care, youth and family services (paras 10.2.1–10.2.14 and 10.2.35–10.2.39, Table 3 items 1, 2 and 10)

#### Floor-area definitions (Table 3)
Net operational floor area (NOFA), unless otherwise stated, is the rounded-up sum of internal dimensions of rooms and spaces in the approved Schedule of Accommodation. It excludes structure and partitions, circulation, staircases, staircase halls, lift landings, toilets, and mechanical and electrical services such as lifts and air-conditioning. Net usable floor area (NUFA), for private and public housing, is NOFA plus ancillary accommodation for the sole use of the facility, including circulation, toilets, internal partitions and structure. It excludes common areas, lift and staircase enclosures, structural elements and service ducts that serve the rest of the building. The approved NOFA must be provided. A deviation of 5% below or above the approved NOFA may be acceptable, depending on the case. NUFA is a guide to the area to reserve. Where the extract lists every capacity, then every NOFA, then every NUFA, the rows below follow that column order. Prose confirms the NOFA values noted in the text; it does not restate NUFA.

#### Aided child care centres (paras 10.2.5–10.2.11, Table 3 item 1, Table 4 item 23)
100 aided places per 25 000 persons, applied flexibly. A child care centre provides day care for children under 3. For 100 places, NOFA is 530 m², excluding toilets, partitions, baby care room and circulation area.

| Capacity (places) | NOFA (m²) | NUFA (m²) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 530, excluding toilets, partitions, baby care room and circulation | 689 |
| 150 | 776, with the same exclusions | 1 009 |
| 200 | 1 035, with the same exclusions | 1 346 |

Locate away from excessive noise, fumes and smells; proximity to open space or playground is preferred. Under the Child Care Services Ordinance (Cap. 243) and the Child Care Services Regulations (Cap. 243A), no part of the premises, except the parapet wall around a roof playground, shall be more than 12 m above ground for a centre for children aged under two, or more than 24 m above ground in any other case, unless the Director of Social Welfare, with the advice of the Director of Fire Services, authorises a greater height. Lower is generally better for escape. Every toilet room needs an opening or openings to external air of at least one tenth of that room’s floor area, unless waived by the Social Welfare Department. Consult the Director of Fire Services on fire hazard from adjacent development.

#### Integrated children and youth services centres (paras 10.2.12–10.2.14, Table 3 item 2, Table 4 item 24)
One centre per 12 000 persons aged 6–24, applied flexibly. NOFA 631 m². Table 3 prints no NUFA. Locate centrally, and where possible with public recreation areas and other daily community facilities.

#### Integrated family service centres (paras 10.2.35–10.2.39, Table 3 item 10, Table 4 item 29)
Each centre serves a defined boundary of 100 000 to 150 000 persons, also reflecting complexity of social problems and district needs. Minimum 12 social workers apart from a supervisor. NOFA 535 m² is based on a staff of 25 to 28, of whom at least 13 are social work staff, and is adjusted to actual staff. Table 3 prints no NUFA. Prefer a concentrated, public-transport location, close to other welfare facilities. Ground floor is preferred; other floors served by lifts are also suitable.

**SD takeaway:** Reserve aided child care at 100 places per 25 000 persons, keep under-two centres within 12 m of ground and other child care centres within 24 m unless a written height authorisation is obtained, and use 631 m² NOFA for an integrated children and youth centre and 535 m² NOFA for an integrated family service centre.

The neighbourhood-centre and day-care rows are one column-order reading of Table 3: capacities 40, 60 and 80, then area figures 303, 267, 358 and 506, then 394, 401, 537 and 759. That order places the prose NOFA of 303 m² on the neighbourhood centre and 267 m² on the 40-place day care centre. The 60-place and 80-place cells are not repeated in the prose. Community care services: 17.2 subsidised places per 1 000 elderly persons aged 65 or above, applied at district level. Table 4 note: there is no rigid split in the Elderly Services Programme Plan; in general, 60% of community-care demand is home-based and 40% is centre-based. Residential care: 21.3 subsidised beds per 1 000 elderly persons aged 65 or above, planned on five clusters (Hong Kong, Kowloon East, Kowloon West, New Territories East and New Territories West), applied at cluster level. All elderly centres and day care centres or units should be on the ground floor or lower floors. If above ground floor, they should not be more than 24 m above ground and should be reached by lifts. Proximity to open space or playground is preferred. Day care centres and units should be close to a lay-by for a private light bus with tail-lift, and for emergency vehicles. A home-based team should preferably be in the same district as its users, or in a neighbouring district. Large residential developments should, where feasible, reserve sites and premises for community care services for their own elderly residents. Residential care homes should be on the ground floor or lower floors, preferably with their own lifts. Regulation 20 of the Residential Care Homes (Elderly Persons) Regulations: unless authorised by the Director of Social Welfare, no part of an elderly home should be more than 24 m above ground level. Child care centres and residential care homes for the elderly are named as sensitive uses; apply Chapter 9.

**SD takeaway:** Apply 17.2 community-care places and 21.3 residential-care beds per 1 000 elderly persons aged 65 or above; if an elderly centre or day-care premises is above the ground floor, keep it not more than 24 m above ground with lift access, and for a residential care home apply Regulation 20 (no part more than 24 m above ground unless the Director of Social Welfare authorises otherwise), with a lay-by for a tail-lift private light bus at day care centres and units.

### 15. Post offices (Section 11, Table 4 item 37)
In urban areas, large population groups should have access within 1.2 km of where they live or work. In rural areas, assume 3.2 km. Also consider catchment population, terrain, barriers such as busy main roads and railway lines, special social or postal need, and whether the place is a daily focal point. Provision is subject to the Postmaster General. No floor area is printed. Table 4 says there is no specific site allocation because the facility is normally in a composite building.

Generally incorporate post offices in private or government buildings that are central to the area served. Individual sites may sometimes be required. All post offices need direct vehicular access and should preferably be at street level. A delivery office should be near a public-transport focal point, with off-street parking for collection and delivery vehicles. Large urban offices need a separate compound for loading and unloading mail vans in secure conditions. Premises must be completely self-contained, with no access to non-postal accommodation through any part of the postal areas.

**SD takeaway:** Place urban post offices so that large population groups are within 1.2 km, rural offices within 3.2 km, at street level with direct vehicular access, and with the postal area sealed off from other accommodation.

### 16. Public mortuaries (Section 12, Table 4 item 38)
Provide storage for all bodies received over about 3 weeks, at five bodies per day per million population, plus additional space for emergencies or disaster. No site area is printed; size depends on the scale of the mortuary. Long-term policy of the Food and Health Bureau, subject to suitable sites: one public mortuary for each of five regions, namely Hong Kong Island, Kowloon West, Kowloon East, New Territories West and New Territories East.

Provide two vehicular access points, one for the public and one for servicing, physically apart as far as possible, with space to park and manoeuvre mortuary vehicles at those points. Locate where the public can reach the mortuary easily, but separate it from residential and commercial development by topography and/or public services, public utilities or open space. Grouping with funeral depots and parlours may be convenient. These are Group A sensitive facilities.

**SD takeaway:** Plan one mortuary for each of the five named regions, sized for about three weeks of storage at five bodies per day per million population, with two separated vehicle accesses and a Group A buffer from housing and commercial development.

### 17. Funeral depots and parlours (Section 13, Table 4 item 39)
As an approximate guide, one funeral depot site of about 0.25 ha for each 350 000 population. A depot is an operating station with a cemetery office, a repository and facilities for collection and temporary storage of unclaimed bodies. In addition, several sites for commercial funeral parlours of about 50 m × 25 m (0.125 ha) should be provided so that there is a choice and monopolies are avoided. No number of parlour sites is printed. A parlour provides embalming rooms, reposing rooms and service halls.

Locate parlours close to main public transport and traffic routes, but separate them from residential and commercial development by topography and/or public services, public utilities or open space. Grouping depots, parlours and public mortuaries in funeral services centres is an advantage. These are Group A sensitive facilities. Table 4 labels funeral depots as regional and does not print a separate parlour row.

**SD takeaway:** Reserve about 0.25 ha of depot land per 350 000 persons and parlour sites of about 50 m × 25 m, close to main transport but buffered from housing and commercial development as Group A uses.
