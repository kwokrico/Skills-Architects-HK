# Standard Master Schedule of Notes to Statutory Plans (Urban Covering Notes)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Header on the PDF: Urban_CN_Eng_8-4-05 | For areas not previously covered by IDPA/DPA plans | Planning Department — Master Schedule of Notes to Statutory Plans

> Scope note: These are the covering Notes for an Outline Zoning Plan whose area was **not** previously covered by an interim or draft Development Permission Area plan. They are part of the plan. They are not the Notes of any one plan, and they do not set plot ratio or building height. Paragraphs marked * are added only where that plan needs them. Where the plan is a new town plan covering Village Type Development zones, paragraph (12) also defines New Territories Exempted House.

## Regulatory Overview

The urban covering Notes state which uses are always permitted and which need Town Planning Board permission, and they apply across every zone on the plan. A use that the Notes permit must still meet other legislation, the Government lease, and other Government requirements.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Existing use until there is a material change (paragraph 3)

| Rule | Effect |
|---|---|
| (3)(a) | No action is required to make an existing use conform until there is a material change of use or the building is redeveloped |
| (3)(b) | A material change of use, any other development, or redevelopment must be always permitted, or must follow a permission. Minor alteration or modification of an always-permitted existing use is excepted |
| (3)(c)(i) before the first plan | A use in existence before the first statutory plan and continued since, or a Buildings Ordinance approval for an existing building |
| (3)(c)(ii) after the first plan | A use permitted under a plan and effected during that plan and continued since, or a Buildings Ordinance approval permitted under the plan then in force |

**SD takeaway:** An existing use can stay. Redevelopment or a material change of use must fit the zone or a permission.

### 2. Permissions lapse, and roads may shift (paragraphs 4 and 5)

When a use, material change of use, development, or redevelopment is carried out as always permitted or under a permission, all permissions for that site lapse, unless the Town Planning Board specifies otherwise. Road junctions, alignments of roads and railway or tram tracks, and boundaries between zones may be adjusted as detailed planning proceeds. The railway, tram, and junction items are added where appropriate.

**SD takeaway:** Do not rely on an old permission once the site is redeveloped under a new always-permitted scheme.

### 3. Temporary uses (paragraph 6)

| Period | Rule |
|---|---|
| Expected to be 5 years or less | Always permitted. No need to conform to the zone or these Notes. Other legislation, the lease, and other Government requirements still apply |
| Expected to be over 5 years | Must conform to the zone or these Notes |

**SD takeaway:** A use of five years or less need not match the zone. Over five years, it must.

### 4. Uses always permitted on any land (paragraph 7)

Always permitted anywhere on the plan, except where the use is in Column 2 of a zone, and except in Site of Special Scientific Interest, Conservation Area, or Coastal Protection Area under paragraph (8):

| Group | Uses |
|---|---|
| (a) | Provision, maintenance, or repair of plant nursery, amenity planting, open space, rain shelter, refreshment kiosk, road, bus or public light bus stop or lay-by, cycle track, taxi rank, nullah, public utility pipeline, electricity mast, lamp pole, telephone booth, telecommunications radio base station, automatic teller machine, and shrine. Where added: light rail, tram, light rail track, Mass Transit Railway station entrance, and Mass Transit Railway structure below ground level |
| (b) | Geotechnical works, local public works, road works, sewerage, drainage, environmental improvement works, marine related facilities, waterworks excluding a service reservoir, and other public works co-ordinated or implemented by Government |
| (c) | Maintenance or repair of watercourse and grave |

**SD takeaway:** A road, a utility pipeline, or Government public works can sit on the zone without a planning application, unless that zone puts the item in Column 2 or the land is SSSI, Conservation Area, or Coastal Protection Area.

### 5. SSSI, Conservation Area, and Coastal Protection Area (paragraph 8)

Added where those zones are on the plan.

| Always permitted | Needs permission |
|---|---|
| Maintenance or repair of plant nursery, amenity planting, sitting-out area, rain shelter, refreshment kiosk, road, watercourse, nullah, public utility pipeline, electricity mast, lamp pole, telephone booth, shrine, and grave | Provision of plant nursery, amenity planting, sitting-out area, rain shelter, refreshment kiosk, footpath, public utility pipeline, electricity mast, lamp pole, telephone booth, and shrine |
| Geotechnical works, local public works, roads, sewerage, drainage, environmental improvement, marine related facilities, waterworks excluding a service reservoir, and other public works co-ordinated or implemented by Government | |

**SD takeaway:** On these zones, repair of a road is as of right. Building a new footpath, kiosk, or pipeline needs permission.

### 6. Road, Undetermined, ancillary uses, and existing building (paragraphs 9 to 12)

| Land | Rule |
|---|---|
| Shown as Road | Everything needs permission except paragraph (7) and, where added, toll plaza, on-street vehicle park, railway track, and tram track |
| Undetermined, where that zone is added | Everything needs permission except paragraph (7) |
| Ancillary | Building, engineering, and other operations incidental to, and uses directly related and ancillary to, a permitted use in the same zone are always permitted. No separate permission |
| Existing building | A building, including a structure, which is physically existing and complies with relevant legislation and the Government lease |

On a new town plan that covers Village Type Development, paragraph (12) also defines New Territories Exempted House: a domestic building other than a guesthouse or hotel, or a building primarily for habitation other than a guesthouse or hotel, whose ground floor may be Shop and Services or Eating Place, and whose building works are exempted by a certificate of exemption under Part III of the Buildings Ordinance (Application to the New Territories) Ordinance (Cap. 121).

**SD takeaway:** Land shown as Road is not a development site. Ancillary uses of a permitted use do not need their own application.
