# TPB PG-No. 8 Underground Development of Commercial and Car Parking Facilities
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
December 1992 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: These guidelines apply to applications under section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance for underground development of commercial and car parking facilities that extend beyond private lot boundaries into areas beneath public roads, open space, or government/institution/community (G/IC) zones. Commercial use in this guideline excludes hotel, general office and service apartment. The introduction of underground development is to encourage more efficient use of land in the urban area where activities are highly concentrated, where the public would gain from better traffic and/or pedestrian circulation, and where the scheme can integrate with neighbouring land and buildings and enhance the quality of the surrounding area.

## Regulatory Overview
An underground commercial or car parking scheme that leaves the private lot is judged on compatibility with surrounding sites and the wider land-use intention, a depth limit of six levels below ground, interfaces with surface uses, railways and utilities, consumer demand, neighbouring business, the road network, the environment during construction and in operation, and fire, evacuation and hazard control. The application is supported by a Layout Plan and the scheme-design guidelines, and, as applicable, a Master Landscape Plan, a traffic impact study, an Environmental Impact Statement and Environmental Management Plan, and an Engineering Method Statement.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Where underground commercial or car parking may be considered (paras. 1.1–1.2)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Uses covered | Commercial and car parking facilities. Commercial **excludes** hotel, general office and service apartment |
| Land covered | Development extending beyond private lot boundaries into areas beneath public roads, open space, or G/IC zones |
| Planning gain | Should be encouraged where the public would experience significant planning gains, such as improvements to traffic and/or pedestrian circulation |
| Integration | Should be located where reasonable integration with neighbouring land and buildings can be achieved in character and uses, and should demonstrate an enhancement to the quality of the surrounding areas |
| Districts that may receive sympathetic consideration | Suitable sites in major urban districts comprising **Central, Wanchai, Causeway Bay, Tsim Sha Tsui, Yau Ma Tei and Mong Kok** |

**SD takeaway:** Limit the brief to underground commercial and car parking, excluding hotel, general office and service apartment, and only where the development extends under a road, open space or G/IC land in a location that can connect to its neighbours.

### 2. Depth, land-use fit and structures that come above ground (section 2, criteria (a)–(d))

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Scale and extent | Should be compatible with the characteristics of the surrounding sites and with the broad land-use intention of the area from a wider planning viewpoint |
| Depth | The proposed development **should not exceed 6 levels below ground** |
| Deeper or more intense schemes | A higher intensity **must** be justified by special studies on engineering feasibility, viability, security and fire safety. Such applications are considered on their individual merits |
| Other surface and sub-surface uses | **Should not** impose any adverse planning and development constraints. The necessary railway protection measures and utility corridors **should** be respected |
| Elements emerging above ground | Staircases, vehicular access points, ventilation shafts, glazed roofs and ancillary structures **should** be sensitively integrated with existing and planned land uses |
| Pedestrian links | The development should improve pedestrian circulation by sub-surface connections to neighbouring uses, for example mass transit railway, hotels, shopping areas and car parks |

**SD takeaway:** Cap the underground building at six levels below ground unless special engineering, viability, security and fire-safety studies justify more, and design every shaft, stair, vehicle entrance and glazed roof as part of the surface uses with a sub-surface pedestrian link.

### 3. Demand, neighbours, traffic, environment and fire (section 2, criteria (e)–(i))

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Consumer demand | Should demonstrate sufficient consumer demand for the commercial/car parking facility, taking account of existing and planned developments in the area |
| Neighbouring business | **Should not** have any adverse impact upon the business activities of neighbouring developments |
| Roads | **Should not** have any adverse effect upon the local and strategic road network in capacity, safety and circulation |
| Parking and loading/unloading | Should be sufficiently provided according to the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines and to the satisfaction of the Transport Department |
| Environment | Schemes **should not** have an adverse effect upon the surrounding environment either during construction or after completion |
| Fire and hazards | Should satisfy fire protection, emergency evacuation and other hazard control requirements administered by the relevant authorities |

**SD takeaway:** Prove demand against existing and planned development, size parking and loading to the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines and the Transport Department, and show the local and strategic road network, neighbouring business, construction, operation, fire protection and emergency evacuation all remain acceptable.

### 4. Documents the application should include (section 3)

| Submission | When it is required |
|---|---|
| Layout Plan, with documentation and design consideration against the scheme design guidelines | The proposed development should be illustrated by a Layout Plan. Requirements are in Annexes 1 and 2 |
| Master Landscape Plan | **Will be required** for developments proposed beneath open space, to show how the two integrate. Requirements are in Annex 3 |
| Traffic impact study | **Will be required** to demonstrate no adverse impact on the transportation network. It should provide improvement measures, including temporary traffic management measures during construction, to the satisfaction of the Transport Department |
| “Environmental Impact Statement” and “Environmental Management Plan” | **Required** to identify likely impacts and mitigation measures (Annex 4) |
| “Engineering Method Statement” | **Required** to demonstrate the engineering and geological feasibility of the proposed development (Annex 5) |

**SD takeaway:** Start schematic design with the Layout Plan, the traffic impact study, the environmental statement and management plan, and the engineering method statement, and add a Master Landscape Plan wherever the development sits under open space.

### 5. Layout Plan and scheme design (Annexes 1 and 2)

The Layout Plan, together with supporting documents, **shall** comprise the items below.

| Layout Plan item | Content |
|---|---|
| Level plans | Layout of each proposed level: proposed uses, including G/IC uses; alignment and widths of internal roads; parking/servicing areas; plant and machinery; all pedestrian and vehicle ingress/egress points |
| Surface relationship | Openings to the surface and points of access for pedestrians and vehicles |
| Pedestrian circulation | A pedestrian circulation plan. Where walkways connect to commercial areas at street level, the ground-level pedestrian realm should be kept active, comfortable and diverse |
| Development schedule | Site formation and levels of development (in metres above or below PD); site area (square metres); site volume (cubic metres); commercial accommodation by use (maximum GFA in square metres); G/IC and open space facilities (types, with the minimum GFA for each facility); car parking spaces; loading/unloading spaces |
| Programme | Proposed phasing, relative timing of phases, and the facilities provided in each phase |
| Explanatory note | Character in relation to the surrounding area; integration with neighbouring land and buildings; enhancement of the quality of the surrounding area |
| Supplementary material | Cross-sections, elevations, photographs and models assist the Board |

| Scheme-design point | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Circulation | Comprehensive pedestrian and vehicular circulation, with clear directional signage, designed for convenience, comfort, orientation and safety |
| Identity | Attractive entrances, quality finishes, and features such as galleria and atria, to provide interest, public identity and visual integration |
| Surface intrusions | Vent shafts and staircases protruding into the surface should be designed to ameliorate intrusions and integrate with ground-level uses |
| Light and air | Natural/artificial lighting and ventilation should be adequate and well designed |
| Operation | Appropriate management, maintenance, security and surveillance measures should be incorporated into the scheme design |

**SD takeaway:** Draw every basement level with uses, road widths, plant, and all pedestrian and vehicle openings, and schedule levels relative to PD, site area, volume, maximum commercial GFA, minimum G/IC and open-space GFA, and parking and loading counts.

### 6. Open space, environment and engineering method (Annexes 3–5)

A Master Landscape Plan is required for underground development situated immediately beneath areas used as public pleasure grounds/open space. It should illustrate:

| Master Landscape Plan item | What to show |
|---|---|
| Existing condition | Survey of existing trees; inventory of existing facilities (for example sitting-out areas and paved areas) |
| Change | Landscaped areas lost during construction, and those retained; areas to be developed as open space on completion; a “before” and “after” illustration with a clear statement of the changes |
| Use of the open space | Pedestrian circulation through the open space; service vehicle access to the open space |
| Structure | Deck loading factors to support landscape works |
| Accommodation | New schedule of accommodation (list of facilities) |

| Environmental assessment | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| Construction — above-ground works | Location of above-ground structures against existing surface land uses; air quality; dust emissions; noise; pedestrian severance; potential loss of amenity; traffic generated by private and construction vehicles |
| Construction — mitigation | Mitigation measures outlined in the document, and the measures the applicant proposes, to reduce adverse environmental impacts during construction |
| Operation — shafts and plant | Emissions of noise and dust from ventilation shafts and plant penetrating the surface lot above the scheme. The implications of such emissions are a key consideration |
| Operation — authority standards | The scheme against the air/noise quality requirements identified by the Environmental Protection Department, so that appropriate mitigation measures are taken to meet those requirements |

The Engineering Method Statement demonstrates engineering and geological feasibility and includes:

| Engineering item | Content |
|---|---|
| Excavation | The proposed method of excavation (for example the open-cut method or tunnel excavation) and a plan showing the areas to be excavated |
| Site working space | Work areas required for plant and equipment and for storage of construction materials |
| Programme | Stages of development, possible disruption, and the proposed mitigation measures |

**SD takeaway:** Where the development is immediately under public pleasure grounds or open space, lock deck loading to the landscape, show trees and facilities before and after, and pair that plan with construction and operation dust and noise control and a stated excavation method.
