# TPB PG-No. 17A Designation of Comprehensive Development Area Zones
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
April 2016 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Governs when a “Comprehensive Development Area” is designated on a statutory plan and how the Board monitors implementation, including phased development and later re-designation. It is not the Master Layout Plan checklist and it does not fix a plot ratio or site coverage.

## Regulatory Overview
A “Comprehensive Development Area”, or the earlier “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Comprehensive Development/Redevelopment Area”, is designated under section 4(1)(f) of the Town Planning Ordinance only where no better alternative zoning can achieve the planning objectives, and only after the planning intention, land status, ownership, constraints and the likely prospect of implementation have been considered. The Board reviews each site at the end of the third year after designation and on a biennial basis after that, to avoid planning blight from withheld piecemeal development and to track implementation.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Why a site is designated (paragraphs 1.1–1.3 and 3.1)

| Objective | What designation is meant to do |
|---|---|
| Urban renewal | Facilitate urban renewal and restructuring of land uses in the old urban areas. |
| Obsolete areas | Provide incentives to restructure obsolete areas, including old industrial areas, and to phase out non-conforming uses such as open storage and container back-up uses in the rural areas. |
| Amalgamation | Provide site amalgamation and restructuring of road patterns, integrate land uses and infrastructure, and optimise development potential. |
| Capacity and interface | Achieve co-ordinated development where traffic, environmental or infrastructure capacity is constrained, and where incompatible land uses create interface problems. |
| Public facilities | Ensure adequate and timely Government, institution or community facilities, transport and public transport facilities, and open space for the development and, where possible, to address a district shortfall. |
| Scale and design | Ensure appropriate control on the overall scale and design in areas of high landscape and amenity value and in locations of special design or historical significance. |
| Public interest | Designation is in the interest of the wider public, although an individual owner’s right is taken into consideration. |
| Last resort | Sites will only be designated where there is no better alternative zoning mechanism to achieve the objectives in paragraph 3.1. |

**SD takeaway:** Treat a “Comprehensive Development Area” as a tool for one coordinated scheme — renewal, phasing out of non-conforming uses, capacity, public facilities, or design control — and not as a default zone.

### 2. Land status, ownership, prospect and size (paragraphs 3.2–3.7)

| Test | What the extract requires |
|---|---|
| Unallocated Government land | Sites subject to modern land grant conditions, including public housing to be implemented by the Housing Authority, **would only be designated in special circumstances**, where control of design and layout is necessary because of special site constraints or the special character of the area. |
| Urban Renewal Authority and Hong Kong Housing Society | Sites covered by an Urban Renewal Authority Development Scheme, or an urban improvement scheme of the Hong Kong Housing Society, are normally designated to prevent piecemeal development or redevelopment that would pre-empt optimum comprehensive redevelopment and urban restructuring. |
| Private land | Sites involving private land, other than Urban Renewal Authority or Hong Kong Housing Society sites, are **normally expected to have a major portion of the private land under single ownership** at designation. No percentage is stated. Each site is considered on its merits. |
| Third-party land | Because designation may affect a third party’s development or redevelopment right, the proponent would be required to indicate the land under his ownership and that he has plans to acquire the remaining portion. |
| Weight of ownership | Ownership is only one factor. It is weighed against urban renewal, restructuring, and incentives to phase out incompatible and non-conforming uses. For Urban Renewal Authority and Hong Kong Housing Society schemes, where acquisition mechanisms exist, ownership will not be an overriding factor. |
| Prospect of implementation | There should be an indication of the likely prospect before designation. Land status, supporting infrastructure, and preliminary assessments of technical feasibility should be provided. A development agency should indicate the likely development programme. |
| Size | A larger site gives a better opportunity for public facilities, restructuring including road patterns, and optimisation of potential. **There is no hard and fast rule** on whether a site is sizable enough. Each site is considered on its merits, the planning intention, and the special characteristics of the site. |

**SD takeaway:** Do not assume a Government or public-housing site will be zoned “Comprehensive Development Area”; for private land, show single ownership of a major portion, a plan to acquire the rest, infrastructure, and a feasibility programme.

### 3. Boundary, intensity and the Planning Brief (paragraphs 4.1–4.2)

| Item | What is fixed, and where |
|---|---|
| Boundary and intensity | Existing land-use pattern, the latest development requirements, and infrastructural capacity constraints in the area should be taken into account. |
| What to incorporate | Where appropriate: Government, institution or community facilities; open space; road widening; public transport and parking facilities; pedestrian linkages. |
| Notes of the outline zoning plan | Appropriate development mix and intensities would be specified in the Notes if the site is subject to constraints such as traffic and infrastructure capacities and environmental constraints. No ratio is stated in this guideline. |
| Planning Brief | Usually prepared by the Planning Department. Detailed requirements, including traffic and environmental mitigation, Government, institution or community facilities, transport and public transport facilities, and open space, would be specified there. |

**SD takeaway:** Take mix, intensity and mitigation from the Notes and the Planning Brief; this guideline does not set those numbers.

### 4. Monitoring and phased development (paragraphs 5.1–5.4)

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| **First review** | At the **end of the third year** after designation. |
| **Later reviews** | On a **biennial** basis. |
| Priority where there is no approved Master Layout Plan, or no implementation agency | The Board may rezone sites with significant implementation difficulties and slim chances of success; revise planning and development parameters to improve incentives; revise the boundary to match land status or ownership, or subdivide into smaller sites for phased development where justified; and revise and update the Planning Brief. |
| Where a Master Layout Plan is approved | Relevant departments report disagreements on compliance with approval conditions to the Board. A proforma is issued to the developer or agent on a **biennial** basis. |
| Phased development | For a site not under single ownership, allowance for phased development could be considered if the developer demonstrates with evidence that due effort was made to acquire the remaining portion and no agreement can be reached. |

Phasing must demonstrate all four points in paragraph 5.4:

| Test | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Intention | The planning intention of the zone will not be undermined. |
| Comprehensiveness | The comprehensiveness of the proposed development will not be adversely affected by the revised phasing. |
| Self-contained phase | The resultant development should be self-contained in layout and in provision of open space and appropriate Government, institution or community, transport and other infrastructure facilities. |
| **Unacquired lots** | **The development potential of the unacquired lots should not be absorbed in the early phases.** Access to those lots should be retained. Individual owners’ landed interest should not be adversely affected. |

**SD takeaway:** If ownership is split, each early phase must be self-contained and must leave the development potential and access of unacquired lots intact.

### 5. Re-designation after completion (paragraphs 6.1–6.4)

| Consideration | Rule |
|---|---|
| Why rezone | After completion, rezoning to other uses such as “Residential (Group A)” or “Commercial” may give flexibility to modify uses later without a revised Master Layout Plan. |
| Keep comprehensive control | The intention of maintaining comprehensive control should not be undermined. If the layout has to provide a buffer against environmental nuisances, **removal of that buffer will not be desirable**. No buffer width is stated. |
| Mixed development on a common podium | Re-designation of different parts to discrete zones may only be possible if the planning intention of each zone can be clearly reflected. |
| **Intensity after excising land** | If part of the site is excluded and rezoned, for example to “Open Space” or “Government, Institution or Community”, the resultant development intensities **will not be higher** than those permitted under the Notes of the outline zoning plan or in the Building (Planning) Regulations. |
| Local views | Taken into account so that the planning intention is not misunderstood. |
| Sites that stay “Comprehensive Development Area” | Some minor amendments to an approved Master Layout Plan submitted under section 16A(2) can be considered by the Director of Planning, the Deputy Director of Planning and the Assistant Directors of Planning under delegated authority, on a fast-track basis. The relevant Town Planning Board Guidelines apply. |

**SD takeaway:** Do not spend the development potential of land later rezoned to open space or Government, institution or community uses, and do not remove an environmental buffer that the layout depends on.
