# TPB PG-No. 15A Eating Place within Village Type Development Zone
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
June 2003 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: TPB PG-No. 15A is printed as applicable to outline zoning plans which have been amended/updated to incorporate the revised MSN. These guidelines set the planning criteria for section 16 applications for eating place use, such as a restaurant or alfresco dining facility, in the “Village Type Development” (“V”) zone in the rural areas, which demarcates existing recognised villages and land considered suitable for village expansion. A selective range of commercial, community and recreational uses may be permitted on application where they serve the needs of villagers and would not adversely affect the character of villages.

## Regulatory Overview
An eating place in the “V” zone should be compatible with surrounding land uses, should not create nuisance or inconvenience to local residents, and should not have adverse impacts on traffic, drainage, sewage disposal or fire safety, or reduce the land available for village type development. Even where the proposal is acceptable in land-use terms and the other criteria are met, under normal circumstances only a temporary approval for a maximum of three years should be considered.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Planning intention and the temporary permission (paras. 1.1–1.4)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Role of the “V” zone | Concentrate village and related development for a more orderly pattern, economic and efficient use of land, and provision of infrastructure and services |
| Eating place | Should be compatible with surrounding land uses; should not create any nuisance or cause inconvenience to local residents; should not have adverse impacts on traffic, drainage, sewage disposal and fire safety; should not reduce the land area available for village type development |
| Sites next to recreation or tourist spots | Favourable consideration may be given if the considerations above are not compromised |
| Length of permission | Under normal circumstances, only a temporary approval for a **maximum of three years** should be considered, so as to retain planning control and to cater for changing circumstances |

**SD takeaway:** Design the eating place as a temporary use with a maximum term of three years, and keep village land, village character, and traffic, drainage, sewage and fire safety intact.

### 2. When planning permission is required (sections 2 and 3)

“NTEH” (New Territories Exempted House) is defined in the Covering Notes in rural outline zoning plans. These guidelines do not restate that definition.

| Eating place | Permission |
|---|---|
| On the ground floor of a NTEH within the “V” zone | Does **not** require planning permission |
| On other floors of a NTEH | Requires planning permission from the Board |
| On open ground as an extension to a ground floor eating place in a NTEH | Requires planning permission from the Board |
| As a free-standing development within the “V” zone | Requires planning permission from the Board |

**SD takeaway:** A ground-floor eating place inside a NTEH can proceed without this permission; any upper floor, any open-ground extension, and any free-standing eating place must be drawn as a section 16 application.

### 3. Nuisance, traffic, drainage, sewage and fire (paras. 4.1–4.3)

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Residents | The eating place **should not** create any environmental nuisance or cause inconvenience to the residents nearby |
| Preferred location | Preferably at the fringe of a village area, for example an area abutting the main road |
| Amid existing village houses | Sympathetic consideration may only be given if there are no objections from local residents |
| Traffic and pedestrians | **Should not** have any adverse traffic impact on surrounding areas, nor affect any pedestrian circulation in the area |
| Drainage, sewage and fire | Sympathetic consideration may also be given to an application which would not have adverse impacts on drainage, sewage disposal facilities or fire safety aspects |

**SD takeaway:** Put the eating place at the village fringe on the main road, keep it off pedestrian routes and local traffic, and obtain a no-objection position from residents if it sits among existing village houses.

### 4. Village land, road-side sites and visitor parking (paras. 4.4–4.6)

These tests apply to an application on open ground as an extension to a ground-floor eating place in a NTEH, or to a free-standing development.

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Village land | The eating place **should not** adversely affect the land availability for village type development |
| Sites that may receive sympathetic consideration | Sites whose configuration or dimensions are not suitable to be delineated separately for village type development, or which are considered not suitable for village type development. The guideline gives as examples sites **within 20m** of public roads constructed/maintained by the Highways Department, or **15m** of other local public roads. Consideration is on individual merits |
| Village next to recreational uses or tourist attraction spots | Favourable consideration may be given to an eating place which will provide catering facilities to serve visitors and tourists |
| Parking in that visitor case | Adequate car-parking spaces **should** be provided to serve the eating place as required by the Transport Department |
| Parking off the site | If it is impossible to provide car-parking spaces at the application site, the applicant should demonstrate that there are adequate car-parking facilities conveniently located in the vicinity |
| Other departments | All other statutory or non-statutory requirements of relevant Government departments should be met |

**SD takeaway:** For an open-ground extension or a free-standing eating place, use land that is not suitable for village type development, such as land within 20m of a Highways Department public road or 15m of another local public road, and where the village serves visitors provide Transport Department parking on site or prove convenient parking nearby.
