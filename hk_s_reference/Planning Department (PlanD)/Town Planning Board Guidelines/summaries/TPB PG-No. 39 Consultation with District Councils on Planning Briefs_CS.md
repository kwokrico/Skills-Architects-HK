# TPB PG-No. 39 Consultation with District Councils on Planning Briefs
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
May 2006 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: This guideline sets which planning briefs go to the District Council, what a brief is expected to contain, and the path through the District Planning Conference, the District Council and, for some cases, the Town Planning Board. It is a consultation procedure. It does not set plot ratio, site coverage, gross floor area or building height.

## Regulatory Overview
District Councils are to be consulted on all planning briefs, so that the public can contribute to the planning parameters of major development and redevelopment. The brief is a statement of planning intention, guidelines and requirements for a site, and after endorsement it is the basis for a detailed Master Layout Plan where the Board’s path applies.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which projects have a planning brief, and who writes it (s. 2)

A planning brief is usually required for:

| Case | Who prepares the brief |
|---|---|
| A major development on a site zoned “Comprehensive Development Area” on an outline zoning plan | Planning Department |
| A development on a Government site that is large and/or significant enough to need a brief to guide the land-disposal documents | Planning Department |
| A housing project of the Hong Kong Housing Authority or the Hong Kong Housing Society | Housing Department or Hong Kong Housing Society. For the Housing Department, only the essential development requirements need be prepared |
| An Urban Renewal Authority development scheme | Urban Renewal Authority |

**SD takeaway:** Treat a “Comprehensive Development Area” site, a large Government disposal site, a Housing Authority or Housing Society project, and an Urban Renewal Authority scheme as brief-led: the Master Layout Plan follows the brief, not the other way round.

### 2. What the brief contains (s. 3 and Appendix I)

There is no fixed format. The brief must give the development agency the site and surroundings information needed to test feasibility and the scope of options, including constraints, opportunities, and whom to consult. It should leave room for creative and innovative ideas. Illustrative sketches of alternatives may be used to set principles and the quality of design sought. The brief may be in text or in a table, and should be supplemented by photos, figures, tables, plans and models where appropriate.

Appendix I lists items that may usually be covered. None of them is given a number in this guideline.

| Heading in Appendix I | Items listed |
|---|---|
| Background | Planning history of the area; general description of the site and surroundings; location and site area; land ownership, disposal and resumption |
| Constraints and opportunities | Land-use zoning and development restrictions; transport, infrastructural and engineering requirements; urban design principles; conservation and heritage |
| Development parameters | Design population; plot ratio and site coverage; maximum gross floor areas and maximum number of flats; maximum building height |
| Transport | Car parking and loading and unloading; public transport facilities; pedestrian facilities and circulation |
| Environment | Air and noise mitigation |
| Government, institution or community facilities and open space | Government, institution or community requirements and ancillary community facilities; open space and landscaping |
| Other facilities | Public utilities; recreation; commercial, retail and market facilities |

**SD takeaway:** Read the brief for the site’s own plot ratio, coverage, gross floor area, flat number and height; this guideline only requires those parameters to be stated, it does not supply them.

### 3. Who is consulted, and the Urban Renewal Authority timetable (s. 4)

| Step | Requirement as printed |
|---|---|
| Drafting | Consult all Government departments, public bodies and utility companies likely to have an interest. Resolve conflicts at draft stage. An unresolved conflict goes to the approving authority |
| District Planning Conference | Every planning brief is submitted for consideration and endorsement. The conference is chaired by an Assistant Director of Planning |
| District Council | The relevant District Council is consulted on all briefs prepared by the Planning Department, the Housing Department or Hong Kong Housing Society, and the Urban Renewal Authority |
| Town Planning Board, other than Urban Renewal Authority schemes | For a “Comprehensive Development Area” and for major development projects, submission to the Board is also required after the District Planning Conference endorses the brief. The brief then bases the detailed Master Layout Plan |
| Urban Renewal Authority scheme, first step | The draft brief goes to the District Planning Conference for endorsement |
| After the commencement notice | Soon after the commencement notice under section 23 of the Urban Renewal Authority Ordinance, the draft brief and the draft Development Scheme Plan submitted under section 25(5) go to the Board. Both are available for public inspection at the Planning Department’s Planning Enquiry Counters until the Board considers them. The public may comment within a specified period. The length of that period is not printed |
| District Council on an Urban Renewal Authority scheme | The Urban Renewal Authority and the Planning Department consult the relevant District Council as early as possible on the draft brief, the notional development scheme, and the scheme boundary of the draft Development Scheme Plan. The Council or its members may send views direct to the Board **no later than two months** after the date of that consultation |
| Board’s consideration of the Urban Renewal Authority package | The Board considers the draft brief and the draft Development Scheme Plan, with the District Council’s views and any other public comments, **within three months** from the date of submission |

**SD takeaway:** For an Urban Renewal Authority scheme, District Council views must reach the Board within **two months** of the consultation, and the Board considers the draft brief and draft Development Scheme Plan within **three months** of submission.
