# TPB PG-No. 42 Designation of Mixed Use Zone
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
November 2011 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Criteria for designating an “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Mixed Use” (“OU(MU)”) zone, and for uses and section 16 applications inside that zone. Mixed commercial, office and clean industrial development on industrial sites is a separate zoning, “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Business”. Plot ratio and building height numbers in the Notes template are placeholders, not territory-wide caps.

## Regulatory Overview
“OU(MU)” is primarily for mixed non-industrial uses, arranged vertically in one building or horizontally across a site, with physical segregation so that non-residential uses do not cause nuisance to residents. New development, redevelopment and wholesale conversion follow Schedules I and II; an existing building that has not been redeveloped or wholesale-converted since the rezoning follows the tighter Schedule III.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which schedule applies (ss. 3.2–3.4, 4.4–4.5, 6.2–6.4)

| Situation | Schedule |
|---|---|
| Building plans or a lease-modification application for new development, redevelopment, or wholesale conversion, received after the site is rezoned to “OU(MU)” | Schedules I and II. After completion, any further partial conversion or change of use also follows Schedules I and II |
| “Conversion” in these schedules | Wholesale conversion of an existing building. It is treated as equivalent to a new development |
| Non-residential building, or the non-residential portion of a composite building, on development, redevelopment or conversion | Schedule I. A wide range of uses similar to those always permitted in the “Commercial” zone |
| Residential building, or the residential portion of a composite building, on development, redevelopment or conversion | Schedule II. Residential use, certain Government uses, and small-scale utility installations are always permitted |
| Horizontal mix of residential and non-residential buildings, with no composite building | Schedules I and II applied to the respective buildings. Illustrative drawings are at Appendix III |
| Composite building inside a vertically mixed development | Schedule I on the non-residential portion and Schedule II on the residential portion, plus the design requirements in paragraphs 7.2 to 7.7. Illustrative drawings are at Appendix II |
| Existing building with no redevelopment or wholesale conversion after the rezoning | Schedule III. The previous mixed-use pattern, especially from a “Commercial/Residential” zone, is tolerated as far as possible, but the always-permitted list is tighter than “Commercial/Residential”. ‘Place of Entertainment’ and ‘Eating Place’, which may generate more significant interface problems, are in Column 2 |

**SD takeaway:** Decide before massing whether the scheme is a new or wholesale-converted building under Schedules I and II, or an existing building still on Schedule III, because the always-permitted uses and the segregation rule change with that choice.

### 2. Building definitions that fix the schedule (s. 4)

| Term | What it includes |
|---|---|
| Residential building | Constructed or intended for habitation. It may include non-domestic accommodation ancillary to the main use, such as car park, club house and plant rooms |
| Non-residential building | Constructed or intended for uses other than habitation. It may include domestic accommodation ancillary to the non-residential uses, such as caretaker’s quarters. **Hotel and hotel-like service apartment are non-residential buildings in planning terms**, although they are for habitation |
| Composite building | Partly residential and partly non-residential, including commercial, educational, cultural and entertainment, and **excluding industrial uses**. The residential portion includes habitation and ancillary, directly related podium, sky or roof gardens, private recreational areas and plant rooms. The non-residential portion is everything else, including a commercial podium or commercial floors, public recreational facilities as opposed to a private clubhouse for residents, and areas for cultural, educational or community uses, with their ancillary accommodation |
| Existing building | A building, including a structure, which is physically existing and complies with any relevant legislation and the conditions of the Government lease |

**SD takeaway:** Count a hotel or hotel-like service apartment on the non-residential schedule, and keep private clubhouse, sky garden and residential plant rooms on the residential side of a composite building.

### 3. Criteria for designating the zone (ss. 5.2–5.8)

These criteria assess whether a site should be zoned “OU(MU)”. They are general reference. Land-use reviews, including of existing “Commercial/Residential” zones, decide specific sites. The long-term intention is to phase out “Commercial/Residential” zoning. Those sites are to be rezoned to “OU(MU)”, “Commercial” or “Residential”, depending on location and site characteristics.

| Criterion | What must be true |
|---|---|
| Compatibility (para. 5.2) | Mixed-use development compatible with existing and planned uses. More suitable where a mixture of commercial, office, residential and other uses already exists. Existing uses, the surrounding area and the ownership pattern are considered. Rezoning should preferably be on a **street-block** basis. Street blocks with greater possibility of site amalgamation have greater potential |
| Greenfield nodes (para. 5.3) | Certain greenfield sites planned for, or near, major activity nodes such as major railway interchanges may also be suitable |
| Central business district (para. 5.4) | Rezoning of the **core** central business district is generally not appropriate, because that land should be planned primarily for office and commercial use. Areas at the **fringe** of the central business district may be suitable |
| Access (para. 5.5) | Good accessibility, along major transportation routes, and preferably served by mass transit railway. Local and district transport networks should have sufficient capacity for the traffic generated |
| Utilities (para. 5.6) | Preferably no major constraint on fresh water, drainage, sewerage and public utilities, or the infrastructure can be upgraded. Adequacy is tested on the pace of development and the types of mixed use envisaged, on a **worst-case scenario** under normal circumstances |
| Community facilities (para. 5.7) | Residential development is permitted as of right in a residential building or in the residential portion. The zone should be in an area with adequate existing and planned community facilities for the residents. The development pattern is to be monitored |

**SD takeaway:** A street block on a major route, preferably at a railway, with water, drainage and sewerage capacity for the worst-case mix, is the designation case; the core central business district is generally left as office and commercial land.

**SD takeaway:** In an unconverted existing building, put eating places and places of entertainment on the lowest three floors, counting basements and ignoring pure car-park, loading and plant floors, or inside an existing purpose-designed non-residential portion; elsewhere those uses need permission.

### 7. Segregation, same-floor mix, and height of sensitive uses (ss. 6.4, 7.2–7.4)

| Rule | Text |
|---|---|
| Physical segregation | On development, redevelopment or conversion to mixed use, the residential and non-residential portions **shall** be physically segregated. The designs named are separate entrances, lift lobbies, staircases, and other appropriate means. Paragraph 8.3 adds separate access |
| Same floor | **No intermixing** of residential and non-residential uses on the same floor, unless permitted under section 16. The Notes repeat that provision of residential and non-residential uses on the same floor will not be permitted |
| Relaxation | Under exceptional circumstances, the Board may relax physical segregation and the same-floor rule on a section 16 application |
| Nuisance | Mitigation through stringent building design and environmental legislation, so that non-residential uses minimise nuisance to residents in the same building |
| Schools | Should not be located at a height **exceeding 24 metres above ground** |
| Residential care home for the elderly, and child care centre for children aged 2 to 3 years | Should not be located at a height **more than 12 metres above ground** |
| Place of public entertainment | Domestic accommodation should not be provided within, or immediately adjoining, a place of public entertainment |
| Schools over shops | Schools should not be located over shops |
| Residential-care footnote | The social-welfare example in paragraph 7.4 covers boys’ and girls’ homes, residential care home for the elderly, residential home for people with disabilities, drug treatment and rehabilitation centre, half-way houses, long-stay care home, and similar facilities |

**SD takeaway:** Give the dwellings their own entrance, lifts and stairs, keep every residential floor free of non-residential uses, and place schools at or below 24 metres above ground and elderly homes and child care centres for ages 2 to 3 at or below 12 metres above ground.

### 8. Parking, open space and environmental mix (ss. 7.5–7.7)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Parking and loading | Provide to the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines for the uses proposed. Design in flexibility for a later change of use. If the provision deviates from those standards, seek Transport Department advice at project-planning stage |
| Open space and community facilities | Have regard to projected demand from the development and to existing and planned provision in the area or the wider district. Planning Department advice may be sought. **Local open space within the development should be provided in accordance with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines** |
| Uses in combination | Consider environmental factors so as to minimise nuisance between uses |
| Outline zoning plan | Plot ratio and building height, if any, are those on the relevant outline zoning plan for that “OU(MU)” zone (para. 7.1). This guideline does not set a numeric plot ratio or building height |

**SD takeaway:** Size car parking and loading for a later change of use, and provide local open space to the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines inside the development.

### 9. Plot-ratio remarks template, and section 16 tests (Appendix I Remarks; s. 8)

The Remarks are a Notes template. Bracketed figures are printed as “[ xxxxx ]”. They are not numeric standards in this guideline.

| Remark | When it is added | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Where appropriate, with remark (c) | No new development, or addition, alteration and/or modification to, or redevelopment of, an existing building shall result in a total development and/or redevelopment in excess of a maximum domestic plot ratio/gross floor area of [ xxxxx ] and a total plot ratio/gross floor area of [ xxxxx ], and a maximum building height of [ xxxxx ], **or** the plot ratio/gross floor area and height of the existing building, **whichever is the greater** |
| (b) | **Only when plot ratio restrictions are imposed** | For a building partly domestic and partly non-domestic, the plot ratio for the domestic part shall not exceed the product of (i) the difference between the maximum permitted non-domestic plot ratio and the actual non-domestic plot ratio proposed and (ii) the maximum permitted domestic plot ratio divided by the maximum permitted non-domestic plot ratio |
| (c) | Where appropriate | Minor relaxation of the plot ratio/gross floor area and building height restriction in (a) may be considered under section 16 |
| (d) | Stated with the segregation rule | Repeats physical segregation, the ban on same-floor mixing, and exceptional relaxation under section 16 |

Section 16 tests for a development in the zone:

| Paragraph | The applicant must show |
|---|---|
| 8.1 | The development is in line with the planning intention; not incompatible with surrounding uses or other uses in the same building; no adverse effect on the character and environment of the neighbourhood; no overstrain of existing and planned transport, drainage, sewerage and water supply; and, through proper assessment, no environmental pollution or nuisance from the mixed-use development |
| 8.2 | A proposal that brings a variety of uses and enhances character, vitality and vibrancy will be given favourable consideration |
| 8.3 | For new development, redevelopment or conversion: physical segregation is provided, including separate access, entrance, lift lobbies and staircases, so that uses in one portion do not create interface problems with the other |
| 8.4 | For an application in an existing mixed-use building before redevelopment or conversion: practical and implementable measures to minimise nuisance or interface problems. Each case is considered on its merits, with departmental comments |
| 8.5 | Car parking and loading/unloading as in paragraph 7.5 |
| 8.6 | All other statutory and non-statutory requirements, including fire safety and building safety, must be met |

**SD takeaway:** Take plot ratio and height from the outline zoning plan for that zone, apply the domestic composite formula only where that plan imposes plot ratio, and on any section 16 scheme draw the separate residential and non-residential cores before the use mix.

### 10. Appendix drawings

Appendix II is titled “Vertical Mixed-use Development”. The text layer of that page does not contain a readable floor diagram. Appendix III is a not-to-scale illustration titled “Horizontal Mixed-use Development”. It labels roads, a non-building area, landscaped gardens, a swimming pool, a tennis court, a playground, a proposed public light bus terminus, proposed footbridges, street-tree planting, a podium roof over a commercial complex and car park, a podium roof over a club house, commercial complex and car park, a proposed hotel, office accommodation, a cultural and art centre, a service apartment, and two towers. The storey counts printed on that illustration are labels on the drawing. The guideline does not state them as a maximum number of storeys.

**SD takeaway:** Use Appendix III only as a picture of horizontal separation of residential and non-residential buildings around shared open space and a public-light-bus terminus, and take the storey count from the outline zoning plan.
