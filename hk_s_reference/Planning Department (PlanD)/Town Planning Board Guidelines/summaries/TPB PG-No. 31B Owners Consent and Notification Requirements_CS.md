# TPB PG-No. 31B Owners Consent and Notification Requirements
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2023 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Procedure guideline on the owner’s consent, owner’s notification and reasonable-steps requirements for a section 12A amendment-of-plan application and a section 16 planning application. It does not itself approve a use, and it does not set plot ratio or building height. The sample notices do require those parameters to be stated where a section 16 application is made.

## Regulatory Overview
If the applicant is not the current land owner or the sole current land owner, the applicant must obtain each owner’s written consent or notify each owner in writing, or demonstrate that reasonable steps to do so were taken before the application, in the form and particulars the Board requires. Failure to satisfy those requirements allows the Board to refuse to process the application.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Who must be covered, and who may make a section 12A application (paras 1.1–1.3, 2.1–2.2)

“Current land owner” means any person whose name is registered in the Land Registry as an owner of the land to which the application relates, as at the start of the period the Board has specified by Gazette notice. The Gazette Notice of 15 April 2005 takes that period as **6 weeks** before the application is made. A more updated Land Registry record is also accepted. “Land” includes premises constructed on it. If the site boundary crosses part of a lot or premises, the owner of that lot or premises is included. Every person registered as an owner is a current land owner. For these requirements, the definition does not include the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, even where Government land is inside the site.

A section 12A applicant must also be an eligible person under section 12A(25), with documentary proof. The eligible persons printed in this guideline are: a person registered as the sole owner or one of the owners of any non-Government land within the site; a person with written consent from at least one such owner; a person with written consent from the Director of Lands in relation to any Government land within the site; a public officer; or a public body as defined by section 2 of the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201).

The applicant signs a declaration in the form. The Secretariat carries out a random check and may require a copy of the Land Registry record or a statutory declaration.

**SD takeaway:** Pull the Land Registry owners no earlier than **6 weeks** before lodgement, and include every registered co-owner of every lot or premises the boundary cuts.

### 2. Owner’s consent and owner’s notification (paras 4, 5.1–5.2; Annexes 1 and 2)

| Route | What must be submitted |
|---|---|
| Consent | The number of consents, and copies of the completed statement of consent. An individual owner’s statement states the Hong Kong Identity Card or passport number. A corporate owner’s statement bears the company seal and is accompanied by a resolution of the board of directors |
| Notification | A written notice sent by registered mail or local recorded delivery (for example courier) to the name in the Land Registry records. The address is the postal address on those records, or the company’s office address in the Companies Registry, or the relevant postal address of the land or premises. A full set of the mail records goes in with the application |
| Lot address with no postal delivery | Sending to a non-postal address, such as a lot address, is not normally accepted unless successful delivery is proved by advice of receipt |

The sample statement and the sample notice (Annexes 1 and 2) ask for the nature of the proposal. For a section 16 application that includes the proposed use or development and the major development parameters, such as site area and the proposed plot ratio, gross floor area, site coverage and building height. For a section 12A application it includes the subject of the amendment.

**SD takeaway:** Freeze site area, plot ratio, gross floor area, site coverage and building height in the consent or notice, because the consent is to that particular application.

### 3. Reasonable steps, the 1 September 2023 notice change, and site-area thresholds (paras 6.2–6.5; Annex 4)

Reasonable steps are shown on the application form, with a full set of the documents. The steps in paragraph 6.2 are either (a) a request for consent posted to every current land owner, with registered or recorded-delivery evidence, or (b) the publication steps below. Other steps may be accepted case by case. The examples for using reasonable steps, which are not prerequisites, are an owner who cannot be contacted because the postal address is missing or incomplete, or a large number of owners (for example above 50).

| Step | Hard requirement printed in this guideline |
|---|---|
| Newspaper | Once in two Chinese local newspapers and one English local newspaper, in the newspapers the Board specifies. Size **not smaller than 30 square centimetres (five square inches)** |
| Site notice posted before 1 September 2023 | At least **A4**, and legible from a public place |
| Site notice posted on or after 1 September 2023 | Annex 4, summarised below |
| Alternative to a site notice under paragraph 6.2(b)(ii) | A notice sent by registered or recorded delivery to the Owners’ Corporation, Owners’ Committee or management office of the building, or where appropriate to the relevant Rural Committee |

Three application types are exempt from the newspaper notice when they use reasonable steps. They must still undertake both of the paragraph 6.2(b)(ii) steps (site notice and notice to the corporation, committee, management office or Rural Committee): change of use of premises within existing buildings; temporary uses with an application site area **less than 1 hectare**; and New Territories Exempted House or Small House developments.

Annex 4 applies to notices posted on or after 1 September 2023. The notice is in a prominent position on or near the site or premises and legible from a public place. It is posted for **at least two weeks** before the application is submitted. If it is obliterated, defaced, destroyed or removed during those two weeks, it is replaced. Possible locations for a site are a major ingress or egress, a major road access, near a bus terminus or stop, a nearby notice board, and main gathering points. Premises locations are the notice board, lift lobby and main entrance. Another conspicuous location may be used with justifications. A large area, many buildings, or several major entrances may require additional notices.

| Application site or premises | Minimum number of notices | Minimum size printed in Annex 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Site area under 5 ha | **3** | **A3: 11.7 inches × 16.5 inches** (297 mm × 420 mm). This is the size printed in the application-site block, beside the under-5 ha row |
| Site area 5–10 ha | **5** | The 5–10 ha row prints the number of notices and does not print a second size |
| Site area over 10 ha | **10** | The over-10 ha row prints the number of notices and does not print a second size |
| Application premises | **1** | **A4: 8.3 inches × 11.7 inches** (210 mm × 297 mm) |

The notice states the location, a plan of the site boundary or premises, whether the application is under section 12A or section 16, the nature of the proposal (for section 16, the use or development and major parameters such as site area, plot ratio, gross floor area, site coverage and building height), the start and end dates of posting, and the applicant’s name.

With the application, submit the notice, a plan of the posting locations, and three sets of photos: the first day, a day between the **sixth and the eighth day**, and the **fourteenth day**. Each photo shows the context and includes a newspaper clipping of that day as proof of the date.

**SD takeaway:** For a notice posted on or after 1 September 2023, post it for **14 days** before lodgement and keep photos on day 1, between day 6 and day 8, and on day 14.

### 4. How long a consent or notification remains usable (para 7)

The Ordinance requires compliance within a reasonable period before the application. What is reasonable depends on the case and the applicant’s justification. In general, **one year** before the application is taken as the reasonable validity period, provided that owner is still the current land owner. Beyond that period the applicant may be required to obtain consent, notify the current land owner, or satisfy reasonable steps again.

A false declaration or statement allows the Board to withdraw its decision under section 40(2)(c) of the Interpretation and General Clauses Ordinance (Cap. 1). Knowingly or wilfully making a false declaration or statement is liable to prosecution under the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200), the Oaths and Declarations Ordinance (Cap. 11), and other relevant Ordinances.

**SD takeaway:** A consent or notification older than **one year**, or given by someone who is no longer the registered owner, has to be done again before lodgement.
