# TPB PG-No. 38 Designation of Rural Use Zone
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
November 2005 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: This guideline sets the tests for zoning land as “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Rural Use” (“OU(RU)”) and for a section 16 application inside that zone. It is not a Column 1 and Column 2 use schedule: those lists are fixed in the Notes of the plan, with local circumstances read against the Master Schedule of Notes.

## Regulatory Overview
The zone is primarily to preserve rural character, and to allow selected rural and passive recreational uses, with Column 2 uses only where the Board is satisfied on ecological, environmental and infrastructural sustainability. Development in the zone is restricted to a maximum plot ratio of 0.4 and a maximum building height of 3 storeys (or 9 m where appropriate) including car park, and a minor relaxation of those restrictions may be considered through the planning permission system on the merits of that proposal.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Planning intention and the bulk cap (ss. 1, 2 and 4.3)

The zone follows the Planning Department’s 2001 review of rural land uses in the northern New Territories. It is meant to preserve rural landscape and character and to provide land for small-scale rural and passive recreational uses and breathing space. Passive recreational examples printed in the introduction are parks, gardens, sitting-out areas, waterfront promenades, paved areas for informal games, children’s playgrounds, and jogging and fitness circuits. Rural character, as described here, includes fung shui woods, trees and green areas, active or fallow agricultural land, traditional and existing village houses and heritage features, passive recreation and open areas, low-rise low-density residential development, associated government, institution or community uses, and supporting infrastructure. Designation and applications are to be read with the conservation principles in Chapter 10 of the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines.

Tolerated or unauthorized temporary factories, industrial uses, workshops, storage and vehicle parks are not a character the zone is intended to keep. The intention is to phase those uses out by incentives and enforcement.

| Control | Limit as printed |
|---|---|
| Uses that contribute to rural character, and passive recreation compatible with the rural environment | Column 1, permitted as of right. The actual list depends on local circumstances and the Master Schedule of Notes |
| Selected active recreation, and low-rise low-density residential development, and other Column 2 uses | Planning permission required. Approval only with, among other considerations, sustainability in ecological, environmental and infrastructural terms |
| Plot ratio | **Maximum 0.4** |
| Building height | **Maximum 3 storeys (or 9 m where appropriate), including car park** |
| Minor relaxation of plot ratio or height | May be considered by the Board through the planning permission system, each case on its merits |

No site-area threshold is printed.

**SD takeaway:** Lock the scheme at a plot ratio of **0.4** and **3 storeys (or 9 m where appropriate) including car park** unless a section 16 case is made for a minor relaxation.

### 2. Where “OU(RU)” may and may not be designated (s. 3)

| Test | Designation consequence as printed |
|---|---|
| Local character, and whether later development would fit existing and planned surroundings | Must be considered. Rezoning should help preserve the general rural character and/or stay compatible with surroundings |
| Scattered temporary structures, abandoned pigsties, or non-conforming existing uses such as container vehicle parks, open storage yards, workshops and warehouses | May be considered if rezoning would help phase them out or upgrade the environment |
| Unauthorized development or degradation done in the hope of a rezoning | Not given sympathetic consideration, and subject to enforcement, including by the Planning Authority |
| Good quality or active agricultural land | Should be retained for agriculture. Consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department. Low-quality or abandoned farmland is not automatically favoured |
| Ecological or conservation importance, high landscape value, potential conservation areas, or land that adjoins or contributes to such assets | **Will not be considered** for “OU(RU)”. High landscape value normally includes “Site of Special Scientific Interest”, “Country Park”, “Conservation Area”, “Green Belt” and “Coastal Protection Area”, Special Areas, and scenic areas. Consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, the Environmental Protection Department, and the Landscape Unit of the Planning Department. Also refer to Chapter 10 of the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines, Unique Areas or Significant Areas in Figure 4 of the 1998 Territorial Development Strategy Review executive report, and the 1995 Landscape Strategy Components |
| Land adjoining or near a Conservation Area, Site of Special Scientific Interest, Green Belt, Country Park, Special Area, active farmland, stream, wetland, marsh, woodland, important habitat, area of beauty, walking trail or tourist route | **Will not be considered** if the rezoning would adversely affect those areas. Future development impact on the surroundings is part of the test |
| Significant archaeological, historical, cultural or architectural interest | **Will not be considered**. Consult the Antiquities and Monuments Office. Subject to that advice, land that provides the setting for heritage may also be unsuitable |
| Access and infrastructure | Prefer good access, close to major roads, where drainage, water and sewage connections are relatively easier. Local and district roads, and existing and planned drainage, sewerage and water supply, should be able to take the demand from possible development after rezoning |

An “existing use” in this guideline is a use in existence immediately before the first publication of the interim or draft development permission area plan for the area, and continued since then, and tolerated under the Town Planning Ordinance even if it does not conform to the current plan.

**SD takeaway:** Do not propose “OU(RU)” on land of ecological, landscape or heritage value, or next to a Conservation Area, Site of Special Scientific Interest, Green Belt, Country Park or similar asset, if the rezoning would harm it.

### 3. Section 16 tests for location, form, ecology, heritage and environment (ss. 4.1–4.10)

A Column 2 application must show that the proposal follows the planning intention, does not harm the rural environment, the conservation of the rural landscape or the rural character of the area and its surroundings, and does not overstrain existing and planned transport, drainage, sewerage and water supply. Each case is judged on sustainability in ecological, environmental and infrastructural terms.

| Topic | Design requirement as printed |
|---|---|
| Intensity | **Plot ratio 0.4** and **3 storeys (or 9 m where appropriate) including car park**, with minor relaxation only through planning permission |
| Built form | Scale, intensity and form compatible with nearby uses, the setting, and the rural landscape, and not harmful to the natural landscape |
| Residential use, and uses that generate substantial traffic or rely on nearby government, institution or community facilities | Generally close to the new towns or major roads |
| Golf course, golf driving range, and holiday camp | May need a more secluded site, away from main populated areas, but not in natural or rural landscape or other conservation land that would be affected by noise, pesticide or light pollution. Location must match infrastructure, access, operation and the expected number of visitors |
| Site works and landscape | No extensive site formation and no extensive clearance of existing natural vegetation. No adverse landscape or visual impact. Visual and landscape impact assessment and/or tree and ecological surveys may be required. Landscape proposals may have to include a tree and vegetation preservation scheme and screening. Native species as far as practicable. The Planning Department may be consulted on landscape and overall design |
| Ecology | If the site encroaches on, or is near, land of ecological value, consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department first. A baseline review, an up-to-date survey, an impact assessment and mitigation may be required |
| Cultural heritage | If the site encroaches on, or is near, possible archaeological or historical interest, consult the Antiquities and Monuments Office. A survey and mitigation may be required, including design requirements for the site and new buildings, in addition to the landscape requirements |
| Environment | No unacceptable environmental impact, and the development should not be open to pollution unless mitigation is adequate. Golf course, golf driving range, holiday camp, place of recreation, sports or culture, private club, and residential development may need an environmental assessment for noise, water and light pollution. Some Column 2 uses might be a Designated Project under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance and may need an Environmental Permit. Consult the Environmental Protection Department |

**SD takeaway:** Keep site formation and vegetation clearance limited, and treat a golf use, holiday camp or low-density house as a permission case that may need landscape, ecological and environmental assessments before the bulk is fixed.

### 4. Transport, drainage, sewerage, water, community facilities and planning gain (ss. 4.11–4.16)

| Topic | What the application must show |
|---|---|
| Traffic | A traffic impact assessment may be required, including practical junction improvements. For recreation or tourism, assess vehicles and pedestrians on the local road network on weekends and public holidays. Parking and loading and unloading inside the development must meet peak-hour demand. Consult the Transport Department. No parking ratio is printed in this guideline |
| Drainage | No adverse impact upstream, adjacent or downstream. In a flood-prone area, or where the scheme would unacceptably raise flood risk upstream, adjacent or downstream, include a drainage impact assessment and flood mitigation where appropriate. Do not build in close proximity to existing streamcourses. The guideline does not print a setback distance. Consult the Drainage Services Department |
| Sewerage | Connect waste water to public sewers leading to Government treatment plants. Where there is no public foul sewer, provide treatment to an acceptable standard for direct discharge, or build a new sewer to Government facilities as agreed by the Environmental Protection Department, with the Drainage Services Department consulted on the connection point. In the rural Deep Bay catchment, demonstrate **no net increase of pollution load** to the sensitive Deep Bay area. A septic tank and soakaway may be allowed if it is not in a water gathering ground and ground conditions are suitable: low groundwater, ample distance from surface or sensitive waters and from a slope, and adequate soil percolation. No numeric separation distance is printed |
| Water | Consult the Water Supplies Department on whether supply is close by. The applicant may have to extend private mains to the nearest Government main and resolve land matters for laying in private lots. Consult the Water Supplies Department again if the site is close to or inside a water gathering ground |
| Government, institution or community facilities and open space | Demand from the proposal should generally be met by existing or planned provision in the district. The applicant may have to address a shortfall so that district facilities are not overstrained. Consult the Planning Department. No provision rate is printed |
| Planning gain | Show that the local community is not adversely affected and, if applicable, that the scheme brings planning gain |

**SD takeaway:** In the Deep Bay catchment, the sewerage case must show no net increase of pollution load, and the layout should stay off existing streamcourses even though this guideline prints no metre setback.
