# TPB PG-No. 26A Lapsing of Planning Permission
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
May 2006 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: When a planning permission lapses because a permitted use is realized or a permitted development is completed, and when a permitted use is treated as abandoned. The lapsing provision does not apply to any planning permission for temporary use. Realization of a temporary use will not affect the planning permission of a permanent use given for the same site.

## Regulatory Overview
All planning permissions granted by the Board are for a particular use or development in respect of a specific site, and where a site has several permissions the owner or developer could choose to implement any one of them prior to completion of the project. Once a permitted use is realized or a permitted development is completed, all planning permissions granted in respect of the site will lapse, and any future change of use or redevelopment will need to conform to the extant statutory plan, regardless of any previous planning permission.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Realization, completion and a later rezoning (paras 2.1 and 3.1–3.3)

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| Permitted use realized, or permitted development completed | All planning permissions granted in respect of the site **will lapse** |
| Large-scale development to be completed by phases | Considered as completed **only when all the phases** of the development are completed |
| A development being carried out in accordance with a planning permission, while the land-use zoning restriction of the site changes | That development **will not be affected** by the change of zoning |
| Planning permission for temporary use | The lapsing provision **does not apply**. Realization of the temporary use will not affect a permanent permission for the same site |

**SD takeaway:** On a phased scheme, the development is completed only when every phase is completed, and after that completion any future change of use or redevelopment will need to conform to the extant statutory plan.

### 2. The permission runs with the premises; abandonment (paras 3.4–3.7)

A planning permission is granted in respect of the land, building or premises. Any change of owner, tenant or operator would not affect the permission granted by the Board. The permission **shall remain valid** until and unless it has lapsed or has been abandoned.

In considering whether there is an abandonment of use, the Board will take into account the facts and circumstances of each case. In general, the following criteria will be adopted:

| Criterion | What the Board looks at |
|---|---|
| Physical condition | Whether the land, building or premises have fallen into such a state that it cannot be put back into the former permitted use |
| Period of cessation | Whether the period of cessation of use is unreasonably long |
| Change of use | Whether there has been a change of use during the period of cessation, and whether that change constitutes a change in the character of the use |
| Intention | Whether there is evidence substantiating the owner’s, tenant’s or operator’s intention to continue or abandon the use |

The onus of proof lies with the concerned owner, tenant or operator, who will be required to provide sufficient evidence to demonstrate that the use has not been abandoned, taking into account those criteria. Where it can be established that a permitted use has been abandoned, any proposed resumption of the abandoned use will be regarded as a change of use, and **has to comply** with the extant statutory plan.

**SD takeaway:** A change of owner, tenant or operator leaves the permission in force; if abandonment is established, resuming the use is a change of use and has to comply with the extant statutory plan.
