# TPB PG-No. 18B Submission of Master Layout Plan
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
December 2023 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Governs the content, format and Land Registry deposition of a Master Layout Plan required under section 4A(2) of the Town Planning Ordinance. It is not the Class A and Class B amendment list in Town Planning Board Guidelines No. 36C, and detailed building plans are not accepted as the Master Layout Plan.

## Regulatory Overview
For permission in a “Comprehensive Development Area”, or in “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Comprehensive Development/Redevelopment Area”, the Board may require the application to be a Master Layout Plan with supporting information, and an approved plan shall be deposited in the Land Registry under section 4A(3). The submission should show the overall development concept with clarity and simplicity; excessive detail produces a rigid plan and complicates later deposition of revisions.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When a Master Layout Plan is required (paragraphs 1.1–1.3 and 2.1–2.5)

| Situation | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Permission in a “Comprehensive Development Area”, or “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Comprehensive Development/Redevelopment Area” | The Board may require the application to be in the form of a Master Layout Plan, supported by other relevant information. |
| Approved plan | **Shall be deposited** in the Land Registry for public inspection under section 4A(3). |
| Standard requirements | Applied with flexibility to individual site circumstances. |
| Change of use in an existing building within a “Comprehensive Development Area”, before comprehensive development is completed | Submission of a Master Layout Plan and the supporting information in paragraphs 3 to 5 **is not required**, whether or not building works are undertaken, unless relevant Government departments consider it necessary. |
| Minor amendments to an approved plan, as set out in Town Planning Board Guidelines No. 36C | Detailed assessments are **generally not required**, unless relevant Government departments consider them necessary. |
| Temporary uses within a “Comprehensive Development Area” | The Master Layout Plan requirement **is not applicable**. |

**SD takeaway:** Prepare a full Master Layout Plan for the comprehensive scheme; do not prepare one for a temporary use, or for a change of use in an existing building before the comprehensive development is completed, unless a department requires it.

### 2. What the plan and the development schedule should show (paragraph 3.1)

Paragraph 3.1 says the following information should be included as appropriate. Once approved, the information in paragraph 3.1(a) and paragraph 3.1(b) shall be deposited (paragraph 6).

| Plan content (paragraph 3.1(a)) | What to show |
|---|---|
| Location and layout | Location of the site and the general layout of the whole development. |
| Blocks | Disposition of building blocks and number of blocks. |
| Uses | Locations of main land uses by block, and disposition of broad uses by floors, such as commercial, residential and “Government, Institution or Community”. |
| Height | Building heights and number of storeys, indicating podium levels if any; maximum height of any major roof-top architectural feature; maximum building height if the Notes of the outline zoning plan stipulate one. |
| Vehicles | Vehicular ingress and egress, and parking and loading/unloading facilities. |
| Internal roads | Layout, alignments and widths, including emergency vehicular access. |
| Facilities | “Government, Institution or Community” facilities, indoor recreational facilities, public transport facilities, and ancillary major utility installations such as a refuse collection point, a sewage treatment and disposal facility, and a liquefied petroleum gas compound. |
| Non-building areas | Allocation of non-building areas, including set-backs and utility reserves. No set-back dimension is stated in this guideline. |
| Phasing | Phasing of development. |
| Open space | Locations and types: public or private, and active or passive. |
| Pedestrians | Pedestrian circulation, including subway, footbridges and footbridge connections. |
| Landscape | A master landscape plan of the hard and soft landscape concept and tree preservation and replanting, including landscaping of non-building areas and set-backs where appropriate. |
| Other | Any other information the Board may require. |

| Development schedule (paragraph 3.1(b)) | Parameters |
|---|---|
| Site | Site area in square metres. |
| Residential | Gross floor area proposed, in square metres; number of flats; average flat size; number of residential blocks or houses; building heights in storeys and/or metres. |
| Commercial | Gross floor area by broad commercial use, for instance office, hotel and retail. |
| Government, institution or community, indoor recreation and open space | Gross floor area and types of facilities. Government, institution or community provision may be given in Net Operational Floor Area as required by departments. Open space in square metres. |
| Parking and loading/unloading | Number of spaces for residential, commercial and/or other uses, **in comparison with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines**. |
| Infrastructure | Transport, public transport and other infrastructure facilities. |
| Programme | Proposed phasing, relative timing of phases, and the Government, institution or community, open space and other public facilities in each phase. |
| Population | Design population and schedule of population intake. |

**SD takeaway:** The deposited set is the general layout plus this development schedule, including phasing of public facilities and a parking comparison with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines.

### 3. Split ownership, format, and what is not the Master Layout Plan (paragraphs 3.2–3.6)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Not under single consolidated ownership | The applicant should be required to demonstrate that phasing has taken due consideration of the development potential of lots not under his ownership. Gross floor area and flat-number distribution, and provision of Government, institution or community, open space and other public facilities in each phase, should be clearly indicated. |
| Sample | A sample submission is referred to at Appendix I. The sample itself is not reproduced in the extract. |
| **Detailed building plans** | **Will not be accepted.** The Board is concerned with the overall development concept, not the details depicted in building plans. Emphasis is on clarity and simplicity. |
| Sheet size | Subject to the scale of the development, the plan should be compiled, as far as practicable, in A3 size. |
| Indicative drawings | Sections, perspectives and detailed floor plans should be **distinguished from** the Master Layout Plan submission, unless the Board considers them necessary to form part of it. |

**SD takeaway:** Submit a simple A3 concept plan, keep sections and floor plans clearly separate unless the Board pulls them in, and do not lodge detailed building plans as the Master Layout Plan.

### 4. What is deposited and what need not be deposited (paragraphs 4.1–4.2, 5 and 6)

| Document | Status |
|---|---|
| Paragraph 3.1(a) plans and paragraph 3.1(b) schedule, once approved | **Shall be signed by the Chairman of the Board and deposited** in the Land Registry. |
| Later revised plans that incorporate approval conditions, or amendments approved by the Board | Should also be deposited as soon as practicable. The applicant is asked to indicate whether the latest approved plan should replace the previous version or versions. |
| On completion | The final version of an approved plan should be deposited for public inspection. |
| Explanatory statement | Supports the plan. It explains land tenure, relevant lease conditions, existing site conditions, the character of the site relative to surrounding areas, layout principles, design population, Government, institution or community, recreation and open space facilities including who bears construction cost and operation or management, and vehicular and pedestrian systems including widths and levels of roads and footbridges and whether they would be handed back to the Government on completion. It **does not form part of the Master Layout Plan**. It may be deposited in the Land Registry for public information. |
| Supplementary studies | Where appropriate: traffic impact assessment, environmental assessment, hazard assessment, visual impact assessment, and drainage or sewage impact studies. **These studies do not form part of the Master Layout Plan and need not be deposited in the Land Registry.** |

**SD takeaway:** Deposit only the approved layout and development schedule, signed by the Chairman; keep the explanatory statement and the impact studies out of the deposited plan.
