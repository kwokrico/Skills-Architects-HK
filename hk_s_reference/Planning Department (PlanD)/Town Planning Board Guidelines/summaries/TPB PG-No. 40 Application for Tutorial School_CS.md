# TPB PG-No. 40 Application for Tutorial School
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
February 2008 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: This guideline sets when a tutorial school needs planning permission under section 16, and the planning tests the Board applies, mainly nuisance and access inside a residential building. It is not a school-design code: fire, means of escape, barrier-free access and registration sit under other ordinances, and a planning approval does not satisfy them.

## Regulatory Overview
In commercial buildings and in the commercial portion of a composite commercial and residential building, a tutorial school is usually always permitted as “school” under the Notes, so planning permission is usually not required. Permission is the control used for a tutorial school in a pure residential building or in the domestic portion of a composite building, in the zones listed in section 1.2, so that the use does not cause nuisance or conflict with other uses in the building or nearby.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Where permission is required (ss. 1 and 2)

The Board’s concern is whether the premises are suitable for the use. Each application is considered on its merits and may be approved with or without conditions. The zones named in section 1.2 are “Residential (Group A)”, “Residential (Group B)”, “Residential (Group C)”, “Residential (Group D)”, “Residential (Group E)”, “Comprehensive Development Area”, “Commercial/Residential”, “Village Type Development”, “Other Specified Uses (Mixed Use)” and “Other Specified Uses (Rural Use)”.

| Term | Meaning as printed |
|---|---|
| School, Education Ordinance (Cap. 279) section 3(1) | An institution, organization or establishment which provides, for **20 or more persons during any one day** or **8 or more persons at any one time**, any nursery, kindergarten, primary, secondary or post-secondary education or any other educational course by any means, including correspondence delivered by hand or through the postal services |
| Tutorial school | One type of private school offering non-formal curriculum, as categorized by the Education Bureau. Courses include tutorial, commercial, language and computer courses |
| On statutory plans | Tutorial school is subsumed under the broad use term “school” |

The 20-person and 8-person figures are the ordinance definition of “school”. They are not printed here as the threshold at which planning permission starts.

**SD takeaway:** Assume a tutorial school in a commercial floor is generally permitted as “school”; assume a section 16 application for one in a pure residential building or in the domestic floors of a composite building in the zones named above.

### 2. Compatibility and separated access (ss. 3.1–3.3)

| Test | Rule as printed |
|---|---|
| Compatibility | The school should not be incompatible with other uses in the same building. Compatibility is normally judged only against the permitted uses in the building or buildings |
| Patronage | Like other commercial uses, the school generally relies on people who do not live in the building. It will normally not be permitted in a residential building, or in the domestic portion of a composite building, unless the proposed access will not disturb or cause nuisance to residents |
| Entrance | Access to a tutorial school in a residential building, or in the domestic portion of a composite building, **must be separated** from the domestic portion by separate stairways and/or lifts or escalators used exclusively by the school. Those stairs, lifts or escalators must cope with the number of students entering and leaving |
| Common areas | Access through the common area of the residential development is generally not supported, because residents’ amenities would be affected, unless the applicant puts forward a practical and implementable proposal showing that nuisance will not be created |
| Means of escape | The access must not affect the existing means of escape. Otherwise, approval from the Building Authority is required |

**SD takeaway:** Give the school its own stair and/or lift or escalator, and do not rely on the residents’ common entrance unless a workable alternative is shown.

### 3. Parking at 120 students, safety comments, and registration (ss. 3.3–3.5 and 4)

| Item | Rule as printed |
|---|---|
| Scale that triggers parking and loading and unloading | **120 or more students attending classes at the same time**. Provision is then in accordance with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines for primary or secondary schools. This guideline does not print the bay numbers |
| Public views | Taken into account |
| Fire Services Department and Buildings Department | Must be satisfied on fire service installations, means of escape, fire resisting construction, barrier-free access, structural suitability, and related fire and building safety requirements |
| School registration | Education Ordinance (Cap. 279) and Education Regulations (Cap. 279A). Planning approval does not mean that other statutory or non-statutory requirements, or contractual obligations, are met. Those should be secured before works are contemplated |

**SD takeaway:** Once **120 or more** students are in class at the same time, provide parking and loading and unloading to the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines for a primary or secondary school, and keep the school access off the existing means of escape unless the Building Authority approves a change.
