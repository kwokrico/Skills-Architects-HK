# TPB PG-No. 30C Publication of Applications and Submission of Comments
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2023 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Procedure guideline on publication of section 16 planning applications and section 17 reviews, and on comments the public may make on them. It does not decide the planning merits of a scheme, and it does not set plot ratio or building height.

## Regulatory Overview
A section 16 application and a section 17 review are made available for public inspection, with a weekly newspaper and website notice during the first 3 weeks and, where local circumstances allow, a site notice at the start of that period. Any person may comment only within those first 3 weeks, and a comment lodged after that limit is treated as not having been made.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is published, and for how long (paras 2.1–2.5, 2.8, 4.2, 4.6)

The applications covered are a section 16 application for planning permission and a section 17 application for review of the Board’s decision on a section 16 application. A section 16 application is made on an application form, or on an electronic form through the Board’s electronic submission system.

| Item | Rule printed in this guideline |
|---|---|
| Public inspection of the application | As soon as reasonably practicable after it is made, until the Board or its Committee has decided. The form, plans and drawings, and supplementary information such as planning statements and technical reports, are included |
| What the public sees | Name is included. Correspondence address, telephone number, fax number and e-mail address are excluded |
| Newspaper and website notice | **Once a week** in two daily Chinese newspapers and one daily English newspaper, and on the Board’s website, **during the first 3 weeks** of the public inspection period |
| Comment period stated in that notice | **Within the first 3 weeks** of the public inspection period. The same **3-week** limit applies to a section 16 application and a section 17 review |
| When comments themselves are available | As soon as reasonably practicable after the first 3 weeks expire, at the Planning Enquiry Counters during normal office hours, until the decision |
| Other notices during the first 3 weeks | Secretariat, Planning Enquiry Counters, the relevant District Planning Office, local community centre, District Office and, where appropriate, the Rural Committee office |
| Buildings near the site | At the beginning of the public inspection period, a notice is sent to the Owners’ Corporation(s) or other committee(s) of buildings **within 100 feet (around 30 m)** from the boundary of the application site |

**SD takeaway:** Treat the application drawings, planning statement and the applicant’s name as public from lodgement until the decision.

### 2. Site notices and their sizes (paras 2.4, 2.6–2.7)

A newspaper and website notice is always published as a matter of practice. A site notice is posted in a prominent position on or near the site at the beginning of the public inspection period, unless the application involves a large area or many buildings, the site is remote and inaccessible by the public, or the owner or management office refuses posting.

| Application | Site notice printed in this guideline |
|---|---|
| Change of use of premises, for example a showroom or “shop and services” in an industrial building, or a kindergarten in an existing building | About **A2**: **16 inches × 23 inches** (420 mm × 594 mm), preferably on the notice board at the entrance or lobby |
| Development on open land, for example open storage or comprehensive development on a vacant site | About **A1**: **23 inches × 32 inches** (594 mm × 841 mm), at or near the site |
| Territorial or major local significance | A notice of about **33 inches × 60 inches** (867 mm × 1,577 mm) may also be mounted on a roadside railing in the locality |
| Other cases | Other measures the Board considers appropriate |

**SD takeaway:** A change-of-use inside an existing building is notified by an **A2** notice at the entrance or lobby, not by the open-land **A1** board.

### 3. Comments that the Board will process (paras 4.1–4.5, 4.7)

A comment is made in writing to the Secretary, Town Planning Board, by hand, post, fax or e-mail. English and Chinese are preferred, or a summary in the other language. The date of submission is the receipt date by hand, the postal-chop date by post, or the receipt date of an online, fax or e-mail submission.

| Content | Effect |
|---|---|
| Lodged after the first **3 weeks** | Treated as not having been made |
| The particular matter in the application, and the details of the comment | Essential. Without them the Board may refuse to process the comment and treat it as invalid |
| Name, address and telephone, fax or e-mail | Advised, so the Secretary and departments can communicate. Paragraph 4.5 does not list these as the essential content |
| What the Board primarily considers | The nature of the comment (support, against, or general concern); planning intention, land-use compatibility and impacts (environment, ecology, traffic, infrastructure, landscape, visual and the local community); comments specific to the proposed scheme; and other considerations the Board considers appropriate |

Only planning-related considerations are taken into account.

**SD takeaway:** A public comment has to identify the particular matter and give details inside the first **3 weeks**, or it is not a comment the Board processes.

### 4. Further information, and when the decision is notified (paras 3, 5.1–5.3)

| Event | Period or rule printed in this guideline |
|---|---|
| Further information to supplement an application | **Two months** after the day of receipt of the application by the Board, and **two months** after the day the Board decides to defer its decision so the applicant can give further information. Both periods are those published in the Gazette |
| Accepted further information that does not result in a material change | Made available for public inspection until the decision, and notified in a like manner as the application |
| Further information that does not affect the substance | The Board or its Secretary may exempt it from publication for public comments |
| How “material change” and exemption are judged | This guideline refers that determination to the Town Planning Board Guidelines on Submission of Further Information. Those tests are not set out here |
| Written notification to the applicant | After confirmation of the minutes, **normally 2 weeks** after the meeting |
| Date of the decision | The date of the meeting |
| Time limit for a review under section 17 and an appeal under section 17B | Starts from the **date of notification** of the Committee’s or the Board’s decision, not from the meeting date |
| Interim reply | May be requested in writing. It is not the formal notification |

On the same day as the meeting, the applicant and the commenter may seek verbal advice or read the Gist of Decision on the Board’s website.

**SD takeaway:** Count the section 17 review and the section 17B appeal from the notification letter, which is normally issued **2 weeks** after the meeting, not from the meeting date.
