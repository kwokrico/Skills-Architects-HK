# TPB PG-No. 33B Deferment of Decision on Representations and Applications
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2023 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Procedure guideline on when the Town Planning Board defers a decision on a representation, a further representation, a section 12A application, a section 16 application and comments on it, a section 16A application, or a section 17 review. It is a timetable guideline. It does not set plot ratio or building height.

## Regulatory Overview
A deferment request is made in writing to the Secretary of the Board, with reasonable grounds and a finite period, and the first deferment for an applicant is normally granted so that further information can be prepared within two months. A second deferment is intended to be the last, and a request to defer a representation hearing is limited to a maximum adjournment of two weeks.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Grounds for deferring an application or review (paras 2.1–2.2, 3.1–3.3)

The Board may defer on request, or of its own volition, and reschedule the meeting. It takes into account all relevant factors and whether the right or interest of other parties will be affected, and it may specify the maximum deferment period. The rescheduled date should be adhered to.

| Ground | What this guideline accepts |
|---|---|
| (a) Consultation | Further consultation with Government departments is required to resolve major technical issues directly associated with the case |
| (b) Important supplementary information | Information essential to the decision is not yet available from the parties or departments. The examples are a new or revised Traffic Impact Assessment, Environmental Impact Assessment or Drainage Impact Assessment, and a refinement to address public comments |
| (c) Imminent government study or infrastructure decision | A major Government planning-related study due to be completed shortly, or a decision on a major infrastructure proposal due soon, which might have significant planning implications for the site and would affect the Board’s decision |
| Financial or market reasons | The need to assess financial or economic viability, or waiting for a better economic climate, should normally not be accepted |
| Accepted further information | The meeting on a section 12A application, section 16 application or section 17 review may be rescheduled to process further information. The practices are in the separate further-information guidelines |
| Outstanding adverse representation | A decision on a section 16 application or section 17 review is deferred if the zoning or development restrictions of the site are still subject to an outstanding adverse representation not yet submitted to the Chief Executive in Council, and the substance of that representation is relevant to the application or review |
| Other | Any other reasonable grounds the Board thinks fit |

**SD takeaway:** A deferment is for a missing technical assessment, a department issue, or an imminent study or infrastructure decision, not for project finance or market timing.

### 2. How many deferments, and the two-month preparation period (paras 2.2, 3.4–3.5, 4.1–4.4)

| Rule | What is printed |
|---|---|
| Preparation time | Normally **two months** to prepare the submission of information, if required, after the Board agrees to defer |
| Return to the Board | After that specified period, and before the statutory time limit expires. The Secretary may schedule the meeting sooner, still before that expiry. Assessments, if required, are submitted in one go |
| First deferment requested by the applicant | Normally granted, to prepare further information on the outstanding issues |
| Second deferment | Should be the last deferment, to avoid delay |
| Second deferment refused | If reasonable action, such as submission of further information, has not been taken; if the site is the subject of repeated applications for more or less the same use with a similar proposal, or of withdrawn applications for the same use at the same site or premises; or for other factors the Board considers relevant, including the public comments, whether the outstanding issues are fundamental or can be dealt with later, and whether enforcement would be affected |
| A further request after the second | Not favourably considered, except under very special circumstances and with strong justifications |

| When the request arrives | What happens |
|---|---|
| Reasonable grounds as in paragraph 3.1, received before the agenda and paper are issued (**normally one week** before the meeting) | Planning Department prepares a simple paper. For a section 12A application or a section 17 review the applicant need not attend |
| Same kind of request, received after the agenda | Reported at the scheduled meeting |
| Board agrees | The applicant is told the further-information period and the rescheduled date |
| Board does not agree | It may decide a section 16 application at the scheduled meeting. For a section 12A application or a section 17 review it may adjourn to the following meeting |
| Request not supported by the Planning Department, for example no reasonable grounds | Submitted with the application paper whether or not the agenda has issued. For a section 12A application or a section 17 review the applicant or representative must attend and explain. Absence allows the Board to proceed. If deferment is not warranted, the Board may decide |
| Section 16A | Power is delegated to the Director of Planning under section 2(5)(b). The request goes to the Secretary as early as possible before the Director decides, and the Director applies these Guidelines |

Receipt of a deferment request is notified on the Board’s website. The meeting is open to the public except the deliberation.

**SD takeaway:** Programme one deferral of **two months**, treat a second deferral as the last, and do not count on a third.

### 3. Representations and further representations (paras 5.1–5.4)

The plan must be submitted to the Chief Executive in Council within **five months** after the expiry of the last plan exhibition period. That limit may be extended, if appropriate, for **two months** and, in exceptional circumstances, for **two further periods of 2 months each** by the Secretary for Development. A deferment of representations can affect that submission and the other parties at the hearing.

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Whether a representation hearing will be deferred | Not entertained unless there are very strong reasons and the other concerned parties have consented |
| Length of any adjournment | If absolutely unavoidable, a maximum of **two weeks** counting from the original hearing date |
| Deadline for the request | As soon as possible, and **not later than two weeks** before the scheduled meeting. A request inside that deadline is circulated as a simple paper. If the Board does not accept it, the hearing proceeds as scheduled |
| Request after that two-week deadline | Considered at the scheduled meeting. The parties or their representatives must attend and explain. If they do not attend, the Board may proceed in their absence. If deferment is not warranted, the hearing proceeds |
| Further representations | No hearing is arranged. The Board normally will not entertain a deferment request from further representers |

**SD takeaway:** A representation hearing moves by at most **two weeks**, and the request has to be in at least **two weeks** before the hearing, with the other parties’ consent.
