# TPB PG-No. 27 Submission of Concept Plan
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
August 2002 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: General requirements for a submission in support of a section 16 application for new development, or for wholesale redevelopment or conversion of an existing building to commercial, residential or mixed commercial/residential uses. Applications for a Small House, and for open storage and port back-up uses, are outside this set and follow their own guidance notes. The attached sample is a block plan and section for illustration. Storey counts drawn on that sample are not a submission standard.

## Regulatory Overview
In a section 16 application the Board takes into account land-use compatibility and the impacts of the proposed use or development, and it is the general development concept, rather than detailed building design, that is relevant, so detailed building design need not be included. The information should be sufficient for the Board to assess the application but broad enough to allow flexibility for subsequent detailed design, and any requirements in the Notes, the Explanatory Statement and/or the Planning Brief for the zone **must** be observed.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Concept plan (section 3.A)

A concept plan of a suitable scale should show these key elements:

| Item | What should be shown |
|---|---|
| (i) | Clear delineation of the application site |
| (ii) | Disposition of the building block or blocks, and the number of blocks |
| (iii) | Location of main land uses by building block, and disposition of broad uses by floors, such as residential, office, shop and services |
| (iv) | Building heights and number of storeys, indicating levels of podium and basement if any, and the maximum building height if a restriction is stipulated under the Notes of the outline zoning plan |
| (v) | Location of vehicular ingress/egress points and of parking and loading/unloading facilities |
| (vi) | If applicable, location of non-building areas, including set-backs and drainage/utility reserves |
| (vii) | A section plan or elevation drawing, where appropriate, to illustrate the above |

For development or redevelopment of a large scale and comprehensive nature, and/or in areas subject to special requirements such as landscaping, environmental and infrastructural considerations, or a requirement for “Government, Institution or Community” facilities, the concept plan should also indicate, whenever applicable:

| Item | Further information |
|---|---|
| (i) | Layout, alignment and width of internal roads, including emergency vehicular access |
| (ii) | Landscaping and, if applicable, tree survey, tree preservation, transplanting and replanting proposals |
| (iii) | Location and types of environmental mitigation measures |
| (iv) | Location of government, institution or community facilities and public transport facilities |
| (v) | Major ancillary facilities such as refuse collection point, drainage facility, sewage treatment and disposal facility, electricity substation and liquefied petroleum gas compound |
| (vi) | Location and types of open space (public/private) |
| (vii) | Location of pedestrian circulation facilities, including subways, footbridges and footbridge connections |

Detailed information on building design, such as external finishes, voids, projection and smoke vent, and other technical details such as floor layout, need not be submitted. If the site is subject to strong urban design consideration, supporting information such as built form, facade treatment, photomontage and the maximum height of each building block would be required. For a housing-related project, submission of a schematic floor layout would be required for assessment of the environmental acceptability of the development. The Explanatory Statement of the outline zoning plan may state whether an area is subject to strong urban design consideration. In case of doubt, clarification could be sought from the District Planning Office.

Detailed calculation of gross floor area for the Buildings Ordinance need not be dealt with. For a scheme which may involve dedication or surrender of land for a public purpose, the broad arrangement, together with the additional gross floor area resulted, if any, could be indicated. For an application involving minor relaxation of building height, site coverage, or gross floor area/plot ratio, only matters of direct relevance to the subject of the application need be shown. In general, the information in (a) above should be indicated. In areas of high landscape or urban design value, applicants should provide built form and photomontage to demonstrate acceptability.

A sample concept plan is attached. It is a sample block plan and section. Its legend states that the building heights and storey numbers indicated on the plan are the maximum figures. Its notes state that a ground plan and other floor plans should be included where appropriate, and that an elevation drawing is to be included where appropriate. The drawing is an illustrative sample. The storey counts on it are not requirements of this guideline.

**SD takeaway:** Show site, blocks, broad use by floor, heights and storeys against any Notes limit, and access and parking; add a schematic floor layout for housing, and built form and photomontage where urban design or landscape value is strong.

### 2. Broad land-use categories (section 3.B)

Applicants are encouraged to indicate broad use categories only. Indication of a detailed use type would not be necessary. For a mixed commercial/residential development, the broad categories are:

| Category | How specific |
|---|---|
| Residential Flat; Hotel; Office; Shop, Services and Eating place; Recreation and Leisure | Broad category |
| Government, Institution or Community facilities | **Need to be specific**, because different facilities may have different land-use implications |
| Open Space | Clear demarcation between public and private open space |
| Major Public Utility Installation | **Need to be specific** (for example a refuse collection point) |
| Major Transport Facility | **Need to be specific** (for example a public transport interchange) |
| Car Parking/Loading and Unloading Facility; Vehicular Access Road | Broad category |
| Potential hazardous installation | The specific type of provision should be specified on the concept plan |

**SD takeaway:** Use broad use labels, and name the particular government, utility, transport or hazardous installation on the concept plan.

### 3. Development parameters, phasing, and the limit of the guideline (section 3.C and paras 4.1–4.2)

To leave flexibility, applicants are encouraged to indicate, as far as practicable, the maximum and/or minimum range for key parameters, so that an amendment is avoided where the change does not exceed the range, and so that traffic, infrastructure and environmental implications can be assessed on the worst case. The example schedule prints blanks, not fixed figures. Residential and commercial gross floor area can be expressed as a maximum provision. Government, institution or community facilities and open space can be expressed as a minimum provision.

| Parameter on the example schedule | Form of the figure |
|---|---|
| Domestic gross floor area; hotel gross floor area; office gross floor area; commercial gross floor area; bonus gross floor area if available | Not more than |
| Number of flats/units | Not more than |
| Flat/unit size | Ranging from … to … |
| Building height; number of storeys | Not exceeding |
| Site coverage | Not more than |
| Gross floor area for government, institution or community facilities | Not less than, and specific for each facility |
| Open space | Not less than, and specific for public and private open space |
| Gross floor area for indoor recreation; parking provision; loading and unloading; anticipated population/capacity | Ranging from … to … |

If phased development is proposed and the provision of public facilities is involved, the proposed phasing, the relative timing of the phases, and the provision of government, institution or community facilities, open space and other public facilities in each phase should also be indicated.

These guidelines serve as general reference. They are not meant to restrict the content of each development proposal, nor to restrict the right of the Board to require further information. Each application will be considered on individual merits. Doubt on the level of detail could be clarified with the District Planning Office.

For a partial conversion of an existing building, or a change of use of a unit or units, the section 16 application form in general requires the floor area and address of the premises, the type of uses or activities proposed, and floor or sketch plans showing the internal layout. Depending on the case, information on vehicular and pedestrian accesses, car parking spaces, loading/unloading areas and means of escape may also be required. The pamphlet “Supplementary Information to be Provided for a Section 16 Application” covers school (including kindergarten and tutorial school), office within an industrial building/workshop, composite industrial-office building, offensive trades, small house, warehouse and storage, rural industrial workshop, petrol or liquefied petroleum gas filling station, large-scale residential development, and uses in a “Comprehensive Development Area”.

**SD takeaway:** State residential and commercial floor area as a maximum and government facilities and open space as a minimum, and show which phase provides each public facility.
