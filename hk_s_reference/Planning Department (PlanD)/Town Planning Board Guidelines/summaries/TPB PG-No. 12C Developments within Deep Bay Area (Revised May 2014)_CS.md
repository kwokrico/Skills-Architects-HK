# TPB PG-No. 12C Developments within Deep Bay Area (Revised May 2014)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Revised May 2014 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Governs applications under section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance for uses and developments in the Deep Bay Area (Deep Bay, Mai Po Marshes and the adjacent area, including Hoo Hok Wai). It is not the Notes of an outline zoning plan, a Ramsar site management plan, or a completed ecological impact assessment.

## Regulatory Overview
The Board adopts a precautionary approach and the principle of “no-net-loss in wetland”, covering both loss in area and loss in function, so that continuous and adjoining fish ponds continue to supply food and roosting grounds for ardeids and other species. Control is applied by a Wetland Conservation Area for existing contiguous active or abandoned fish ponds and a Wetland Buffer Area of about 500 m along the landward side of that area.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Precautionary approach and no-net-loss (paragraphs 1–5)

| Rule | What the extract requires |
|---|---|
| Status of the area | Deep Bay Area is treated as a wetland of international importance. Five Sites of Special Scientific Interest are named: Mai Po Village, Mai Po Marshes (including Lut Chau), Tsim Bei Tsui, Tsim Bei Tsui Egretry, and Inner Deep Bay. Mai Po Marshes, Inner Deep Bay and the surrounding fish ponds have been a Ramsar “Wetland of International Importance” since 1995. |
| Fish-pond function | Fish ponds have intrinsic value as a food source and as roosting and foraging habitat, and are fundamentally linked with the Mai Po Marshes. The Hoo Hok Wai survey (completed June 2013) confirms high ecological value of the fish ponds and freshwater marshes there, including for ardeids, spoonbills, Eurasian Otter and other wetland-dependent species. |
| Contiguous ponds | Higher bird usage correlates with ponds that are contiguous and of greater continuous area, as against fragmented and isolated ponds. Removal of individual ponds is difficult to justify because different ponds are used preferentially in different seasons. |
| Disturbance | Loss of fish ponds reduces the food source. Developments adjoining or near fish ponds that cause disturbance, in particular open storage, industrial uses, dispersed village-type development and roads, lead to a reduction in bird usage. |
| **No-net-loss** | **No decline in wetland or ecological functions served by the existing fish ponds should occur.** Alternative uses are suitable only if they do not result in loss of the ecological function of the original ponds and they complement the ecological functions of the wetlands and fish ponds in and/or around the Deep Bay Area. The replacement habitat should be able to provide food supplies in a sustainable manner so that birds, particularly egrets and herons, are not put at risk. |

Figure A shows the Wetland Conservation Area and Wetland Buffer Area boundaries. The metre ticks on that figure are a graphic scale. The only buffer width stated in the text is the Wetland Buffer Area of **about 500 m**.

**SD takeaway:** Do not fragment contiguous fish ponds; an alternative habitat has to avoid any decline in wetland function, including a sustainable food supply for egrets and herons.

### 2. Wetland Conservation Area (paragraphs 6 and 6.1–6.3)

| Rule | What the extract requires |
|---|---|
| What the area is | All existing continuous and adjoining active or abandoned fish ponds in the Deep Bay Area. They should all be conserved. |
| **New development** | **Would not be allowed** unless it is required to support conservation of the ecological value of the area, or it is an essential infrastructural project with overriding public interest. |
| Ecological impact assessment | Required for any such development, to demonstrate no net loss in wetland function and no negative disturbance impact. Also required for any redevelopment which requires planning permission. |
| Pond filling | Wetland compensation is required for any development involving pond filling. Mitigation against disturbance is necessary. Both are imposed as planning approval conditions. |
| Uses that may be considered | Subject to an ecological impact assessment, activities must relate to conservation, environmental education, or essential infrastructure (paragraphs 6.2.1–6.2.3). |
| Conservation uses | Land uses should be devoted to conservation management so that habitat integrity is maintained and disturbance and/or fragmentation are avoided. Alternative uses that perform ecological functions similar to or better than the ponds replaced may be considered, such as a recreated nature reserve, a wetland wildlife reserve for bird watching, or sports fishery and aquaculture. |
| Education uses | Research and educational uses are encouraged. Nature trails, an ecology study centre and a field study centre, with controlled access, would be considered. |
| Setting and pollution | Conservation and education uses should be appropriate to the rural and wetland setting, should be able to enhance visual and landscape quality, and **should not add to the pollution loading of the Deep Bay Area**. |
| Essential infrastructure | Rail, emergency vehicular access and footpath, road, drainage and flood protection, and public utility projects may be considered only where no suitable alternative location outside the Wetland Conservation Area can be identified. The project should include a practical wetland compensation scheme and **should not add to the pollution loading of the Deep Bay Area**. |
| Private-public partnership | Limited low-density private residential or recreational development may be considered at the **landward fringe** of the Wetland Conservation Area, in exchange for committed long-term conservation and management of the remaining ponds within the development site. No density figure is stated. |
| Siting of that development | Should require **minimum pond filling** and be located **as far away from Deep Bay and/or adjoining an existing development site**. |
| Submission for that development | An ecological impact assessment with an acceptable and feasible wetland enhancement and management scheme, showing that the development would not result in, or is able to fully compensate for, any loss of the total ecological function of the original ponds on the site, and that the impact can be mitigated. A mechanism is required so that long-term wetland management can be practically implemented and monitored. |
| Route of scrutiny | Through the objection consideration process, or by a request to rezone the site to “Other Specified Uses (Comprehensive Development and Wetland Enhancement Area)”. |

**SD takeaway:** Inside the Wetland Conservation Area, lock the scheme to conservation, controlled-access education, or essential infrastructure with wetland compensation; a private residential scheme is only a landward-fringe exchange for long-term pond management, with minimum pond filling.

### 3. Wetland Buffer Area (paragraphs 6.4–6.7)

| Rule | What the extract requires |
|---|---|
| Purpose and width | Protects the ecological integrity of the fish ponds and wetland in the Wetland Conservation Area and prevents development with a negative off-site disturbance impact. Designated as **a buffer area of about 500 m** along the landward boundary of the Wetland Conservation Area. |
| Degraded land | A substantial amount of fish ponds in the Wetland Buffer Area have already been lost through filling, and certain areas are degraded by open storage. Those degraded areas may be target areas for an appropriate level of residential or recreational development, as an incentive to remove open storage and/or restore some lost fish ponds. |
| Ecological impact assessment | Required for development or redevelopment which requires planning permission, except the local and minor uses in Appendix A (including temporary uses). |
| **Refusal test** | Development or redevelopment which may have negative impacts on the ecological value of the Wetland Conservation Area **would not be supported**, unless the ecological impact assessment demonstrates that the negative impacts could be mitigated through positive measures. |
| Pollution | The assessment should also demonstrate that the development **will not cause a net increase in pollution load to Deep Bay**. |
| **Open storage and container back-up** | Applications for new open storage or container back-up uses, including workshops, whether temporary or permanent, **would normally not be allowed**, in view of disturbance to birds, in particular where the use involves filling of contiguous ponds. |
| Limited exception | Open storage or container back-up close to the Lok Ma Chau crossing and **without pond filling** might be sympathetically considered, because of the need to facilitate cross-boundary movement of goods. No distance from the crossing is stated. |
| Residential or recreational on degraded sites | Proposals that remove or replace existing open storage or container back-up uses and/or restore lost wetlands may be given sympathetic consideration, subject to satisfactory ecological and other impact assessments. |
| Sites directly abutting the Wetland Conservation Area | The development **should provide a wetland and visual buffer** to separate the development from the Wetland Conservation Area, and should restore some of the lost fish ponds to an appropriate form of wetland adjoining the Wetland Conservation Area. **No buffer width is stated.** |
| Wetland restoration | Proposals to restore lost fish ponds, or to replace existing undesirable uses by wetland habitats, are encouraged. |
| Recreation | Appropriate recreational use may be considered, having regard to compatibility with any adjoining fish-pond area and to other planning and environmental implications. |
| Residential | Residential projects which include replacement of existing open storage and port back-up uses and/or detailed wetland restoration may be given special consideration, subject to satisfactory ecological and other impact assessments. They should be compatible with surrounding land uses and the rural setting. |

**SD takeaway:** Treat the Wetland Buffer Area as an about-500 m disturbance screen; on land directly abutting the Wetland Conservation Area, provide a wetland and visual buffer and restore adjoining wetland, and do not propose new open storage or pond filling.

### 4. Field investigation and other tests (paragraphs 6.8–6.9)

| Rule | What the extract requires |
|---|---|
| **Field investigation** | For a planning application that requires an ecological impact assessment in either area, field investigation **normally covering a period of not less than 12 months** should be included, to record existing wildlife habitats, flora and fauna, and their seasonal changes, and to study effects on them. |
| Scope of that survey | Exact requirements depend on the scale and nature of the development and on whether any direct loss of fish ponds is involved. Advice on technical requirements should be sought from the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department. |
| Other planning tests | Development intensity, compatibility with surrounding land uses, environmental impact (noise, air and water qualities), traffic and drainage impacts, provision of infrastructure, and visual impact. No numerical standards for these tests are stated in this guideline. |

**SD takeaway:** Programme not less than 12 months of ecological field work before a scheme that needs an ecological impact assessment, and test intensity, compatibility, noise, air, water, traffic, drainage, infrastructure and visual impact as well as ecology.

### 5. Uses exempted from ecological impact assessment in the Wetland Buffer Area (paragraph 6.5 and Appendix A)

The exemption is from an ecological impact assessment as part of the submission. It is not an exemption from planning permission.

| Group | Uses |
|---|---|
| Unmarked | Temporary uses; agricultural use (**except in a Site of Special Scientific Interest zone**); ancestral hall; barbecue spot; burial ground; **electricity substation of single storey**; house, limited to **alteration, modification and/or redevelopment to the existing building bulk only**; New Territories Exempted Houses; on-farm domestic structure; playground or playing field **in “V” and “R(D)” zones only**; police post or police reporting centre; public convenience; **pumping station of single storey**; refreshment kiosk; shrine; tent camping site. |
| Marked # — other than a free-standing building | Bank; barber shop; beauty parlour; off-course betting centre; photographic studio; private club; retail shop; showroom **excluding a motor-vehicle showroom**. |
| Marked * — other than a free-standing building **exceeding 3 storeys** | Clinic or polyclinic; post office; public library; school; social welfare facility. |
| Marked ^ — **not applicable** to the “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Eco-lodge” zone on the Ma Tso Lung and Hoo Hok Wai Outline Zoning Plan | Government refuse collection point; public utility installation limited to an **electricity mast, lamp pole, pipeline and telephone booth only**. |

**SD takeaway:** In the Wetland Buffer Area, a house exemption covers only the existing building bulk, and a new free-standing building or a free-standing building over 3 storeys falls outside the marked exemptions.
