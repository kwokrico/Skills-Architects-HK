# TPB PG-No. 22D Development within Other Specified Uses (Business) Zone
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2007 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Defines non-polluting industrial use, office use and an industrial-office building in the “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Business” zone, and states which uses are permitted as of right and which require planning permission. It is not the user schedule printed on a particular outline zoning plan, and it does not set plot ratio or site coverage.

## Regulatory Overview
The zone is primarily for general employment and is a combination of commercial and industrial character, without replacing the “Commercial” or “Industrial” zones. In a new business building a mix of non-polluting industrial, office and other commercial uses is permitted as of right, while in an existing industrial or industrial-office building only less fire-hazard-prone office use that does not provide customer services or goods directly to the general public is permitted as of right.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Definitions (paragraphs 1.2 and 3.1–3.9)

| Term | Definition and limits |
|---|---|
| Offensive trades | Industrial uses involving offensive trades declared under the Public Health and Municipal Services Ordinance **will not be permitted** in the zone (paragraph 1.2). |
| Non-polluting industrial use | Any industrial use which **does not** involve activities that are a detriment to occupants of the building and the amenity of the area by reason of noise, waste water discharge, vibration, smell, fume, smoke, soot, ash, dust or grit. |
| How it is judged | Reference to the Air Pollution Control Ordinance, Water Pollution Control Ordinance, Waste Disposal Ordinance, Noise Control Ordinance, their technical memoranda and regulations, and the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines. Advice may be sought from the Director of Environmental Protection case by case. |
| What it includes | Prototyping, production, design, research and development, alteration, testing, quality control, adaptation, repair, assembly, packaging, storage and distribution of goods and materials **without generating environmental pollution and nuisance**, plus information technology support and training for those processes. Examples include custom-tailoring, fashion design and production, computer-aided design, design and prototyping of electronic products and components, label, badge or button embossing, editing of newspapers, books and magazines for printing off the premises, photo-typesetting and typesetting, film developing and editing, packaging and quality inspection, and after-sale repair of electrical appliances, furniture and office equipment. |
| **What it excludes** | Industrial uses involving **offensive trades** declared under the Public Health and Municipal Services Ordinance; **Specified Processes** under the Air Pollution Control Ordinance; activities which produce **chemical waste**; and activities which generate residuals such as waste water, noise, aerial emissions, solid waste, runoff and odour, or physical changes causing unacceptable impacts. |
| Non-polluting industrial use excluding Dangerous Goods | Establishments within paragraph 3.1 which **would not involve** use and/or storage of substances classified as Dangerous Goods requiring a licence under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance. Use and/or storage of **small quantities exempted from licensing will be permitted**. |
| Office excluding direct provision of customer services or goods | An office that **would not attract frequently a large number of visitors** by providing direct services or goods to customers or visiting members of the general public. |
| Examples treated as that office | Headquarters or back-office operations; professional consultants such as architects, engineers, surveyors, planning consultants, solicitors and accountants; business services such as advertising agencies, management consultants, public relations agencies and interior or graphic design offices. |
| Treated as general office, not as that office | Travel agents, property agents, employment agencies, investment broker firms, money lending offices, ticketing and sales offices, and tourist information offices. In industrial and industrial-office buildings, **planning permission is required**, except in the purpose-designed non-industrial portion on the lower floors of an existing building. |
| Industrial-office building | A dual-purpose building in which **every unit**, other than a unit in the purpose-designed non-industrial portion, can be used flexibly for both industrial and office purposes. The building **must comply** with all relevant building and fire regulations applicable to **both** industrial and office buildings, including floor loading, compartmentation, lighting, ventilation, means of escape and sanitary fitments. |

**SD takeaway:** An industrial-office building must meet both industrial and office construction rules, and a public-facing office is a general office that needs permission in the industrial portion.

### 2. As-of-right uses in new development (paragraphs 4.1–4.3)

The following are permitted **as of right** as new development, or as redevelopment or conversion of the whole building:

| Building type | Uses permitted as of right |
|---|---|
| Business building | A mix of non-polluting industrial use (**excluding** industrial undertakings involving use or storage of Dangerous Goods), office, and other commercial uses. |
| Office building | Office, with or without retail and other commercial uses. |
| Industrial building | Non-polluting industrial use (**excluding** Dangerous Goods use or storage) and office use (**excluding** direct provision of customer services and goods). |
| Industrial-office building | Non-polluting industrial use (**excluding** Dangerous Goods use or storage); offices (**excluding** direct customer services and goods) on upper floors; general offices with or without commercial uses in the purpose-designed non-industrial portion on the lower floors, **separated from the industrial uses on the upper floors by a buffer floor**. No buffer-floor dimension is stated. |

| Further rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Column 1 of Schedule I | All uses in Column 1 of Schedule I of the user schedule are permitted as of right in new development and in redevelopment or conversion of a whole existing industrial or industrial-office building to a business building. The schedule itself is not printed in this guideline. |
| New industrial or industrial-office building | Development whose general building plans are submitted and approved under this zoning **has to conform** to Column 1 of Schedule II unless the Board permits otherwise. |
| General office in that new building | Permitted **as of right** only if no industrial undertakings involving offensive trades or use or storage of Dangerous Goods will be accommodated, and that intention is **clearly specified in the building-plan submission**. Otherwise, only office use that does not involve direct provision of customer services or goods is permitted as of right. |
| **Dangerous Goods and customer-service offices** | For a building with office uses involving direct provision of customer services or goods, **planning permission will not be granted** for any proposed industrial undertaking involving use or storage of Dangerous Goods that requires a licence under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance. |

**SD takeaway:** If the building-plan submission does not clearly exclude offensive trades and Dangerous Goods, general office is not as of right, and a customer-service office blocks a later Dangerous Goods industrial use.

### 3. Existing industrial and industrial-office buildings (paragraphs 4.4–4.7)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Existing use | As stated in the covering Notes, no action is required to make an existing use conform. All existing uses in an existing industrial or industrial-office building will be tolerated. Any **material change of use** has to be permitted in terms of the plan. |
| As of right in the existing building | Only office use which would **not** involve direct provision of customer services or goods to the general public. Schedule II so provides, because polluting and hazardous industrial uses may still be present. |
| Column 2 of Schedule II | Other office, commercial and institutional uses specified there **require planning permission**. |
| Shop and services | The provision for a planning application is **restricted to the ground floor only**, because of fire safety. |
| **Ancillary showroom** | Up to **20% of the total usable floor area** of an industrial firm in the same premises or building is permitted **without application**. Above that threshold, an ancillary showroom may be permitted **on any floor** upon application. |
| **Aggregate commercial floor area on the ground floor** | As a general principle, should **not exceed 460 m²** where the building has a sprinkler system, and **not exceed 230 m²** where it does not. |
| Slight exceedance | The applicant has to demonstrate that the fire safety concern can be satisfactorily addressed. Each case is considered on its own merits. |
| Floor area excluded from that calculation | Staircases, common circulation areas, lift landings, lavatories, water closets, and any space occupied by machinery for any lift, air-conditioning system or similar service provided for the building, may be excluded. |
| Uses to which the 230 m² / 460 m² limits do not apply | Uses ancillary to, or supporting, the industrial activities and the routine activities of workers: bank; fast food counter **sited at street level without seating accommodation and licensed as a food factory**; electrical shop (electrical accessories, usually with repairing, and small in scale); local provisions store (cigarettes, drinks, canned food and other local convenience goods, and small in scale); showroom in connection with the main industrial use. |
| Low-zone conversion | The 230 m² / 460 m² criteria **do not apply** if the commercial portion is **completely separated** from the industrial or industrial-office portion on the upper floors by a **buffer floor of non-hazardous occupancy**, such as a car-parking floor. No floor thickness is stated. |
| Means of escape | **In all cases, separate means of escape should be available for the commercial portion.** |
| **Basement** | The Fire Services Department **will not support** commercial uses in the basement or basements of an industrial or industrial-office building. |
| Purpose-designed non-industrial portion of an existing industrial-office building | Commercial uses and general office are permitted **as of right** on the lower floors, **excluding basement or basements** and floors containing wholly or mainly car parking, loading/unloading bays and/or plant room, provided they are separated from the industrial or industrial-office use above by a buffer floor of non-hazardous occupancy, such as a car-parking or loading/unloading floor. |
| No mixed industrial use there | **No industrial uses will be permitted** to co-exist with the commercial uses in the non-industrial portion. |

**SD takeaway:** On an existing building, keep commercial floor area on the ground floor within 230 m² without sprinklers or 460 m² with sprinklers, give the commercial portion its own means of escape, and do not put commercial use in the basement.

### 4. Material change of use and other requirements (paragraphs 4.8 and 5.1)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Material change of use in part of an existing building | A change from one industrial use to another, or from non-industrial to industrial, must be permitted in terms of the extant outline zoning plan. |
| **As of right after that change** | **Only** non-polluting industrial uses **without** use or storage of Dangerous Goods that requires a licence under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance, so that interface problems are not perpetuated or aggravated. |
| Parking and loading/unloading | For all new development, redevelopment, conversion and material change of use, adequate spaces should be provided in accordance with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines. No space numbers are stated in this guideline. |
| Other controls | All other statutory or non-statutory requirements of relevant departments must also be met, including building structure, means of escape and fire safety, which will be considered at building-plan submission. |

**SD takeaway:** A material change to an industrial use is as of right only when it is non-polluting and does not need a Dangerous Goods licence, and parking and loading still follow the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines.
