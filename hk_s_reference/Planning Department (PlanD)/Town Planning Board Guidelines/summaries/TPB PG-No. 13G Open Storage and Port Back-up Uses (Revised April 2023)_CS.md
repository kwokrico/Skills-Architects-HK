# TPB PG-No. 13G Open Storage and Port Back-up Uses (Revised April 2023)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Revised April 2023 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Section 16 criteria for open storage and port back-up uses, including temporary uses on rural statutory town plans outside the zones where those uses are generally permitted. It is a set of assessment tests and submission expectations. It is not a zoning map that assigns a village or a district to Category 1, 2, 3 or 4, and it is not a permission.

## Regulatory Overview
Open storage and port back-up uses are to be rationalised inside designated zones, and temporary proposals outside those zones are assessed against four location categories plus access, drainage, environment and previous-approval tests. A temporary permission, where granted, is for a period the Board fixes on the merits, and that period would not exceed 3 years.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What counts, and where the use is already ruled out (ss. 1.1–1.6)

| Term or zone | Rule printed in the guideline |
|---|---|
| Open storage | The greater part of the site, generally assumed to be **more than 50% of the site area**, is uncovered and used for storage, repair or breaking, other than container-related uses |
| Ancillary uncovered storage | Uncovered storage ancillary to industrial, workshop and warehousing on the same site is **excluded** if it does **not exceed 50%** of the site area |
| Still open storage | Temporary structures that do not radically differ from open accommodation, for example galvanised sheeting used for carports on dumping and vehicle repair sites; and open storage with on-site commercial activity, for example display and sale of vehicles |
| Examples listed | Storage of rattan and bamboo, logs and timber, ceramic/pottery, processed agricultural products, used electrical appliances/scrap metal, cans/tanks, construction material, construction equipment, recyclable materials such as waste paper and waste plastics; cement/sand; chemical products; dangerous goods; vehicles for stripping, breaking or repairing; vehicles and vehicle parts for sale or disposal; vehicle depot |
| Port back-up | Port-related activity **off-port**, beyond the perimeter of the container terminals, river trade terminals, and public and private cargo working areas. The listed activities are container storage/repair yard, container freight station, container vehicle park/container vehicle repair yard, freight forwarding services center, and logistic centre |
| Generally permitted | In “Open Storage” (“OS”), “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Port Back-up Uses” (“OU(PBU)”) and “Industrial (Group D)” (“I(D)”), open storage and port back-up uses are generally permitted |
| Still needs permission inside those zones | Container storage, storage of dangerous goods, and container trailer/tractor park, so the Board can test environmental, drainage, traffic and other impacts |
| Prohibited | In “Site of Special Scientific Interest” (“SSSI”), “Conservation Area” (“CA”), “Coastal Protection Area” (“CPA”), “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Comprehensive Development and Wetland Enhancement Area” (“OU(CDWEA)”) and “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Comprehensive Development and Wetland Protection Area” (“OU(CDWPA)”) |
| Elsewhere on a rural statutory town plan | Temporary open storage and port back-up may be permissible on application. The exact period is fixed on the merits and **would not exceed 3 years** |

**SD takeaway:** If more than half the site is uncovered storage, repair or breaking, design it as an open-storage application; if the uncovered ancillary yard stays at or under half the site, it falls outside this definition.

### 2. Category 1, 2, 3 and 4 (s. 2.1(a)–(d))

The category is a location test in the numbered paragraphs. Plans 1a, 1b, 2a, 2b, 3 and 4 are said to indicate the broad coverage (North West New Territories northern and southern parts, North East New Territories northern and southern parts, South East New Territories, and South West New Territories). The extract of those plans is a place-name and zoning-label dump. A village or district name on those pages is not a category assignment. The plan remarks state that the land-use zoning boundaries do not necessarily correspond to the classification of sites, and that the boundaries may change.

| Category | Where the paragraph places it | How an application is treated |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Areas considered suitable. In general, sites zoned “OS”, “OU(PBU)”, “Industrial” (“I”) or “I(D)”, with compatible industrial, public-utility, port back-up and open-storage activity. Other suitable sites in the **North East and North West New Territories** are also included in this category. Uses are generally permitted as of right in such zones | Favourable consideration will normally be given, subject to no major adverse departmental comments and local objections, or to those concerns being addressed by approval conditions. Submit technical assessments where the use may cause significant environmental and traffic concerns. The examples named are container trailer/tractor park and open storage of chemical products/dangerous goods |
| **2** | Areas mostly without a clear planning intention or a fixed development programme; areas to be affected by major upcoming infrastructural projects; areas within or close to clusters of open storage, port back-up or other brownfield sites/temporary uses. The areas **should not be subject to high flooding risk** | Submit technical assessments, where appropriate or if required, on drainage, traffic, visual, landscaping and environmental impacts. Subject to no adverse departmental comments and local objections, or to those concerns being met by conditions, permission **could be granted on a temporary basis up to a maximum period of 3 years** |
| **3** | Areas **outside Category 1, 2 and 4**. “Existing” and approved open storage and port back-up uses are to be contained. **Further proliferation is not acceptable** | An application would **normally not be favourably considered** unless the site has a previous planning approval, whether or not the applicant is the same person. Sympathetic consideration may be given if genuine efforts to comply with previous conditions are shown and/or the fresh application includes the required technical assessments or proposals on drainage, traffic, visual, landscaping and environmental impacts. Same temporary ceiling: **up to a maximum period of 3 years**, subject to departmental comments and local objections being absent or addressed by conditions |
| **4** | Areas with ponds or wetland, or with extensive vegetation, or close to environmentally or ecologically sensitive areas; areas mostly used for residential purposes or proposed for them; areas near existing major village settlements; or areas subject to **extremely high flooding risk** | Applications would **normally be rejected** except under exceptional circumstances. A site with a previous approval may receive sympathetic consideration on the same compliance and technical-assessment tests as Category 3, subject to departmental comments and local objections. The intention is to **phase out** these non-conforming uses as early as possible. Permission for a **maximum period of 3 years** may be allowed so the applicant can identify a relocation site. A renewal is assessed on its individual merits |

**SD takeaway:** Lock the schematic to the category test in section 2.1: Category 1 is the zone-led suitable area, Category 2 is a temporary three-year case if flooding risk is not high, Category 3 only continues a previously approved site, and Category 4 is drawn to be rejected and phased out.

### 3. Adjacency, cross-boundary parking, and relocation (ss. 2.1(e), 2.2(a), 2.6)

| Test | Rule |
|---|---|
| Noisy, polluting or visually intrusive open storage | Port back-up sites, and open storage that generates adverse noise, air pollution and visual intrusion, should not be located adjacent to residential dwellings, hospitals, schools and other community facilities. The examples are dump sites, vehicle repair, scrap metal and vehicle breaking, and storage of wind-blown materials such as sand and cement, together with frequent heavy-vehicle traffic |
| Local-road access past sensitive receivers | Container storage/repair yards, container tractor/trailer parks and container freight stations would not be permitted unless traffic generation to and from the site is demonstrated to be acceptable |
| Cross-boundary parking | Taking account of demand for cross-boundary parking, such a use at suitable sites close to the boundary crossing points may also be considered. The example named in the prose is the **San Tin** area, particularly near the existing cross-boundary link in **Lok Ma Chau**. Notwithstanding the Category 3 and Category 4 tests in paragraphs 2.1(c) and (d), this kind of application is assessed on its own merits, including nature, scale and local circumstances, and on satisfactory demonstration of no adverse drainage, traffic, visual, landscaping and environmental impacts |
| Previous conditions not met | If there is no evidence of any genuine effort to comply with the conditions of a previous approval, the Board may refuse permission even where the other criteria in this guideline are met |
| Relocation after government resumption or clearance | Sympathetic consideration could be given, **except on Category 4 land, where only minor encroachment may be allowed**, if (i) the relevant bureau gives policy support for relocation of the affected use to the site and (ii) there are no adverse departmental comments and local objections, or those concerns can be addressed by approval conditions |
| Merits | Every application is still assessed on its own merits, including nature, scale and local circumstances |

**SD takeaway:** Keep container yards, trailer parks, dumps, vehicle breaking and wind-blown storage off the boundary of housing, schools, hospitals and community facilities, and do not rely on a local road past those receivers unless the traffic case is demonstrated.

### 4. Site planning and access (ss. 2.3–2.4)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Screening | Adequate screening by landscaping and/or fencing should be considered at the periphery, **inside the site boundary**, especially next to public roads or where the site is visible from surrounding residential areas |
| Buffer | The application should demonstrate no adverse impact on the amenity of surrounding sensitive receivers, and adequate buffering between those receivers and noise emitters such as container trailer/tractor parks and container storage/repair sites |
| Container stack, visual | For container storage/repair sites that would cause significant visual intrusion to surrounding or adjoining residential uses in rural areas, a **maximum stacking height restriction of 3 units** is recommended |
| Stack near the fence | For safety, the stacking height of materials stored **within 5 metres** of the periphery should not exceed the height of the boundary fence, if any |
| Port back-up access | Port back-up uses are major traffic generators. Container trailer/tractor parks generate the highest traffic per unit area. In general, port back-up sites should have good access to the strategic road network, or be accessed by purpose-built roads |
| Unpaved track | Sites accessed by **unpaved tracks should not be considered for port back-up uses** |
| Traffic impact assessment | Subject to Transport Department advice, a traffic impact assessment should be carried out for port back-up and open storage uses that generate substantial traffic. On local roads it must show that traffic does not interfere with sensitive receivers, that volumes do not exceed the capacity of the local road network, or that mitigation such as junction improvements is practical and effective |
| Gate | A clearly defined exit and entrance, **usually restricted to one**, unless a traffic impact assessment demonstrates that separate points are required, usually for large port back-up uses. The points must meet Transport Department requirements |
| On-site holding | Adequate parking and queuing for operations and visitors, and adequate manoeuvring for container-related and other vehicles, **within the curtilage**, so that parking and queuing do not occur on the street. State the type and number of vehicles visiting, and the length of stay |
| Sight line | Adequate setbacks from public roads to meet Transport Department sight-line requirements |

**SD takeaway:** Draw one gated entrance, on-site queuing and manoeuvring, a planted or fenced edge inside the boundary, container stacks held to 3 units where they face rural housing, and no stack within 5 metres of the periphery higher than the fence.

### 5. Environment, drainage, water and fire (s. 2.5)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance (Cap. 499) | Check Schedules 2 and 3 to determine whether the proposal is a Designated Project |
| Environmental code | Adopt the measures in the latest “Code of Practice on Handling the Environmental Aspects of Temporary Uses & Open Storage Sites” issued by the Environmental Protection Department |
| Major noise emitters | Container storage/repair sites and container trailer/tractor parks. Minimise impact by screening, mounding, protection by noise-tolerant buildings or structures, or by empty container boxes not subject to on-site activities, and/or by ensuring noise sources have **no line of sight** to noise-sensitive uses. A traffic noise impact assessment may be combined with the traffic impact assessment. Where necessary, noise modelling must show impacts within those recommended under the Noise Control Ordinance. Hours of operation may be specified |
| Other noise | A noise impact assessment may be needed for heavy machinery, manoeuvring of heavy vehicles, and workshop activities |
| Contamination and dust | Proper treatment and/or disposal of storage materials, wastes, oils and fuels, mostly for container storage/repair yards, container trailer/tractor parks, and dumping, breaking or repairing of motor vehicles and used electrical appliances/scrap metals. **Paving** helps avoid land and water contamination and reduces dust from vehicles, container handling and related operations. A technical assessment may be required |
| Water | Demand from temporary open storage and port back-up is presumed to be low. If water-intensive uses or operations are proposed, include a water impact assessment and mitigation |
| Agriculture and fish ponds | General presumption against conversion of active or good-quality agricultural land and fish ponds to other uses on an ad-hoc basis in rural areas |
| Flooding | For flood-prone areas (flood plains), or sites that would obstruct natural drainage channels and overland flow, seek Drainage Services Department advice if in doubt. Applications in such zones may need a drainage impact assessment and flood mitigation. Sites should have adequate drainage installations and proper discharge points of adequate capacity |
| Refuse | Adequate on-site provision for refuse and waste collection and disposal |
| Dangerous goods and waste | Refer to the Dangerous Goods Ordinance and the Waste Disposal Ordinance for storage, handling and licensing |
| Fire | The application should demonstrate that **all fire safety requirements have been met** |

**SD takeaway:** Pave the working yard, break the line of sight from container and trailer noise to sensitive uses, and put a drainage impact assessment on any flood-plain or channel-obstructing site before the layout is frozen.

### 6. New Development Areas (s. 3)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| When section 3 replaces paragraph 2.1 | For applications in New Development Areas (NDAs) with new or amended outline zoning plans gazetted, use paragraphs 3.2 to 3.4 **instead of** the Category 1 to 4 tests in paragraph 2.1 |
| Named areas | Kwu Tung North/Fanling North NDA, Hung Shui Kiu/Ha Tsuen NDA, and Yuen Long South Stages 1 and 2 Development, and future NDAs once the relevant new or amended outline zoning plans are gazetted |
| Continued operation | For an existing open storage or port back-up site with previous planning approval(s) and/or permitted under the previous outline zoning plans, sympathetic consideration may be given, by the same or a different applicant, **until the site is required for NDA implementation**, provided relevant approval conditions, if any, have been complied with. Technical assessments may be needed on drainage, traffic, visual, landscaping and environmental impacts |
| New uses | New open storage and port back-up uses are generally not encouraged to infiltrate the NDAs |
| Relocation into an NDA | Sympathetic consideration may be given for relocation of uses affected by government projects onto sites designated for development in the NDA, **including sites planned for government, institution and community uses and for open space**, as a temporary use before the site is required for NDA development. The tests are the policy-support and no-adverse-comment tests in paragraph 2.6(b) |
| Incompatible NDA sites | General presumption against such uses where there is a land-use compatibility issue, for example close to existing residential dwellings that may suffer environmental nuisance. Applications will normally be rejected unless under exceptional circumstances |
| Interim only | Any approved temporary use should not jeopardise the long-term NDA development on the site or the surrounding area. The site would be required by the Government at any time during the approval period for government projects |

**SD takeaway:** Inside a gazetted NDA, do not use the Category 1 to 4 map test; draw only a continued or relocation use that can stop when the site is taken for the NDA.

### 7. How long the permission and the conditions last (ss. 1.3, 4 and 5)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Temporary permission | The Board fixes the period on the merits. **In any event it would not exceed 3 years** |
| Normal condition clocks | Under normal circumstances, time allowed for **submission** of technical proposals is **6 months**, and for **implementation** **9 months**, from the date of approval |
| Extension of a condition | Assessed on individual merits. No extension if the applicant has shown no genuine effort. The Board may grant a shorter period than requested |
| Ceiling on a 3-year temporary approval | The maximum period for compliance with approval conditions is **normally 18 months**. Beyond that, no further extension is allowed and the planning approval will be revoked, unless under very exceptional circumstances |
| Renewal | If there has been no major change in planning circumstances since the last approval, new technical assessments are not required, only updates, provided previous conditions have been complied with and there are no adverse departmental comments and local objections. Procedures and criteria are in the Board’s Guidelines No. 34D, or its latest version, on renewal of planning approval and extension of time for compliance with planning conditions for temporary use or development |

**SD takeaway:** Programme condition compliance inside 6 months for submission and 9 months for implementation, and treat 18 months as the normal last date on a three-year open-storage or port back-up approval.

### 8. Drawings and compliance steps in the Guidance Notes (April 2023)

The same PDF continues, after the plans, as “Application for Permission for Temporary Open Storage and Port Back-up Uses under Section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance (Cap. 131) — Guidance Notes”, dated April 2023. It repeats the 3-year maximum and the prohibited zones in section 1. The drawing and compliance rules below are additional.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Village-track access | If access is via a village track, the plan must cover the **entire** track from the site to the local feeder road, and that feeder should be **at least up to single two-lane road standard**. Submit photographs of the entire track showing cross-section, sightline and pavement condition |
| Use details | Operation hours, type and stacking height of stored materials, number of parking spaces for stored vehicles or container tractors/trailers, industrial or mechanical processes, vehicular access, and vehicular trips. Details of any structure to be erected |
| Drainage | Drainage proposals for earth filling, hard paving and structures, showing how rainwater falling on or flowing to the site will be collected, conveyed and discharged. A drainage impact assessment may be required in flood-prone areas such as low-lying areas and flooding blackspots, or where the site is adjacent to or encompasses a major stream, channel or river. The assessment must show that, with mitigation, the use will not cause an unacceptable increase in flood risk upstream, adjacent or downstream |
| Landscape | Show impacts on existing landscape resources and proposed tree treatments; species, size, spacing, quantity and location of planting. **Planting in movable containers or pots will not be accepted.** Mature trees in good health and structural condition should be preserved on site as far as practicable |
| Noise and air | Noise impact assessment may be required for heavy machinery, heavy-vehicle manoeuvring and workshops, with mitigation proposals. An air-quality proposal must show that the latest environmental code of practice for temporary uses and open storage sites will be followed |
| Traffic | Subject to Transport Department advice, a traffic impact assessment for uses generating substantial traffic, in particular a container trailer/tractor park, including swept-path analyses of the appropriate goods vehicles at critical bends and junctions where appropriate. Where residential or school developments are nearby, provide pedestrian counts from pedestrian surveys |
| When to lodge accepted proposals | For a normal case with a **6-month** compliance period, submit the proposals **at least 6 weeks** before that period expires. The District Planning Office circulates them and informs the applicant **within 6 weeks** of receipt whether they are acceptable. Revised proposals must still be accepted inside the compliance period |
| When works must be finished | Once proposals are accepted, complete **all works at least 6 weeks** before the specified time limit expires. Works that do not need a submitted proposal start once the application is approved. After notification of completion, inspection and a decision follow **within 6 weeks**. Completed works, landscape and mitigation must be maintained |
| Reinstatement condition | Where imposed, reinstatement to an amenity area on expiry usually means demolition of the structure or shelter, removal of hard paving, and restoration of amenity, for example grassing the site |
| More time | An extension application should be submitted **no less than 6 weeks** before the time limit, with the technical proposals so far submitted, an account of works done, and a justification. Assessment criteria are in the renewal and extension-of-time guidelines |
| Missed conditions | The permission is **revoked automatically** without further notice. Continuation is an unauthorised development. A fresh section 16 application is required, normally with technical assessments or proposals on traffic, landscape, drainage and environmental mitigation, and without major adverse departmental comments |
| “Existing use” | Claims of “existing use” are outside the purview of the Board and the Town Planning Appeal Board |

**SD takeaway:** If the yard is reached by a village track, the schematic must show that track continuously to a feeder of at least single two-lane standard, with in-ground planting rather than pots, and with drainage that does not raise flood risk off the site.
