# TPB PG-No. 43A Transfer of Plot Ratio
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
August 2026 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Requirements for one section 16 application that transfers gross floor area from one or more sending sites to one or more receiving sites. The scheme began as a July 2023 pilot in Mong Kok and Yau Ma Tei. This August 2026 set is the refined scheme. It does not itself fix an outline zoning plan plot ratio, and it does not authorise a transfer without that planning application, the lease and the building-plan path.

## Regulatory Overview
Transfer of plot ratio moves gross floor area from a sending site to a receiving site by raising the maximum permissible plot ratio or gross floor area of each receiving site by up to 30 percent, with a minor exceedance of that cap only where the applicant justifies it to the Board. Both ends of the transfer must sit in a single section 16 application and be carried out as one project.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the transfer is (ss. 1.3–1.7)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Objectives (para. 1.3) | (a) Move development potential from small, isolated sites occupied by ageing dilapidated residential buildings with low redevelopment potential onto larger sites more suitable for higher intensity, so as to incentivise demolition and redevelopment. (b) Restructure and replan the old urban area and bring planning gains, for example public-realm improvements, additional public spaces and community facilities, and preservation of buildings with conservation value |
| Definition (para. 1.4) | Transfer of gross floor area from sending site(s) to receiving site(s) by increasing the maximum permissible plot ratio/gross floor area of **each** receiving site by **up to a maximum of 30%** (the 30% cap), so as to accommodate the gross floor area transferred from the sending site. The basis is paragraphs 4.1 to 4.5 |
| More than one site | Each section 16 application may involve **more than one** sending site, and **more than one** receiving site, so that all the transferable gross floor area is accommodated |
| Minor exceedance | A minor exceedance of the 30% cap at each receiving site may be allowed, to give flexibility in pairing sites, **subject to the applicant’s justifications**. The guideline states no separate percentage for that exceedance |
| One application (para. 1.5) | A **single** section 16 application must cover the sending site(s) and the receiving site(s). Where the sites fall under different outline zoning plans, the application shall be made in accordance with the provisions of the respective plan (footnote 2) |
| What the application may include | One or more of: (a) relaxation or minor relaxation of the plot ratio/gross floor area of each receiving site to absorb the transferred gross floor area; (b) relaxation or minor relaxation of other restrictions on each receiving site, such as building height and/or site coverage, to absorb the transferred gross floor area; and/or (c) a Column 2 use that is an integral part of the proposed development, for instance the residential component of a mixed-use development in a commercial-based zoning |
| No plot ratio on the receiving site’s plan (para. 1.6) | The permissible **domestic** plot ratio under the First Schedule of the Building (Planning) Regulations is the basis for the 30% cap. Detail is in paragraph 4.5. Permission under paragraph 1.5(b) and/or (c) may still be required |
| Technical case (para. 1.7) | The proposal must be technically feasible without overstraining infrastructural capacity, and without unacceptable environmental, visual and air-ventilation impacts. Conditions may cover the receiving site and/or the sending site |
| Site area that does not count (footnote 1) | Land not intended for building development and carrying no development right under the lease, such as non-building areas or land reserved for garden purposes, shall not be counted towards the site area for the maximum transferable gross floor area of the sending site or for the 30% cap of the receiving site |

**SD takeaway:** Freeze the receiving-site envelope at the outline-zoning-plan maximum plus up to 30 percent, and put every sending site and receiving site into the same section 16 application.

### 2. Sending-site eligibility (s. 2)

A sending site must meet **both** geography and occupation.

| Test | Rule |
|---|---|
| Geography (para. 2.1(a)) | Inside the planning scheme area of the **Sai Ying Pun and Sheung Wan, Wan Chai, Ma Tau Kok, Mong Kok, Yau Ma Tei, Cheung Sha Wan or Tsuen Wan** outline zoning plan. Footnote 3: these are the seven designated areas in the Schedule to the Land (Compulsory Sale for Redevelopment) (Specification of Lower Percentage) Notice (Cap. 545A) |
| Occupation (para. 2.1(b)) | Irrespective of zoning, at the time of the application the site is occupied by (i) or (ii) below |
| (i) Age and use | Building(s) with a residential element and **aged 50 or above** |
| Building with residential element (footnote 4) | A building wholly or partly constructed for, or intended to be used for, domestic purpose as approved by the Building Authority, **including composite buildings**, and **excluding** (i) hotels and guesthouses; (ii) residential facilities managed or operated by an organisation to house persons who meet eligibility criteria, for example boarding houses, dormitories, residential institutions, staff quarters, student hostels, residential care homes for the elderly or for persons with disabilities, and similar accommodation; and (iii) New Territories Exempted Houses. The Occupation Permit is the main reference for use and age. If approved building plans and/or an Occupation Permit are not available, an Authorized Person must demonstrate the use and/or age |
| (ii) Conservation, any age and use | Building(s) worthy of conservation, irrespective of age and use. Footnote 5: historic buildings with **actual grading (not nil grading)** confirmed by the Antiquities Advisory Board. A building without that grading may be eligible if its heritage value is supported by a **district-based** study, not an individual site-based study, and agreed by the relevant authorities. The examples named are the District Study for Yau Ma Tei and Mong Kok and similar Urban Renewal Authority district studies. Footnote 6 disapplies the age and residential-element tests for these buildings. The objective is conservation of privately owned residential, commercial or mixed-use historic buildings. Private historic buildings used for specialised purposes, such as schools, religious institutions, hospitals, private clubs, and public utility installations, are generally not eligible. The Board may still consider individual merits |
| Not accepted as a sending site | A heritage building that has already benefited from development-cum-conservation support, with the development already materialised. The example in footnote 7 is **8 Waterloo Road**, zoned “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Residential Development with Historical Building Preserved” on the Yau Ma Tei outline zoning plan. Also a site zoned purely for heritage preservation with no additional new development envisaged. The example in footnote 8 is **618 Shanghai Street**, zoned “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Historic Buildings Preserved for Commercial and/or Cultural Uses” on the Mong Kok outline zoning plan |
| Favourable points for a paragraph 2.1(b)(i) site (para. 2.2) | (a) Redevelopment on its own would be a pencil-type development, not conducive to an efficient layout, modern building-code compliance, and car parking and amenities. (b) The site would be unlikely to be amalgamated because it is sandwiched between new buildings **aged 30 years or less**, or buildings with no or low redevelopment potential. (c) Existing excessive intensity, for example a building approved under the “Volume Regulation” of the then Building (Planning) Regulations, so that in-situ redevelopment up to the existing bulk is infeasible. (d) Other justifiable reasons that redevelopment on its own is not viable |

**SD takeaway:** A sending site is one of the seven named outline zoning plans, occupied by a non-hotel residential or composite building aged 50 or above, or by a graded or district-study conservation building, and it should be a pencil site, a sandwiched site, or a Volume Regulation bulk that cannot be rebuilt in place.

### 3. Receiving-site eligibility (s. 3)

| Test | Rule |
|---|---|
| Geography (para. 3.1) | Any outline zoning plan in the territory, except paragraphs 3.2 and 3.3 |
| Minimum private land (para. 3.1(a)) | Site area comprising private land of **not less than 700 m²**. Footnote 9: this size is to secure an efficient layout, modern codes, car parking and amenities. A slightly smaller site may be considered on individual merit with strong justifications. The guideline states no lower figure. **Government land is not counted** towards the 700 m², and does not form the basis of the 30% cap, unless justified and assessed case by case |
| Zoning (para. 3.1(b)) | Development zones primarily intended for general residential, commercial, business, industrial or industry-related uses |
| Zones that do not qualify (footnote 10) | Zones with another primary intention, for example “Village Type Development” and “Government, Institution or Community”, and non-development zones, for example “Agriculture”, “Green Belt” and “Conservation Area”, except residual portions included with strong justifications to rationalise the boundary, and only where the outline zoning plan provides for the proposed use with or without an application. That residual area **shall not** form the basis of the 30% cap, except a minor discrepancy between the zoning boundary and the development lot, in which case the **development-lot area** is adopted |
| Existing buildings on the receiving site (footnote 10) | In most cases they shall be demolished and redeveloped as part of the scheme. A building with conservation value as defined in footnote 5 may be retained |
| Same district council (para. 3.2) | Where the receiving site is **not** subject to plot ratio/gross floor area control on the outline zoning plan, **and/or** that control is **equivalent to the maximum permissible plot ratio under the Building (Planning) Regulations**, the receiving site must fall within the **same district**, meaning the coverage of the **same district council**, as the sending site. Footnote 11 examples of this intensity: “Residential (Group A)”, “Commercial” and “Other Specified Uses” annotated “Mixed Use” on Hong Kong Island; the “Commercial” zones along Nathan Road in Mong Kok and Yau Ma Tei; and sites whose plan control equals the Building (Planning) Regulations maximum, such as the “Commercial” zone in Quarry Bay and the “Residential (Group A)” zone in Shau Kei Wan |
| Low-density presumption (para. 3.3) | General presumption against receiving sites in well-recognised low-density areas with a distinct built character. The list is not exhaustive. Examples named: **Kowloon Tong, Kadoorie Hill, the Peak, Shouson Hill, Shek O, South Lantau Island, and sparsely populated outlying islands** |

**SD takeaway:** The receiving site needs at least 700 m² of private land in a general residential, commercial, business or industrial zone, stays out of the named low-density areas, and stays in the sending site’s district council when the plan has no plot ratio or a plot ratio already equal to the Building (Planning) Regulations maximum.

### 4. How much gross floor area can leave the sending site (ss. 4.1–4.3)

| Case | Transferable gross floor area |
|---|---|
| Plan states a plot ratio or gross floor area (para. 4.1) | The maximum in the Notes, **or** the plot ratio/gross floor area of the existing building, **whichever is the greater**. An Authorized Person confirms any claim of existing plot ratio/gross floor area before submission |
| What is ignored in an existing-bulk claim (footnote 13) | Unauthorised structures are not counted. Facilities, features and floor area already exempted from plot ratio/gross floor area under the Buildings Ordinance or the Building (Planning) Regulations are disregarded. For a “Volume Regulation” building, the facilities, features and floor space that could be exempted under the **prevailing** Buildings Ordinance and Building (Planning) Regulations are disregarded. A claim is generally allowed if the Notes allow redevelopment to the existing intensity |
| Composite formula on the sending site (footnote 12) | Where the maximum plot ratio is set by a composite formula, the example given is “Residential (Group A)” on the **Tsuen Wan** outline zoning plan, adopt the **maximum domestic plot ratio**, because the zone is primarily for residential use |
| Plan states no plot ratio or gross floor area (para. 4.2) | The applicant proposes the potentially achievable intensity: the applicable permissible **domestic** plot ratio under the Building (Planning) Regulations, subject to other restrictions on the plan such as building height and site coverage, **or** the existing-building gross floor area if that is greater. An Authorized Person prepares a hypothetical scheme and confirms the figure against the Building (Planning) Regulations and the plan before submission. Footnote 14: this principle applies to sending sites in zones primarily for general residential, commercial, business, industrial or industry-related use. In a non-development zone, or a zone not intended for those uses, for example “Government, Institution or Community” and “Open Space”, the applicant proposes the maximum transferable gross floor area case by case, taking this principle into account |
| Full transfer (para. 4.3) | In most cases the maximum transferable gross floor area determined under paragraph 4.1 or 4.2 is transferred **in full**. The exception for a partial transfer is paragraph 5.2 |

Land-premium-free bonus gross floor area may be retained or built on the sending site as part of the proposal:

| Bonus | Amount and conditions |
|---|---|
| Public space (para. 4.3(a)) | If the sending site is developed into public space for public enjoyment, and the public can gain reasonable access, bonus **commercial** gross floor area equivalent to **plot ratio 0.4 of the sending site or 200 m², whichever is the less**, in supporting uses such as retail, eating place or other commercial uses, **excluding any form of residential use**. It is to fund long-term management and maintenance and to add vibrancy |
| Graded historic building on the sending site (para. 4.3(b)) | If a graded historic building is conserved and the public can gain reasonable access to part or the whole, then **after** the gross floor area of that existing graded building is transferred to the receiving site, gross floor area **equivalent to that of the existing graded historic building** may be provided as bonus gross floor area on the sending site, with the support of the Commissioner for Heritage |
| Same bonus on the receiving site (footnote 15) | Similar bonus treatment applies to the receiving site for conservation of a graded historic building there |
| Not yet graded (footnote 15) | A building not yet graded by the Antiquities Advisory Board, but shown worthy of conservation by a district-based study under footnote 5, may be retained on the sending site and **is accountable for gross floor area**. The transferable gross floor area is reduced by deduction of that existing building. This is not the graded-building bonus |
| Government, institution or community, or socially beneficial facilities (para. 4.3(c)) | If provided on the sending site with the support of the relevant bureaux or departments, the gross floor area of those facilities may be treated as bonus. Footnote 16 examples of socially beneficial facilities: community living room, makerspace, and senior citizens’ learning centre. Footnote 17: this is largely for plans whose Notes do not already require specific facilities and disregard their gross floor area. If the Notes expressly require specific facilities **without** a remark disregarding that gross floor area, **this bonus shall not apply** |
| Premium (footnote 26) | The land-premium-free bonus under paragraph 4.3 is not taken into account in the land-premium valuation for the sending site |

**SD takeaway:** Transfer the greater of the plan maximum and the existing accountable bulk, and if the sending site becomes public open space keep only the lesser of plot ratio 0.4 and 200 m² as non-residential supporting floor.

### 5. The 30% cap on the receiving site (ss. 4.4–4.6)

| Case | Base of the 30% |
|---|---|
| Plan states a plot ratio or gross floor area (para. 4.4) | A **30% increase from the highest** plot ratio/gross floor area restriction in the Notes |
| More than one control (footnote 18) | Where there is a maximum domestic figure, a maximum non-domestic figure, and/or a total, use the **total** (the highest control), regardless of the use applied for |
| Composite-formula exception (footnote 18) | Where the maximum is a composite formula, the example is “Residential (Group A)” on the **Kwai Chung** outline zoning plan, adopt the **maximum domestic plot ratio** for the 30% cap, regardless of the use applied for. This matches footnote 12 for the sending site |
| Grandfathered existing bulk (para. 4.4) | If the existing building’s gross floor area **exceeds** the highest restriction in the Notes, that exceedance may stay on the **same** receiving site and **need not be counted towards the 30% cap**. An existing-bulk claim follows paragraph 4.1 and footnote 13 |
| Plan states no plot ratio or gross floor area (para. 4.5) | The applicable permissible **domestic** plot ratio under the First Schedule, subject to other plan restrictions such as building height and site coverage, is the basis of the 30% cap, **regardless of the type of redevelopment**. Even a purely non-domestic building uses that domestic plot ratio for the **30% cap portion**. The as-of-right portion of that non-domestic building continues to follow the applicable permissible **non-domestic** plot ratio under the First Schedule. The applicant demonstrates this to the Board. Paragraph 3.2 (same district council) also applies |
| Other intensification plus this transfer (para. 4.6) | A sending site and a receiving site may also use other Government intensification measures. Any additional gross floor area from those measures may be added only if the **overall** development, being gross floor area transferred under this scheme **plus** that additional gross floor area, **does not exceed the 30% cap** |
| The two measures named (footnote 19) | Relaxation of the maximum permitted non-domestic plot ratio by **up to 20%** under the Revitalisation Scheme for Industrial Buildings, and bonus plot ratio of **up to 20%** under the Bonus Plot Ratio Pilot Scheme (Joint Practice Note No. 9), for in-situ redevelopment meeting the criteria of those measures |
| Outside the 30% cap (para. 4.6) | Other bonus or concessionary gross floor area under the buildings regime, such as bonus plot ratio under **regulation 22** of the Building (Planning) Regulations and gross floor area concession for the Sustainable Building Design Guidelines, **will not be counted towards the 30% cap** |

**SD takeaway:** Calculate the receiving-site uplift as 30 percent of the plan’s highest plot ratio, or of the domestic plot ratio where the control is a composite formula or the plan is silent, and put any Joint Practice Note No. 9 or industrial-building uplift inside that same 30 percent.

### 6. Pairing, partial rebuild, and domestic / non-domestic interchange (s. 5)

| Rule | Text |
|---|---|
| Zoning and plan (para. 5.1) | Sending and receiving sites need not share a zone and may lie on different outline zoning plans. There is **no restriction on the number** of sending or receiving sites, provided there is a single section 16 application implemented as **one project** |
| Apportionment | The application must state how the transfer works among the sites, the apportionment from the sending sites and among the receiving sites, and the management and maintenance responsibility for any public space or public facilities. This is to allow later general building plans and lease modification or land exchange |
| No unexpended balance (para. 5.2) | No unexpended gross floor area can be retained for a future planning application. Footnote 20: a site may be the subject of more than one approved transfer application. The applicant may choose which approval to pursue, as long as lease modification or land exchange for the sending and receiving sites has not yet been executed |
| Partial transfer | The applicant may redevelop the sending site so that the gross floor area is fully used. Examples: an excessively intense existing building under paragraph 2.2(c), or a full transfer that would considerably exceed the receiving site’s 30% cap. Transfer **in part**, and redevelopment of the sending site into a new building with the **remaining** gross floor area, compliant with the permissible plot ratio/gross floor area of the plan, may be accepted |
| New building on the sending site (footnote 21) | It may be built **up to or lower than** the maximum plot ratio/gross floor area in the Notes. Redevelopment **up to the plot ratio of the existing building is not allowed**, even if the plan would otherwise allow the existing intensity. The new building is not intended to exceed the prevailing plan restrictions. Redevelopment together with bonus plot ratio from the measures in footnote 19 may be allowed even if the result exceeds the plan restriction, case by case |
| Interchange (para. 5.3) | Domestic and non-domestic gross floor area may be interchanged on transfer. The examples are permissible non-domestic gross floor area from a sending site used for residential development on the receiving site, or the reverse. The applicant justifies compatibility with the planning intention and surrounding uses, and technical feasibility |

**SD takeaway:** Spend the whole transferable gross floor area in this application, either on the receiving site or in a new sending-site building that stops at the current plan maximum, and state whether the transferred floor changes between domestic and non-domestic.

### 7. Graded buildings and conservation (paras. 2.1(b)(ii), 4.3(b); footnotes 5 to 8 and 15)

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Actual Antiquities Advisory Board grading, not nil | Eligible as a sending-site building regardless of age and use, subject to footnote 6 on specialised private uses and to the two exclusions below |
| District-based study, not yet graded | May be eligible as a sending site if the authorities agree. If the building is **retained** on the sending site, its existing gross floor area is deducted from what can be transferred. It does not receive the graded-building bonus |
| Graded building conserved on the sending site, with reasonable public access | Its existing gross floor area is transferred, and an equivalent gross floor area may come back as bonus on the sending site with the Commissioner for Heritage’s support |
| Graded building conserved on the receiving site | Similar bonus treatment (footnote 15) |
| Already used a Government development-cum-conservation scheme, and that development has materialised | Not accepted as a sending site. Example: 8 Waterloo Road |
| Zoned only for preservation, with no additional new development envisaged | Not accepted as a sending site. Example: 618 Shanghai Street |
| Conservation building on the receiving site | May be retained (footnote 10). Other existing buildings on the receiving site are, in most cases, demolished and redeveloped |
| Submission (para. 8.2 and footnote 24) | Preservation and conservation management proposals covering historical and cultural background, heritage significance, heritage fabrics and character-defining elements, mitigation such as in-situ preservation and photographic or 3D scanning records, adaptive re-use and public appreciation, and future management and maintenance |

**SD takeaway:** A graded building can send its plan-based gross floor area away and keep an equal bonus on the sending site if the public can enter; an ungraded district-study building that stays on the sending site reduces the gross floor area that can be transferred.

### 8. Planning merits (s. 6)

The application is assessed as a whole. Achieving paragraph 1.3, in particular redevelopment of ageing dilapidated residential buildings with low redevelopment potential and preservation of buildings with conservation value, is generally a planning gain, provided the sending and receiving proposals together have no unacceptable adverse impacts.

Favourable consideration is also given where the two ends together show one or more of the following (para. 6.2):

| | Merit |
|---|---|
| (a) | Complementary with the master renewal plan promulgated by the Urban Renewal Authority. Footnote 22 points to the Master Renewal Concept Plan Framework for Yau Ma Tei and Mong Kok in Diagram 3.2 of that study’s Information Booklet. The Authority is preparing similar plans for Sham Shui Po and Tsuen Wan |
| (b) | Socially beneficial facilities of the applicant’s choice |
| (c) | Community facilities or public open space that are in shortfall, with support from the departments that will monitor compliance |
| (d) | Linkage or extension of an existing or planned open-space network |
| (e) | Better access to, or synergy with, points of interest and/or a major pedestrian destination |
| (f) | Opportunity for vehicular and pedestrian traffic improvement |
| (g) | Better streetscape or good-quality street-level public space, for example setback and canopy |
| (h) | Better air ventilation, for example a breezeway or wind corridor to inner areas |
| (i) | Opportunity to unblock an important vista |
| (j) | Urban design measures in the Urban Design Guidelines of the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines |
| (k) | Greening and design measures in the Sustainable Building Design Guidelines |
| (l) | Any other justifiable planning gain |

**SD takeaway:** Pair the transfer with a demonstrable gain on the sending site, such as removal of a dilapidated residential building, a graded building opened to the public, or a setback, canopy and shortfall open space that departments will monitor.

### 9. Ownership, validity, lease and premium (ss. 7 and 9)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Government land (para. 7.1) | May be included, for example residual Government land incapable of separate alienation. Subject to footnote 9 and to completion of lease modification or land exchange, such land within a sending or receiving site may be regarded as site area **subject to land premium** and integrated into the proposal. Inclusion depends on the scheme the Board approves and on the **final decision of the Land Authority** |
| Consent (para. 7.2) | Full ownership is not a prerequisite for making the application or for the Board granting permission. On submission, the applicant must obtain owners’ consent or notify the owners of the sending and receiving sites in accordance with the Ordinance |
| Who can build the transfer (para. 7.3) | Land not owned by the proponent is not entitled to effect the transfer, even if the Board agrees the application. Joint ownership is allowed, including lots held in the separate ownership of joint-venture partners. Footnote 23: the lease-modification application is submitted by **all** lot owners |
| Validity (para. 9.1) | A **longer validity period** could be considered case by case, depending on the complexity of the scheme. The guideline states no fixed number of years. Conditions are imposed so that planning gains are realised in time, in particular socially beneficial facilities of the applicant’s choice, public facilities, or preservation works on the sending site |
| Building plans held to the lease (para. 9.1) | A condition may require execution of lease modification and/or land exchange of the sending and receiving sites **before** approval of general building plans for the proposed development on the receiving site under the Buildings Ordinance |
| Above the Building (Planning) Regulations plot ratio (footnote 25) | Seek a Building Authority modification under **section 42** of the Buildings Ordinance. The Building Authority will favourably consider that modification in light of the Board’s acceptance. General building plans must expressly indicate the amount of gross floor area derived from the sending site and the location of that sending site |
| Completion tie (para. 9.2) | Lease modification or land exchange, and the necessary building-plan approval and building works, are required for both sites. The Land Authority decides whether the instrument is a modification, an exchange, or another land document. Pre-sale consent, or a certificate of compliance, or consent to assign, as appropriate, for the **new development on the receiving site** will be tied to completion of the public facilities and/or preservation works on the sending and receiving sites, if applicable. Ongoing management may be tied to the commercial portion of the receiving site so that the duty is not passed to individual flat owners |
| Combined premium (para. 9.3) | Premium for the sending site(s) and receiving site(s) may be assessed **together** even if the land transactions are separate. The premium is the difference between the total value of the sending site(s) and receiving site(s) under the **original** lease conditions and the total value of the receiving site(s), **including the transferred gross floor area**, and the sending site(s), **excluding the bonus gross floor area, if any**, under the modified lease conditions. The before-and-after values are offset against each other, even if separate instruments are executed at the same time |
| What the premium excludes | Footnote 26: the paragraph 4.3 bonus is not taken into account in the land-premium valuation for the sending site |

**SD takeaway:** Do not submit general building plans for the receiving site until the lease modification or exchange of both sites is in place, and show the transferred gross floor area and the sending-site location as a separate line in the plot-ratio calculation.

### 10. What the layout plans must contain (s. 8)

Layout plan(s) for the receiving site and the sending site must set out all of the following (para. 8.1):

| On the drawing or in the submission | Content |
|---|---|
| Uses | Proposed land uses |
| Bulk | Development parameters of all buildings |
| Public content | Proposed community, recreational and socially beneficial facilities, transport facilities, and public spaces |
| Design | Landscaping and urban design proposals |
| Context | Existing conditions; the character of the sites in relation to the surrounding areas; design population |
| Title | Land ownership records from the Land Registry |
| Programme | An implementation programme covering both the sending site and the receiving site for the duration of the development |
| Proof | Assessments showing that both ends are technically feasible and would not cause unacceptable adverse impact on transport and other infrastructure, the surrounding environment, visual aspects, air ventilation, and other technical aspects |
| Where the plan has no plot ratio | An Authorized Person’s information for the transferable gross floor area of the sending site and the permissible domestic plot ratio under the Building (Planning) Regulations for the receiving site |
| Other Government bonus | If paragraph 4.6 floor is included, a clear statement of the proposed bonus gross floor area or plot ratio under the relevant scheme |
| Preservation | The conservation management proposals in paragraph 8.2 |
| Public space | Long-term management and maintenance arrangements for public space or public open space in private developments |
| Handover | The applicant is generally responsible for implementation and long-term management, and for public access under paragraph 4.3, unless departments agree to take over the facilities on completion of the sending site’s lease modification. Facilities intended to be handed to the Government must be discussed with the departments **before** submission, including who monitors construction and who takes over management. If a commercial element on the receiving site and/or sending site is to fund operation and maintenance, that arrangement must be stated |

Appendix A is a checklist of the same site, merit and submission tests. It says it is not exhaustive and that the applicant should follow the main text.

**SD takeaway:** The section 16 set is a paired layout of both sites, with parameters, design population, ownership, a joint implementation programme, and a plot-ratio line that separates as-of-right floor, transferred floor and any paragraph 4.3 bonus.

### 11. Worked examples in Appendices B to H

These pages illustrate the paragraphs above. The plot ratios, site areas and resulting gross floor areas are the figures printed on the examples. They are not a territory-wide plot ratio. Several examples assume a “Residential (Group A)” control of maximum domestic plot ratio 8.5 and total plot ratio 9, and they apply the **total** of 9, which is the highest-control rule in footnote 18, not the composite-formula exception.

**Appendix B — full transfer, plan control on both sites, public-space bonus.** Both sites “Residential (Group A)”, maximum domestic/total plot ratio 8.5/9. Sending site **200 m²**, residential element, aged 50 or above. Maximum transferable gross floor area **1,800 m²** (9 × 200 m²), transferred in full. Remaining gross floor area on the sending site **0 m²**. Bonus **80 m²** (plot ratio 0.4). The sending site is to be rezoned to “O” when the opportunity arises. Receiving site **750 m²**. Plan maximum **6,750 m²** (9 × 750 m²). 30% cap **2,025 m²** (6,750 m² × 30%). Total on the receiving site **8,550 m²** (plot ratio 11.4) = 6,750 m² + 1,800 m². The 1,800 m² sits inside the 2,025 m² cap.

**Appendix C — partial transfer of an excessive existing bulk.** Sending site **400 m²**, existing total plot ratio **14**, residential element, aged 50 or above, on the same 8.5/9 “Residential (Group A)” control. Plan maximum on the sending site **3,600 m²** (plot ratio 9). Excessive gross floor area (plot ratio 14 − 9 = 5) **2,000 m²**. Maximum transferable **5,600 m²** (14 × 400 m²). **2,000 m²** is transferred. **3,600 m²** is redeveloped on the sending site as a new building compliant with the plan. Receiving site **750 m²**, plan maximum **6,750 m²**, 30% cap **2,025 m²**, total **8,750 m²** (plot ratio 11.7) = 6,750 m² + 2,000 m².

**Appendix D — sending site with no plot ratio, only a building-height restriction.** Sending site zoned “C”, **no** plot ratio/gross floor area restriction, building height **140 mPD**, site **200 m²**. The example classifies the site as Class C and states a maximum Building (Planning) Regulations domestic plot ratio of **10**, demonstrated by a hypothetical scheme that takes account of the height restriction. Maximum transferable **2,000 m²** (10 × 200 m²), transferred in full. Remaining gross floor area **0 m²**.
