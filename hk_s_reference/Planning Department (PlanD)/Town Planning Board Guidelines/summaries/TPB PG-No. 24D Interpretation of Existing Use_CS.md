# TPB PG-No. 24D Interpretation of Existing Use
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
SEPTEMBER 2023 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: The Board’s intention on “existing use” in the Covering Notes of outline zoning plans for the urban and new town areas, and its approach in the particular situations in section 4. The intention on existing use is not applicable to a regulated-area portion of an outline zoning plan. The Planning Department and the Board do not prove the claim; the person who claims an existing-use right will need to provide sufficient evidence. Any use allowed by a plan must also conform to any other relevant legislation and the conditions of the Government lease.

## Regulatory Overview
The Covering Notes provide that no action is required to make the existing use of any land or building conform to a statutory plan until there is a material change of use or the building is redeveloped. The intention is to allow an owner whose land or building is affected by a new land-use zoning restriction to continue that use until there is a change of use or the building is redeveloped.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the Covering Notes mean by existing use (paras 3.1–3.3)

The Covering Notes provide that “existing use of any land or building” means:

| When | What is an existing use |
|---|---|
| Before the publication in the Gazette of the notice of the first statutory plan covering the land or building | A use in existence before the publication of the first plan which has continued since it came into existence; or a use or a change of use approved under the Buildings Ordinance which relates to an existing building |
| After the publication of the first plan | A use permitted under a plan which was effected during the effective period of that plan and has continued since it was effected; or a use or a change of use approved under the Buildings Ordinance which relates to an existing building and permitted under a plan prevailing at the time when the use or change of use was approved |

“Existing building” means a building, including a structure, which is physically existing and is in compliance with any relevant legislation, the conditions of the Government lease concerned, and any other Government requirements, as may be applicable.

Generally, the use of any land or building which was in existence when a new statutory plan or a new land-use zoning restriction came into effect will be regarded as the existing use, and will be allowed to continue whether or not it conforms. If the use was effected (came into existence) when a statutory plan was in force, it **must** be a use permitted under that statutory plan before the existing-use right can be claimed.

Under section 21A of the Town Planning Ordinance as amended in 2023, the Secretary for Development is empowered to designate areas within outline zoning plans in the New Territories, previously not covered by Development Permission Area plans, as regulated areas. Where an outline zoning plan involves a regulated area, the Covering Notes comprise Part A and Part B. The Board’s intention on existing use is not applicable to the regulated-area portion.

**SD takeaway:** An existing-use claim is the use that has continued since it came into existence, or a Buildings Ordinance use of a building that is physically existing and compliant, and that claim stops at a regulated-area portion.

### 2. Approved building use, and when the right is forfeited (paras 3.4–3.5)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Same broad use | The Board allows a change from one use to another within the same broad use, for example retail shop to fast food shop, both within ‘Shop and Services’ |
| Building plans, occupation permit, section 25 | The owner may continue the actual use in existence when the new restriction is imposed, and also the use or uses designated on the building plans or the occupation permit approved or issued under the Buildings Ordinance, or any subsequent change of use approved under section 25 of the Buildings Ordinance, provided that such use should be a use permitted under the prevailing statutory plan, if any, when the building use was approved |
| Proposed building | This right applies only to a use or a change of use approved under the Buildings Ordinance which relates to an existing building. Uses shown on approved building plans for a proposed building are not regarded as existing uses |
| Forfeiture | The existing-use right will be forfeited upon a change of use of the land, or a change of use of the building outside the scope approved under the Buildings Ordinance, or when the building is redeveloped. Any change of use and any other development or redevelopment should conform to the extant statutory plan |
| Minor works | Minor alteration works, including maintenance works, are allowed provided they do not constitute a change of use or give rise to any planning implication. Renovation of the external facade, or alteration of the internal layout, without any change of use will be permitted and no planning permission is required |

**SD takeaway:** Drawings for a proposed building do not create an existing use; facade or internal alteration keeps the right only while there is no change of use.

### 3. Examples in section 4 (paras 4.1.1–4.3)

| Situation | Board’s approach |
|---|---|
| Use in existence before any statutory plan (para 4.1.1) | A non-conforming use may continue until a change of use or redevelopment. A long-established school in a pure residential neighbourhood zoned “Residential (Group C)” will be tolerated until the school building is put to some other uses or the building is redeveloped |
| Approved building use before the first plan (para 4.1.2) | The owner may continue the use designated on the approved building plans or occupation permit, or approved under section 25 of the Buildings Ordinance, until that approved building use changes or the building is redeveloped. A building approved for non-domestic use before the first plan will be allowed for that purpose even if it later falls within a “Residential (Group C)” zone |
| Use permitted on a previous version of the plan and effected during that plan (para 4.1.3) | The owner may continue a use which was permitted under the previous plan and effected during its effective period, provided the use has continued since it was effected |
| Buildings Ordinance use approved while a plan was in force (para 4.1.4) | Allowed so long as it conforms to the zoning on the statutory plan which applied when the use was approved. The Board takes into account both the approved building use and the permitted uses under that plan. Where the approved building use of premises previously zoned “Residential (Group A)” is non-domestic, and the premises are later rezoned “Residential (Group C)” for pure domestic use, the owner may use the premises **only** for those non-domestic uses which were permitted under the previous “Residential (Group A)” zone, such as bank, shop and restaurant. Commercial bathhouse and massage establishment, which fall outside the uses permitted under that previous zone, will not be allowed. Permitted uses of a zone are read from the Notes of the respective statutory plan |

The examples only illustrate particular situations in which uses may continue despite a change in zoning. Other cases will be considered on their own facts. It is not for the Planning Department or the Board to provide evidence. Any person who intends to claim an existing-use right will need to provide sufficient evidence to support the claim.

**SD takeaway:** Where the approved building use was non-domestic under a previous “Residential (Group A)” zone, a later “Residential (Group C)” zoning leaves only the non-domestic uses then permitted, such as bank, shop and restaurant, and the person claiming that right will need to provide sufficient evidence.
