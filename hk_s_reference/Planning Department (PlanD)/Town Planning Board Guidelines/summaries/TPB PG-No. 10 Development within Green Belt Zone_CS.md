# TPB PG-No. 10 Development within Green Belt Zone
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
July 1991 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: These guidelines set the criteria by which the Town Planning Board considers applications under section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance for development within the “Green Belt” (“GB”) zone. The zone is primarily to promote conservation of the natural environment and to safeguard it from encroachment by urban-type developments. Uses other than the compatible public-purpose uses that are always permitted, including government/institution/community (G/IC) uses, residential development and public utility installations, require planning permission and are assessed on individual merits.

## Regulatory Overview
There is a general presumption against development, other than redevelopment, in a “GB” zone, and in general the Board will only be prepared to approve applications for development in the context of requests to rezone to an appropriate use. A new development will only be considered in exceptional circumstances and must be justified with very strong planning grounds, with scale and intensity compatible with the surrounding character.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the Green Belt is for (paras. 1.1–1.4)

| Point | What the guideline states |
|---|---|
| Land covered | Mainly slopes and hillsides, most of which is naturally vegetated. Some “GB” areas are also designated as Country Parks. Most of the land is Government land, with small pockets of private land, generally near built-up areas |
| Conserve | Existing landscape features, areas of scenic value, and areas of recognised “fung shui” importance |
| Buffer | Define the outer limits of urbanized districts and serve as a buffer between and within urban areas |
| Recreation | Provide additional outlets for passive recreational uses |
| Always permitted | Compatible uses which are essential and for public purpose, such as waterworks, water catchment areas, nature reserves, agriculture, forestry and certain passive recreational uses |
| Needs permission | Other uses, including G/IC, residential development and public utility installations. Each proposal is assessed on its individual merits |

**SD takeaway:** Treat a “GB” site as conservation, buffer and passive-recreation land, and start from the short list of always-permitted public-purpose uses before assuming a building needs a section 16 application.

### 2. Presumption against new building, and residential intensity (section 2, criteria (a)–(d))

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Development other than redevelopment | General presumption **against** development. In general the Board will only be prepared to approve applications for development in the context of requests to rezone to an appropriate use |
| New development | Will only be considered in exceptional circumstances and **must** be justified with very strong planning grounds |
| Scale | Plot ratio, site coverage and building height should be compatible with the character of surrounding areas |
| Residential plot ratio | With the exception of New Territories Exempted Houses, a plot ratio **up to 0.4** for residential development may be permitted |
| New Territories Exempted Houses | Applications with satisfactory sewage disposal facilities and access arrangements may be approved if the sites are in close proximity to existing villages and in keeping with the surrounding uses, and where the development is to meet the demand from indigenous villagers |
| Redevelopment of existing residential development | Will generally be permitted **up to the intensity of the existing development** |

**SD takeaway:** Do not draw a new building in the “GB” zone except as a rezoning case with very strong planning grounds, and keep new residential plot ratio at or below 0.4 unless the case is a New Territories Exempted House meeting the village, sewage, access and indigenous-demand tests, or a redevelopment held to the existing intensity.

### 3. G/IC uses, public utilities and passive recreation (section 2, criteria (e)–(f))

| Use | Test |
|---|---|
| G/IC uses and public utility installations | **Must** demonstrate that the proposed development is essential and that no alternative sites are available |
| Plot ratio for that G/IC site | May **exceed 0.4** so as to minimize the land to be allocated for G/IC uses |
| Passive recreational uses | Uses compatible with the character of surrounding areas may be given sympathetic consideration |

**SD takeaway:** A G/IC or public-utility building must be shown to be essential with no alternative site, and its plot ratio may go above 0.4 only to keep the land take small.

### 4. Landscape, access, infrastructure, water gathering grounds and slope (section 2, criteria (g)–(m))

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Design and layout | Should be compatible with the surrounding area |
| Vegetation and views | **Should not** involve extensive clearance of existing natural vegetation, affect the existing natural landscape, or cause any adverse visual impact on the surrounding environment |
| Access and parking | The vehicular access road and parking provision should be appropriate to the scale of the development and comply with relevant standards. Access and parking **should not** adversely affect existing trees or other natural landscape features. Tree preservation and landscaping proposals **should** be provided |
| Infrastructure | **Should not** overstrain the capacity of existing and planned infrastructure such as sewerage, roads and water supply. It should not adversely affect drainage or aggravate flooding in the area |
| Water gathering grounds | **Must** comply with the development controls and restrictions of areas designated as water gathering grounds |
| G/IC capacity | **Should not** overstrain the overall provision of G/IC facilities in the general area |
| Pollution | Should not be susceptible to adverse environmental effects from pollution sources nearby, such as traffic noise, unless adequate mitigating measures are provided, and it should not itself be the source of pollution |
| Slope | Any proposed development on a slope or hillside **should not** adversely affect slope stability |

**SD takeaway:** Keep the layout off extensive vegetation clearance, protect existing trees from the access and parking, submit tree preservation and landscaping, and prove sewerage, roads, water, drainage, flooding, water-gathering-ground controls and slope stability before the massing is fixed.
