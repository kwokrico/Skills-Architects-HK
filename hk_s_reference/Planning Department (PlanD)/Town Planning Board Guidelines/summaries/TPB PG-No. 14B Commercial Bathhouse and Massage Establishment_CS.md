# TPB PG-No. 14B Commercial Bathhouse and Massage Establishment
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
June 2003 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: These guidelines define commercial bathhouse and massage establishment and set the planning criteria for section 16 applications for those uses in the “Residential (Group A)”, “Commercial/Residential”, “Commercial” and “Comprehensive Development Area” zones. The Board’s concern is whether the premises are suitable in planning terms, including compatibility with residential use and with non-domestic uses such as a children and youth centre, kindergarten, or religious, educational or community institutions. Control of vice and obscenity is left to the licensing and other relevant authorities, and planning approval does not mean that licensing or other statutory requirements under other Ordinances and Regulations are met.

## Regulatory Overview
Commercial bathhouse and massage establishment attract visits by outsiders and will normally not be permitted within a residential neighbourhood, in a wholly residential building, or in the residential portion of a composite commercial/residential building. Where they are accepted, they sit in a commercial building or in the non-domestic portion of a composite building, remain compatible with the uses on the occupation permit, and, in a composite building, must take access only from stairways, lifts or escalators that serve the non-domestic portion.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What these guidelines control (paras. 1.1–1.3)

| Point | Rule |
|---|---|
| Where the uses are commonly found | Commercial buildings and composite commercial/residential buildings |
| Why planning control is applied | Such uses should not cause nuisance to, nor be incompatible with, other uses in the same building or in surrounding developments |
| Zones covered | “Residential (Group A)”, “Commercial/Residential”, “Commercial” and “Comprehensive Development Area” |
| What planning does not decide | Control of vice and obscenity is left to the licensing and other relevant authorities |
| Licensing | The uses are also subject to licensing or other requirements under other Ordinances and Regulations. Applicants should obtain professional advice on whether licensing and other government approval is required before contemplating any works |

**SD takeaway:** Confirm the premises fall in one of the four named zones and treat licensing as a separate approval that planning permission does not grant.

### 2. Definitions and premises that fall outside the use (paras. 2.1–2.2)

| Term | Meaning in the guideline |
|---|---|
| Commercial bathhouse | Any premises maintained or intended to be maintained for the use, on payment of a fee, of persons requiring a bath, as defined in the Commercial Bathhouses Regulation of the Public Health and Municipal Services Ordinance (Chapter 132) |
| Massage establishment | Any place used, intended to be used, or represented as being used for the reception or treatment of persons requiring massage or other similar service or treatment, as defined in the Massage Establishments Ordinance (Chapter 266) |

The massage-establishment definition does **not** include:

| Excluded premises | Limit stated in the guideline |
|---|---|
| Hospital or maternity home | Excluded |
| Medical treatment | Establishment operated by a registered medical practitioner |
| Physiotherapy | Establishment operated by a registered physiotherapist |
| Hair salon or beauty parlour | Where massage is administered in the premises in full view of all customers |
| Limited massage | Where no massage other than face, scalp, neck, shoulder, hand, arm or foot (**up to knee**) massage is administered to customers, or no full-body massage is administered to a customer by a person of the opposite sex |
| Chinese medicine | Premises for practising Chinese medicine operated by a registered/listed Chinese medicine practitioner |
| Chiropractic | Premises operated by a registered chiropractor |

**SD takeaway:** Apply this guideline only to a fee-paying bathhouse under Chapter 132, or to a massage establishment under Chapter 266 that is not one of the excluded medical, salon, limited-massage, Chinese-medicine or chiropractic premises.

### 3. Neighbourhood and which part of the building (paras. 3.1–3.2)

| Location | Planning position |
|---|---|
| Residential neighbourhood | Will **normally not** be permitted. These uses, like other commercial uses, may attract visits by outsiders |
| Commercial building | The proposed use **should** be located here, or in the non-domestic portion described below |
| Non-domestic portion of a composite commercial/residential building | Should be located here, such as the commercial podium |
| Wholly residential building | Will **normally not** be permitted |
| Residential portion of a composite commercial/residential building | Will **normally not** be permitted |

**SD takeaway:** Place the premises in a commercial building or in the commercial podium or other non-domestic portion, and keep them out of a residential neighbourhood and out of any domestic floor.

### 4. Same-building compatibility, separated access, and authority clearance (paras. 3.3–3.7)

| Test | Rule |
|---|---|
| Other uses in the same building | The proposed use **should not** be incompatible with them |
| Which uses count | Consideration will normally be given only to the uses permitted in the occupation permit issued by the Building Authority |
| Access in a composite building | Where the use is in the non-domestic portion, access (entrance) to the application premises **must** be separated from that of the domestic portion by separate stairways and/or lifts/escalators exclusively serving the non-domestic portion, so as to avoid nuisance to occupants |
| Local residents | Their views will be taken into account |
| Fire safety | The Fire Services Department and the Buildings Department should be satisfied with the proposals in respect of fire service installations and means of escape, and related fire safety requirements |
| Other requirements | All other statutory or non-statutory requirements of relevant Government departments **must** be met |

**SD takeaway:** Give the premises its own entrance from stairways, lifts or escalators that serve only the non-domestic portion, check compatibility against the occupation permit, and obtain Fire Services Department and Buildings Department acceptance of fire service installations and means of escape.
