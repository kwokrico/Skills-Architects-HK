# TPB PG-No. 25D Use and Development within Industrial Zone
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2007 | Town Planning Board

> Scope note: Revised definitions of industrial and industrial-related terms in the User Schedule of the “Industrial” zone, the uses permitted as of right in that zone, and the criteria for planning applications for office buildings and for commercial uses in industrial or industrial-office buildings in the zone. General commercial and office uses remain subject to planning control. Heavy and obnoxious industries listed in para 3.2 are for special industrial areas or designated “Other Specified Uses” zones.

## Regulatory Overview
The planning intention of the “Industrial” zone is to reserve land primarily for general industrial uses so that there is an adequate supply of industrial floor space, and information technology and telecommunications industries are treated as compatible with that zone because of requirements such as uninterrupted power supply, heavy floor loading and high ceiling. Offices related to industrial use, and trading firms which require large storage space and frequent loading/unloading, are permitted as of right; general commercial and office uses will still be subject to planning control.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Definitions, including information technology and telecommunications (paras 3.1–3.6)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Industrial Use | Use of any place, premises or structure for the manufacture, alteration, cleansing, repairing, ornamenting, finishing, adaptation for sale, breaking up, or demolishing or transformation of goods and materials; for the storage, loading, unloading or handling of goods and cargo; or for the training, research, design and development, quality control and packaging in relation to the above processes |
| Not accommodated in the “Industrial” zone | Heavy and obnoxious industries which are large-scale and/or require free-standing purpose-designed premises: aircraft maintenance and repair plant, chemical and biochemical plant, cement manufacturing plant, electric power station, gas works, material recovery/recycling facility, mining and quarrying, oil depot, oil refinery and petrol-chemical plant, refuse disposal installation, resource recovery park and sand depot |
| Office Related to Industrial Use | Any premises where the activities of a firm are in direct support of and complementary to an associated industrial operation, which may or may not be located in the same premises or building, or in the same general industrial area |
| IT and Telecommunications Industries | Establishments involved in the manufacture, design, development, production, operation, processing or assembly of, or research into: (a) electronic and micro-electronic systems, goods and components; (b) information-technology products and services, including any computer hardware and software, contents and applications; and (c) telecommunications facilities and telecommunications services, having the same definitions as in the Telecommunications Ordinance (Cap. 106) |
| Excluded from that term | Activities involving direct provision of customer services, retail services or goods related to information technology and telecommunications uses. General commercial/office uses are also excluded, notwithstanding that they may adopt equipment or facilities that fall within para 3.4 |
| Research, Design and Development Centre | Any premises used for research and design of new or substantially modified products or industrial processes, and research and development of information technology and telecommunications |

An industrial-office building is a dual-purpose building in which every unit, other than a unit in the purpose-designed non-industrial portion, can be used flexibly for both industrial and office purposes. The building must comply with all relevant building and fire regulations applicable to both industrial and office buildings, including floor loading, compartmentation, lighting, ventilation, provision of means of escape and sanitary fitments.

**SD takeaway:** Use the para 3.4 list for an information-technology or telecommunications industry, and keep out direct customer service, retail of related goods, and general commercial or office use.

### 2. Uses permitted as of right (paras 4.1–4.6)

In addition to general industrial uses, information technology and telecommunications industries, office related to industrial use, and a research, design and development centre are permitted as of right. Because of fire safety, only activities that do not attract a large number of visiting members of the public due to the direct provision of customer services or goods will be permitted.

| Use | Rule |
|---|---|
| Facilities | Gateways, exchanges, switching centres, radio base stations, paging centres, data centres, data processing/computer centres, telesites or carrier hotels are permitted as of right in industrial and industrial-office buildings. Activities involving direct provision of customer services or goods related to these uses are excluded |
| Office related to industrial use | Always permitted in both industrial and industrial-office buildings. There is no requirement for the related industrial operation to be in the same premises or building, or in the same industrial area |
| Trading firm | Permitted where it requires frequent loading/unloading and large storage space of **not less than 30%** of the total usable floor area of the firm within the same premises or building, and cannot normally be accommodated in a conventional commercial/office building |
| Eating place, office, and shop and services specified in the Notes | Permitted in the purpose-designed non-industrial portion on the lower floors of an existing building, provided they are separated from the industrial uses above by a buffer floor of non-hazardous occupancy, such as a parking or loading/unloading floor. No industrial uses will be permitted to co-exist with the commercial uses in the non-industrial portion |
| Ancillary showroom | Up to **20%** of the total usable floor area of an industrial firm in the same premises or building will be permitted without application |
| Motor-vehicle showroom | Permitted as of right on the ground floor of an industrial building |
| Other ancillary uses | All uses ancillary and directly related to a permitted use, other than ancillary showroom use exceeding the **20%** usable-floor-area threshold, are always permitted and no separate planning permission is required |

**SD takeaway:** Size a trading firm’s store at not less than 30% of that firm’s usable floor area, keep an ancillary showroom at or under 20%, and place Notes commercial uses only in a non-industrial portion below a non-hazardous buffer floor.

### 3. When permission is required, and the floor-area limits (paras 5.1 and 6.1–6.2)

A pure office building, and commercial uses in an industrial building or on the upper floors of an industrial-office building, other than those specified in Column 1 of the Notes, will require planning permission under section 16. Each application will be considered on its individual merits. This includes general office use such as consultant firms and various types of agencies, and showroom use unrelated to any industrial operation in the same premises or building, or exceeding the 20% usable-floor-area threshold.

| Office building (para 6.1) | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Need | Demonstrate a shortfall in office and other commercial floor space to serve the industrial activities in the area, and that there are no suitable alternative sites in the vicinity |
| Location | Easily accessible to public transport facilities, in particular railway stations |
| Renewal or buffer | Favourable consideration may be given on a site within the part of an industrial area requiring renewal or restructuring, where the development will induce significant improvement to the general amenity and environment, or will alleviate existing interface problems by an environmental buffer between industrial uses and residential or other environmentally sensitive uses, such as school and community facilities |
| Vehicles | Adequate parking and loading/unloading within the site in accordance with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines and to the satisfaction of the Transport Department |

| Commercial use in an industrial building, or on the upper floors of an industrial-office building (para 6.2) | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Need | Genuine need, no suitable alternative accommodation in the vicinity, and location and scale justified on operational grounds |
| Showroom above 20% | The Board may grant temporary permission for ancillary showroom use exceeding 20% of the total usable floor area of an industrial firm in an industrial building, depending on the case and the availability of industrial-office or business buildings in the vicinity |
| Bulky goods | Provided other criteria could be met and with good justifications, favourable consideration would be given to showrooms requiring a large floor area for bulky goods and adequate on-site loading/unloading, which could not normally be accommodated in conventional commercial/office buildings |
| Traffic | Adequate parking and loading/unloading within the site in accordance with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines and to the satisfaction of the Transport Department. The proposed commercial use should not adversely affect traffic conditions in the local road network |
| Ground-floor aggregate commercial floor area | Owing to fire safety, the aggregate commercial floor areas on the ground floor of an existing industrial or industrial-office building should as a general principle **not exceed 460 m²** with sprinkler systems and **230 m²** without. For a slight exceedance, the applicant has to demonstrate that the fire safety concern can be satisfactorily addressed, and each case will be considered on its own merits |
| Area excluded from that calculation | Staircases, common circulation areas, lift landings, lavatories, water closets, and any space occupied by machinery for any lift, air-conditioning system or similar service provided for the building |
| Uses to which the 230 m² / 460 m² limits do not apply | Uses ancillary to or for supporting the industrial activities and the routine activities of the workers: bank; fast food counter sited at street level without seating accommodation and licensed as a food factory; electrical shop (selling electrical accessories, usually with repairing services, and small in scale); local provisions store (selling cigarettes, drinks, canned food and other local convenience goods, and small in scale); showroom in connection with the main industrial use |
| Separated low zone | The 230 m² / 460 m² criteria do not apply to conversion of the low zone of an existing industrial or industrial-office building for commercial uses if the commercial portion is completely separated from the industrial or industrial-office portion on the upper floors by a buffer floor of non-hazardous occupancy such as a car-parking floor. In all cases, separate means of escape should be available for the commercial portion |
| Basement | The Fire Services Department will not support commercial uses in the basement or basements of an industrial or industrial-office building |

A prospective applicant may check with the Planning Department on the aggregate commercial floor area figure in the building.

**SD takeaway:** A pure office, or commercial use on an upper floor, needs section 16 permission; keep ground-floor aggregate commercial floor area within 230 m² without sprinklers or 460 m² with sprinklers, and do not rely on a basement for that commercial use.

### 4. Modification, partial conversion, and other requirements (paras 7.1–7.2 and 8.1)

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Modification or change of use | The proposed use **must** be permitted in terms of the extant statutory plan |
| Partial conversion to a use that requires permission | It **must** be demonstrated that the proposed use would be acceptable in terms of fire safety, land use, traffic and environmental considerations |
| Place of entertainment, educational institution, religious institution | There is no provision for a planning application for partial conversion of an industrial building for these uses. Conversion of an industrial building in whole for these and other compatible uses may be permitted on application to the Board based on individual merits |
| All new development, redevelopment, conversion and modification/change of use | All other statutory and non-statutory requirements of relevant Government departments **must** also be met, including the building structure, means of escape and fire safety requirements, which will be considered at the building plan submission stage |

**SD takeaway:** Do not submit a partial conversion for a place of entertainment, an educational institution or a religious institution; a whole-building conversion of those uses is an application on its merits, and structure, means of escape and fire safety must still be met at building-plan stage.
