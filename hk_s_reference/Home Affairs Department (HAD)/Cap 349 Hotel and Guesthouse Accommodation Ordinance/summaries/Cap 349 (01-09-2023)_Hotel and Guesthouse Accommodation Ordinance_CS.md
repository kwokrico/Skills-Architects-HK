# Hotel and Guesthouse Accommodation Ordinance (Cap. 349)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 1 September 2023 | The Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs is the Hotel and Guesthouse Accommodation Authority (sections 2 and 4)

> Scope note: This Ordinance licences premises held out as providing sleeping accommodation for a fee, and it refuses a licence where a deed of mutual covenant or Government lease restricts hotel, guesthouse, commercial, or non-residential use, or where the premises fail a suitability test. It does not set room sizes, corridor widths, or fire-service installation standards. Those are not in this consolidation. Fit-and-proper tests for the operator are omitted except where they block the licence.

## Regulatory Overview

Premises are a hotel or guesthouse if they are held out as providing sleeping accommodation to any persons presenting themselves who are willing to pay a fee for the sleeping accommodation, and inference may be drawn from any representation and from the state or use of the premises (section 2A). A person commits an offence who operates, keeps, manages, or otherwise has control of any premises that are a hotel or guesthouse while no licence is in force, and it is not a defence that the person did not know that no licence was in force (section 5).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Two licence categories, and a mandatory refusal (sections 12A, 12B, and 12J)

The Authority may issue a hotel licence or a guesthouse licence, and may specify different eligibility criteria and subcategories of guesthouse licences. A guesthouse licence may be subject to a condition that the business name must not contain the term “hotel” or “酒店” (section 12A).

The Authority must refuse an application if the premises do not satisfy the no-use restriction requirement, or if the applicant fails to comply with a section 12O request. The Authority may refuse if the premises do not satisfy the suitability requirement, or if the applicant is not fit and proper (section 12B).

Premises are not free from a use restriction if, for any part covered by a deed of mutual covenant, a restrictive provision applies under that deed, or, for any part not so covered, a restrictive provision applies under the Government lease. A restrictive provision is an express provision that the part is prohibited from being used as a hotel or guesthouse, or from being used for commercial purposes, or is permitted to be used only for private residential purposes (section 12J).

**SD takeaway:** Do not plan a hotel or guesthouse on a part whose deed of mutual covenant or Government lease expressly limits it to private residential use, prohibits commercial use, or prohibits hotel or guesthouse use. That is a mandatory refusal.

### 2. Suitability of the building (section 12K)

Premises satisfy the suitability requirement only if it does not appear to the Authority that:

| Ground | Detail |
|---|---|
| Unsuitable premises | Not suitable to be used as a hotel or guesthouse for reasons connected with the situation, means of ingress or egress, design, construction, size, equipment, or type of building, or with the protection of life and property under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95) |
| Other statutory requirements | The premises do not comply with a requirement relating to design, structure, fire precautions, health, sanitation, or safety set out in the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) |
| Supervision | The operation would not be under the continuous and personal supervision of the applicant or licence holder |

**SD takeaway:** Ingress and egress, and compliance with Cap. 123 requirements on design, structure, fire precautions, health, sanitation, and safety, are licence grounds. This Ordinance does not replace those requirements with its own dimensions.

### 3. Authorized person’s certificate if the licence exceeds 36 months (sections 12I and 12M)

If the licence period exceeds 36 months, the licence holder must, within the period beginning 1 month before and ending 1 month after each anniversary, submit a certificate in the form specified by the Authority, signed by an authorized person. Authorized person has the meaning given by section 2(1) of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123).

The certificate must state that, since the date of issue or the immediately preceding anniversary, the premises have not undergone any alteration resulting in a substantial deviation from the latest plan showing the layout deposited with and agreed to by the Authority; have been operated in a way that does not contravene any licence condition; and have been maintained in a proper condition with regard to building safety and fire safety.

The operation requirement in section 12M is not satisfied if that certificate is not submitted within the period, or if a certificate submitted is incomplete, incorrect, or false in a material particular.

**SD takeaway:** After licensing, do not alter the layout so that it substantially deviates from the plan deposited with and agreed by the Authority. On a licence longer than 36 months an authorized person must certify that each year.

### 4. Owner and tenant of an unlicensed hotel or guesthouse (section 5A)

If premises are a hotel or guesthouse while no licence is in force, each of the owners and tenants commits an offence. Owner includes a person holding the premises direct from the Government, a person receiving the rent, and a person who would be entitled to receive the rent if the premises were let. Tenant does not include a person who becomes a tenant only through being provided sleeping accommodation on the premises. A person charged is entitled to be acquitted if sufficient evidence raises an issue that the person did not know and had no reason to suspect the premises were an unlicensed hotel or guesthouse, or could not with reasonable diligence have prevented that, and the contrary is not proved beyond reasonable doubt.

**SD takeaway:** The landlord who receives the rent is in the offence unless the section 5A knowledge or diligence issue is raised, so a residential flat held out as paid sleeping accommodation is not only an operator problem.
