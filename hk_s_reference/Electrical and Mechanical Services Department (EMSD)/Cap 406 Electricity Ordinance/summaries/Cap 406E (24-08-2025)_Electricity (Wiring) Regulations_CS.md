# Cap. 406E — Electricity (Wiring) Regulations
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated 24 August 2025 | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: Cap. 406E sets the safety duties for a fixed electrical installation: circuit segregation, isolation, earthing, overhead-line height, switchroom access, and periodic inspection. It applies to fixed electrical installations, and, except for regulation 5(1), it does not apply to a category 2 circuit (reg. 3). It does not publish the millimetre clearances in front of switchgear or the bathroom zone diagrams. Those are in the Code of Practice for the Electricity (Wiring) Regulations. It does not set energy-efficiency limits under the Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance (Cap. 610).

## Regulatory Overview

These regulations apply to fixed electrical installations, and they require the installation to be designed, constructed, installed and protected so as to prevent danger, with adequate access and working space for equipment that needs operation or maintenance (regs. 3 and 4).

Category 1, 2, 3 and 4 circuits must be segregated from one another as far as necessary to prevent danger and electrical interference, and a category 4 circuit must not share a conduit, ducting or trunking with any other category (reg. 5).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Circuit categories (reg. 2)

| Category | What it is |
|---|---|
| Category 1 | A circuit that operates at low voltage, excluding a category 3 circuit |
| Category 2 | A circuit for telecommunications, radio, telephone, sound distribution, intruder alarm, bell and call, or data transmission, supplied from a safety source, excluding a category 3 circuit |
| Category 3 | A circuit for emergency lighting, air pressurisation systems, and fire services installations, including fire detection and alarm, fire pumps, fireman's lifts, and smoke extraction |
| Category 4 | A high-voltage circuit |
| Switchroom | Premises, or an enclosed part of premises, that contain equipment for switching, controlling or regulating electricity at low voltage and above, and that are large enough to admit a person after the equipment is in position |
| Substation | Premises, or an enclosed part of premises, that contain equipment for transforming or converting energy to or from high voltage (other than solely for switching devices or instruments), or for switching, controlling or regulating energy at high voltage, and that are large enough to admit a person after the equipment is in position |

**SD takeaway:** Keep the fire-services and emergency-lighting circuits (category 3) and any high-voltage circuit (category 4) in their own containment. A category 2 circuit is outside these regulations except for the segregation duty in regulation 5(1).

### 2. Switchroom access, adverse conditions, and overhead lines (regs. 4, 15, 16, 17)

| Rule | Hard requirement |
|---|---|
| Working space (reg. 4(4)) | Equipment that requires operation, maintenance or attention must have adequate and safe means of access and working space |
| Unauthorised entry (reg. 4(5)) | The owner and the person in control of a substation or switchroom must prevent unauthorised entry |
| Entrances kept clear (reg. 4(6)) | Entrances and exits of a switchroom or substation must be kept clear of obstructions that could prevent easy access |
| Weather, heat, corrosion, fire or explosion (reg. 15) | The affected part must be specially constructed, installed or protected |
| Low-voltage overhead line (reg. 16(2)) | Height above ground at any point of the span not less than **5.8 m** across any place accessible to vehicular traffic, and **5.2 m** otherwise |
| High-voltage overhead line (reg. 16(3)) | No high-voltage overhead line without the Director's approval |
| Entrance notice (reg. 17(1)) | An identification label and a warning notice including the words "Danger" and "危險" at every entrance of a substation and switchroom |
| Periodic-test notice (reg. 17(4)) | At or near the main distribution board of an installation in regulation 20, a notice that the installation must be tested and certified by a stated grade of electrical worker before a stated date |

**SD takeaway:** Size the switchroom so a person can enter after the gear is in place, keep both the door and the route from a public area clear, and do not route a low-voltage overhead line below 5.8 m over a vehicle path or 5.2 m elsewhere.

### 3. Circuits, isolation, and earthing (regs. 6–12)

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Division into circuits (reg. 6) | Enough circuits to avoid danger on a fault and to allow safe operation, inspection, testing and maintenance. Each part that needs separate control has its own circuit |
| Final circuits (reg. 6(3)) | Where there is more than one final circuit, each is separately protected against overcurrent, connected to a separate isolation device in a distribution board, electrically separated so that an isolated circuit cannot be energised indirectly, and unaffected by failure of another final circuit |
| Current demand (reg. 7) | Each circuit must suit the maximum current demand of the equipment connected or intended to be connected. The maximum current demand of the installation must not exceed the approved loading |
| Isolation (reg. 8) | Effective means, readily accessible, to cut off all voltage from the installation, from each circuit, and from equipment, as necessary to prevent or remove danger. A motorised drive with accessible moving parts that may cause injury needs a local disconnection |
| Neutral (reg. 10) | A single-pole switch goes in the phase conductor of a single-phase circuit only. No fuse, and no circuit breaker, isolator or switch other than a linked device, in a neutral conductor. A linked device in the neutral must also break the related phase conductors |
| Earthing (regs. 11 and 12) | Metalwork that may become charged must be earthed so as to discharge safely, or another effective means must prevent danger. The installation must have an effective earthing arrangement, including a connection to earth electrodes |
| Main bonding of extraneous metal (reg. 11(4)) | Where earthed metalwork is accessible simultaneously with a substantial extraneous conductive part, that part must be connected to the main earthing terminal. This applies to an installation completed on or after **1 January 1985**, or substantially altered on or after that date |
| Supplier transformer in the premises (reg. 12(2)) | The main earthing terminal must be bonded to the supplier's bonding terminal at the earthed point of the transformer. Applies to an installation completed or substantially altered on or after **1 June 1992** |

**SD takeaway:** The approved loading is a ceiling, not a target to exceed at fit-out, and each final circuit needs its own protective device and isolator on the distribution board.

### 4. Periodic inspection (reg. 20)

The owner must have the fixed electrical installation inspected, tested and certified, and must deliver the certificate to the Director for endorsement within **2 weeks**, with the endorsement fee stated in the regulation.

| Premises | Interval |
|---|---|
| A place of public entertainment under the Places of Public Entertainment Ordinance (Cap. 172), other than a sea-going vessel; premises for manufacturing or storing dangerous goods described in the Dangerous Goods (Application and Exemption) Regulation 2012; a high-voltage installation fed directly from a high-voltage supply (reg. 20(1)) | At least once every **12 months** |
| A low-voltage installation with approved loading exceeding **200 A**, single or three phase, in a factory or industrial undertaking under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59), if it is not already in reg. 20(1) (reg. 20(2)) | At least once every **5 years** |
| A low-voltage installation with approved loading exceeding **100 A**, single or three phase, in any other premises (reg. 20(3)) | At least once every **5 years** |
| A hotel; a private hospital; a scheduled nursing home with an exemption in force; a nursing home under the Residential Care Homes (Elderly Persons) Ordinance (Cap. 459); a school; premises of the institutions listed in the Education Ordinance (Cap. 279); a registered child care centre; and premises the Director specifies by notice (reg. 20(4)) | At least once every **5 years**, including where the approved loading is not above the reg. 20(2) or 20(3) threshold |

Before energising, including after repair, alteration or addition, the completed work, or the affected part, must be inspected, tested and certified by a registered electrical worker (reg. 19). An alteration or addition must not push the maximum current demand above the existing approved loading unless the electricity supplier approves a new loading (reg. 18(2)).

**SD takeaway:** A hotel, school, hospital, nursing home or child care centre is on the 5-year certificate even if the approved loading is modest, and a place of public entertainment or a high-voltage supply is on a 12-month certificate.
