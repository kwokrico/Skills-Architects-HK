# Cap. 406 — Electricity Ordinance
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated 1 July 2022 | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: Cap. 406 regulates electrical installations, electricity suppliers, generating facilities, electrical products, and the registration of electrical workers and electrical contractors. It sets who may do electrical work and when a supply may be connected or disconnected. It does not set switchroom clearances, circuit segregation distances, or bathroom zones. Those sit in the Electricity (Wiring) Regulations (Cap. 406E) and the Code of Practice for the Electricity (Wiring) Regulations. It does not set lighting power density or air-conditioning efficiency. Those sit in the building energy code under the Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance (Cap. 610).

## Regulatory Overview

A fixed electrical installation is a low-voltage or high-voltage installation fixed to premises, and it excludes equipment supplied through a socket that can be disconnected without a tool (s. 2).

The owner must not employ anyone other than a registered electrical contractor to carry out electrical work, unless the owner employs a registered electrical worker of the appropriate grade full time at a regular wage or salary to work on that installation (s. 35).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Voltage bands and what is fixed (s. 2)

| Term | Limit in this Ordinance |
|---|---|
| Extra low voltage | Normally not exceeding **50 V** root mean square alternating current, or **120 V** direct current, between conductors or between a conductor and earth |
| Low voltage | Exceeding extra low voltage, but normally not exceeding **1 000 V** root mean square alternating current or **1 500 V** direct current between conductors, or **600 V** root mean square alternating current or **900 V** direct current between a conductor and earth |
| High voltage | Voltage normally exceeding low voltage |
| Fixed electrical installation | A low-voltage or high-voltage electrical installation fixed to premises. Equipment supplied after a socket that can be disconnected without a tool is not part of it |
| Wiring installation | The part of a fixed electrical installation used to distribute and control electricity, including fittings, accessories, devices and switches, excluding current-using equipment |
| Electrical work | Installation, commissioning, inspection, testing, maintenance, modification or repair of a low-voltage or high-voltage fixed electrical installation, including supervision, certification of that work, and certification of the design |
| Owner | A person in possession or control of the installation, or a person who holds the premises, including an agent, tenant or occupier |

**SD takeaway:** Draw the fixed installation only up to the socket; plug-in equipment is outside Cap. 406, and anything above low voltage is a high-voltage installation.

### 2. Connection, inspection, and the owner's duty after an accident (ss. 5, 6, 8, 12, 13, 19, 20)

| Event | Rule |
|---|---|
| Before first connection (s. 12(1)) | An electricity supplier must not connect supply to a fixed electrical installation unless the supplier has inspected it and is satisfied that it is safe |
| Reconnection without inspection (s. 12(2)) | A supplier may reconnect without inspection within the prescribed period if the disconnection was not for safety or for a contravention. Cap. 406E regulation 23 sets that period at **4 months** |
| Compliant installation (s. 13(1)) | Where the installation complies, the supplier must connect within a reasonable time after the owner requests connection |
| Director's rectification notice (s. 5) | If an installation could cause an electrical accident, affects safe and stable operation of another installation, or contravenes the Ordinance, the Director may require rectification within a stated time and may disconnect, or order the supplier to disconnect, if it is not rectified |
| Emergency disconnection (s. 6) | If there is an imminent risk of an electrical accident, the Director may disconnect without notice |
| No reconnection until certified (s. 8) | After a s. 5 or s. 6 disconnection, the owner must not reconnect until the Director has received a certificate, signed by a registered electrical worker, that the installation complies. Reconnection by a registered electrical worker for testing is allowed |
| After an accident (s. 19) | Within **2 weeks** of an electrical accident that involves or affects a fixed electrical installation, the owner must have the affected part inspected by a registered electrical worker, and must not use it until a compliance certificate is given, except in a life-threatening emergency. A copy goes to the Director within **2 weeks** of the certificate |
| Known unsafe condition (s. 20) | If the owner knows the condition is likely to cause an electrical accident, the owner must have it rectified immediately |

**SD takeaway:** Allow programme time for the supplier's safety inspection before energising, and do not treat a disconnection for safety as something that can be switched back on inside the 4-month window.

### 3. Who may do the work (ss. 30, 33, 35)

| Rule | Effect at schematic design |
|---|---|
| Registered electrical worker (s. 30) | Electrical work on a fixed installation is registered-worker work |
| Registered electrical contractor (ss. 33, 35(1)) | The person employed to carry out electrical work must be a registered electrical contractor |
| In-house worker (s. 35(3)) | An owner may instead employ a registered electrical worker of the appropriate grade full time, at a regular wage or salary, to do electrical work on that installation |
| Endorsement (s. 34(11)) | Where a registered electrical worker must report or certify, the registered electrical contractor, or the owner where s. 35(3) applies, endorses the report or certificate |

**SD takeaway:** Name a registered electrical contractor on the electrical package. An in-house registered worker is lawful only as a full-time employee of the owner on that installation.

### 4. Generating facilities (ss. 21–22)

A generating facility is an electrical installation used to produce electricity at low voltage or high voltage (s. 2).

The owner of a generating facility that is in use or on standby must register it with the Director unless it is one of the following (s. 21(1)):

| Paragraph | Not required to be registered |
|---|---|
| (a)–(c) | Used on an aircraft, a watercraft, or a hovercraft |
| (d) | On a land vehicle and not connected to a wiring installation outside the vehicle |
| (e) | Used in construction work under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59) |
| (f) | Forms part of an electrical installation that must submit a periodic test certificate to the Director under this Ordinance |
| (g) | Only supplies an electrical installation owned by the same owner |

A registered facility must display its certificate of registration (s. 21(3)). The owner of a registered facility, and the owner of a facility in s. 21(1)(e), (f) or (g), must keep it in continuous safe working order and, unless s. 35(3) applies and there is no registered electrical contractor, display the name and registration number of the registered electrical contractor employed to maintain it (s. 22).

**SD takeaway:** A standby generator or photovoltaic system that supplies only the owner's own installation is not registered under s. 21, but it still has to be maintained in continuous safe working order and is still a fixed electrical installation under Cap. 406E.
