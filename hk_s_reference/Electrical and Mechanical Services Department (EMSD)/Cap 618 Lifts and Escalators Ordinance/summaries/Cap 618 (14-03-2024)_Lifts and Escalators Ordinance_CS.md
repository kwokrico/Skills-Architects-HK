# Cap 618 — Lifts and Escalators Ordinance
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated: 14 March 2024 | Cap. 618 | Director of Electrical and Mechanical Services

> Scope note: Cap. 618 regulates the safety of lifts and escalators: who may carry out works, examination before use, use permits, major alterations, and incidents. It applies to the Government (s. 4). It does not set shaft, pit, machine-room or lobby dimensions, and it does not set energy-efficiency power limits. Those sit in codes of practice the Director may issue under section 145, and, for energy, in the building energy code under Cap. 610. Commencement of the use-permit regime in the operative text is tied to the date section 157 came into operation; many schedule pages in this consolidation are marked 17 December 2012.

## Regulatory Overview

Subject to section 3(2), the Ordinance applies to every lift and every escalator, including a mechanized vehicle parking system as a lift, and including a passenger conveyor as an escalator.

A lift or escalator must not be used without a use permit in force, must be examined by a registered engineer before it is put into use, and must not resume normal use after a major alteration until a resumption permit is issued.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What counts as a lift or escalator (s. 2)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lift | A lifting machine or appliance with a carrier whose direction of movement is restricted by one or more guides, or a mechanized vehicle parking system. An escalator is not a lift |
| Mechanized vehicle parking system | A mechanical plant with a power-operated mechanism for conveying a vehicle to a parking space within the plant |
| Escalator | An inclined continuous stairway driven by mechanical power and used to raise and/or lower passengers, or a passenger conveyor that is a continuous walkway driven by mechanical power and used to convey passengers on the same or between different traffic levels |
| Goods lift | A lift used, or intended to be used, exclusively for conveying goods or materials, or both |
| Service lift | Rated load not more than **250 kg**, and a car or cage with floor area not more than **1 m²** and height not more than **1.2 m** |
| Responsible person | For a lift or an escalator: a person who owns it, or any other person who has the management or control of it (s. 2). If there are two or more, compliance by one is compliance by the others (s. 5) |

**SD takeaway:** A service lift is only a lift within Cap. 618 if it stays at or below 250 kg, 1 m² and 1.2 m; a larger goods lift, a passenger lift, and a mechanized car-parking system are lifts and need the use-permit path.

### 2. Installations outside the Ordinance (s. 3(2))

| Paragraph | Excluded installation |
|---|---|
| (a) | An amusement device, including an amusement ride as defined in the Amusement Rides (Safety) Ordinance (Cap. 449) |
| (b) | A belt, bucket, scoop or roller conveyor or any similar machine |
| (c) | A hoist, including a skip hoist, used mainly for charging furnaces or similar appliances |
| (d) | A hoist used solely for lifting or feeding material directly into a machine, or solely for both |
| (e) | A lift with height of travel not exceeding **3.5 m**, that does not pass through any floor, and is used solely for carriage, stacking, loading or unloading of goods or materials, or any two or more of those purposes |
| (f) | A lift with height of travel not exceeding **3.5 m**, that does not pass through any floor, and is used solely for raising motor vehicles |
| (g) | A lift or escalator installed in any ship |
| (h) | A lift installed in any aircraft |
| (i) | A lift provided in connection with a building under construction, solely for persons employed in that construction, solely for carrying construction materials, or for both |
| (j) | A ramp connected to a wharf or pier |
| (k) | A service lift used in an industrial undertaking (Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59)) |
| (l) | A stage or orchestra lift |

**SD takeaway:** A goods or vehicle lift that travels more than 3.5 m, or that passes through a floor, is inside Cap. 618 even if it never carries passengers.

### 3. Use, overloading, and who may do the work (ss. 8–11, Sch. 4)

| Prohibition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who may do lift works or escalator works (ss. 8, 42) | Only a qualified person or a specified person, or a person under the direct supervision of a qualified person at the place of the works. For commissioning or examination, a qualified person is a registered engineer, or a registered worker who is qualified to carry out the works and who is employed by the registered contractor undertaking the works, has undertaken the works as that contractor, or is a partner in the partnership that has undertaken the works as that contractor (s. 2) |
| Use during works (ss. 9, 43) | No use or operation after installation, major alteration, demolition, or works likely to affect safe operation have begun, until the works are completed |
| Use permit (ss. 9(2), 43) | No use or operation if no use permit is in force |
| After major alteration (ss. 9(3), 43) | No use or operation until a resumption permit has been issued for that alteration |
| Schedule 4 lifts — no passengers (s. 10) | A person must not travel in, and a person must not cause or permit the carriage of any person in, a service lift, a goods lift, or a mechanized vehicle parking system. The section does not apply to a responsible person for the lift |
| Overloading (s. 11) | Sections 10 and 11 apply to those Schedule 4 lifts |

**SD takeaway:** Label goods lifts, service lifts and mechanized parking systems as no-passenger lifts; they are the Schedule 4 list, and passenger travel in them is prohibited.

### 4. Examination, use permit, and display (ss. 20–27, 39, Sch. 5)

| Event | Duty |
|---|---|
| Before first use (s. 20) | The responsible person causes a registered lift engineer to examine the lift with load in accordance with s. 24(2) and to examine thoroughly all associated equipment or machinery. Section 20 does not apply to a lift installed and put into use before the date section 157 came into operation |
| After major alteration (s. 21) | Before normal use resumes, a registered lift engineer thoroughly examines the lift and all associated equipment, or examines the affected part under s. 25(1) |
| Periodic examination (s. 22, Sch. 5 Part 2) | Thorough examination at intervals not exceeding the examination period. That period runs from the day after the last examination to the **1st anniversary** of the completion date. An examination in the last **2 months** of the period starts the next period from the preceding expiry, still one year long |
| Examination with load (s. 23, Sch. 5 Part 3) | The with-load examination period runs to the **5th anniversary** of the last with-load examination, with the same last-two-months rollover |
| Periodic maintenance (s. 15, Sch. 5 Part 1) | The maintenance interval in s. 15(2)(b) is **one month** after the date periodic maintenance was last completed. The same one-month interval applies to escalators (s. 46, Sch. 5 Part 4) |
| Escalator periodic examination (s. 53, Sch. 5 Part 5) | **6 months** from the day after the last examination, with the same last-two-months rollover |
| Use permit validity (s. 27) | A permit under s. 26(1)(a) expires on the **1st anniversary** of the date of completion of the examination. A renewal permit under s. 26(1)(b), where the examination was completed within the **2 months** ending on expiry of the preceding permit, expires on the 1st anniversary of that preceding expiry. Any other s. 26(1)(b) permit expires on the 1st anniversary of completion of its examination |
| Resumption permit (s. 28) | Issued for resumption after a major alteration only if the Director is satisfied the affected part is in safe working order |
| Display (s. 39) | A lift that has a car, other than a Schedule 4 lift, displays the use permit in a conspicuous position **in the car**. Any other lift displays it in a conspicuous position adjacent to the landing or, if more than one landing, the **main landing** |

The escalator use-permit provisions are the parallel Part 3 duties (ss. 51–57).

**SD takeaway:** Provide a conspicuous in-car position for the use permit on every passenger lift, and a main-landing position for a goods lift, service lift or mechanized parking system, which have no in-car permit.

### 5. Major alteration — what forces a resumption permit (Sch. 1)

For a lift, major alteration includes, among other items:

| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| (a), (e), (f), (g) | Adding an automatic door device; adding, removing or replacing a lift-way door; adding electrical contacts or an interlocking device to the lift-way door or car door |
| (l), (m) | Any change in the number or size of the suspension ropes, or in the size or type of any guide rail |
| (o), (p) | Any change in the type of control or operation; any modification that increases the dead weight of the carrier |
| (q) | Any modification that decreases or increases the distance the carrier travels |
| (r), (s) | Any increase in rated load, or any increase in rated speed |
| (t), (u), (v) | Replacement, including a change in type, of the controller, the driving-machine brake, or the driving machine |
| (j), (w) | Addition, or replacement other than a safety circuit containing an electronic component, of any safety component or safety equipment |

For an escalator, major alteration includes adding any safety component or safety equipment; any change in the type of control or operation; any increase in speed; and replacement, including a change in type, of any safety equipment, the drive, the braking system, the non-reversal device, or the overspeed protection device.

Safety components for a lift (Sch. 2 Part 1) include a safety gear, an overspeed governor, a door locking device, a buffer other than an energy-accumulation type buffer, an ascending-car overspeed protection means that acts if the carrier ascends at **115%** or more of rated speed, an unintended-car-movement protection means, and any safety circuit containing an electronic component. Safety equipment (Sch. 3) includes the driving-machine brake and an overload device.

**SD takeaway:** Changing travel, rated load, rated speed, control type, guide-rail size, door interlocks, or the driving machine is a major alteration and stops normal use until a resumption permit is issued.

### 6. Incidents and codes of practice (ss. 40, 145)

If there is an incident relating to a lift, the responsible person must, within **24 hours** after the incident comes to the person’s knowledge, notify the Director and the registered lift contractor in writing (s. 40). Incidents are those specified in Schedule 7.

The Director may issue a code of practice for practical guidance on the safety, use and operation of lifts or escalators, and for persons who carry out lift works or escalator works (s. 145). A code takes effect on the date specified in a Gazette notice.

**SD takeaway:** Shaft, pit and machine-room geometry is not numbered in this Ordinance; lock those dimensions to the current safety code of practice issued under section 145, and keep the Cap. 618 use-permit and major-alteration duties on top of that code.
