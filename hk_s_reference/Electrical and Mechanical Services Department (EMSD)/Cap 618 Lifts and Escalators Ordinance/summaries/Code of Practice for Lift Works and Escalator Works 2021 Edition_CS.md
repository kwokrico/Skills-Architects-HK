# Code of Practice for Lift Works and Escalator Works (2021 Edition)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
2021 Edition | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: This Works Code is issued by the Director of Electrical and Mechanical Services under the Lifts and Escalators Ordinance (Cap. 618). It sets the minimum industry standard for lift works and escalator works, including installation, commissioning, examination, maintenance, repair, alteration and demolition, and the procedures that go with those activities. It is written for trade practitioners. It does not set shaft, pit, headroom or lobby dimensions, and it does not set the energy-efficiency power limits in the building energy code under the Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance (Cap. 610). European standards are the reference, and an equivalent national or international standard is acceptable as an alternative (Foreword).

## Regulatory Overview

Lift works and escalator works are the installation, commissioning, examination, maintenance, repair, alteration or demolition of a lift or an escalator, or of any associated equipment or machinery, as defined in section 2(1) of the Ordinance (clause 4.13.1).

Preparation of structural openings, concrete plinths, fire-resisting lift shafts, machine-room doors, the machine-room ventilating system, power sockets, lift-pit waterproofing, lifting beams in the machine room, landing-door painting, partitions in a common lift shaft, fixed working platforms in the pit, sprinklers underneath an escalator, and an escalator shelter wall are not lift works or escalator works, and where they are building works under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) they comply with that Ordinance (clause 4.13.2).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the architect still has to provide (clause 4.13)

| Item treated as builder's work, not as lift works | Why it still affects the lift or escalator |
|---|---|
| Fire-resisting lift shafts, landing-door painting, partitions for a common shaft | A shortfall can create a hazard to safe operation or a risk of injury to users or to people maintaining the lift (cl. 4.13.3) |
| Machine-room doors and the machine-room ventilating system | Same. The registered contractor liaises so that this work is complete before the next step of lift works |
| Lift-pit waterproofing and fixed working platforms in the pit | Same |
| Lifting I-beams in the machine room, concrete plinths, structural openings and backfilling, power sockets | Same |
| Sprinklers underneath an escalator, and the escalator shelter wall | Shelter-wall clearance is agreed among the responsible person, the builder and the registered escalator contractor (cl. 4.21.6) |

Fire-resisting construction for building works associated with lifts and escalators is specified in the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings issued by the Buildings Department. Landing doors are normally required to provide resistance against the spread of fire (clause 4.20.1).

**SD takeaway:** Draw the machine-room door, the machine-room ventilation, the pit waterproofing, the lifting beam and the escalator shelter wall as building works, and agree the shelter-wall clearance with the registered escalator contractor before the escalator is installed.

### 2. Landing doors during maintenance and replacement (Annex C of Appendix III)

| Situation | Limit |
|---|---|
| Ordinary maintenance or replacement | Landing doors remain closed |
| A door kept open for the works | Normally not more than one landing door in a lift shaft is open at one time. Any opening other than the one where the car is level with the landing is attended by lift workers |
| More than one door open | Not more than **three** landing doors open at the same time, and no hot work or welding |

Before work in the shaft, the safe spaces and clearances under the car in the pit, and the safe headroom above the car at the top of its travel, are ascertained. If those spaces are limited, warning signs are displayed and other precautions are considered (clause 4.28.1). This code does not itself publish the pit and headroom dimensions.

**SD takeaway:** The fire door at each landing stays the fire barrier during lift works. Do not plan a sequence that leaves more than one landing door open unless the extra conditions, including a maximum of three and no hot work, are in the method statement.
