# Cap 610 — Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated: 20 September 2026 | Cap. 610 | Director of Electrical and Mechanical Services

> Scope note: Cap. 610 requires compliance with codes of practice for the energy efficiency of air-conditioning installations, electrical installations, lift and escalator installations and lighting installations, and energy audits, for prescribed buildings. It does not set the numerical design standards; those sit in a code of practice issued or approved under section 40. It does not apply to every building, and it does not apply to the installations listed in Schedule 2.

## Regulatory Overview

The Ordinance covers four building services installations — air-conditioning, electrical, lift and escalator, and lighting — in the building types listed in Schedule 1, with a separate energy-audit duty for the buildings listed in Schedule 4.

At schematic design the locks are building classification, the main-switch and small-building exclusions, which installations are central versus unit-only, and whether superstructure consent falls after 21 September 2012 or, for data centres, after the relevant date of 20 September 2026.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Buildings and installations outside the Ordinance (ss. 4–5, Sch. 2)

| Exclusion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Main electrical switch | Approved loading not exceeding **100 A**, 1-phase or 3-phase |
| Small building (all three must be met) | Not more than **3 storeys**; roofed-over area not more than **65.03 m²**; height not more than **8.23 m** |
| Heritage | Proposed monument or proposed historical building under Cap. 53 s. 2A, or a monument or historical building declared under Cap. 53 s. 3 |
| Short-life building | Director satisfied on the owner’s declaration that the building will cease to exist within **12 months** |
| Threshold measurement | Approved loading, installation rating, or floor area is calculated when the relevant works have been completed, unless otherwise prescribed (s. 5) |

Schedule 2 installations are outside the Ordinance even inside a prescribed building:

| Item | Sole use |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fire suppression and/or fire extinguishing |
| 2 | Surgical operation, clinical treatment, blood processing, life-protection environment settings, or any combination |
| 3 | Construction site, for construction works only |
| 4 | Industrial manufacturing |
| 5 | Research in an educational institution |
| 6 | Lighting solely for exhibit/product display, decoration (architectural feature or festival), visual production (performance, entertainment, television), or any combination |
| 7 | Air traffic regulation, safety, or control, or any combination |
| 8 | Railway traffic regulation, safety, or control, or any combination |

Lighting that is maintained-type emergency lighting is a lighting installation. Non-maintained type emergency lighting is not (s. 2).

**SD takeaway:** Confirm the main-switch approved loading and the Schedule 2 carve-outs before treating the project as a full Cap. 610 building.

### 2. Prescribed buildings (s. 2, Sch. 1)

A prescribed building is a building of a type in Schedule 1.

| Type | Building |
|---|---|
| 1 | Commercial building: offices, shops or entertainment facilities, or any trade, business or profession, but not an industrial building |
| 2 | Portion of a composite building that is not for residential or industrial use |
| 3 | Hotel or guesthouse (Cap. 349 s. 2A) |
| 4 | Common area of a residential building |
| 5 | Common area of a residential or industrial portion of a composite building |
| 6 | Common area of an industrial building |
| 7 | Occupied principally for education |
| 8 | Occupied principally for community services, including a community hall, a social services centre, and a composite building occupied as both |
| 9 | Occupied principally for municipal services, including a market, cooked food centre, library, cultural centre, indoor games hall, and a composite building occupied as two or more such places |
| 10 | Occupied principally for medical and health care services, including a hospital, clinic and rehabilitation centre |
| 11 | Owned by the Government and used principally to accommodate people during performance of a Government function |
| 12 | Airport passenger terminal building |
| 13 | Railway station (MTR station platforms, concourses, entrances, exits, adits and plant rooms; not tracks, tunnels, viaducts, ancillary buildings, ventilation shafts, sidings, or a North-west Railway designated rail stop) |
| 14 | Occupied principally as a data centre (added by 24 of 2025) |
| 15 | Portion of an industrial building occupied principally as a data centre (added by 24 of 2025) |

Residential building means a building solely used for residential purposes, including student hostels, staff quarters, and the residential portion of a composite building. A hotel or guesthouse is not a residential building.

Common area includes, unless an instrument registered in the Land Registry reserves it for exclusive use, car parks, entrance lobbies, lift lobbies, corridors, staircases, common toilets, common store rooms, plant rooms, switch rooms, pipe ducts, cable ducts, refuse rooms, material recovery chambers, covered podia, covered playgrounds, occupants’ clubhouses and building management offices.

A central building services installation is one that does not solely serve a unit, or, in a prescribed building with no common area, any installation other than one that solely serves a unit and is owned by someone who is not the owner of the building.

A unit may be two or more parts occupied by the same occupier for the same undertaking and interconnected by an internal corridor, internal staircase or other internal access. A common area is not a unit.

**SD takeaway:** Classify every portion — commercial, residential common area, industrial, hotel, data centre — because residential flats themselves are not a prescribed building, while their common areas are.

### 3. Design-stage and occupation-stage declarations (ss. 7–9)

| Gate | Rule |
|---|---|
| Part 2, types 1–13 | Applies where consent to commencement of superstructure building works is given **after 21 September 2012**. Types 14 and 15 are excluded from this limb |
| Part 2, types 14 and 15 (data centres) | Applies where that consent is given **after the relevant date**. Editorial note: operation date of the relevant date is **20 September 2026** |
| Stage one declaration (s. 8) | Developer submits within **2 months** after the day superstructure consent is given. A registered energy assessor certifies that suitable design provisions are incorporated so that the developer’s building services installations will be designed, installed and completed to the specified standards |
| Stage two declaration (s. 9) | Developer submits within **4 months** after the day occupation approval is issued, with the prescribed fee. The registered energy assessor’s certification has no effect unless the assessor personally inspected the covered installations in the **30 days** before certification |
| Stage two extension | Director may extend on a written application if satisfied there is a reasonable ground. A withdrawn extension can be followed by a direction to submit within a stated period |
| Offences | Stage one: fine of **$500,000**, plus **$10,000** per day if continuing. Stage two or a direction under s. 9(10): fine of **$1,000,000**, plus **$10,000** per day if continuing |

Occupation approval means an occupation permit (including a temporary occupation permit under Cap. 123 s. 21(2)) or an equivalent approval where no occupation permit is required.

**SD takeaway:** Freeze building-services design to the applicable code before superstructure consent, because the stage one declaration is due two months after that consent.

### 4. Certificate of Compliance Registration (ss. 10–15)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Issue | After a stage two declaration, the Director must issue a Certificate of Compliance Registration within **3 months** of receipt, unless the declaration or accompanying document is reasonably believed false or misleading, or further information under s. 9(4) is still outstanding |
| Validity | **10 years**, subject to s. 13(5) |
| Owner duty | A certificate must be in force at all times. Central installations must be maintained to a standard not lower than the first certificate, or the latest Form of Compliance where one has been issued |
| Unit responsible person | Installations serving the unit that are not central installations are held to the same standard |
| Renewal certification | No effect unless the registered energy assessor personally inspected the covered central installations in the **30 days** before certification. An application made during the **12 months** before expiry takes effect from the expiry date |
| Exemption (s. 15) | Developer, owner, or responsible person of a unit may apply in writing. The Director may exempt an installation where heritage conservation or technical or operational reasons make compliance undesirable or impracticable. Withdrawal takes effect no earlier than **28 days** after the notice |

**SD takeaway:** The first Certificate of Compliance Registration is the maintenance baseline for central plant and for unit installations the developer provides; later major retrofits raise that baseline only for the installations covered by a Form of Compliance.

### 5. Major retrofitting works (ss. 16–18, Sch. 3)

Part 3 applies to a prescribed building of types 1–13 where major retrofitting works are completed after 21 September 2012, and to types 14 and 15 where those works are completed after the relevant date.

| Trigger | Test |
|---|---|
| Sch. 3 item 1 | Addition or replacement of a building services installation specified in a code of practice, covering one or more places with a floor area or total floor area of **not less than 500 m²**, under the same series of works within **12 months**, in a unit or a common area |
| Clubhouse and car park | Each is a separate common area. All other common areas together are one common area |
| Same series | Aggregate the floor areas. Relevant factors include a single contractor, single agreement, single works order, timing, how the contractor is paid, and whether the works are one project in the plans and programme |
| Sch. 3 item 2(a) | Addition or replacement of a complete electrical circuit at a rating of **400 A or above** |
| Sch. 3 item 2(b) | Addition or replacement of a unitary air-conditioner or air-conditioning chiller of cooling or heating rating **at or exceeding 350 kW** |
| Sch. 3 item 2(c) | Addition or replacement of the motor drive and mechanical drive of a lift, an escalator or a passenger conveyor |

The responsible person of the unit, the owner of the common area, or the owner of the central installation, as at completion, must obtain a Form of Compliance within **2 months** after completion. Completion is when the installation, or all installations if more than one, is ready to be used for its principal function as designed.

The Form of Compliance is issued by a registered energy assessor who has personally inspected the installations in the **30 days** before the declaration and is satisfied they comply with the specified standards. The installation must then be maintained to the standard applied in that form.

**SD takeaway:** A fit-out of 500 m² or more, a new 400 A circuit, a chiller or unitary air-conditioner at or above 350 kW, or a lift or escalator drive replacement, each starts a two-month Form of Compliance clock.

### 6. Energy audit (ss. 21–25B, Schs. 4–6)

Part 4 applies to a Schedule 4 building. The owner must cause an energy audit of the **central** building services installations, carried out by a registered energy assessor in accordance with a code of practice.

| Schedule 4 type | Building |
|---|---|
| 1 | Commercial building |
| 2 | Portion of a composite building that is for commercial use |
| 3 | Principally education |
| 4 | Principally community services (community hall, social services centre, or both) |
| 5 | Principally municipal services (market, cooked food centre, library, cultural centre, indoor games hall, or two or more) |
| 6 | Principally medical and health care (hospital, clinic, rehabilitation centre) |
| 7 | Government building used principally to accommodate people during a Government function |
| 8 | Airport passenger terminal |
| 9 | Railway station |
| 10 | Principally a data centre |
| 11 | Portion of an industrial building occupied principally as a data centre |

| Relief | Rule |
|---|---|
| s. 21(2) | Director satisfied the building will cease to fall within Schedule 4 within **12 months** |
| s. 21(3) | Gross floor area, as in Building (Planning) Regulations reg. 23(3)(a), does **not exceed 7 000 m²**, and the audit is required on or after the relevant date. This relief does **not** apply to types 10 and 11 |
| s. 21(4) | Head of a Government department advises in writing that an audit would be contrary to the interests of national security. “Building” here includes a portion of a building |
| s. 25 | Written exemption for heritage conservation or technical or operational reasons |
| s. 25B | If s. 21(2) or (3) applies, the owner must notify the Director in writing within **28 days** of becoming aware of a change that may affect that non-application |
| Come-back audit | Where Part 4 or s. 22 later applies under s. 25A, the owner must cause an audit within **12 months** (extendable) |

Interval after an audit (Sch. 5):

| Building | Preceding audit date | Next audit |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 or 2 | Before the relevant date | **10 years** after the preceding audit date |
| Type 1 or 2 | On or after the relevant date | **5 years** after the preceding audit date |
| Types 3–11 | On or after the relevant date | **5 years** after the preceding audit date |

First audit where a Certificate of Compliance Registration has been issued (Sch. 6):

| Building | Certificate first issued | First audit within |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 or 2 | Before the relevant date | **10 years** after first issue |
| Type 1 or 2 | On or after the relevant date | **5 years** after first issue |
| Types 3–10 | Before the relevant date | The later of **10 years** after first issue, or **1 year** after the relevant date |
| Types 3–10 | On or after the relevant date | **5 years** after first issue |

First audit where no certificate has been issued, by date of occupation approval:

| Occupation approval | Type 1 or 2 | Types 3–10 |
|---|---|---|
| On or after 1 January 1988 | 12 months after 21 September 2012 | 12 months after the relevant date |
| After 31 December 1977 and before 1 January 1988 | 24 months after 21 September 2012 | 24 months after the relevant date |
| After 31 December 1969 and before 1 January 1978 | 36 months after 21 September 2012 | 36 months after the relevant date |
| On or before 31 December 1969 | 48 months after 21 September 2012 | 48 months after the relevant date |

Type 11 (industrial-building data-centre portion): if the data centre starts operation before the relevant date, the first audit is within **1 year** after the relevant date; if on or after the relevant date, within **5 years** after the start-of-operation date. That date is the date the central building services installations serving that portion start to consume electricity.

The owner must exhibit a copy of the Energy Audit Form in force in a conspicuous position at the main entrance. The registered energy assessor must, within **30 days** after issuing the form, send the Director a copy of the form, an energy audit report, and a Data Disclosure Form for publication of the technical information in the report.

**SD takeaway:** Provide a conspicuous main-entrance position for the Energy Audit Form, and treat central plant of a Schedule 4 building above 7 000 m² — and every data centre, regardless of that floor-area relief — as an audit building on a 5-year cycle once the relevant date has passed.

### 7. Codes, improvement notices, and joint owners (ss. 6, 26, 40)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Code of practice (s. 40) | The Director may issue or approve a code providing building energy efficiency standards, assessment requirements, and energy-audit requirements. A code is identified by Gazette notice with a commencement date |
| Specified standards | The standards and requirements for building energy efficiency or an energy audit set out in a code of practice |
| Improvement notice (s. 26) | May direct remedy of a contravention and may frame measures by reference to a code of practice |
| Joint owners (s. 6) | Compliance by one joint owner, or one of several responsible persons of a unit, is compliance by the others |
| Government (s. 3) | The Ordinance applies to the Government. The Government is not liable to prosecution or to any fee |

**SD takeaway:** Design to the Gazette code in force for the consent or the works, and name that edition in the stage one declaration.
