# LPG Code of Practice, Module 2 — Underground LPG Pipework (Issue 2)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Issue 2, May 2024 | Gas Standards Office, Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: Module 2 sets the recommended practice for design, installation, testing, commissioning, operation and maintenance of underground pipework carrying LPG vapour at medium or low pressure from an LPG compound or cylinder store to users. It covers the pipes, fittings and associated works, and it is read with the Gas Safety Ordinance (Cap. 51). It does not lay out the compound or the cylinder store. That is Module 1. It does not authorise an LPG gas main along or across a public road. Cap. 51B regulation 17(4) prohibits that, and Appendix C of this module is guidance where that regulation is in issue. The preface states that the requirements are guidance and are not a set of rules that cannot be changed.

## Regulatory Overview

Underground LPG vapour pipes from the compound or cylinder store to the users are laid with the cover in section 3.4, or with the extra mechanical protection in section 3.5 where that cover cannot be achieved.

A pipe through a solid wall or floor is sleeved, and a pipe through a poorly ventilated area uses the pipe-in-pipe arrangement in Appendix E (sections 3.6 and 3.7).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Depth of cover (sections 3.4 and 3.5)

| Location | Cover, measured from the surface to the top of the pipe |
|---|---|
| Private property, and footpaths with no likelihood of traffic (s. 3.4.1) | Minimum **600 mm**, unless extra protection under section 3.5 is provided |
| Carriageway (s. 3.4.1) | Minimum **1 000 mm**, unless that extra protection is provided |
| Even with extra protection (s. 3.5.1.2) | Not less than **100 mm** below the ground surface |
| Concrete slab (s. 3.5.3.1) | Not less than **40 mm** thick |
| Steel plate (s. 3.5.3.1) | Not less than **3 mm** thick, placed **100 mm** to **300 mm** above the LPG pipe or the casing pipe |
| Casing (s. 3.5.2) | Steel, sized for the pipe, with the LPG pipe held clear of the casing by insulators or centering devices. A casing under a carriageway is long enough and strong enough to limit external loading on the LPG pipe |
| Valve pit for a casing (s. 3.5.2.4) | Located **1.5 m** away from any building opening |

Appendix B2 illustrates the depth of cover and the extra mechanical protection.

**SD takeaway:** An LPG vapour pipe under a road is 1 000 mm to the top of the pipe, and under a private footpath 600 mm, and even a protected pipe stays at least 100 mm below the surface.

### 2. Sleeving, poorly ventilated routes, and settlement (sections 3.6, 3.7 and 3.9)

| Situation | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Solid wall or floor slab (s. 3.6 and Appendix D1) | Sleeved. Appendix D1 is the typical arrangement |
| Polyethylene-to-steel transition (Appendix D2) | Typical sleeving arrangement for the transition fitting |
| Poorly ventilated area (s. 3.7 and Appendix E) | Pipe-in-pipe |
| Ground settlement (s. 3.9 and Appendix I) | A settlement loop for underground steel pipes, as the typical example in Appendix I |
| Trench and identification (ss. 4.4 and 4.5) | Trench work and pipe identification follow section 4, and records and drawings are kept under section 4.6 |

**SD takeaway:** Do not run the underground LPG pipe through an unventilated void as a bare pipe. Use the pipe-in-pipe detail, and sleeve it through every solid wall and slab.
