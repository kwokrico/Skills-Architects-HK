# Code of Practice GU 21 — Town Gas for Catering
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
March 2016 | The Gas Authority

> Scope note: GU 21 sets requirements for town-gas installations for catering in restaurants and food-preparation establishments. It is issued by the Gas Authority. It does not cover LPG catering. That is Code of Practice GU 06. Domestic water heaters up to 70 kW remain GU 03, and a mechanical exhaust system up to 70 kW is GU 12, which this code cites for water heaters below that rating. Low pressure in this code is the Cap. 51C figure of not more than **7.5 kPa**.

## Regulatory Overview

Gas supply pipework inside the premises operates at not more than low pressure, **7.5 kPa** (760 mm water gauge), and installation pipes downstream of the gas meters should normally operate at not more than **2 kPa** (200 mm water gauge) wherever possible (paragraph 4.1.11).

No flueless gas water heater and no open-flued (natural draught) gas water heater shall be installed (paragraph 6.2.1).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Pipes, meters, and valves (section 4)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Route (para. 4.1.1) | As short as practicable. Diagonal routes are avoided. Joints are kept to a minimum |
| Separation (para. 4.1.6) | Pipes within the premises are separated from any other service, such as an electric conduit or cable, by at least **25 mm** |
| Unventilated places (para. 4.1.4) | Not installed in a place without adequate ventilation, so that leakage cannot accumulate |
| Identification (para. 4.1.5) | Pipes within the premises are readily identified |
| Pressure (para. 4.1.11) | Supply pipework inside the premises not exceeding **7.5 kPa**. Downstream of the meter, normally not greater than **2 kPa** |
| Meter on a sole means of escape (para. 4.2.1) | Not in a common area, of premises with more than one occupier, that is the only means of escape in case of fire, and not in a protected lobby. A meter installed in such a location before 1 April 1991 is replaced under Cap. 51C regulation 10(1) |
| Emergency control (para. 4.3) | As near as practicable to the point of gas-service entry, in an accessible location, labelled under Cap. 51C regulation 8 |
| Meter more than **2 m** from that control (para. 4.2.3) | A permanent English and Chinese notice on or near the meter indicates where the control is |
| Fire-safety valve (para. 4.4.1) | A gas isolation valve external to the kitchen, accessible, and labelled, for Fire Services officers |
| Valve outside the building (para. 4.4.2) | Where a service riser supplies a single kitchen, the valve may be on an outside wall accessible to the Fire Services, for example at ground or podium level, protected against unauthorised interference. One valve may serve as both the emergency control and the fire-safety valve where that is possible |
| Valve at the kitchen entrance (para. 4.4.3) | If it cannot be outside the building or outside the kitchen, it may be immediately within the kitchen entrance, with a permanent notice outside that entrance |

**SD takeaway:** Run town-gas catering pipe at not more than 7.5 kPa inside the premises and normally not more than 2 kPa after the meter, 25 mm off electrical conduit, with the fire-safety valve outside the kitchen or, if that is impossible, immediately inside the kitchen door and labelled outside.

### 2. Appliances and ventilation (sections 6 and 7)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Water heaters (para. 6.2.1) | No flueless heater and no open-flued (natural draught) heater |
| Heaters under **70 kW** (para. 6.2.2) | Preferably flued independently to open air, and installed to GU 12 |
| Flue into a kitchen exhaust duct (para. 6.2.3) | Allowed only with an interlock that disables the water heater if the exhaust fan fails or the duct blocks, under Cap. 51C regulation 24(4) |
| Natural ventilation for a water heater (para. 6.2.5, citing BS 6644) | Low-level vent at least **4 cm²/kW** of total rated net heat input. High-level vent at least **2 cm²/kW** |
| Mechanical ventilation for open-flued fanned-draught appliances (para. 6.2.5) | At least **2.8 m³/h** for each kW of total rated net heat input |
| Seating area (para. 6.3) | An accessible isolation valve near the point where the piped supply enters the seating accommodation, with bilingual open and shut instructions. Pipes inside the seating area, other than the final connection, are rigid steel with screwed joints |
| Serving counter (para. 6.4.3) | Supply pressure not exceeding low pressure of **2 kPa** (200 mm water gauge). Appliances have automatic ignition and a flame-failure device |
| Combustion air (paras. 7.1 and 7.4) | Fresh air for combustion and removal of products, under Cap. 51C regulation 23(1). **1 m³** of town gas requires at least **4 m³** of air for complete combustion and produces about **4.75 m³** of products of combustion to be removed to open air |
| Mechanical exhaust (paras. 7.2 and 7.3) | A permanent notice tells the user to turn the system on before any gas appliance is used. For a new system after this code takes effect, an electrical interlock, an air-pressure switch, or an equivalent keeps the exhaust running whenever the gas appliances are in use |
| Exhaust termination (para. 7.6) | Terminates in a well-ventilated safe location so that exhaust air is not recirculated |

**SD takeaway:** Size the kitchen fresh-air path for at least 4 m³ of air per 1 m³ of town gas, interlock the extract fan with the appliances, and do not specify a flueless or natural-draught open-flued water heater.
