# Code of Practice GU 03 — Domestic Gas Water Heaters (up to 70 kW)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Issue 2, October 2001 (Rev. 1, October 2014) | The Gas Authority

> Scope note: GU 03 is the Gas Authority's code, under section 9 of the Gas Safety Ordinance (Cap. 51), for installing domestic gas water heaters with a rated heat input up to **70 kW**. It is read with the manufacturer's instructions and does not override those instructions unless they conflict with statute. It does not cover a commercial kitchen water heater as such. Town-gas catering installations are in GU 21, and a mechanical exhaust system up to 70 kW is in GU 12. A bathroom still has to satisfy Cap. 51C regulation 27 and regulation 35A of the Building (Planning) Regulations (Cap. 123F).

## Regulatory Overview

For any new installation, a room-sealed gas water heater is the first choice, and where that is not feasible in a location other than a bathroom or shower room, a fanned-draught model should be used (section 3).

A bathroom or shower room takes a room-sealed heater for a new installation, and an open-flued fanned-draught heater is allowed there only as a replacement of an open-flued fanned-draught heater installed before 1 January 1994 (Appendix A).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which heater may go in which room (Appendix A)

| Room | Room-sealed, new | Open-flued fanned draught, new |
|---|---|---|
| Bathroom or shower room | Yes | No. A replacement is allowed only where an open-flued fanned-draught heater was installed before 1 January 1994 and is replaced by another of that type |
| Kitchen or utility | Yes | Yes |
| Balcony | Yes | Yes |
| Bedroom or living room | Not preferred | No |

Room ventilation communicating directly with outside air is not required for the room-sealed column. For an open-flued fanned-draught heater with rated heat input in excess of **7 kW** and up to **70 kW**, the minimum room ventilation is **5 cm²/kW**.

Compartment ventilation, which does not communicate directly with outside air, as free area per kW:

| Opening | Room-sealed | Open-flued fanned draught |
|---|---|---|
| To the room, high level | **10 cm²/kW** | **10 cm²/kW** |
| To the room, low level | **10 cm²/kW** | **20 cm²/kW** |
| To outside, high level | **5 cm²/kW** | **5 cm²/kW** |
| To outside, low level | **5 cm²/kW** | **10 cm²/kW** |

The asterisk on the room-sealed figures in Appendix A applies to natural-draught models. Fanned-draught models follow the manufacturer's instructions. Openings are permanent, with grilles or louvres of minimal resistance (section 4).

**SD takeaway:** Do not place an open-flued water heater in a bathroom, bedroom or living room on a new plan. A kitchen or balcony may take either a room-sealed heater or an open-flued fanned-draught heater.

### 2. Terminal position (Appendix B and the flue-termination row of Appendix A)

Natural-draught flue termination complies with regulation 35A of the Building (Planning) Regulations. A fanned-draught terminal is a minimum of **300 mm** below any opening into a building.

Appendix B, acceptable locations of room-sealed terminals:

| Dimension | Minimum |
|---|---|
| A, from any corner of the building | **300 mm**. May be reduced for a fanned-draught model. See the manufacturer's instructions |
| B, from any adjacent opening into the building | **300 mm** |
| C, from a facing wall or boundary | **1 500 mm**. May be reduced for a fanned-draught model |
| D, minimum plan width of a re-entrant | **1 500 mm**. May be reduced for a fanned-draught model |
| E, below a balcony or similar projection | **300 mm**. May be reduced for a fanned-draught model |
| Light-well plan (F × G), 10 storeys or fewer | **3 000 mm** |
| Light-well plan, 11 to 19 storeys | **4 000 mm** |
| Light-well plan, 20 or more storeys | **5 000 mm** |

**SD takeaway:** Keep a room-sealed terminal 300 mm off an opening and a corner, 1 500 mm off a facing wall, and size a light well to 3 m, 4 m or 5 m on plan according to the number of storeys.

A light well has no obstruction to the vertical flow of air. It is vented to external air below the lowest terminal. The vents have a minimum area of **0.05 m²** for each terminal facing into the light well, and a minimum internal dimension of **200 mm**. Extra vents are provided where air-conditioners take air from the light well. A tie beam should not reduce the open area of the face of a re-entrant by more than **20%** at any storey.

The internal view of Appendix B gives unobstructed dimensions K **100 mm**, N **150 mm** and Q **50 mm**, a height above floor P of **200 mm**, and a standard aperture L × M of **420 × 320 mm** or **240 × 240 mm**.

### 3. Outdoor heaters (Appendix C)

Separation is the shortest distance between the edge of the outdoor heater and the nearest edge or corner of another opening or object. The code states **1 MJ/h = 0.28 kW**.

| Dimension | Case | Natural draught | Fanned draught |
|---|---|---|---|
| P, point-to-point from an opening on a lateral wall | Not above **150 MJ/h** | **500 mm** | **300 mm** |
| P | Above 150 MJ/h up to **200 MJ/h** | **1 500 mm** | **500 mm** |
| P | Above **200 MJ/h** | **1 500 mm** | **1 500 mm** |
| P, horizontally in the direction of discharge, fan-assisted | — | — | **1 500 mm** |
| Q, horizontally from an opening on the wall behind the heater | Not above **150 MJ/h** | **500 mm** | **300 mm** |
| Q | Above **150 MJ/h** | **1 500 mm** | **300 mm** |
| R, vertically from an opening on the wall behind the heater | Not above **150 MJ/h** | **1 000 mm** | **300 mm** |
| R | Above **150 MJ/h** | **1 500 mm** | **300 mm** |

**SD takeaway:** An outdoor natural-draught heater rated above 150 MJ/h needs 1 500 mm clear of an opening on the lateral wall and on the wall behind it.
