# Code of Practice GU 06 — LPG for Catering in Commercial Premises
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
2nd Edition (Government Printer imprint April 1998) | The Gas Authority

> Scope note: GU 06 sets minimum safety standards for liquefied petroleum gas vapour used for catering in commercial premises such as restaurants and food-preparation establishments. It is addressed to restaurant operators and registered gas contractors. For town gas, the code points to the Hong Kong and China Gas operating procedures, and town-gas catering is now also covered by Code of Practice GU 21. It does not design an LPG compound or a cylinder store above the notifiable threshold. Those are Module 1 and Cap. 51B. The text layer of the body is a scan, and the figures below are taken from the cover and from the printed clauses on cylinder chambers.

## Regulatory Overview

LPG cylinders are not installed inside commercial premises where the aggregate water capacity exceeds **130 litres**, and a chamber is used for cylinder supply below that threshold (section 2.3).

A chamber is not placed where natural low-level ventilation cannot be provided, which precludes a basement, and it is not located above a drain or less than **1 m** from a drain (clauses 2.3.6.3 and 2.3.6.4).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Cylinder chamber (section 2.3)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Above 130 litres aggregate water capacity | Not an in-premises cylinder installation. Bulk storage above that capacity is outside this chamber arrangement |
| Basement (cl. 2.3.6.3) | No chamber where natural low-level ventilation cannot be provided. A basement is precluded |
| Drains (cl. 2.3.6.4) | Not above a drain, and not less than **1 m** from a drain |
| Doors (cl. 2.3.6.5) | Do not open directly into a restaurant seating area inside the premises, into an air-conditioned pedestrian walkway, or onto a highway |
| Naked flame (cl. 2.3.6.6) | A chamber with two or more cylinders inside a commercial kitchen or food-preparation area is at least **2 m** from any fixed source of naked flame |
| Chamber construction (cl. 2.3.6.7) | Concrete, or a material of at least **2 hours** fire resistance (the code cites BS 476), with enough strength to protect the cylinders |
| Doors (cl. 2.3.6.8) | Steel doors are preferred. As a minimum, at least **1 hour** fire resistance. Kept closed, and locked if the chamber is external |
| Electrical apparatus (cl. 2.3.6.10) | None, unless flameproof to an international standard (the code cites BS 4683) |
| Above **72 litres** and not exceeding **130 litres** (cl. 2.3.7.2) | Ventilated direct to open air. The code equates 72 litres to about 32 kg of LPG and 130 litres to about 50 kg |
| Not exceeding **72 litres** (cl. 2.3.7.3) | Still vented direct to open air if possible, or by a duct. Only where each cylinder has its own low-pressure cylinder regulator may the chamber vent into the kitchen, and only if that kitchen is well ventilated to open air |
| Free area (cl. 2.3.7.4) | High and low level. Low level: **100 cm²** or **1/100** of the enclosed floor area, whichever is greater. High level: **50 cm²** or **1/200** of the enclosed floor area, whichever is greater |
| Direction of vents (cl. 2.3.7.1) | Not directed into adjacent buildings, a designated means of escape, an indoor seating area, or a hazardous area |
| Label | "LPG CHAMBER" in English and Chinese on the outside of the doors |

Outlet pressure from the chamber should not exceed the normal inlet pressure for low-pressure appliances, stated as **300 mm** water gauge. Up to **760 mm** water gauge is allowed if a service regulator, or an individual appliance regulator, protects appliances designed for 300 mm water gauge. The code equates 760 mm water gauge to **7.5 kPa**.

**SD takeaway:** Put restaurant LPG cylinders in a 2-hour chamber at ground level, at least 1 m from a drain and 2 m from a naked flame, with low-level free area of at least 100 cm² or one-hundredth of the floor, and do not vent it into the seating area or the escape route.

### 2. Pipework and the fire-safety valve (section 2.2)

Gas pipes inside the premises are separated from any electric conduit or cable by at least **25 mm**. Pipework is not installed in an unventilated void or in a room that houses air-conditioning or ventilation equipment for the premises.

A fire-safety valve is located so that Fire Services officers can use it. Where it cannot be external to the building or external to the kitchen, it may be immediately within the kitchen entrance, with a permanent notice outside that entrance stating where the valve is.

**SD takeaway:** Keep the LPG pipe 25 mm off electrical conduit, out of unventilated voids, and give the kitchen a labelled fire-safety valve at the entrance if it cannot sit outside.
