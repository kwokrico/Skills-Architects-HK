# Avoidance of Damage to Gas Pipes (2nd Edition)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
2nd Edition, 31 August 2018 | The Gas Authority

> Scope note: This code sets the safe system of work for construction, demolition and utility work near gas pipes, so that buried and aboveground gas pipes are located before the ground is opened. It is approved guidance under the Gas Safety Ordinance (Cap. 51). It does not authorise a new gas installation, and it does not replace construction approval for a notifiable gas installation under the Gas Safety (Gas Supply) Regulations (Cap. 51B). Registered gas supply companies and the public-road LPG crossings are listed in the supplementary information issued with this edition, not in the code body.

## Regulatory Overview

Before digging, the working party obtains the gas-pipe plans, carries out an underground utility survey, and opens hand-dug trial holes, and mechanical plant is not used until the pipe position is known (sections on safe systems of work, trial holes, and mechanical excavators).

Table 1 sets the distance within which the stated work is "works in the vicinity of gas pipes" and therefore requires the precautions in this code, including consultation with the gas-pipe owner.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is "in the vicinity" (Table 1)

| Type of works | Distance |
|---|---|
| Trench or other excavation in stable ground, up to **1.5 m** deep | **10 m** |
| The same, over **1.5 m** and up to **5 m** deep | **15 m** |
| The same, over **5 m** deep | **20 m** |
| Excavation under a gas pipe, including tunnelling, boring, caverns, driving headings and pipe jacking | Always consult, regardless of depth under the gas pipe |
| Welding or other hot work near exposed metallic gas pipes or aboveground installations | **10 m** |
| Welding or other hot work near exposed polyethylene gas pipes | **20 m** |
| Works near an LPG storage compound | **15 m** |
| Piling, percussion moling, pipe bursting or jacking | **15 m** |
| Works near high-pressure pipelines and associated above-ground and below-ground installations | **20 m** |
| Ground investigation and any drilling, core sampling or ramming | **30 m** |
| Vertical, horizontal or inclined penetration, including sheet piling and soil nails | **30 m** |
| Use of explosives | **60 m** |

The supplementary information repeats that no work should be attempted within **15 m** of an LPG storage compound until the owner or operator has been consulted and a representative is in attendance.

**SD takeaway:** A site investigation borehole is a 30 m consultation, sheet piling is a 30 m consultation, and any work within 15 m of an LPG compound waits for the gas operator on site.

### 2. Trial holes and mechanical plant (sections 50–56)

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Trial holes (s. 50) | Dug only by hand tools to expose and confirm buried gas pipes |
| Hand-held power tools breaking paved surfaces (s. 51) | A horizontal clearance of **1 m** from the side of any gas pipe. Depth limited to **150 mm** in footpaths and **300 mm** in roads |
| Hand-held power tools over a marked gas pipe (s. 54) | Not used directly over the marked position unless the pipe has already been found by hand digging and is at least **300 mm** below the surface to be broken, or a physical barrier stops the tool striking it |
| Mechanical excavators, hand-held power tools and vertical penetration machines (s. 55) | A minimum clearance of **1 m** from the side of any gas pipe to the point where the machine is used. At a sheet-pile crossing, the gas pipe is exposed and protected before work starts |
| Where 1 m cannot be achieved (s. 56) | The gas-pipe owner or operator is consulted, and the main contractor, or whoever is in charge of the site, keeps the record |

**SD takeaway:** The first metre beside a gas pipe is hand digging. An excavator, a breaker and a piling rig stay at least 1 m off the side of the pipe unless the owner has agreed a closer method in writing.

### 3. Cover is a guide, not a guarantee (s. 58)

| Location | Normal depth of cover stated in the code |
|---|---|
| Footway | **450 mm** |
| Carriageway | **900 mm** |
| High-pressure and intermediate-pressure B pipelines | **1.1 m** |

The code states that these depths are only a guide. Pipes may be shallower or deeper. Service pipes are normally connected to the top of mains and may be shallower, including near the entry to a building. Earlier pipes may have had their cover altered by later roadworks.

**SD takeaway:** Do not set a pile or a trench depth from the 450 mm or 900 mm figures alone. Prove the pipe with a hand-dug trial hole.
