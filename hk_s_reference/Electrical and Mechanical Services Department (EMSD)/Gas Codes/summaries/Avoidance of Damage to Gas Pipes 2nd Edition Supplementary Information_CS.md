# Avoidance of Damage to Gas Pipes — Supplementary Information
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Supplementary information to the 2nd Edition | The Gas Authority

> Scope note: This note lists the registered gas supply companies that have piped gas systems in Hong Kong and states how a contractor contacts them before work near their pipes. It supplements the Code of Practice on Avoidance of Damage to Gas Pipes, 2nd Edition (31 August 2018). The PDF does not print a separate issue date on its cover. It does not itself approve new pipe routes. It records where underground LPG pipes already exist in public roads, and it repeats the stand-off from an LPG compound.

## Regulatory Overview

At the date of this note there are six registered gas supply companies with piped gas systems, and most underground gas pipes belong to The Hong Kong and China Gas Company Limited, whose reticulated systems cover the urban areas of Hong Kong Island and Kowloon and the new towns (paragraphs 1 and 3).

No work may be attempted within **15 m** of an LPG storage compound until the gas-pipe owner or operator has been consulted and a representative is in attendance, and some works still require the prior approval of the Gas Authority under the Gas Safety (Gas Supply) Regulations (Cap. 51B) (paragraph 8).

**SD takeaway:** Ask Hong Kong and China Gas for the street mains, and ask the estate's LPG distributor, using the 24-hour number at the compound entrance, before any work inside an LPG-piped estate.

### 2. LPG estates and public-road crossings (paragraphs 6–10 and Table 2)

LPG compounds and piped systems in large residential estates are generally operated by gas distributors. Before work in those estates, the distributor or the estate management office is contacted. The distributor keeps the plans of the pipes in the development (paragraph 6).

Table 2 lists underground LPG pipes in public roads, including road crossings and footpath runs at Butterfly Estate, Tai Yuen Estate, South Horizons, Heng Fa Chuen, Greenwood and Neptune Terraces, Kwong Fuk Estate, Ma Wan, Wah Fu Estate, Lower Baguio Villa, Nga Ning Court, and Mei Foo Sun Chuen, with the supply company named against each location (Table 2).

A damaged or leaking gas pipe is reported to the emergency services on 999, and then to the LPG company's 24-hour number. Unauthorised repair is not attempted (paragraph 9).

The note also records that naphtha pipelines to the Tai Po and Ma Tau Kok plants are not gas pipes within the scope of the code, and that care is still required because they are of strategic importance to gas supply (paragraph 3, footnote).

**SD takeaway:** Treat the public-road LPG crossings in Table 2 as live gas pipes even though Cap. 51B regulation 17(4) otherwise forbids a new LPG gas main along or across a road.
