# Code of Practice GU 12 — Mechanical Exhaust for Gas Appliances (up to 70 kW)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
January 2004 | The Gas Authority

> Scope note: GU 12 covers a mechanical exhaust system used to vent flue gas from a gas appliance of rated heat input up to **70 kW**, mainly in non-domestic premises, though an isolated domestic installation may also occur (section 1.1). It is approved guidance under the Gas Safety Ordinance (Cap. 51). Section 3 on ventilation applies to gas appliances with or without a flue terminal, unless the code says otherwise. The later sections on physical connection apply where a flue pipe or duct joins the appliance to the system. It does not size a domestic room-sealed terminal. That is GU 03. It does not replace the Buildings (Ventilating Systems) Regulations (Cap. 123J) or the Air Pollution Control (Furnaces, Ovens and Chimneys) (Installation and Alteration) Regulations (Cap. 311A).

## Regulatory Overview

The mechanical exhaust system always discharges to outside air, and the discharge must not adversely affect a sensitive receptor (section 3.2).

The system is designed and operated under negative static pressure at all times, with the fan as near as possible to the terminal, and an interlock stops the appliance if the exhaust fan is not in safe working order (sections 3.4 and 5.2).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Fan, pressure, and fire compartment (sections 3 and 5)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Discharge (s. 3.2) | Always to outside air |
| Insulation (s. 3.3) | Internal thermal insulation is non-combustible and complies with the Buildings (Ventilating Systems) Regulations (Cap. 123J) |
| Pressure (s. 3.4) | Negative static pressure at all times. The fan is as near as possible to the exit |
| Draught (s. 3.5) | Satisfies the appliance manufacturer's draught requirements and the Fire Services Department's fire-protection requirements. The contractor keeps that record for at least **two years** after the owner or user starts to operate the system |
| Combustion air (s. 3.6.1) | Adequate combustion air at all times for simultaneous operation of all fully loaded gas appliances |
| Interlock (s. 5.2) | An electrical interlock or an air-pressure switch, with fail-shut and lockout, is the first choice. It links the appliance power supply to the suction-fan supply and includes a means to ensure the fan is in normal safe working order. Another design is considered only when a competent assessor has shown in writing that fail-shut and lockout are met |
| One compartment (s. 5.3) | Appliances in one fire compartment are not connected to a mechanical exhaust system that serves more than one fire compartment |
| Failure of the fan (Cap. 51C reg. 24(4), applied in s. 3.1) | The appliance is prevented from operating if the fan provided for the flue fails |

**SD takeaway:** Put the exhaust fan at the discharge end so the duct is under suction, interlock every gas appliance to that fan, and do not let one exhaust system serve two fire compartments.
