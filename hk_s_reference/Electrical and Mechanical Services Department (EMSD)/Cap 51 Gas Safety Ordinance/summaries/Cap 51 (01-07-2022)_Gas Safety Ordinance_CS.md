# Cap. 51 — Gas Safety Ordinance
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated 1 July 2022 | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: Cap. 51 regulates the importation, manufacture, storage, transport, supply and use of town gas, liquefied petroleum gas, natural gas, and mixtures of those gases, and it is the ordinance under which the Gas Authority approves codes of practice. It defines the notifiable gas installation, the store, and the pressure classes used by the subsidiary regulations. It does not set flue-terminal dimensions, kitchen ventilation rates, or LPG-compound separation distances. Those sit in Cap. 51B, Cap. 51C, and the gas codes of practice. It does not apply to the use of liquefied petroleum gas for and incidental to entertainment special effects under the Entertainment Special Effects Ordinance (Cap. 560) (s. 3(3)).

## Regulatory Overview

Subject to the fee exemption for the Government, the Ordinance binds the Government (s. 3(1) and (2)).

A failure to observe an approved code of practice is not in itself a civil or criminal liability, but where a charge alleges contravention of a requirement for which a code was approved, a relevant provision of that code is admissible in the criminal proceedings (s. 10).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What counts as gas, a pipe, and a store (s. 2)

| Term | Meaning for layout |
|---|---|
| Gas | Town gas, liquefied petroleum gas, natural gas, or any mixture, whether liquid or vapour. Supply through pipes or in containers is included. Supply of liquefied petroleum gas in disposable cylinders is not "supply" |
| Town gas | Any gas which is primarily a mixture of hydrogen and methane |
| Natural gas | Primarily methane from natural strata, and includes substitute natural gas and synthetic natural gas |
| Liquefied petroleum gas | A mixture of hydrocarbons primarily consisting of butanes, butylenes, propane or propylene, or all or any of those hydrocarbons |
| In bulk | Liquefied petroleum gas in a vessel with a water capacity of more than **150 litres** |
| Mini-tank | A receptacle with a water capacity of more than **150 litres** but not more than **450 litres**, used to contain liquefied petroleum gas |
| Tank | A bulk tank or a mini-tank |
| Gasholder | A receptacle for town gas or natural gas with a storage capacity of more than **140 standard cubic metres**, excluding a receptacle that is a gas main |
| Standard cubic metre | One cubic metre of the gas at **15.0°C**, at **101.325 kPa** absolute, under dry conditions |
| Store (noun) | A place where there is a gasholder that contains or has contained town gas or natural gas; a liquefied petroleum gas container with a liquid-withdrawal facility from which gas is or is to be supplied to consumers by gas pipes; or liquefied petroleum gas containers in one place whose aggregated nominal water capacity is more than **130 litres** |
| Domestic premises | Premises constructed or intended to be used for habitation. Any other premises are non-domestic premises |
| Gas appliance | An appliance which uses gas to provide lighting, heating or cooling. A boiler within the Boilers and Pressure Vessels Ordinance (Cap. 56) is not a gas appliance |
| Installation pipe | A pipe used to supply gas to a particular consumer, including gas fittings used with it, but not a service pipe (other than one comprised in a primary meter installation) and not a pipe comprised in a gas appliance |
| Service pipe | A pipe used to supply gas from a gas main, or from a store of liquefied petroleum gas, to not more than one building, including the gas meter control valve and the service valve |
| Service riser | The vertical sections of a service pipe used or capable of being used to supply gas to more than one floor, including the horizontal sections between those vertical sections |
| Service valve | A valve in a service pipe, for controlling the supply, not situated inside any premises |
| Works | Construction works, including building works under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), site formation, and related ground and marine works listed in s. 2 |

**SD takeaway:** Cylinder storage whose aggregated nominal water capacity is more than 130 litres is a store, and a vessel of more than 150 litres water capacity is bulk liquefied petroleum gas.

### 2. Notifiable gas installation (s. 2)

A notifiable gas installation is a gas installation which is, consists of, or uses any of the following:

| Paragraph | Installation |
|---|---|
| (a) | A terminal for the importation of liquefied petroleum gas, or of natural gas in liquid form |
| (b) | Works for the manufacture of town gas, substitute natural gas, or synthetic natural gas |
| (c) | Gasholders, including the pressure-raising and control equipment used with them |
| (d) | High-pressure gas mains or service pipes |
| (e) | A pressure-regulating installation supplied from an intermediate-pressure or high-pressure gas main or service pipe, unless its volumetric capacity is less than **30 standard cubic metres per hour** |
| (f) | A store containing liquefied petroleum gas, including, where gas is supplied to consumers by pipes from that store, the outlet pipework, vaporisers and pressure-regulating installations used with it |
| (g) | A process that transfers liquefied petroleum gas in liquid form from one container, other than a disposable cylinder, to another, except transfer from a ship or road tanker into a tank or road tanker |

Construction and use of a notifiable gas installation need approval under the Gas Safety (Gas Supply) Regulations (Cap. 51B).

**SD takeaway:** An LPG store, including the pipework that leaves it, is a notifiable gas installation, and so is a pressure-regulating installation of 30 standard cubic metres per hour or more fed from intermediate or high pressure.

### 3. Codes of practice (ss. 9 and 10)

The Gas Authority may approve and issue codes of practice, or approve codes issued by others, to give practical guidance on requirements under the Ordinance, and must identify the code and its effective date by notice in the Gazette (s. 9).

Failure to follow an approved code is not itself an offence. In a prosecution for contravening a requirement for which a code was approved at the time, a provision of the code that appears relevant is admissible, and a failure to observe it may be relied on (s. 10).

**SD takeaway:** Treat an approved gas code as the practical test of the regulation it supports, and check the Gazette notice for the edition that is in force.
