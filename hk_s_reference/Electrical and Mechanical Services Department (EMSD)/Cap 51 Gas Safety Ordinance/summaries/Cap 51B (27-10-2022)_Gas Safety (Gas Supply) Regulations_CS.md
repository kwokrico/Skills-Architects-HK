# Cap. 51B — Gas Safety (Gas Supply) Regulations
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated 27 October 2022 | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: Cap. 51B governs construction and use of a notifiable gas installation, liquefied petroleum gas containers and storage installations, vaporisers, gas pipes, and pressure-regulating installations. Pressure classes in these regulations are the classes used to decide whether an installation is notifiable under Cap. 51. It does not set domestic flue-terminal positions or bathroom water-heater types. Those are in Cap. 51C and Code of Practice GU 03. It does not set the metre separations inside an LPG compound. Those are in Module 1 of the LPG code of practice.

## Regulatory Overview

No person may construct a notifiable gas installation unless construction approval has been obtained, and no person may use a notifiable gas installation, or supply gas to it, unless use is approved under regulation 6 (regs. 3 and 6).

A gas pipe must be of a size and strength to convey the gas safely at its operating pressure, and a gas main for liquefied petroleum gas must not be installed along or across a road (reg. 17).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Pressure classes (reg. 2)

| Class | Pressure |
|---|---|
| Low pressure | Not more than **7.5 kPa** |
| Medium pressure | More than **7.5 kPa** but not more than **240 kPa** |
| Intermediate pressure | More than **240 kPa** but not more than **700 kPa** |
| High pressure | More than **700 kPa** |

**SD takeaway:** A main or service above 700 kPa is high pressure and is a notifiable gas installation under Cap. 51, and a pressure-regulating installation fed from above 240 kPa is notifiable unless its capacity is under 30 standard cubic metres per hour.

### 2. Notifiable gas installation and LPG storage (regs. 3, 6, 10, 11)

Construction approval and approval of use are required before a notifiable gas installation is constructed or used, and gas must not be supplied to it in contravention of regulation 3 (regs. 3 and 6).

The owner of an LPG storage installation must keep it well ventilated and accessible for cylinder or tank replacement, maintenance, and fire-fighting (reg. 11(a) and (b)). Radiation walls, fixed water-drenching systems, diversion kerbs, and the distances from the tanks or cylinders to a property boundary, a fixed source of ignition, and a building other than the building in which the installation sits, must minimise ignition of escaping gas before it disperses and must protect the installation from radiation of a fire outside it (reg. 11(c)). Ventilation and explosion-relief measures other than explosion-relief panels are required (reg. 11(d)).

A fixed maximum liquid level on a tank must be set so that at **47.5°C** the tank will not be more than **97%** full of liquefied petroleum gas in liquid form, and at **52.5°C** it will not be full (reg. 10(1)). No person may fill a tank or cylinder so that, at **47.5°C**, the tank is more than **97%** full or the cylinder more than **95%** full, or so that at **52.5°C** either is full (reg. 10(2)).

**SD takeaway:** An LPG store is not a plant room that can be boxed in. It needs ventilation, fire-fighting access, and separation from the boundary, ignition sources, and other buildings, with the distances taken from the approved LPG code rather than from this regulation's qualitative test alone.

### 3. Gas pipes (regs. 17–19, 23A)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| LPG main on a road (reg. 17(4)) | No person shall install a gas main for liquefied petroleum gas along or across a road |
| Service pipe of **50 mm** internal diameter or more, a special-risk premises, or a pipe supplying more than one primary meter (reg. 17(6)) | A valve in a readily accessible position outside, and as near as practicable to, the boundary of the premises |
| Above ground (reg. 17(7)) | Protected, so far as reasonably practicable, against accidental damage |
| Service pipe in a service duct (reg. 17(9)) | The duct must be ventilated so that an escape can disperse without prejudicing the safety of occupants |
| Service riser (reg. 17(10)) | A service riser must not be installed in domestic premises, or in non-domestic premises other than an industrial undertaking, where the Building Authority approved the building works on or after **1 January 1991**, unless the riser is in a service duct ventilated so that any escape disperses to air outside the premises |
| Through a solid floor or standing (reg. 18(1)) | Enclosed in a sleeve that does not prevent normal movement of the pipe |
| Through a cavity wall (reg. 18(3)) | Shortest practicable route, in a gastight sleeve |
| Structure and fire resistance (reg. 19) | The pipe must not be installed in a way that would impair the structure of the premises or the fire resistance of any part of its structure |
| Works nearby (reg. 23A) | Before works in the vicinity of a gas pipe, all reasonable steps to ascertain its location and position, and all reasonable measures to protect it from damage likely to prejudice safety |

Where the drop across a pressure-regulating installation may take the gas temperature below **0°C**, the installation must be protected against ice (reg. 21(1)(c)). Regulation 21(1) does not apply to an installation that will be supplied only at low pressure (reg. 21(2)).

**SD takeaway:** On a building approved on or after 1 January 1991, put the service riser in a duct that vents to outside air, and do not run an LPG gas main along or across a road unless a separate approval path, recorded in the gas-pipe code, already covers that pipe.

### 4. Where a pressure-regulating installation may sit (reg. 22)

Regulation 22(1) does not apply to an installation supplied only at low pressure (reg. 22(4)). For any other installation, relief gas must be able to disperse safely, maintenance access is required, and the installation must be protected so far as reasonably practicable from flooding, subsidence, and traffic if it is near a road (reg. 22(1)).

A pressure-regulating installation must not be put in a room unless the room has space for maintenance, a sufficient means of egress, gastight common walls, walls of solid construction, good ventilation, and explosion-relief measures other than explosion-relief panels (reg. 22(2)).

Below ground, the location must not be subject to flooding, or the regulator breathers must be extended above the likely flood level so that water will not enter the diaphragm chambers, and the pit must carry the expected surface loading, keep surface water from damaging the installation, and allow access for maintenance and ventilation (reg. 22(3)).

**SD takeaway:** An intermediate-pressure or high-pressure regulator room is a ventilated solid room with explosion relief and a way out. It is not a cupboard on a means of escape, and it is not an unvented pit.
