# Cap. 51C — Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated 4 August 2022 | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: Cap. 51C governs gas fittings in premises: emergency controls, meters, installation pipes, flues, and gas appliances, including which water heaters may serve a bathroom. Low pressure in these regulations is a pressure of not more than **7.5 kPa** (reg. 2). It does not set the millimetre positions of a room-sealed terminal or the free-area table for a kitchen hood. Those are in Code of Practice GU 03, GU 12 and GU 21. It applies to reinstallation of a gas fitting as it applies to installation, with a limited saving for a fitting lawfully installed before commencement when it is taken out only for maintenance or for other work and put back (reg. 3).

## Regulatory Overview

A new gas supply must have an accessible emergency control at the point where the gas pipe enters the premises, and, where domestic premises are supplied, also as near as reasonably practicable to the point of entry into those domestic premises (reg. 8).

No person shall install a flueless gas water heater, and no person shall install an instantaneous open-flued (natural draught) gas water heater (regs. 35(1) and 36).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Emergency control, meters, and the means of escape (regs. 8, 10, 13)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Emergency control (reg. 8(1)) | Required before a new supply is provided. Accessible, and as near as reasonably practicable to the point of entry. If the pipe enters outside the domestic premises, a control is also required as near as reasonably practicable to that point of entry |
| Handle (reg. 8(2)) | The key or lever is parallel to the pipe when open. If it does not move only horizontally, gas cannot pass when the key or lever has been moved downwards as far as practicable. Open and shut are marked in English and Chinese |
| Notice (reg. 8(3)) | If the control is not part of a primary meter installation, a permanent bilingual notice "Gas Emergency Control 氣體緊急控制閥" is displayed on or near the operating means, with the shut-off instruction, the 999 call, and the gas company's emergency number |
| Meter on a sole means of escape (reg. 10(1)) | No new meter in a common area that is the only means of escape in case of fire, unless it replaces an existing meter and either the meter or its box is of fire-resisting construction with automatic self-closing doors, or the pipe immediately upstream incorporates a device that cuts off the gas if the device exceeds **95°C** |
| Meter far from the control (reg. 13(2)) | If the meter is more than **2 metres** from, or out of sight of, the nearest upstream emergency control, a permanent bilingual notice on or near the meter must indicate where that control is |
| Electrical continuity (reg. 9) | Where necessary to avoid danger, electrical cross-bonding is provided and maintained until the work is completed |
| Earth terminal (reg. 16(2)) | When an installation pipe is connected to a primary meter installation and cross-bonding may be necessary, an earth terminal is provided not more than **600 mm** downstream of that installation |

**SD takeaway:** Do not put a new gas meter in a common corridor that is the only escape route unless it is a fire-resisting replacement or it has an upstream thermal cut-off at 95°C, and keep the emergency control at the point of entry.

### 2. Installation pipes (regs. 7, 16–18, 21, 22)

No person may alter premises so that a gas fitting would contravene these regulations if it were installed after the alteration, and no person may affect a gas fitting, flue, or means of ventilation so that later use is dangerous (reg. 7).

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Position (reg. 16(1)) | Not in a position where it cannot be used with safety, having regard to other pipes, drains, sewers, cables, conduits, electrical apparatus, and the structure |
| Through a wall, floor or standing (reg. 17(2)) | Shortest practicable route. In a wall, protected against corrosion by painting and enclosed in a sleeve or wrapped. In a floor or standing, painted and enclosed in a sleeve |
| Cavity wall (reg. 17(3)) | Shortest practicable route, in a gastight sleeve |
| Foundations (reg. 17(4)) | Not under the foundations, and not in the ground under the base of a load-bearing wall or under footings |
| Unventilated void (reg. 17(5)) | Not in an unventilated shaft, duct or void |
| Structure (reg. 18) | Must not impair the structure or the fire-resistance of any part of the structure |
| Non-domestic marking (reg. 21) | An accessible part of an installation pipe elsewhere than in domestic premises is permanently marked, in English and Chinese, so that it is readily recognisable as a gas pipe |
| Large consumer (reg. 22) | Where a service pipe of internal diameter not less than **50 mm** supplies at least two floors of non-domestic premises, or a floor that has areas with a separate gas supply, each incoming installation pipe to those floors or areas has an isolation valve in a conspicuous and readily accessible position, and a permanent line diagram near the primary meters showing every installation pipe of internal diameter **25 mm** or more, together with meters, emergency controls, isolation valves, pressure test points, condensate receivers, and electrical cross-bonding |

**SD takeaway:** Keep installation pipes out of unventilated voids and out from under foundations and load-bearing wall bases, and on a large non-domestic supply provide an accessible isolation valve and a line diagram at the meters.

### 3. Appliances, flues, and bathrooms (regs. 23, 23A, 24, 27, 29, 35, 36)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Domestic appliance with a flue (reg. 23(2)) | Connected to a piped supply only by a permanently fixed rigid pipe |
| Isolation at the appliance (reg. 23(4)) | Means of shutting off the supply at the inlet, unless that is not reasonably practicable |
| Type approval (reg. 23A) | A domestic gas appliance which burns gas to provide lighting, heating or cooling must comply with the Gas Safety (Miscellaneous) Regulations, except reinstallation of an appliance installed before **1 January 2003** |
| Flue (reg. 24(1)) | An appliance may be installed to a flue only if the flue is so constructed and in such condition that the appliance can operate safely with it |
| Enclosure around a flue (reg. 24(3)) | The enclosure is sealed so that spillage of products of combustion cannot pass into any room other than the room in which the appliance is installed |
| Fan on a flue (reg. 24(4)) | If a power-operated fan is installed for a flue, means must safely prevent operation of the appliance if the fan fails |
| Bathroom, from **1 January 1994** (reg. 27(3)) | No gas water heater other than a room-sealed gas water heater in any bathroom, except that an open-flued (fanned draught) heater may replace an open-flued (fanned draught) heater that is already in that bathroom |
| Suitable provision (reg. 27(1) and (2)) | Where suitable provision has been made for a room-sealed gas water heater to serve a bathroom, whether the provision is in the bathroom or elsewhere, only a room-sealed heater may serve that bathroom, and it must use that provision. "Room-sealed gas water heater" and "suitable provision" have the meanings in regulation 35A(4) of the Building (Planning) Regulations (Cap. 123F) |
| Manual damper (reg. 29(2) and (3)) | No manually operated damper on a domestic gas appliance, and no domestic appliance on a flue that has a manual damper unless the damper is permanently fixed open |
| Flueless water heater (reg. 35(1)) | No person shall install a flueless gas water heater |
| Open-flued natural draught instantaneous heater (reg. 36) | No person shall install an instantaneous open-flued (natural draught) gas water heater |

**SD takeaway:** A bathroom water heater is room-sealed. Do not draw a flueless heater or an instantaneous open-flued natural-draught heater, and if the flue is fan-assisted, the appliance must shut down when the fan fails.
