# Code of Practice for Building Energy Audit (2024 Edition)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
EAC 2024 | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department | Issued under Part 9 of the Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance (Cap. 610)

> Scope note: This code (the Energy Audit Code, EAC) sets the technical guidance for an energy audit of central building services installations under Cap. 610. An audit conducted in accordance with this EAC is deemed to satisfy the Ordinance in the technical aspects (cl. 1.2). It is not the design standard for new installations; that is the Building Energy Code. This file is the 2024 edition without Addendum No. 1/2025.

## Regulatory Overview

The EAC applies to energy audits of the central building services installations in a building of a category prescribed in Schedule 4 of the Ordinance (cl. 3.1).

To satisfy the Ordinance, the audit is conducted to this EAC for those central installations as a minimum, save for an exclusion or exemption under the Ordinance (cl. 4.1).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is audited (cls. 2, 4.1–4.3)

Central building services installation has the Ordinance meaning: an installation that does not solely serve a unit, or, in a prescribed building with no common area, any installation except one that solely serves a unit and is owned by a person who is not the owner of the building.

The code’s examples:

| Installation | Building with a designated common area | Building without a designated common area |
|---|---|---|
| Lighting | In the common area | Anywhere, unless it is in an individual unit and separately owned by the responsible person of that unit who is not the building owner |
| Air-conditioning | Not separately owned by the responsible person of an individual unit | Anywhere, unless separately owned by the responsible person of an individual unit who is not the building owner |
| Electrical | On the incoming side of an electricity supplier’s meter for an individual unit | Anywhere, unless on the outgoing side of that meter for a unit whose responsible person is not the building owner |
| Lift and escalator | In the common area, unless solely serving an individual unit | Anywhere, unless it solely serves an individual unit and is separately owned by that unit’s responsible person who is not the building owner |

In a composite building, clause 4.1 applies only to the central building services installation solely serving the commercial portion (cl. 4.2).

The owner may apply in writing under section 25 of the Ordinance to exempt a central installation from the section 22(4) audit if an audit is technically or operationally undesirable, provided the total rating of all circuit protective devices (whichever are nearer the supply side) solely governing the electricity supply of that central installation does not exceed **100 A, 1-phase** (cl. 4.3).

**SD takeaway:** Meter and document central plant, common-area lighting, landlord risers on the incoming side of tenant meters, and shared lifts; a composite building’s audit stops at the central services that solely serve the commercial portion.

### 2. What the audit must collect (cls. 5–7)

An energy audit identifies energy management opportunities (EMO) in three categories (cl. 6.2):

| Category | Investment |
|---|---|
| I | Housekeeping. Practically no cost and no disruption to building operation |
| II | Changes in operation, with relatively low cost |
| III | Relatively higher capital cost |

Essential information (cl. 7.2) includes building type, major usage and nominal operating hours; number of blocks and floors and total internal floor area; approximate portion that is common area; date of occupation approval; types, quantities, rated capacities, rated power, coefficient of performance where applicable, and controls; total power factor and total harmonic distortion; energy bills for at least **12 consecutive months**; operational data from metering or a building management system; and EMO implemented in the past **5 years**.

Internal floor area is the floor area of all enclosed spaces measured to the internal faces of enclosing external and/or party walls. Energy utilization index (EUI) is total energy consumption of the central building services installations for a specific period divided by the total internal floor area (kWh/m²/annum).

Analysis (cl. 7.3) records total annual energy consumption with at least 12 consecutive months of data, a breakdown among major installations, and annual EUI. Net energy consumption of electric-vehicle charging may be deducted if a separate meter is provided (cl. 7.3.2). Where records are incomplete, calculation from partial records, in-situ measurements and equipment ratings is acceptable if the method is recorded in the report (cl. 7.3.3).

The report must cover equipment of rated power **5 kW or above** for air-conditioning fans and pumps, and for other equipment, plus chiller, cooling tower, heat pump, VRF and unitary air-conditioner at that threshold, lighting, renewable energy, and lifts, escalators and passenger conveyors (cl. 7.6.2(c)). It compares EUI with the previous audit if available. Category II and III EMO need a cost-benefit analysis that considers service life and efficiency degradation over the payback period, and states the energy prices used (cl. 7.5). The report is completed on the EMSD template (cl. 7.6.3).

**SD takeaway:** Provide 12 months of metered energy data, a separate electric-vehicle meter if that load is to be deducted, and nameplate data for every central fan, pump and other item at 5 kW or above.

### 3. Energy Audit Form (s. 8)

The Energy Audit Form is a specified form under the Ordinance. The EUI per annum from the audit is indicated on the form for public inspection. Clause 4.4 requires the form, with the documents specified in it, to be submitted to the Director.

**SD takeaway:** The published output of the audit is the annual EUI on the Energy Audit Form, so the internal-floor-area basis of that index has to be fixed and repeatable.
