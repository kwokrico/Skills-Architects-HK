# Code of Practice for Building Energy Audit (2024 Edition, Addendum No. 1/2025)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
EAC 2024 incorporated with Addendum No. 1/2025 | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department | Issued under Part 9 of the Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance (Cap. 610)

> Scope note: This file is the Code of Practice for Building Energy Audit, 2024 edition, incorporated with Addendum No. 1/2025. It governs the technical content of an energy audit of central building services installations under Cap. 610. An audit conducted in accordance with this EAC is deemed to satisfy the Ordinance in the technical aspects (cl. 1.2). It is not the design standard for new lighting, air-conditioning, electrical, or lift installations.

## Regulatory Overview

The EAC applies to energy audits of the central building services installations in a building of a category prescribed in Schedule 4 of the Ordinance (cl. 3.1).

Compliance is an audit to this EAC of those central installations as a minimum, save for an exclusion or exemption under the Ordinance, with extra rules for a Schedule 4 type 2 commercial portion and a type 11 industrial data-centre portion (cls. 4.1–4.3).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Scope for composite buildings and data centres (cls. 4.2–4.3)

Central building services installation has the Ordinance meaning. The code’s examples match the split between common-area and whole-building cases:

| Installation | Building with a designated common area | Building without a designated common area |
|---|---|---|
| Lighting | In the common area | Anywhere, unless in an individual unit and separately owned by the responsible person of that unit who is not the building owner |
| Air-conditioning | Not separately owned by the responsible person of an individual unit | Anywhere, unless separately owned by that person |
| Electrical | Incoming side of an electricity supplier’s meter for an individual unit | Anywhere, unless on the outgoing side of that meter where the responsible person is not the building owner |
| Lift and escalator | In the common area, unless solely serving an individual unit | Anywhere, unless it solely serves an individual unit and is separately owned by that unit’s responsible person who is not the building owner |

| Schedule 4 type | How this EAC applies |
|---|---|
| Type 2 — portion of a composite building that is for commercial use | The gross floor area in section 21(3) of the Ordinance means the gross floor area of the portion that is solely for commercial use. The audit applies only to the central building services installation solely serving that commercial portion (cl. 4.2) |
| Type 11 — portion of an industrial building occupied principally as a data centre | Central building services installations means installations solely serving that data-centre portion, except installations owned by a person who is not the owner of that portion (cl. 4.3) |

**SD takeaway:** On a composite building, audit only the central services of the commercial portion and use that portion’s gross floor area for the Ordinance’s 7 000 m² test; on an industrial data-centre portion, audit the services that solely serve that portion.

### 2. Small-installation exemption (cl. 4.4)

The owner may apply in writing under section 25 of the Ordinance for an exemption from the section 22(4) audit if an audit is technically or operationally undesirable, provided the total rating of all circuit protective devices (whichever are nearer the supply side) solely governing the electricity supply of the relevant central installation does not exceed **400 A, 3-phase**.

Circuit protective devices governing the electricity supply of electric-vehicle charging facilities may be excluded from that total.

**SD takeaway:** A central installation at or below 400 A three-phase, ignoring the electric-vehicle charging devices, is the written-exemption band in this edition; above that rating the audit proceeds.

### 3. Audit steps and the 5 kW inventory (cls. 5–7)

EMO categories (cl. 6.2): Category I housekeeping with practically no cost and no disruption; Category II operational changes at relatively low cost; Category III relatively higher capital cost.

Information to collect (cl. 7.2) includes type, usage and nominal operating hours; blocks, floors and total internal floor area; approximate common-area portion; occupation-approval date; rated capacities, power and coefficient of performance and controls; total power factor and total harmonic distortion; at least **12 consecutive months** of energy bills; metering or building-management data; and EMO implemented in the past **5 years**.

EUI is total energy consumption of the central building services installations for a period, divided by total internal floor area (kWh/m²/annum). Internal floor area is measured to the internal faces of enclosing external and/or party walls.

Electric-vehicle charging energy may be deducted from total annual consumption if separately metered (cl. 7.3.2). Incomplete records may be reconstructed from partial data, in-situ measurements and ratings if the method is recorded (cl. 7.3.3).

The report inventory (cl. 7.6.2(c)) covers chiller, cooling tower, heat pump, VRF, unitary air-conditioner, air-conditioning pump and fan of rated power **5 kW or above**; lighting; renewable energy; lifts, escalators and passenger conveyors; and other equipment at **5 kW or above**; plus electrical power quality. It includes EUI and a comparison with the previous audit if available. Category II and III items need cost, energy saving, service life, efficiency degradation over the payback period, and the energy prices used (cl. 7.5). The report uses the EMSD template (cl. 7.6.3).

**SD takeaway:** Keep a 5 kW-and-above schedule of central plant, 12 months of bills, and a separate electric-vehicle meter so that charging energy can be deducted and the published EUI stays comparable.

### 4. Forms submitted to the Director (cls. 4.5, 8, 9)

Clause 4.5 requires the Energy Audit Form, the Energy Audit Report and the Data Disclosure Form, with the documents specified in the forms, to be submitted to the Director.

The Energy Audit Form is a specified form. The EUI per annum is indicated on it for public inspection (s. 8).

The Data Disclosure Form is a specified form. EMSD may release to the public the technical information contained in the Data Disclosure Form submitted (s. 9).

**SD takeaway:** The audit package is three documents — Energy Audit Form, energy audit report, and Data Disclosure Form — and the technical information on the Data Disclosure Form may be released publicly.
