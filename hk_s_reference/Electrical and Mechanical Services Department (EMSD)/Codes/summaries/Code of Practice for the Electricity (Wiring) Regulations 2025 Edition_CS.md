# Code of Practice for the Electricity (Wiring) Regulations (2025 Edition)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
2025 Edition (First Print) | Electrical and Mechanical Services Department

> Scope note: This code gives the technical guidelines for meeting the Electricity (Wiring) Regulations (Cap. 406E). It applies to low-voltage and high-voltage fixed electrical installations in buildings and premises, including domestic and commercial buildings, factories and industrial undertakings, and to gantry and tower cranes, hoists, conveyors, traction equipment and ropeways that are permanently connected to a low-voltage or high-voltage supply (Code 3A). It does not apply to installations the Director exempts, or to mobile units such as aircraft, motor vehicles and sea-going vessels (Code 3A). Category 2 circuits supplied from a safety source are not regulated by the Wiring Regulations except regulation 5(1) (Code 3B). It does not set lighting power density or chiller efficiency. Those are in the building energy code under the Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance (Cap. 610). Lightning protection is not a statutory requirement under the Wiring Regulations (Code 26I).

## Regulatory Overview

Compliance with this code should achieve compliance with the relevant aspects of the Wiring Regulations, and an installation which complies with the 2020 edition is also deemed to have met the Wiring Regulations if it was completed and connected to electricity supplies on or before **31 December 2026** and it complies with the relevant electricity supplier's Supply Rules (Code 1).

Clearance in front of low-voltage switchgear is **600 mm** up to 100 A and **900 mm** above 100 A, and the clearance in front of high-voltage equipment of that kind is at least **1 400 mm** (Code 4E).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Working space in front of switchgear (Code 4E)

| Equipment | Clearance |
|---|---|
| Low-voltage switchgear not exceeding **100 A**, such as consumer units and isolation switches | Minimum **600 mm** for the full width, in front |
| Low-voltage control panels and switchgear exceeding **100 A**, such as switchboards, distribution panels and motor control centres | Minimum **900 mm** for the full width, in front |
| The same equipment operating at high voltage | The front clearance in the row above increased to at least **1 400 mm** |
| Access from behind or the side for connection and maintenance | Minimum **600 mm**, unless the manufacturer specifies otherwise. Not required where there are no renewable parts such as fuses or switches and no parts that need access from that side |
| Draw-out gear and doors | The front clearance is not less than the space needed to operate draw-out equipment or to open doors or hinged panels to at least **90 degrees** |
| Height of the 600 mm space in (a) | Not less than **1 000 mm** above finished floor |
| Height of the spaces in (b), (c), (e) and (f) | Not less than **1 800 mm** above finished floor |
| Bare live parts exposed in normal operation | Height of those clearance spaces not less than **2 100 mm** |
| Obstruction | The working space is free and ready for access at all times |

**SD takeaway:** A main switchboard above 100 A needs 900 mm clear in front for the full width, and 1 400 mm if that board is high voltage, with the space kept empty.

### 2. Switchroom and substation (Code 4F)

A switchroom includes a main switchroom, a sub-main switchroom, an electrical room and a meter room (Code 2).

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| High-voltage enclosure | Locked enclosure with a suitable warning notice. Every high-voltage switchroom or substation is kept locked except when manned, with a duplicate key in a key box at a designated location (Code 4F(1)) |
| Low-voltage enclosure not readily accessible to the public | A suitable warning notice may be used to prevent unauthorised access (Code 4F(1)(a)) |
| Main switchroom exit | At least one exit opens outwards and is clearly identified. The main switchroom is where the point of supply is electrically nearest to the supplier's system, or is taken directly from the consumer's transformer (Code 4F(2)(a)) |
| Live parts at the door | Conductors near the entrance or exit are arranged or protected so that a person entering or leaving cannot accidentally touch live metal (Code 4F(2)(b)) |
| Route from a public area | Every entrance and exit is kept free of obstruction, including locks other than those in Code 4F(1), structures, goods, materials, litter and waste (Code 4F(2)(c)) |
| Lighting | Minimum **150 lux** at floor level, and an average vertical illumination of **120 lux** minimum. Where the equipment must be operated on mains failure, emergency electric lighting independent of the mains, capable of **30 minutes**, is provided (Code 4F(3)(a)) |
| Ventilation | Ventilation or air-conditioning so that ambient air around the equipment does not exceed the temperature permissible for that equipment (Code 4F(3)(b)) |
| Storage | Must not be used for storage, other than tools and spare parts for the switchgear inside (Code 4F(4)) |

**SD takeaway:** Give the main switchroom an outward-opening exit, 150 lux on the floor, half an hour of emergency lighting, and no store.

### 3. Segregation of circuit categories (Code 5)

Category 4 cables are not drawn into the same conduit, duct, ducting or trunking as any other category, and category 1 cables are not drawn into the same conduit, duct or ducting as category 3 cables (Code 5B and 5C).

| Situation | Separation |
|---|---|
| Category 1, 2 and 3 cables without enclosure, or underground (Code 5B(4)(a)) | Minimum **50 mm** horizontal and vertical |
| Those cables underground or in a trench, where 50 mm cannot be achieved (Code 5B(4)(b)) | Not less than **25 mm**, with concrete slabs such that the shortest path around the concrete exceeds **75 mm** |
| Surface crossing of category 1, 2 and 3 (Code 5B(4)(c)) | A bridge of durable insulating material at least **6 mm** thick, overlapping the cables by at least **25 mm** on either side |
| Category 4 and any other category, underground or without enclosure (Code 5C(c)(i)) | Minimum **300 mm** horizontal or vertical |
| Category 4 underground, where 300 mm cannot be achieved (Code 5C(c)(ii)) | A concrete slab at least **50 mm** thick, such that the shortest path around the concrete exceeds **180 mm** |

**SD takeaway:** Keep fire-services and emergency-lighting cables (category 3) out of the same conduit as ordinary power, and keep high-voltage cables 300 mm from every other category unless a concrete slab provides the longer path around it.

### 4. Rooms with a bath or shower, saunas, and pools (Code 26)

In a room containing a fixed bath or shower, except for separated extra-low voltage (SELV), disconnection on a fault to earth occurs within **0.4 s** where equipment is simultaneously accessible with exposed or extraneous conductive parts. Every circuit supplying electrical equipment with exposed conductive parts within **2.25 m** above finished floor is protected by a residual current device with a residual operating current not exceeding **30 mA** (Code 26A). The space below a bath is accessible only by a tool. Supplementary bonding may be omitted only where the building already has protective equipotential bonding to Code 11E, every bathroom final circuit disconnects automatically to Code 11B and has additional residual-current protection to Code 11J, and every extraneous conductive part is connected to that bonding.

Figure 26(1), extracted from BS 7671, sets the zones:

| Zone | Extent shown on the figure |
|---|---|
| Zone 0 | Interior of the bath or shower basin |
| Zone 1 | The volume around zone 0, out to the vertical limit drawn on the figure |
| Zone 2 | **0.60 m** horizontally beyond zone 1, and up to **2.25 m** above finished floor. Beyond that height the figure marks the space as outside the zones |
| Shower without a basin | Zone 0 and zone 1 extend **1.20 m** radially from the fixed water outlet |
| Partition | The zone is cut by a permanent fixed partition. Zone 2 finishes at a closed door and extends into a cupboard when the door is hung on the opposite side |
| Under the bath | A space accessible only with a tool is outside the zones. A space under the bath accessible without a tool is zone 1 |

A fixed air-conditioner or space heater is on its own radial final circuit (Code 26A). If a water heater is in a bathroom, the double-pole switch is outside the bathroom.

Sauna zones in Figure 26(2): zone 1 contains the heater and extends **0.5 m** from its surface, or to the cold side of the wall insulation if the heater is closer than 0.5 m to a wall; zone 2 is outside zone 1, up to a horizontal surface **1.0 m** above the floor; zone 3 is outside zone 1, from that **1.0 m** surface up to the cold side of the ceiling insulation (Code 26L).

Swimming-pool and fountain zones (Code 26M):

| Zone | Extent |
|---|---|
| Zone 0 | Interior of the basin, including recesses, foot baths, and the space below waterjets or waterfalls |
| Zone 1 | From zone 0 to a vertical plane **2 m** from the rim, up to **2.5 m** above the floor or the surface expected to be occupied. Around a diving board, springboard, starting block or chute, **1.5 m** from the periphery and **2.5 m** above the highest surface expected to be occupied |
| Zone 2 | **1.5 m** beyond the vertical plane of zone 1, up to **2.5 m** above the occupied surface. There is no zone 2 for fountains |
| Zone 0 equipment | SELV not exceeding **12 V** AC root mean square or **30 V** ripple-free DC, with the source outside zones 0, 1 and 2 |

**SD takeaway:** Keep switches and socket outlets outside zone 2, which is 0.60 m beyond the bath or shower and 2.25 m high, and treat the pool surround for 2 m, plus another 1.5 m, as a special-risk zone.

### 5. Rising mains and renewable generation (Codes 26J and 26P)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Buildings of more than four floors, including the ground floor (Code 26J(a)) | 3-phase electrical rising mains with a 3-phase 4-wire tee-off at each floor, unless the electricity supplier agrees otherwise |
| Design (Code 26J(b)) | Agreed by the electricity supplier |
| Riser earthing (Code 26J(c)) | Separate riser earthing conductors. Minimum cross-section **70 mm²** copper or **150 mm²** aluminium, and otherwise sized to Code 11C(2) |
| Meter to main switch (Code 26J(d)) | Installed and maintained by the consumer, not less than **4 mm²** stranded copper |
| Renewable energy power system above **200 kW** aggregated (Code 26P) | Referred to the power company. Code 26P applies to solar photovoltaic and wind turbine systems. Other renewable systems, such as hydroelectric and energy from waste, including landfill gas or biogas, are designed to the relevant parts of this code and to national or international standards |

**SD takeaway:** A building of five storeys or more, counting the ground floor, needs a three-phase rising main with a tee-off on every floor, and a photovoltaic system over 200 kW is a power-company referral, not only an in-building final circuit.
