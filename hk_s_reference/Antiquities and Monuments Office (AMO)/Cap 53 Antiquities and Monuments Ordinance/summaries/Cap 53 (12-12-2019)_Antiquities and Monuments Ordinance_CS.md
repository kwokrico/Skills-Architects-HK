# Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance (Cap. 53)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 12 December 2019 | Authority means the Secretary for Development (section 2)

> Scope note: This Ordinance controls proposed monuments and monuments, discovery of antiquities, and excavation or metal-detector search for antiquities. It does not set building, fire, or structural standards, and it is not a heritage-licence design code. Regulations under section 22 may prescribe forms, fees, and management of monuments; they are not in this consolidation.

## Regulatory Overview

A place, building, site, or structure becomes a proposed monument by a Gazette declaration under section 2A, or a monument under section 3 if the Authority, after consulting the Antiquities Advisory Board and with the Chief Executive’s approval, considers it of public interest by reason of historical, archaeological, or palaeontological significance. An antiquity includes a relic and any place, building, site, or structure erected, formed, or built by human agency before the year 1800, and the ruins or remains of any such place, whether or not later modified (section 2).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Proposed monument and monument declarations (sections 2A, 2B, 2C, 3, and 4)

| Rule | Section | Schematic implication |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed monument | 2A(1) | After consulting the Board, the Authority may by Gazette notice declare a place, building, site, or structure a proposed monument, proposed historical building, or proposed archaeological or palaeontological site or structure, in order to consider whether it should be a monument |
| Adjoining land | 2A(2), 3(2) | The declaration may include adjoining land required for fencing, covering, or protecting the monument, or for access |
| Plan | 2A(4), 3(4) | A signed plan showing the situation is deposited in the Land Registry. On private land, a proposed-monument declaration is also registered, served on the owner and any lawful occupier, and fixed to the land. A monument declaration on private land is registered before publication |
| Duration | 2B | A proposed-monument declaration lasts 12 months unless withdrawn earlier. Only a declaration that is not on private land may be extended, after consulting the Board and with the Chief Executive’s approval, by periods of not more than 12 months |
| Objection, proposed monument on private land | 2C | The owner or lawful occupier may apply for withdrawal. Within one month the Authority withdraws or refuses. Within one month of a refusal the owner or occupier may petition the Chief Executive. The Chief Executive may withdraw the declaration or refer the objection to the Chief Executive in Council, whose direction is final |
| Monument | 3(1) | Subject to section 4. Gazette declaration after Board consultation and Chief Executive approval |
| Objection before a monument on private land | 4 | Before the declaration, the Authority serves a written notice of intention and a plan on the owner and any lawful occupier, and fixes a copy to the land. Within 1 month, or longer if the Chief Executive allows, the owner or occupier may petition the Chief Executive. The Chief Executive may direct that the declaration not be made, or refer the objection to the Chief Executive in Council |

**SD takeaway:** Before freezing demolition, alteration, or site formation, check the Land Registry for a section 2A or section 3 plan: a proposed monument on private land is already a 12-month control, and adjoining land needed for fencing or access can be inside the declared area.

### 2. Works prohibited without a permit (section 6)

Subject to subsection (4), no person shall, except in accordance with a permit granted by the Authority:

| Act | Section 6(1) |
|---|---|
| On or in a proposed monument or monument | Excavate, carry on building or other works, plant or fell trees, or deposit earth or refuse |
| To a proposed monument or monument | Demolish, remove, obstruct, deface, or interfere with it |

A person aggrieved by a refusal may, within 14 days, petition the Chief Executive, who may confirm, vary, or reverse the refusal. The decision is final (section 6(2) and (3)). The Authority may, after consulting the Board and with the Chief Executive’s approval, declare a proposed monument or monument exempt from section 6 (section 6(4)).

**SD takeaway:** Building works, excavation, tree felling, and deposit of earth on a proposed monument or monument need an Authority permit unless that monument has been gazetted as exempt from section 6.

### 3. Discovery, and excavation only under licence (sections 11, 12, and 13)

| Rule | Section | Hard point |
|---|---|---|
| Report | 11(1) | Any person who discovers, or knows of the discovery of, an antiquity or supposed antiquity shall forthwith report it to the Authority or a designated person |
| Protect | 11(3) | The discoverer shall take all reasonable measures to protect it |
| Entry | 11(6) and (7) | The Authority, and a designated person authorized in writing, may enter and inspect the site. Residential premises need the lawful occupier’s written consent, or not less than 48 hours’ written notice |
| No search without a licence | 12 | No person other than the Authority and an authorized designated person shall excavate or search for antiquities except under a licence; use a metal detector at a proposed monument or monument except under a licence to excavate and search; or remove a hitherto undiscovered relic from a proposed monument or monument, or collect or remove a supposed relic from its discovery site, otherwise than to protect it or under a licence |
| Who may be licensed | 13(2) | The Authority must be satisfied of sufficient scientific training or experience, sufficient staff and financial or other resources, and ability to conduct or arrange a proper scientific study of discoveries |
| Term | 13(3)–(5) | A licence may be granted or renewed for not more than 12 months, must specify the area of land, and is not transferable |
| Conditions | 13(6) | Conditions may cover conduct of the excavation, reporting, mapping and documenting, use of a metal detector, preservation, removal, examination and return, and casts, rubbings, squeezes, and other reproductions |

Discovery means finding in Hong Kong, in, on, or under land or sea, in or on anything growing on land, or attached to or within the fabric or foundations of a structure, an antiquity or supposed antiquity whose presence was previously unknown to the owner (section 2). A relic is a movable object made or modified by human agency before the year 1800, or fossil remains or impressions (section 2).

**SD takeaway:** Foundation and site excavation that searches for antiquities, and any metal detector on a proposed monument or monument, need a section 13 licence; a find in the fabric or foundations must be reported forthwith and protected.

### 4. Entry to inspect, and what regulations cannot do (sections 5 and 22)

Section 5 lets the Authority, and a designated person authorized in writing, enter and inspect a proposed monument or monument at all reasonable times. Fencing, repair, maintenance, preservation, restoration, excavation, or search for and removal of hitherto undiscovered relics needs the Chief Executive’s prior approval. Residential premises need written consent of the lawful occupier or not less than 48 hours’ written notice. Section 5 does not authorize excluding the owner, lawful occupier, a person beneficially interested, or a person they authorize, and it does not authorize excavation or search for relics in residential premises without the consent of the owner and the lawful occupier.

Section 22 lets the Chief Executive in Council regulate excavations, management of antiquities and monuments, and access and entrance fees. No such regulation shall prohibit or restrict access by the owner, lawful occupier, a person beneficially interested, or a person they authorize, or confer a right to enter private land that the person would not otherwise have had.

**SD takeaway:** A monument declaration does not of itself shut the owner or lawful occupier out, and a regulation under section 22 cannot create a new right to enter private land.
