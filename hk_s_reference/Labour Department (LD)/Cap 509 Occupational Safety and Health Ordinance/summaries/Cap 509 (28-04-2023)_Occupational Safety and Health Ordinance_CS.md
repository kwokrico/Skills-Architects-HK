# Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 28 April 2023 | Commissioner means the Commissioner for Labour (section 3)

> Scope note: This Ordinance places safety and health duties on employers and on occupiers of premises where a workplace is located, and it allows an improvement notice or a suspension notice to stop an activity or a use. It does not set room sizes, guard-rail heights, or scaffold details. Those sit in regulations and workplace codes of practice under sections 40 and 42, and, for an industrial undertaking, in the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59) and its regulations. A Cap. 509 regulation prevails over an inconsistent Cap. 59 regulation, and a Cap. 509 code of practice prevails over an inconsistent Cap. 59 code (section 46).

## Regulatory Overview

The purposes are to ensure the safety and health of employees when they are at work, to prescribe measures that contribute to making workplaces safer and healthier, to improve standards for certain hazardous processes, plant, and substances, and generally to improve the safety and health aspects of working environments (section 2). A workplace is any place where employees work, and it excludes an aircraft or vessel in a public place, the driver’s seat of a vehicle in a public place, domestic premises at which the only employees are domestic servants, a place at which only self-employed persons work, and any other place of a kind prescribed by regulation (section 3).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Who owes the duty on a premises the employer does not control (sections 3, 4, 6, and 7)

| Person | Section | Duty that changes the plan |
|---|---|---|
| Employer | 6(1) and (2) | So far as reasonably practicable, ensure the safety and health at work of all the employer’s employees. The failures include failure to maintain a workplace under the employer’s control in a safe condition, and failure to provide or maintain safe means of access to and egress from that workplace |
| Occupier, where the workplace is not under the employer’s control | 7(1) | Ensure that the premises, the means of access to and egress from the premises, and any plant or substances kept at the premises, are, so far as reasonably practicable, safe and without risks to health |
| Who is an occupier | 3(1), (5), and (6) | Includes a person who has any degree of control. A person who under a lease or contract must maintain or repair the premises, or must provide for the safety of or the absence of risks to health from plant or substances on the premises, is taken to be an occupier. So is a person obliged to provide, maintain, or repair a means of access or egress. Subsections (5) and (6) do not apply to persons in their capacity as occupiers of domestic premises |
| Person responsible for a workplace | 3(2) | The employer of the employees employed to carry out work there, or, if the employer does not exercise any degree of control over the relevant part or aspect, the occupier |
| Independent contractors and self-employed persons | 4 | The Ordinance applies to them only in their capacity as employers or as occupiers of premises where workplaces are located |
| At work | 3(4) | A person is at work only while actually at a workplace |

Plant includes machinery, equipment, appliances, furniture, furnishings, and fittings (section 3). Risks arising out of the activities of persons at work include risks attributable to the manner of conducting an undertaking, to the plant or substances used, and to the condition of premises so used (section 3(3)).

**SD takeaway:** On a shell or a landlord’s premises, the occupier who controls access, egress, plant, or substances owes the section 7 duty even though the tenant is the employer, so shared stairs, plant rooms, and landlord services are part of the safety layout.

### 2. Improvement notice and suspension notice (sections 9 and 10)

| Notice | When | Effect on the works |
|---|---|---|
| Improvement notice | The Commissioner is of the opinion that the employer or occupier is contravening this Ordinance or Cap. 59, or has contravened either in circumstances that make it likely the contravention will continue or be repeated | Written notice requiring the contravention to be remedied within a period that must be reasonable, or requiring the employer or occupier to refrain from continuing or repeating it. Failure without reasonable excuse: fine of $400,000 and imprisonment for 12 months. It is a reasonable excuse if it was not reasonably practicable to comply |
| Suspension notice | Because of an activity on the premises, or the condition or use of the premises or of any plant or substance located on the premises, there is an imminent risk of death or serious bodily injury | Directs the activity not to be undertaken, or the premises, plant, or substance not to be used, while the notice remains in force. It takes effect on the date it is served, or on a later date specified, and remains in force until revoked. Contravention without reasonable excuse: fine of $1,000,000 and imprisonment for 12 months, and a further fine of $100,000 for each day or part of a day during which the offender knowingly and intentionally continues the contravention |

Serious bodily injury includes any bodily injury that results in admission at a hospital or clinic for treatment or observation (section 3). The Commissioner must revoke a suspension notice when satisfied the activity, condition, or use no longer creates, and is no longer likely to create, an imminent risk of death or serious bodily injury. Within 28 days after service the employer or occupier may apply in writing for a review (section 11).

**SD takeaway:** A layout or a site activity that creates an imminent risk of death or serious bodily injury can be stopped by a suspension notice from the date of service, without waiting for a prosecution.

### 3. Codes and regulations are the detailed rules, and they prevail over Cap. 59 (sections 40, 41, 42, and 46)

A workplace code of practice is practical guidance. Contravening it does not of itself incur civil or criminal liability, but in legal proceedings it is admissible and proof of contravention or compliance may be relied on as tending to establish or negate a matter in issue. It is not subsidiary legislation. It takes effect on the date notice is published in the Gazette, or on a later date specified in the notice (sections 40 and 41).

Section 42 lets the Commissioner make regulations for the safety and health of employees at work, including precautions to prevent outbreaks of fire and to protect employees from the consequences of fire, including provision of adequate means of escape, removal and avoidance of dangers, and regulating specified plant and substances. Section 46: a regulation under this Ordinance prevails over any inconsistent regulation under Cap. 59, and a code of practice under this Ordinance prevails over any inconsistent code of practice under Cap. 59.

**SD takeaway:** Where a Cap. 509 regulation and a Cap. 59 regulation conflict, follow the Cap. 509 regulation, and do not treat a workplace code as if it were itself an offence provision.
