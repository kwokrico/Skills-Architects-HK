# Education Ordinance (Cap. 279)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 1 August 2026 | Permanent Secretary is the registration authority under Part II

> Scope note: This Ordinance requires a school to be registered and, where the premises were not designed and constructed as a school, requires certificates on loading, structural timber floors, fire risk, and means of escape before registration. It does not set classroom sizes, corridor widths, or sanitary fitments. Those are not in this consolidation. Manager registration and incorporated management committees are omitted here.

## Regulatory Overview

A school is an institution, organization, or establishment which provides, for 20 or more persons during any one day or 8 or more persons at any one time, any nursery, kindergarten, primary, secondary, or post secondary education or any other educational course by any means, including correspondence delivered by hand or through the postal services (section 3). Every school shall be registered or provisionally registered, and an aided school or a DSS school that provides evening instruction in addition to other education is deemed a separate school which shall also be registered or provisionally registered (section 10).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Premises not designed and constructed as a school (section 12)

Where the additional documents in section 11(b)(ii) are required, they are:

| Document | From | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Suitability | Competent authority | Opinion on suitability for a school, having regard to the loading for which the premises, or the part, are designed and constructed |
| Floors | Competent authority | The premises do not have structural timber floors |
| Fire risk | Director of Fire Services | Use as a school would not expose the persons in the school premises to any undue risk of fire |
| Means of escape | Competent authority | In the event of fire, the means of escape for all persons in the premises, including the persons in the school premises, would be adequate |
| Section 25 of Cap. 123 | Building Authority, if the Buildings Ordinance applies | A notice in writing that he does not intend, under section 25 of that Ordinance, to prohibit the use for the purposes of a school |
| Sound structure | Authorized person, but only if one of three cases applies | The premises are in sound structural condition |

The authorized-person certificate is required only if the competent authority has certified that the premises are not suitable having regard to loading, or the premises were erected prior to 1 January 1946, or at the time of erection the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) did not apply.

An application for a certificate or notice shall be in the form the Permanent Secretary specifies and accompanied by a plan, to the scale and in the number of copies the Permanent Secretary specifies, of the premises or the part. The competent authority is the Housing Authority for premises upon land vested in the Housing Authority or over which the Housing Authority has control and management, and in any other case the Director of Buildings and any officer of the Buildings Department he appoints. Nothing in section 12 affects the powers of the Building Authority under Cap. 123.

**SD takeaway:** A school in premises not designed and constructed as a school needs a no-structural-timber-floors certificate, a Director of Fire Services certificate of no undue fire risk, a means-of-escape certificate for everyone in the building, and, where Cap. 123 applies, a Building Authority notice that he does not intend to prohibit the school use under section 25.

### 2. Refusal, and the premises named on the certificate (sections 14, 19, and 20)

The Permanent Secretary may refuse to register a school if it appears that the proposed school premises are or are likely to be for any reason unsuitable for use for the purposes of a school (section 14(1)(b)).

No school shall be operated, and no teacher shall teach, in any premises other than the premises specified in the certificate of registration or provisional registration (section 19).

A change that specifies additional or alternative premises, where those premises are not designed and constructed for the purposes of a school, shall be accompanied by the certificates and the notice in section 12(1). The application shall include 3 copies of a plan or diagram, specifying dimensions, of the premises or the part, and the names and addresses of the landlord and the tenant (section 20).

School premises include school recreation rooms, residential facilities, playing fields and playing grounds, and any other places used for the purposes of a school (section 3).

**SD takeaway:** The certificate names the only premises in which the school may operate, and a later wing or a converted floor that was not designed as a school repeats the section 12 certificates.

### 3. Later changes that increase fire risk or make escape inadequate (section 21)

| Opinion | Who | What may follow |
|---|---|---|
| A change in the design or structure of the premises, or in the use of any part, exposes persons in the school premises to any undue risk of fire | Director of Fire Services | A written notice to the Permanent Secretary specifying the provision the school should make, or stating that no provision can be made. The Permanent Secretary may by written notice require the management authority to make a specified provision |
| Such a change means that in the event of fire the means of escape for all persons in the premises, including persons in the school premises, would not be adequate | Building Authority | The same form of notice, and the Permanent Secretary may require the specified provision |

**SD takeaway:** A fit-out or a change of use in any part of the building, not only in the school rooms, can trigger a fire-risk notice or a means-of-escape notice covering every person in the premises.

### 4. Post secondary education (section 18A)

No management authority of a school shall cause or allow post secondary education to be provided at the school unless it has received the approval of the Permanent Secretary. An approval in relation to the provision of post secondary education through a programme at the sub-degree level expires at midnight on 31 July 2028. Sub-degree means an associate degree or a higher diploma (section 3).

**SD takeaway:** A sub-degree programme on an existing school registration still needs a separate approval, and an approval for a sub-degree programme expires at midnight on 31 July 2028.
