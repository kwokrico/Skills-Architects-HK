# ARB Code of Professional Conduct
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
5 March 2024 | Architects Registration Board, Hong Kong Special Administrative Region

> Scope note: The Board states that this code is established under section 9 of the Architects Registration Ordinance as mandatory rules for regulating and disciplining a registered architect, and that it sets out behaviour for which a registered architect risks being disciplined, including suspension or revocation of the privilege to practise. It is not the Architects Registration Ordinance, and it does not approve plans or set design standards. The four-page text does not contain a client-architect agreement.

## Regulatory Overview

The code is organized under five aspects: competence, integrity, managing conflict of interest, statutory and regulatory compliance, and regard for public interest, which the Board reckons are the essential qualities expected of a registered architect as a trustworthy professional. While an architect is obliged to serve the client’s best interest, a registered architect is also deemed to have a supervening duty to safeguard the safety and the well-being of the public.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Competence and the administration of contracts

A registered architect shall diligently carry out the duties he or she undertakes under the Client / Architect Agreement to the best of his or her ability, up to the professional standards expected of a member of the profession.

A registered architect shall act impartially at all times and shall administer all building contracts and/or consultancy agreements under his or her control with fairness and integrity.

**SD takeaway:** The code ties the standard of diligence to the Client / Architect Agreement. This PDF does not set out that agreement.

### 2. Status that must be made known, and promotion

A registered architect shall at all times, whether in the course of business or not, conduct himself or herself with honesty, integrity, and propriety to be expected of a member of an honourable profession.

A registered architect shall make known his or her updated personal and professional status to any persons or parties who are expected to be affected by the services he or she proffers in the capacity of an architect, once he or she becomes an undischarged bankrupt, or once his or her application for renewal or registration is rejected by the Architects Registration Board.

A registered architect shall promote his or her abilities and/or services for business advancement honestly and responsibly, shall not maliciously criticize the work of another architect, and shall not direct and/or make undue influence on any other architect to act in any manner which could violate this code.

**SD takeaway:** Bankruptcy, or a rejected renewal or registration, must be made known to persons expected to be affected by services proffered as an architect.

### 3. Conflict, unlawful conduct, and the public

| Aspect | Rule as printed |
|---|---|
| Conflict of interest | A registered architect shall not have any interest, financial or personal, in connection with any business which may be inconsistent or in conflict with his or her professional obligations, unless such interest or connection is fully declared to all parties likely to be affected |
| Statutory and regulatory compliance | A registered architect shall not at all times, whether in the course of business or not, act in any unlawful or unethical manner which is liable to bring the profession of architecture in Hong Kong into disrepute. A registered architect shall co-operate with the Board on its disciplinary investigation, either as a respondent or witness, with full disclosure of facts to the best of his or her knowledge |
| Public interest | A registered architect shall have a proper regard for the safety and well-being of all those who may be expected to use or enjoy the result of his or her efforts |

**SD takeaway:** A financial or personal interest that may conflict with professional obligations has to be fully declared to all parties likely to be affected, and the safety of people who use or enjoy the result is a stated duty alongside the client’s interest.
