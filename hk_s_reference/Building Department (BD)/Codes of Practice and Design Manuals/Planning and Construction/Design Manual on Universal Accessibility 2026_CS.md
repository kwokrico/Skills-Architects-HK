# Design Manual on Universal Accessibility 2026
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue September 2026 | Buildings Department (BD)

> Scope note: Design requirements for access in new buildings and in alterations and additions, revamped from the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008 (2025 Edition). Save for the provisions already covered by regulations 72 and 73 and the Third and Fourth Schedules of the Building (Planning) Regulations (B(P)R), the requirements are at present advisory. BD will propose to put obligatory provisions that are not yet in those schedules into the B(P)R; Appendix E summarises that proposal. Many figures are marked “ALL DIMENSIONS ARE IN mm” without the drawing dimensions surviving in the text extract; those figure-only sizes are not stated below. This Manual is not itself the B(P)R, and it does not replace the Disability Discrimination Ordinance (Cap. 487).

## Regulatory Overview
This Manual, first issued in September 2026, classifies requirements as Obligatory Design Requirements, which shall be complied with unless an alternative is not inferior, and Advisory Design Requirements, which are enhanced provisions for reference. Save for regulations 72 and 73 and the Third and Fourth Schedules of the B(P)R, the requirements under this Manual are at present advisory, and BD will propose legislative amendments to include the obligatory provisions not yet covered, as summarised in Appendix E.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Extent of application and exemptions (Chapters 1–3)
The Disability Discrimination Ordinance section 84 requires a public authority, including the Building Authority, not to approve plans for a new building or for alterations or additions unless reasonable access for persons with a disability (PwDs) will be provided, subject to practicability and unjustifiable hardship. Section 84(3) does not apply that duty to buildings of 13 m or less in height above ground level used or intended for occupation by a single family, or to temporary buildings or contractor’s sheds in Part VII of the B(P)R. This Manual applies to the design and construction of new buildings and of alterations and additions; Government authorities also refer to it for their buildings.

“Persons with a disability” means persons who, on account of injury, disease or congenital deformity, are impaired in vision, hearing or locomotion, including persons with ambulant disabilities, wheelchair users, persons with visual impairment, the blind, persons with hearing impairment and the deaf. “Specified premises” are shopping malls and other retail premises with gross floor area of more than 30 000 m², and cinemas, theatres, concert halls, sport facilities, stadiums, museums and theme parks with an occupant capacity of more than 3 000 people. “Large scale specified premises” have an occupant capacity of more than 10 000 people. “Access route” runs from a prominent point on the lot boundary, accessible to a public street or pedestrian way, to an entrance commonly used by the public (or a point adjacent to it) and to an accessible lift. “Wet areas” of a residential unit are bathroom, toilet and kitchen.

**SD takeaway:** Apply the full common-area package above four domestic storeys, and still size every residential entrance door and every bathroom, kitchen and toilet floor even in a building of four storeys or fewer.

Leg space depth 400 mm to 600 mm and height not less than 680 mm above finished floor |
| Obligatory | Assistive listening (10.7) | Induction loop, infrared, or similar, at the main public information/service counter and at least one other such counter if any, for the Table 2 categories, and in the hall and auditorium of such a building if any |

Manual call points in places intended for wheelchair users: prominent and accessible, installed in accordance with the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment, the inspection and maintenance code, or a Fire Services Department circular letter. **SD takeaway:** Where Table 2 requires them, run the tactile guide path to within 300 mm of the lift, accessible toilet and counter, and build at least one counter portion at not more than 750 mm high and 750 mm wide with knee space.

### 10. Advisory guidelines for elderly people, dementia and outdoor areas (Chapter 11)
The whole of Chapter 11 is advisory. Paragraph 11.1 says these are desirable features for a more inclusive environment, and that the industry may adopt them at its own pace. It is not a second obligatory code.

| Strand | What the extract sets out |
|---|---|
| Elderly-friendly (11.2) | Recommended requirements in four groups: Mobility and Accessibility (A1–A17), Design Adaptability (B1), Well-being (C1–C9) and Gerontechnology (D1). Examples printed as recommendations include bathroom turning space not less than 1 200 mm diameter, doorways not less than 850 mm, and showers not less than 1 500 mm × 900 mm. The chapter encourages adoption as far as practicable |
| Dementia (11.3) | Principles of simplicity, predictability, sensory consideration and flexibility; colour and tonal contrast; avoidance of shiny floors and strong patterns; logical sequences of spaces; shorter corridors; lifts visible from unit entrances; covered walkways; sensory gardens; acoustic glazing and sound-absorbing materials; natural light without glare |
| Outdoor areas (11.4) | Accessible routes to outdoor facilities. Routes of concrete, asphalt or board: surface obstacles not exceeding 6 mm. Clear width at least 1 800 mm. If the drop is more than 200 mm, an upstand at least 75 mm high and 75 mm wide. Outdoor facilities illuminated to an average of 20 lux. Outdoor steps: minimum clear width 1 200 mm, treads not less than 300 mm, risers not more than 150 mm, vertical rise between landings not exceeding 1 500 mm, landings minimum length 1 500 mm. Resting areas at 50 m intervals, not less than 1 800 mm long and 1 200 mm wide. Swimming-pool water entry is optional notwithstanding 3.2.2: ramp maximum gradient 1:14, extending to a depth of not less than 900 mm and not more than 1 100 mm below the stationary water level; platform-lift location where water depth is not more than 1 300 mm |
| Management (11.5) | Advisory guidance on keeping accessible facilities in operation, including temporary unavailability of an accessible toilet |
| Alterations and additions (11.6) | The Manual’s requirements apply to alterations and additions. Where spatial or structural constraints prevent full compliance, and unjustifiable hardship is substantiated, 11.6 gives compensatory alternatives. One printed circumstance is non-provision of initial access where an accessible ramp, including landing and projecting handrail, occupies more than one-third of the area or the frontage, and/or there are structural or topographical constraints; mitigating measures include an intercom at 900 mm to 1 200 mm |

**SD takeaway:** Do not treat Chapter 11 as mandatory layout; if an outdoor accessible route is adopted, the printed advisory width is 1 800 mm, with rests every 50 m.

### 11. Appendix E — obligatory provisions proposed for the B(P)R
Appendix E is a summary of the obligatory provisions in this Manual that are proposed for inclusion in the B(P)R. Its opening text states that, for obligatory provisions not yet covered by regulations 72 and 73 and the Third and Fourth Schedules, BD will propose inclusion through legislative amendments, and that before enactment those provisions, including interpretations and obligatory design requirements, are advisory. The appendix pairs “Obligatory Provisions in this Manual (Currently Advisory in Nature)” with “Corresponding Provisions currently in Force under the B(P)R”, and says reference should be made to the corresponding provisions currently in force under regulations 72 and 73 and the Third and Fourth Schedules.

Rows that are printed clearly in the extract include: interpretations in paragraph 2.1 against section 1 of the Third Schedule and section 1 of the Fourth Schedule; Table 1 against Part 4 of the Third Schedule; paragraph 4.2 against section 11 of the Third Schedule and section 2 of the Fourth Schedule; paragraph 4.3(1) against section 13 of the Third Schedule; paragraphs 4.4(1) to 4.4(10) against sections 8, 9 and 10 of the Third Schedule; paragraphs 5.2(1) and 5.2(2) against section 12(3) of the Third Schedule and section 3(2) of the Fourth Schedule; paragraph 5.3(1) against section 12(1) of the Third Schedule and section 3(3) of the Fourth Schedule; paragraph 5.4(1)(a) against section 31(1) of the Third Schedule; paragraph 5.4(3) against section 35(3) of the Third Schedule; paragraph 5.5(1) against section 38 of the Third Schedule and section 6 of the Fourth Schedule; and paragraph 5.5(2) against section 39(1) of the Third Schedule. The table continues on later pages. Some later paragraph numbers in the extract are split across lines and are not reconstructed here.

**SD takeaway:** Until the B(P)R is amended, design to the Manual’s obligatory sections, and use Appendix E to see which of those sections BD proposes to make statutory and which B(P)R provisions are already in force.
