# Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction 1996
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
January 1996 revision (first issue November 1989) | Building Authority / Buildings Department

> Scope note: Guidance on compliance with **Part XV of the Building (Construction) Regulations** — passive fire resisting construction only (compartmentation, FRP, separations, openings, shafts, façades, basements, bridges/tunnels, doors, refuge floors). Means of escape, means of access, and active FSI are in other Codes (MOE 1996 / MOA / FSD CoP). **Superseded for new work by FS Code 2011 Part C** (unified MOE + FRC + MOA); keep this summary for legacy A&A, historic approvals, and understanding the FRP logic that Part C inherited.

---

## Regulatory Overview

This Code sets the **passive fire-resisting envelope** for buildings: maximum compartment volumes, fire resistance periods (FRP) by use class, separation between uses/occupancies, protection of openings and shafts, façade/spandrel rules, basements, bridges/tunnels, fire doors, and refuge floors. It applies wherever Building (Construction) Regulations Part XV fire-resisting construction requirements must be demonstrated to the Building Authority.

Compliance is by **prescriptive provisions** (deemed reliable) or an **alternative / fire engineering approach** for special size, height, use, design, construction or location — assessed against MOE, MOA, FSI, FRC, size, height, use, location and management as a package.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 0. Definitions that lock schematic decisions (§4)

| Term | Meaning — why it matters at SD |
|---|---|
| **Compartment** | Portion of a building assessed as **one unit under Table 2**, separated by walls/floors of the required FRP. Your GA “zone” must match this, not leasing demise alone. |
| **Compartment volume** | Volume within the **external surfaces** of the compartment walls and floors. Count full clear height × plan — atria and double-height retail inflate volume fast. |
| **Element of construction** | Floor, beam, column, hanger; loadbearing wall/member (**not** roof unless roof acts as floor/exit); **required staircase** incl. landings and supports. These inherit Table 2 FRP (subject to basement / enclosure exceptions). |
| **FRP** | Time resisting fire under **BS 476 : Parts 20–24 : 1987**, or deemed-to-satisfy Tables A–F. Criteria = stability / integrity / insulation per Table 3. |
| **Basement** | Any storey **below** the lower/lowest ground storey, with **all** required exits going **upward**. Triggers **4 hr** FRP (§6.2). |
| **Ground storey** | Storey with street entrance. Multi-street / split-level sites can have **more than one** ground storey — each can change “basement” / podium logic. |
| **Required staircase** | MOE stair **or** access stair in a firefighting & rescue stairway. Whole §11 shaft package applies. |
| **Balcony approach** | External approach to a common stair serving **≥2 occupancies**. Triggers §9.4 wall/door FRP unless escape already in two directions. |
| **Refuge floor** | Protected temporary refuge floor. FRC controls **construction FRP** (§18); **frequency / area** come from MOE / later FS Code Part B. |
| **Place of public entertainment / industrial undertaking** | As defined in Cap. 172 / Factories & Industrial Undertakings Ordinance — map licensing use to Table 2 classes 5 and 9–10 early. |

**SD takeaway:** Wrong “compartment” boundary or wrong “basement” call is the most expensive FRP mistake — it cascades into structure thickness, core walls, smoke outlets, and podium strategy.

### 1. Two compliance paths (§3) — when DTC fails

#### 1.1 Prescriptive path
Follow Part II + Tables 1–3 + A–F. Reliable DTC for BA.

#### 1.2 Alternative / fire engineering path (§3.2–3.6)
Use when size, height, use, design, construction or location make prescription impractical (large atria, oversized compartments, complex links).

BA assesses against **all** of:
- Means of escape · means of access · fire service installations · fire resisting construction
- Size · height · use · location · management

May enhance / trade off among:
- Fire prevention · early detection/warning · MOE standard · smoke control
- Rate of fire spread · resistance to fire effects · containment capacity · fire separation
- Active extinguishment · firefighting/rescue facilities · management · trained personnel · maintenance arrangements

Quantitative risk methods allowed if assumptions are documented from competent authorities.

**SD takeaway:** Oversized Table 1 volume, missing spandrels on all-glass façades, or deep linked basements usually force fire engineering — budget time and FSD/BA interface at concept, not DD.

### 2. Lock use class + compartment volume first (Tables 1 & 2 / §5–6)

Every FRP, separating wall, and basement strategy hinges on Table 2 use class and Table 1 volume. Set these before fixing cores, atria, warehouses, or podium stacking.

#### 2.1 Compartment construction basics (§5)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Every building | Divide into compartments by walls and floors to inhibit fire spread |
| Joints | Compartment walls/floors, separations and lobbies: joints **completely filled with non-combustible material** — no smoke/flame paths |
| Volume cap | No compartment may exceed Table 1 |

#### 2.2 Maximum compartment volume (Table 1)

| Use | Max compartment volume |
|---|---|
| **Bulk storage / warehouse** | **7 000 m³** if compartment floor is **above basement** and building height ≤ **30 m** (B(P)R 23(1)); **3 500 m³** if at **basement** level **or** height **> 30 m** |
| **All other uses** | **28 000 m³** |

Oversized compartments → case-by-case only, with enhanced MOE / MOA / FSI / improved FRP (Table 1 Note).

**Worked SD check (retail atrium):** 40 m × 25 m floor × 8 m clear ≈ **8 000 m³** already — Class 4–8 needs **2 hr** FRP and is still under 28 000 m³; warehouse of same volume fails Table 1 unless split or fire-engineered.

#### 2.3 Fire resistance period by use (Table 2)

| Class | Use | Compartment volume | Min FRP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Domestic | ≤ 28 000 m³ | **1 hour** |
| **2** | Hotel bedrooms | ≤ 28 000 m³ | **1 hour** |
| **3** | Office | ≤ 28 000 m³ | **1 hour** |
| **4–8** | **4** Shop / restaurant / hotel foyer; **5** PPE; **6** Hospital; **7** Place of assembly; **8** Carparking | ≤ **7 000 m³** | **1 hour** |
| **4–8** (larger) | Same as above | **> 7 000 m³** but ≤ **28 000 m³** | **2 hours** |
| **9** | Bulk storage / warehouse | ≤ **7 000 m³** | **2 hours** |
| **10** | Industrial undertaking (excl. bulk storage / warehouse) | ≤ **28 000 m³** | **2 hours** |

**Notes baked into Table 2:**
1. Different use classes → separate per §8.
2. Special hazards → separate per §14.
3. Uses not listed → BA sets FRP from fire load, hazard, FSI and other features.

**SD takeaways:**
- Classes **4–8** jump from **1 hr → 2 hr** once volume exceeds **7 000 m³** — split retail / F&B / PPE / carpark volumes early or accept thicker structure / covers.
- Warehouse / bulk storage are volume-capped much tighter than “all other uses,” and start at **2 hr**.
- Domestic / hotel bedroom / office stay at **1 hr** up to the 28 000 m³ volume cap (unless basement override or use-mix separation forces higher).

#### 2.4 What must achieve that FRP (§6.1 + Table 3)

Within each compartment:
- Every **element of construction**
- Every **compartment wall and compartment floor**

…must have FRP ≥ Table 2 (subject to basement **4 hr** override).

### 3. Basement FRP override — always 4 hours (§6.2 + §15.1)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Every element of construction in a basement | FRP ≥ **4 hours** |
| Every basement compartment wall / floor | FRP ≥ **4 hours** |
| Separation between basement and any adjoining storey | FRP ≥ **4 hours** |

#### 3.1 Uniting basement with upper storeys of same use (§15.1)

Allowed **only if**:
- Basement use = G/F (and any united upper storeys) use; **and**
- Every element / compartment wall / floor in those united storeys **and** the construction separating the **uppermost** united storey from the storey above all have the **same FRP as the basement** (i.e. **4 hr**)

**SD takeaway:** Continuous retail from B2 through 2/F means **4 hr** structure through the entire podium — slab thickness, column covers (Table E), and steel protection (Table D) all jump. Prefer a hard **4 hr** compartment cut at basement ceiling unless the client explicitly wants a united volume and pays for it.

---

### 4. Single-storey unprotected steel — rare exemption (§6.3)

Unprotected steel allowed only if **all** apply:
- Single-storey building
- Volume ≤ **7 000 m³**
- Height ≤ **7.5 m**
- Clear unobstructed open space ≥ **6 m** to adjoining building **or** site boundary

External columns/beams still need separate corrosion consideration.

**SD takeaway:** Low warehouse / workshop sheds can avoid fire protection to steel; anything multi-storey or tight to boundary cannot use this clause.

### 5. Certification of non-table construction (§6.4–6.5)

| Path | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Deemed-to-satisfy | Tables A–F constructions |
| Otherwise | Tested or assessed against **BS 476 : Parts 20–24 : 1987** |
| Test report | Laboratory recognised by **HOKLAS** or BA |
| Assessment report | HOKLAS/BA lab **or** BA-recognised establishment / professional with appropriate FR construction qualifications |

Table 3 sets which of **stability / integrity / insulation** each element must satisfy, and the method of exposure (each side / underside / landing side only, etc.).

### 6. Separation between uses (§8) — podium stacking driver

| Situation | Separation FRP |
|---|---|
| Parts of building in **different Table 2 use classes** | Longer of the two Table 2 FRPs, **never < 2 hours** |
| Ancillary small offices, caretakers’ quarters, small storage / loading & unloading in an **industrial** building | **No** such separation required |

**Typical SD stacks and the 2 hr floor they force:**

| Upper / lower mix | Min separating FRP |
|---|---|
| Domestic over shop | ≥ **2 hr** |
| Hotel over retail / F&B | ≥ **2 hr** |
| Office over carpark | ≥ **2 hr** |
| Industrial + ancillary small office (same industrial building) | Separation waived (§8.2) |
| Shop + office under §9.2 common management | Use separation still follows §8 if different Table 2 classes; occupancy separation may be waived under §9.2 |

**SD takeaway:** Flat over shop, office over carpark, hotel over retail = **≥ 2 hr** compartment floors/walls even when each use alone would only need 1 hr.

---

### 7. Separation between occupancies (§9) — multi-tenant / flat corridor rules

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Different occupancy **types** | Walls/floors FRP ≥ that required for elements of construction of the compartment, **capped at 2 hours** |
| Retail **or** office tenancies under **common effective management** + common fire alarm actuated by **sprinkler alarm or break-glass** | Occupancy separation **waived** (BA must accept that management is common and effective) |
| Internal corridor serving rooms/flats of different occupancies (**except shopping arcades**) | Corridor walls ≥ **1 hr**; door from room/flat ≥ **½ hr**; fixed lights ≥ **½ hr** only if installed at height ≥ **1.8 m** |
| Balcony approach serving rooms/flats of different occupancies | Same as corridor (**1 hr** walls / **½ hr** door / **½ hr** fixed lights @ ≥ **1.8 m**) — **except** where means of escape is already in **two directions** |

**SD takeaways:**
- Shopping arcade internal malls are the explicit corridor exemption — still mind use-class and compartment rules.
- Corridor / balcony glazing below 1.8 m cannot be ordinary glass — need FR fixed lights or solid wall.
- “Common management” waiver is a management/legal argument, not a GA free pass — BA must be satisfied.

### 8. Protection of adjoining buildings / boundary (§7 + Diagram 1)

#### 8.1 General enclosure (§7.1)
Every building suitably enclosed by fire-resisting **external walls and roof** to protect adjoining buildings.

#### 8.2 Same-site buildings (§7.2–7.3)

| Condition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Buildings on same site **< 1.8 m** apart | Treated as **adjoining buildings** |
| Any part within **1.8 m** of adjoining building | **Imperforate** external wall/roof at **same FRP as internal elements of construction** |
| Openings in that zone | Allowed only as **fixed lights** ≥ **½ hr** (≥ **1 hr** on required stair / lobby), and ≥ **900 mm** from any part of the adjoining building that has FRP **less than** that of the internal elements |
| Unprotected openings | Allowed if ≥ **1.8 m** from unprotected openings of the adjoining building |

#### 8.3 Common boundary with adjoining site (§7.4)

| Condition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Any part within **900 mm** of common boundary | Imperforate wall/roof at internal-element FRP |
| Openings | Fixed lights ≥ **½ hr** (stair/lobby ≥ **1 hr**) if opening is ≥ **450 mm** from boundary |

#### 8.4 Diagram 1 — dimension cheat-sheet

| Label (Diagram 1) | Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | min **900 mm** | Protected opening to adjoining building / critical interface |
| **b** | min **1 800 mm** | Unprotected opening to unprotected opening; also same-site adjoining threshold |
| Boundary fixed light | ≥ **450 mm** from boundary | Absolute minimum setback for FR glazing to lot line |

**SD takeaways:**
- Twin towers / linked blocks < 1.8 m apart force blank FR façades or FR fixed lights only — kills curtain-wall continuity in the gap.
- Party-wall / near-boundary flats: vision glass within 900 mm of lot line is not free; use FR fixed lights and 450 mm setback minimum.
- Measure in **3D** (Diagram 1 example e) — diagonal proximity counts, not only plan gap.

### 9. Openings through fire-resisting walls & floors (§10)

#### 9.1 Compartment-wall doors / shutters (§10.1)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Purpose | **Communication** between compartments — **not combination** into one compartment |
| Protection | Door or fire shutter with **same FRP as wall** for **integrity + insulation** |
| Insulation waiver | Insulation not required if **total width** of openings ≤ **25%** of the compartment wall **length** |

**SD takeaway:** Wide mall openings / multiple fire shutters along a short compartment wall quickly exceed 25% — then shutters need full insulation rating (cost + thickness).

#### 9.2 Escalators / non-required stairs through floors (§10.2)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Allowed | Unenclosed in **one** compartment if enclosed in the **adjoining** compartment |
| Enclosure FRP | ≥ **longer** of the two compartments’ element FRPs |
| Doors / shutters in enclosure | FRP ≥ **½** of enclosing wall |

#### 9.3 Ramps across compartment divisions (§10.3)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Default | Fire shutter ≥ **longer** of the two element FRPs |
| If shutter impractical | BA may accept **drenchers** in lieu — design/install to **Director of Fire Services** |

#### 9.4 Vertical shafts through compartment floors (§10.4)
Openings for vertical shafts allowed **subject to §11**.

#### 9.5 Services through compartment walls/floors (§10.5)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| A/C ducts, vent ducts, electrical trunking, conduits, pipes, wires + leftover construction holes | Protect with **fire dampers** or other suitable **fire stops** to maintain wall/floor FRP |
| If ducts / pipes / wires / insulation are **combustible** | Contain within enclosure FRP ≥ wall/floor FRP |
| Access openings in that enclosure | Doors FRP ≥ **½** enclosure FRP |

#### 9.6 Services through non-compartment FR walls/floors (§10.6)

If combustible services pass through FR (but non-compartment) walls/floors **without** a §10.5 enclosure, provide a sealing system around the service with FRP ≥ wall/floor, and:
1. Tested/assessed to BS 476 : Part 20 : 1987 general principles  
2. Performance not affected by moisture/dampness  
3. Life ≥ life of the duct/pipe/wire  
4. Firmly fixed  

#### 9.7 Authority split (§10.7)

| Item | Authority |
|---|---|
| Fire shutter construction / installation / assembly | **Building Authority** |
| Fire dampers; operation of fire shutters & dampers (design, install, test, maintain) | **Director of Fire Services** |

### 10. Vertical shafts (§11) — cores, lifts, required stairs

#### 10.1 Liftwells (§11.1–11.2 + Diagram 2)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Liftwell enclosure | Walls/floors ≥ **2 hours**, except openings for doors, ventilation, or between liftwell and machine / pulley room |
| Holes around landing-door frames/reveals, floor indicators, call buttons | Properly filled and sealed to maintain wall FRP |
| Landing doors / other doors in liftwell wall | Integrity ≥ **1 hour**; **also insulation ≥ 1 hour** where Diagram 2 requires type **F** |

**Diagram 2 — when lift doors need insulation (F) vs integrity only (S):**

| Arrangement (concept) | Typical door type |
|---|---|
| Lift landings **not** isolated from accommodation | Often **F** (integrity + insulation) |
| Lift landings isolated; or backed by separate FR doors; or in hall/open space; or fully surrounded by stairwell (case-dependent) | May be **S** (integrity only) |
| Arrangement not shown | Choose by comparison with the nearest Diagram 2 case |

**SD takeaway:** Open lift lobby onto corridor/office often drives **insulated** lift doors — specify early; affects lobby depth and door supplier.

#### 10.2 Required staircases + intercepting lobbies (§11.3–11.5)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Separation from rest of building | Walls ≥ FRP required for elements of the connected compartment |
| Imperforate | Except doorways to the building |
| Door to accommodation | Integrity ≥ **lesser of (½ wall FRP) or 1 hour**; insulation ≥ **½ hour** |
| Door omitted (balcony approach → required stair) | Only if stair is open to external air for ≥ **50%** of its plan perimeter, measured from top of balustrade/parapet to soffit of flight above |
| Services inside stair enclosure | **Emergency only**: fire hydrants, sprinklers, emergency lights, exit signs — unless enclosed at stair-wall FRP |
| Access to such service enclosure | Door integrity ≥ lesser of ½ wall or 1 hr; insulation ≥ ½ hr |
| Stair structure inside compliant FR walls | Need **not** itself have FRP, but must be **non-combustible** (§11.4) |
| Single-stair building, G/F not domestic/office (§11.5) | Stair G–1/F separated at **longer** of the use FRPs; enclosing wall returned ≥ **450 mm** along G/F occupancy frontage at main entrance |

#### 10.3 External wall of required stair / lobby (§11.6–11.8 + Diagrams 3–4)

Subject to §7, stair/lobby external walls may be unprotected with openings — **unless** §11.7–11.8 bite.

**Distance test (§11.7):** If any part of that external wall opposes (directly or indirectly) and is within **6 m** of:
1. Opposite side of a street; **or**
2. Common boundary with adjoining site; **or**
3. Any other external wall of **lower** FRP than the stair separating wall, or unprotected opening not protected by fixed light ≥ **½** stair-wall FRP; **or**
4. Any other building on the same site  

→ that part of the external wall must have FRP ≥ stair separating-wall FRP, and be imperforate except:

| Opening type when **d ≤ 6 m** | Limit |
|---|---|
| Fixed light | ≤ **25%** of the area of the external wall of that storey; FRP ≥ ½ stair/lobby wall FRP |
| Door at G/F discharge or roof | FRP ≥ **½** wall FRP |
| When **d > 6 m** | External wall and openings may be unprotected |

**Same-plane façade return (§11.8 + Diagram 4):**  
If stair/lobby external wall continues in the same plane as other weaker external walls of the building, the remaining FR walls of the stair/lobby must extend ≥ **450 mm** at any angle at the junction — except at G/F discharge, the extension must **not project beyond** the external wall.

**SD takeaways:**
- Stair on street frontage or near boundary within 6 m → blank FR wall or limited FR fixed lights, not open louvres.
- Flush stair next to vision-glass office façade needs the **450 mm** FR return — shows as a pier / fin on elevation.

### 11. Inter-floor / atrium / façade fire spread (§12) — SD killers

#### 11.1 Unprotected floor openings within a compartment (§12.1 + Diagrams 5–6)

Applies to escalators, circulation staircases, atrium walkways, and similar **internal unprotected openings**.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Vertical barrier around opening | Depth ≥ **450 mm** measured vertically **downward from underside of floor** |
| If false ceiling nearby | Barrier extends ≥ **450 mm below the false ceiling** |
| Barrier FRP | ≥ **1 hour** |
| Purpose (Code rationale) | Stop heated air/smoke drafts; allow local sprinklers to control fire |
| Alternatives if headroom / false ceiling makes barrier impractical | Smoke reservoirs; perforate or open ceilings; dynamic smoke extraction; smoke-detector-operated devices — BA case-by-case on effectiveness + post-completion control/management |

**SD takeaway:** Atrium and open escalators need either a deep FR downstand (watch clear headroom / signage / lighting) or an early smoke-control fire-engineering package.

#### 11.2 Curtain wall / multi-storey envelope (§12.2)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Curtain wall or similar protecting the building against the elements and extending beyond one storey | Constructed entirely of **non-combustible materials** |
| Void between curtain wall and building perimeter | **Solidly infilled at each floor level** with non-combustible material FRP ≥ **floor FRP** |

**SD takeaway:** Continuous cavity behind CW without floor-edge fire stop = vertical flue. Detail the fire stop with façade consultant at SD, not shop drawing stage.

#### 11.3 Spandrel (§12.3 + Diagram 7)

Subject to §7, external wall at any floor must be separated from the external wall at the floor next below by a spandrel that:
1. Is ≥ **900 mm** in height; **and**
2. Is of non-combustible materials with FRP ≥ intervening floor FRP

Diagram 7 shows panel wall / beam / floor all ≥ floor FRP forming the spandrel depth **b ≥ 900 mm**.

**SD takeaways:**
- Full-height vision glass floor-to-floor without 900 mm FR spandrel (or equivalent floor-edge construction counted as spandrel) fails.
- Spandrel may be opaque FR panel, upstand + downstand combination, or structural beam + FR panel — coordinate with structural depth.
- §7 adjoining-building rules can still force higher local façade FRP even where spandrel is satisfied.

### 12. Roofs (§13)

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| All roofs + members forming the roof structure | **Non-combustible** |
| Single-staircase building where highest floor is > **13 m** above ground | Roof FRP ≥ **1 hour** |
| Main roof or any other roof used / intended as refuge floor (or part) | Roof FRP ≥ **2 hours** |

**SD takeaway:** Lightweight combustible roof decks are out. Refuge-on-roof means **2 hr** roof construction — affects roof build-up, plant screening, and helipad / PV coordination.

### 13. Special hazards (§14)

| Hazard | Enclosure |
|---|---|
| Areas for electrical or hazardous installations, or storing dangerous goods | Non-combustible ≥ **2 hours**; **4 hours** where adjoining required staircases; permitted openings with doors ≥ **1 hour** |
| Other high fire-risk areas directly associated with normal occupancy | Non-combustible ≥ **2 hours** |
| Domestic premises with **single exit door**, kitchen adjacent to that door | Kitchen separated by walls ≥ **1 hour**; kitchen entrance door ≥ **½ hour** |

**SD takeaways:**
- Transformer / DG / major switch rooms next to escape stairs → **4 hr** box — keep them off the core face or accept thick walls.
- Studio / single-exit flat layouts: open kitchen at the entrance door needs FR enclosure — plan early.
- “High fire risk associated with normal occupancy” catches commercial kitchens, paint stores, etc. even outside formal DG licensing.

### 14. Basement smoke outlets (§15.2–15.4) — plant / layout drivers

#### 14.1 Without dynamic smoke extract (§15.2)

Every basement must have smoke outlets that:

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Spacing | ≤ **30 m** apart |
| Siting | Along street frontages or adjacent to external walls |
| Level / distribution | High level; evenly around perimeter; arranged to create a **through draught** |
| Coverage | Every basement compartment |
| Aggregate free area | ≥ **0.5%** of floor area served; ≥ **2.5%** for bulk storage / warehouse |
| Least dimension | ≥ **1 m** |
| Relation to stairs | As far as possible from points of discharge of all required staircases; suitably indicated on external face |
| Cover (firefighter-accessible) | Stall-boards or pavement lights easily broken by firefighters |
| Cover (inaccessible open air) | Maintained unobstructed, or grille/louvre of metal **other than aluminium** |

#### 14.2 With dynamic smoke extract to FSD satisfaction (§15.3)

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Number | ≥ **1 smoke outlet per 3 500 m³** of compartment volume, and in no case < **1 outlet per floor** |
| Access | Readily accessible to firefighting personnel in emergency |
| Other §15.2 rules | Still apply except (d) area % and (h) cover rules where incompatible with (a)/(b) |

#### 14.3 Smoke outlet shafts (§15.4)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Shaft extending into / through another storey | FRP of shaft, or of its enclosure, ≥ longer of FRP required for elements of the storey served **or** storeys passed through |
| Adjoining smoke outlet shafts | Separated from each other to the same FRP standard |
| Unenclosed shaft | Resist accidental mechanical damage; satisfy hard-body impact test per **BS 5669** |

**SD takeaways:**
- Basement GA must reserve perimeter smoke outlets — they compete with shopfronts, planters, and EVA.
- 0.5% of a 2 000 m² basement = **10 m²** aggregate free area; warehouse basement at 2.5% = **50 m²** — this is a real plan driver.
- Dynamic extract reduces area % but still needs ≥1 outlet / 3 500 m³ and firefighter access.

### 15. Bridges & tunnels (§16)

#### 15.1 Bridges uniting two or more buildings (§16.1)

| Arrangement | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Default at each end | Fire shutter ≥ **2 hr** + by-pass lobby (walls ≥ **2 hr**, doors ≥ **1 hr**) |
| Two fire shutters ≥ 2 hr at either end | By-pass lobby **not** required unless bridge **> 18 m** |
| If lobby waived but bridge > 18 m | Bridge treated as **integral part** of the building that does **not** have the shutters |
| Bridge structure | Non-combustible; FRP ≥ **lower** of the FRPs of the buildings united |
| Façade near junction | No opening within **900 mm** of bridge junction; walls within that distance non-combustible ≥ **2 hr** |

#### 15.2 Bridge exemption (§16.2)

§16.1 does **not** apply if the bridge:
1. Is constructed wholly of non-combustible materials; **and**
2. Has at the sides only barriers or parapets **≤ 1.2 m** high  

(i.e. open-sided non-combustible link bridge)

#### 15.3 Tunnels (§16.3)

| Arrangement | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Default at each end | Fire shutter ≥ **4 hr** + by-pass lobby (walls ≥ **4 hr**, doors ≥ **2 hr**) |
| Two shutters ≥ 4 hr at either end | Lobby waived unless tunnel **> 18 m** (same “integral building” logic) |
| Tunnel structure | Non-combustible ≥ **4 hr** |

**SD takeaways:**
- Short open-sided non-combustible bridge can avoid shutters/lobbies; enclosed skybridge cannot.
- Budget 900 mm blank façade each side of the connection — kills shopfront continuity at the link.
- Pedestrian tunnels are a **4 hr** problem — treat like basement construction.

### 16. Fire doors (§17)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| All doors required to have FRP | **Self-closing** |
| Doors to required staircases and intercepting lobbies | Must **remain closed** |
| Other FR doors — hold-open allowed only if | Hold-open releases **manually** and upon smoke detection **or** fire alarm actuation (system designed/installed to FSD satisfaction), so door becomes self-closing again |
| Signage (unless hold-open compliant with §17.1) | Both sides, English + Chinese, letter height ≥ **10 mm**: **FIRE DOOR / TO BE KEPT CLOSED** · **防火門 / 應常關** |
| Edge fit (§17.3) | Closely fitted to impede smoke/flame; bottom gap ≤ **4 mm** |
| Test (§17.4a) | Door + frame: BS 476 : Part 22 : 1987; certified per §6.5 for integrity (+ insulation if required); each side separately; **lift doors: landing side only** |
| Hinges (§17.4b) | Entirely materials with melting point ≥ **800°C**, unless proven as part of tested door assembly |
| Uninsulated glazing (§17.4c) | Where insulation not required: max **25%** of door leaf area |
| Aggregate door openings in wall (§17.5) | ≤ **½** of overall wall area — otherwise doors need FRP ≥ **wall FRP** |

**SD takeaways:**
- Hold-open on corridor FR doors is an FSI coordination item (detectors/alarm), not just ironmongery.
- Glass vision panels in FR doors eat the 25% leaf budget fast on 1-hour doors.
- Multiple double doors in a short FR lobby wall can exceed the ½ wall-area rule and force full-wall-rated doorsets.

### 17. Refuge floors (§18)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Refuge area separation | Walls and floors ≥ **2 hours** from the rest of the building, **including** vertical shafts or ducts passing through the floor |
| Shafts / ducts through refuge floor | Must **not open directly** onto that floor |
| Open side (where required to be open) | Not within **6 m**, directly **or diagonally**, of: (a) opposite side of a street; (b) common boundary; (c) any other external wall of same building with FRP < 2 hr, or unprotected opening not protected by ≥ 1 hr fixed light; (d) any other building on the same site |

**Out of scope of this Code (coordinate separately):**
- Which buildings need refuge floors, at what intervals, and minimum refuge area → **MOE 1996** / **FS Code 2011 Part B**
- Fireman’s lift / EVA / FSI on refuge floor → MOA / FSD CoP

**SD takeaway:** FRC only builds the **2 hr box** and **6 m** open-side clearances. Do not site an open refuge face toward a near tower, boundary, or weak façade within 6 m.

### 18. FRP test criteria (Table 3) — what “FRP” means for each element

| # | Element | Stability | Integrity | Insulation | Method of exposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Structural frame, beam or column | Y | N | N | Exposed faces only |
| 2 | Floor incl. compartment floor | Y | Y | Y | Each side separately |
| 3 | Roof forming part of exit route or performing as a floor | Y | Y | Y | From underside |
| 4 | Loadbearing wall **not** forming separating / compartment wall | Y | N | N | Each side separately |
| 5 | External wall | Y* | Y | Y | Each side separately |
| 6 | Compartment wall | Y* | Y | Y | Each side separately |
| 7 | Protected shaft, lobby and corridor | Y* | Y | Y | Each side separately |
| 8 | Fire shutter, fire stop or barrier | N | Y | N | Each side separately |
| 9 | Smoke outlet shaft | Y | — | — | From outside; reducing cross-section to ≤ **75%** of original = stability failure |
| 10 | Enclosure / sealing system for duct, pipe or wire | N | Y | N | From outside |
| 11 | Door (incl. frame & fixing) | N | Y | N† | Each side separately (lift doors: landing side only) |
| 12 | Fixed light (frame, glazing & fixing) | Y* | Y | Y | Each side separately |
| 13 | Enclosure around services in staircase/lobby | N | Y | Y | Each side separately |

Y* = stability required for **loadbearing** elements only.  
† Insulation required when specified (e.g. some Diagram 2 lift doors; doors when aggregate openings > ½ wall area per §17.5).

**Notes:**
- Lintels, posts or jambs of openings in separating/compartment walls = integral part of that wall.
- Test / assessment basis: **BS 476 : Parts 20–24 : 1987** (§6.4–6.5).

### 21. Schematic design checklist (lock before GA freeze)

1. Assign **Table 2 use class(es)** and forecast **compartment volumes** (retail / F&B / warehouse first).
2. Confirm **basement** definition on split-level sites; decide hard **4 hr** cut vs united podium.
3. Map **≥ 2 hr use separations** at every stack change (domestic / office / retail / carpark / industrial).
4. Resolve multi-tenant corridors / balcony approaches (§9) and any “common management” waiver strategy.
5. Size atria / escalator voids with **450 mm × 1 hr** barriers (or BA smoke strategy + management plan).
6. Fix façade: **non-combustible** CW, **floor-edge fire stops**, **900 mm** FR spandrels.
7. Protect stair/lobby external walls within **6 m** of streets, boundaries, weak walls, other buildings; add **450 mm** returns where flush with vision façade.
8. Apply adjoining-building / boundary rules: **1.8 m / 900 mm / 450 mm** (Diagram 1).
9. Enclosure lift wells at **2 hr**; pick Diagram 2 door type (F vs S).
10. Keep non-emergency services out of required stairs unless in FR enclosures; apply §11.5 return wall for single-stair shop houses.
11. Locate basement smoke outlets (0.5% / 2.5% area, ≤30 m spacing, ≥1 m least dimension, clear of stair discharge) or dynamic-extract outlet count.
12. Special hazards: DG / electrical / kitchens at single-exit flats → **2–4 hr** boxes with **1 hr** doors.
13. Bridges / tunnels: shutters, lobbies, **18 m** length trigger, **900 mm** blank façade; prefer open-sided ≤1.2 m parapet bridge if possible.
14. Fire door schedule: self-closing, gaps ≤4 mm, signage, hold-open + FSI, 25% glazing, ≤½ wall opening area.
15. Refuge floors: **2 hr** separation, no shaft openings onto floor, **6 m** open-side clearances; area/frequency from MOE/FS Code.
16. Hand Tables A–F / FRP hours to RSE for covers and steel protection — especially **4 hr** basement.
17. For **new** projects after 2011, migrate constraints into **FS Code 2011 Part C** (FRR terminology, updated tables) — do not submit FRC 1996 alone as the current DTC path.
