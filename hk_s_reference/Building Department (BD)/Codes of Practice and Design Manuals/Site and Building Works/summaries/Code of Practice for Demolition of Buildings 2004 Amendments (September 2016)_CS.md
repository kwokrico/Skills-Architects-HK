# Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings 2004 — Amendments (September 2016)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2016 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: This is the September 2016 amendment sheet to the Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings 2004. It pulls Minor Works Control System demolition out of this code and tightens scaffold, catch-fan, vibration and debris rules. It is **not** the 2004 code, and it is **not** the later October 2023 amendment sheet.

## Regulatory Overview
The code, as amended, does not cover demolition under the Minor Works Control System (Type G). Full demolition that remains under this code must now include the Registered Geotechnical Engineer where slopes or retaining structures are involved, and it must meet the scaffold, vibration and debris limits below.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What this code no longer covers (clause 1.1)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Out of scope | Demolition under the Minor Works Control System (Type G), and major civil engineering works (underpinning, excavation, highway or railway bridges, dams) |
| Type G | Follow the Technical Guidelines on the Minor Works Control System, and the Building (Minor Works) Regulation. Prior approval and consent are not required, but the regulation and the precautionary measures still apply |
| Other methods | Allowed if the Authorized Person, Registered Structural Engineer, Registered Geotechnical Engineer and Registered Specialist Contractor (Demolition), or their consultants, recommend them on research and engineering assessment |
| No record plans (clause 2.1.1(A)) | Retrieve record plans of the building and adjoining streets. If they are not available, do an on-site survey and, if necessary, material testing |

**SD takeaway:** A Type G minor-works demolition is not designed under this code; a full demolition without record plans needs a survey before the method is fixed.

### 2. Scaffold, nets and catch platforms (clauses 3.3.1, 3.3.2, Table 3.3)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Bamboo scaffold ties | To sound anchors at not more than **4 m** horizontally and vertically, plus the Code of Practice for Bamboo Scaffolding Safety and the Guidelines on the Design and Construction of Bamboo Scaffolds |
| Scaffold higher than **15 m** | Steel brackets to the building or another support, at not more than **15 m** |
| Cantilever above the top floor | Ties and struts of adequate strength |
| Unsecured scaffold after ties are cut | Unbraced section not higher than **2 m** from the nearest anchor |
| Polyethylene net (Table 3.3) | Polyethylene; string diameter **1 mm**; **16** plys; mesh opening **20 mm**; weight **2130 g/m** (as printed) |
| Extra net, where appropriate | Heavy-duty nylon, minimum diameter **3.5 mm**, mesh opening **50 mm** maximum, in addition to the tarpaulin and the polyethylene net |
| Catch platform | On top of the covered walkway per clause 3.2, and underneath projecting structures over a street (canopies, balconies, and where needed external features and prestressed elements). Install before demolition. Design for the anticipated demolition load |

**SD takeaway:** Scaffold ties are at 4 m, a scaffold over 15 m needs steel brackets at 15 m, and any projection over a street gets a catch platform before demolition starts.

### 3. Propping, vibration and complex structures (clauses 3.5.5, 3.8.7, 6.2)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Props | Lateral restraint in at least **2** directions, with cross-bracing at the perimeter and the end bays |
| Departure from Table 3.4 | Full structural substantiation by the Registered Structural Engineer. Old buildings designed to the 1952 London County Council permissible-stress codes have a lower capacity; clause 3.5.3(C)(4) allows sleepers and base plates to spread load |
| Vibration | Peak particle velocity at any adjoining structure not exceeding **15 mm/s** transient and **7.5 mm/s** prolonged and continuous, from mechanical demolition. Aligns with PNAP APP-137 as stated in the sheet |
| Site engineer | Required for flat slab, prestressed concrete, transfer plate, hanger, beam longer than **10 m**, steel frame, cantilever over a street with span greater than **1.2 m**, or a building that retains ground with a level difference exceeding **1.5 m** |
| Who | Registered Professional Engineer (structural, civil or building), or as the current practice note requires. Ground support during demolition is engineered by a Registered Geotechnical Engineer |

**SD takeaway:** A street cantilever longer than 1.2 m, a beam over 10 m, or a 1.5 m retaining level difference puts a site engineer on the demolition, and adjoining vibration stays within 15 mm/s transient and 7.5 mm/s continuous.

### 4. Debris (clause 3.10 and the worked example in the sheet)

| Location | Limit |
|---|---|
| Lift shafts | Accumulation not exceeding **1 m** high |
| Floors | No debris accumulation unless justified by structural calculations. The previous **100 mm** floor cap is withdrawn |
| Ground floor | Not exceeding **1 m** above the slab |
| Cantilevered structures | No debris |

**SD takeaway:** Floor debris is no longer automatically allowed up to 100 mm; it needs a calculation, and cantilevers stay clear.
