# Guide to Fire Safety Design for Caverns
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
1994 | Building Authority and Fire Services Department

> Read with: Minimum FSI CoP (Cap. 123 s.16(1)(b)); MOE Code; B(P)R **41B**; GEOGUIDE 4 (cavern engineering). This guide is a **restricted-use interim** standard — DG / oil / gas storage is **not** covered.

---

## Regulatory Overview

This guide applies only to **underground rock caverns** used for **public utilities** (e.g. sewage treatment works, refuse transfer stations, water service reservoirs) that have a low population of mainly familiar on-site workers, a localized / controlled / relatively low fire load, **no sleeping risk**, and an effective management scheme plus emergency contingency plan. It does **not** cover storage of oil, gas or other dangerous goods, and is not a general-occupancy cavern code.

---

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. SD kill-switches — use gate before massing

| Condition | Must be true |
|---|---|
| Occupancy | Public-utility type cavern (low population) |
| Users | Mainly on-site workers **familiar** with the cavern |
| Fire load | Localized, closely controlled, relatively **low** |
| Sleeping | **None** |
| Management | Effective management + satisfactory contingency plan |

**Hard outs:** oil / gas / DG storage → outside this guide (seek specialist / overseas practice). Early FSD + BD discussion required for emergency access strategy.

---

### 2. Definitions that lock layout

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Place of safety** | The **open air** |
| **Place of safe passage** | Escape stairs, corridors or another fire compartment adequately protected from fire and smoke — the protected route to a place of safety |
| **Setting-down point** | Area between the **access tunnel** and the **underground cavern compartment** — turning for FS appliances, space for controls, and the firefighting frontier |

---

### 3. FSI package — Minimum FSI CoP applies underground

Fire safety requirements in the **Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment** apply. Features particularly relevant:

- Audio/visual advisory systems  
- Automatic actuating devices  
- Automatic fixed installations (water and non-water) — sprinkler, drencher, water spray  
- Deluge (where fire may spread faster than progressive sprinkler operation)  
- Dust / gas detection systems  
- Dynamic smoke extraction; gas extraction  
- Emergency generator; emergency lighting  
- Exit and directional signs  
- Manual fire alarm; fire detection systems  
- Fire control centre  
- Fire hydrant / hose reel; fixed foam; portable appliances  
- Pressurization of staircases  
- Ventilation / AC control systems  
- Communications systems  

---

### 4. Detection / early warning

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Objective | Early warning + direct link to **Fire Services Communication Centre** |
| Preferred system | **Smoke detection** |
| Humid / dusty areas | Infra-red or other suitable detectors |
| Complex layout | **Addressable** detection |
| Total gas flooding zones | **Cross-zone** detection (reduce unwanted alarms) |
| Manual fire alarm | Linked to automatic detection + FS Communication Centre |
| Actuation roles | May also trigger suppression, fire shutters, smoke extraction |

---

### 5. Compartmentation

| Situation | Max compartment volume |
|---|---|
| General cavern | **≤ 28,000 m³** (departure possible by fire load / fire-spread limitation) |
| Vehicular access tunnels | **No volume limit** |
| Warehousing / storage use | **≤ 7,000 m³** |

**Fire shutters** used for compartmentation: automatic closing actuated by fire detection **plus** manual control.

---

### 6. Fire resisting period (FRP)

Cavern stability does **not** depend on internal structural support like a basement; rock provides comprehensive fire separation. **FRP ≥ 4 h is not automatic.**

| Condition | Typical FRP |
|---|---|
| ≤ **4 levels**, MOE does **not** require stair ascent | **1–2 h** by fire load |
| Very low fire load (e.g. water service reservoir) | **≥ 1 h** |
| MOE requires ascent of stairs | Higher FRP |
| **≥ 4 levels** | Generally **≥ 4 h** |

| Rock support | Fire protection |
|---|---|
| Rock bolts / shotcrete / fibrecrete lining | No special protection |
| Rock anchors | Case-by-case (heat → load loss implications) |

---

### 7. Means of escape — hard numbers

**Principle:** Occupants must reach a place of safety (directly or via place of safe passage) before the fire-affected cavern becomes untenable. Safe passage must match flow capacity and fire/smoke protection of an escape stair in an equivalent above-ground tall building.

#### 7.1 Population

Design population = normal users **+** visitors **+** casual / maintenance workers. Mark dedicated visitor routes / viewing areas (visitors are unfamiliar with layout).

#### 7.2 Travel distance to place of safe passage

Subject to adequate smoke control and fire safety measures (distances may exceed normal above-ground limits):

| Travel option | Max distance |
|---|---|
| **Single direction** | **18 m** |
| **Two or more choices of direction** | **72 m** (may extend if fire loads are low and escape routes are extremely simple) |

| Exit-time check | Value |
|---|---|
| Normal individual exit time limit | **2½ minutes** |
| Assumed travel speed | **1 m/s** |

MOE provisions assume a suitable **smoke control** system (§6).

#### 7.3 Place of safe passage — mandatory provisions

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Smoke | **Pressurization**; if impracticable (large volume / connected to open air) → suitable **smoke extraction** |
| Enclosure | Fire-resisting construction to required FRP |
| Widths | Per **MOE Code** for max expected population |
| Clear height | **≥ 2 m** |
| Doors into safe passage | Self-closing; FRP **≥ ½** of safe-passage enclosure FRP |
| Upward vertical travel **> 10 m** | Design for max population **+ 25%** (slower travel) |
| Total horizontal distance in safe passage | Max **750 m** (must not unduly stress occupants) |
| Roadway as safe passage | Separate footway **≥ 1 m** wide |
| Ramp as safe passage | Nowhere steeper than **1:12** |
| Staircase as safe passage | Conform to MOE Code |
| Emergency lighting | Per §7 |

**Alternative approaches:** Departure from these figures is allowed only if §5.1 principles are proven (people-movement analysis + fire growth / smoke-flow predictions).

---

### 8. Smoke management

| Objective | Detail |
|---|---|
| Limit | Lateral and vertical smoke movement |
| Extract | Smoke and hot air |
| Support | Rescue / firefighting; keep escape routes free of smoke and heat |
| Means | Suitably designed **mechanical** smoke control |
| Extraction rate | Design fires + calculation method **agreed with FSD** |

---

### 9. Emergency lighting and signage

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Why critical | Artificial light is the **only** illumination |
| Location | Prefer **low level** on escape routes (remain usable in smoke) |
| Design standard | **BS 5266: Part 1: 1975** |
| Exit / facility signs | Legible from reasonable distance, easily recognized, illuminated |
| Directional signs | **BS 5499: Part 1: 1984**, Table 10 |

---

### 10. Emergency access / setting-down (massing drivers)

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| EVA / tunnel access | Adequate carriageway, headroom, loading, gradient, turning for major FS appliances |
| Setting-down point | Normally at **end of access tunnel**, just before cavern entry |
| Setting-down FRP | **≥ 4 hours** — highest absolute safety for firefighting / rescue |
| Exemption | Only if fire load low, access tunnel short, and low familiar population (e.g. water service reservoir) |
| Integration | Integrate with MOE — FS personnel expected to use protected escape routes for entry / passage |
| Additional access | May be required by use/design — early FSD + BD discussion |
| Fireman’s lifts (if provided) | **B(P)R Regulation 41B** |

---

### 11. Plant / control siting

| Element | Location / rule |
|---|---|
| **Fire Control Centre** | Fire-rated enclosure at **tunnel portal**; **direct access from open air** (may share Management / Security Office) |
| Generator, main switchgear, transformers, fire control rooms | **Outside** the underground cavern |
| Water for FSI | Water mains in the **access tunnel** |
| Secondary power | Independent supply source **or** suitable emergency generator for FSI and essential services |

---

### 12. Communications and operations

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Hard-wired | Intercom / telephones at **regular intervals** to Fire Control Centre |
| Radio | Two-way system (antennae, base/repeater, handhelds) incorporating an **FSD mobile frequency** for inside/outside coordination |
| Contingency plan | Operation manual with evacuation plan; detailed layout plans at strategic locations; fire drills at suitable intervals |
| Maintenance | FSI installed and maintained to satisfaction of Director of Fire Services |

---

**SD takeaway:** Confirm utility-only eligibility (no DG / sleeping) before sketching. Lock compartment volumes (**28,000 / 7,000 m³**), MOE distances (**18 / 72 m**), safe-passage geometry (2 m clear, 1:12 ramp, 750 m max, +25% if >10 m ascent), **4 h** setting-down at tunnel–cavern interface, portal FCC with open-air access, and all major plant **outside** the cavern — these drive tunnel length, portal works and plant siting from day one.
