# Technical Guidelines on the Minor Works Control System (2024)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
2024 revision, with the October 2025 amendment and the August 2026 amendment | Buildings Department

> Scope note: The three source PDFs are in Chinese. This summary is written from the extracted Chinese text of the 2024 technical guidelines and from the two Chinese amendment sheets. It is not taken from the English general guidelines. Suggested details in Appendix VII are largely drawings; dimensions that appear only on those image sheets are not copied here. The text comparison tables and the statutory procedure chapters were extracted.

## Regulatory Overview
Minor works may still proceed under section 14(1) of the Buildings Ordinance with prior approval and consent, or under section 14AA and Part 6 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation without that prior approval. Class I and Class II require a commencement notice at least 7 days before work starts, Class III does not, and every class must submit the completion certificate or notice within 14 days after completion.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Classes, types, and items (sections 2.1–2.3)
The Minor Works Control System came into operation on 31 December 2010. Works are divided by nature, scale, complexity, and safety risk.

| Class | Items in this edition | Who prepares and signs the prescribed documents | Notice before start |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 58 | Prescribed building professional and prescribed registered contractor | Not less than 7 days |
| II | 68 | Prescribed registered contractor | Not less than 7 days |
| III | 61 | Prescribed registered contractor | No submission before start |
| All three | 187 | — | Completion documents within 14 days |

Each class is further divided into eight types by trade. Section 2.2 and Appendix I set out the types; the text layer of section 2.2 does not print the eight type names (they sit in a figure). Item numbers use the class as the first digit. Item 1.1, erection or alteration of an internal staircase that is not a means of escape or a firefighting and rescue stair, is a Class I item. The Buildings Department carries out sample checks.

**SD takeaway:** Identify the item number before schematic lock, because the class sets who must be appointed and whether a 7-day commencement notice is required.

### 2. Recurring design locks in the definitions (section 1.3)
| Term | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Extra load | In general, minor works should not put extra load on any cantilevered slab, and should not alter any other structural member. They should not be carried out on a floor or roof that is designed as a cantilevered slab |
| Cantilevered structure | A beam or slab, or a combination, supported at one end only. Examples given include canopies, balconies, window sills, air-conditioner hoods, architectural fins, and drying racks |
| Display area of a signboard | The largest face of a virtual rectangular prism that contains the signboard and its supporting structure, excluding a single pole of not more than 100 mm diameter, or members that only stop sideways movement. The note on the figure states a rectangular face of 20 m² as the Class I maximum and a thickness of 600 mm as the maximum. See PNAP APP-126 Appendix B |
| Protective barrier | Required at the edge of a balcony, verandah, floor, roof, stair, landing, or projection, and where the difference between two adjacent levels exceeds 600 mm. Height is measured from the finished floor of the adjacent surface that can be stood on. PNAP APP-110 applies |
| Openable window as a barrier | If any openable part is less than 1.1 m above the adjacent surface that can be stood on, replacement must add a protective barrier under the Building (Planning) Regulations, which is minor works item 1.6 |
| Fixed window as a barrier | If the original design already uses a non-openable window within 1.1 m of that surface as the barrier, repair or replacement to the original design is also repair or replacement of the barrier, which is item 2.5 |
| Window or glass wall used as a barrier | If the bottom of the main frame is at least 0.5 m and less than 1.1 m above the adjacent ground of a residential building or stair, including a landing, strengthening may follow Appendix X |
| Original design | The approved plans, or the prescribed plans submitted under the simplified requirements. Repair of an individual component to the original design (hinges, glass, handles, local concrete repair, replacement of a curtain-wall glass pane) need not be brought up to current standards. Other repair or replacement to the original design must follow current standards |
| Building services installation | Includes a solar water-heating system, photovoltaic system, antenna, transceiver, air-conditioner, cooling tower, light, or pump, and the associated pipes. It does not include a water tank, lift, stairlift, lifting platform, ventilation duct, or radio communications station |

**SD takeaway:** Do not add minor works onto a cantilevered slab, and treat any openable window within 1.1 m of a standable surface as needing a protective barrier.

### 3. Window and glass-wall class split (section 3.1, as a pattern for the comparison tables)
| Item | What it allows | Dimensional lock printed in the comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.60 | Repair or replacement of a window or glass wall to the original design, without altering other structural members except a simply supported beam that is not prestressed and does not support a column, flat slab, or ribbed beam, and without extra load on a cantilevered slab | Highest point more than 3.5 m above ground. Repair or replacement of a window or glass wall with a structural span greater than 6 m may still use item 1.60 if it follows the original design |
| 2.8 | Construction, alteration, repair, or replacement | Highest point not more than 100 m above ground, and structural span not more than 6 m. If the highest point is more than 3.5 m and not more than 100 m, the work involves only a sub-frame not longer than 1.2 m. If the highest point is not more than 3.5 m, the span is not more than 6 m |
| 3.6 | Construction, alteration, repair, or replacement, and not item 2.8 | If the highest point is not more than 100 m, the work involves the main frame, or a sub-frame longer than 1.2 m |
| Above 100 m | Construction or alteration under the comparison for item 1.60 | Opening area not more than 6 m², and the shorter of length or width not more than 1.8 m. Repair or replacement is to the original design, or within those opening limits |
| Removal | Items 2.9 and 3.7 | Height of the window or glass wall not more than 6 m, and highest point not more than 3.5 m above ground, for the Class III removal comparison |

Other locks printed with these items: no window within 5 m of an opening of an MTR ventilation shaft (PNAP APP-24 and PNRC 14); a balcony exempted from gross floor area under section 42 of the Buildings Ordinance must not be enclosed; common parts need the consent of the owners’ corporation, co-owners, or management office where applicable.

**SD takeaway:** Place a new or altered window by its height above ground, span, and whether the main frame is touched, because those three figures choose Class I, II, or III.

### 4. Who is appointed, and the submission clock (section 4)
| Route | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Prior approval and consent | Appoint an authorized person and, where needed, a registered structural engineer and/or registered geotechnical engineer. Appoint a registered general building contractor, registered specialist contractor, or registered minor works contractor. The authorized person certifies within 14 days after completion that the work follows the approved plans and is structurally safe, and submits the completion certificate |
| Simplified requirements | Only Class I requires a prescribed building professional. The authorized person designs and supervises. A registered structural engineer or registered geotechnical engineer is added for the structural or geotechnical part. Class II and Class III may be designed, supervised, and built by the prescribed registered contractor without a prescribed building professional |
| Specified Class I construction | Under regulation 37(4), an authorized person may also prepare and sign the foundation plans, structural details, or calculations if the structure is not higher than 10 m, member spans are not more than 6 m, members are timber, masonry, steel, plain concrete, or reinforced concrete, the spread footing bears at not more than 300 kPa and is not more than 2 m below existing ground, and existing load-bearing members are not structurally altered |
| Registered general building contractor | May carry out every minor works item |
| Registered specialist contractor | Only the items in regulation 28 for the category of registration |
| Registered minor works contractor (company) | Only types registered for the authorized signatory, at the registered class or below |
| Registered minor works contractor (individual) | Only the registered Class III items |
| Class I supervision plan | Submit the supervision plan required by paragraph 6.4 of the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans 2009 |
| Photographs | Same position and angle before and after, with a scale rule when a critical dimension must be shown |
| Plans | Not smaller than 1:100 for plans and sections. Details may be enlarged to 1:5 or 1:10. A3 is suggested. Block plan not smaller than 1:500. Colour existing and new work (PNAP ADM-9; colours also in Appendix VI of the guidelines) |
| Receipt | The acknowledgement confirms receipt only. It is not a statement that the documents comply |
| Audit | The Department checks that the appointed person is qualified for the item, and samples submissions, including site inspection |
| Minor works before demolition of the parent building | The same project team submits Form MW01 and the demolition plans to the New Buildings Division 1 at least 7 days before those minor works start. **SD takeaway:** For anything other than Class I, do not assume an authorized person must be appointed under the simplified route, and still lodge completion documents within 14 days.

### 5. October 2025 amendment (scaffold coverings)
Section 11.2.10 already requires scaffold design, erection, substantial extension, alteration, maintenance, dismantling, and use to follow the Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations and the Labour Department codes for bamboo scaffolds and metal scaffolds. The October 2025 sheet adds that every protective net, protective screen, tarpaulin, or plastic sheet installed to cover an external wall and the related scaffold must have suitable flame-retardant properties and meet a recognised standard. Examples printed on the sheet are GB 5725-2009 (or the former GB 16909-1997), BS 5867-2:2008 Type B, and NFPA 701:2019 Test Method 2. Another standard may be used if it has equivalent flame-retardant performance.

**SD takeaway:** Specify a flame-retardant scaffold net or tarpaulin to one of the three named standards, or to an equivalent, before the scaffold package is ordered.

### 6. August 2026 amendment (roof finish and slab openings)
Roof finish, items 1.62 and 2.34, types A and F. Laying or repair of a roof finish must not alter other structural members. For laying or repair, the thickness shall not exceed the original design thickness, and the work must not be item 2.34 and must not be designated exempted works item 7. The original thickness is the difference between the structural level and the finish level on the approved plans, or design superimposed load in kilopascals multiplied by 1,000 and divided by 23 MPa, using the load shown on the approved structural plans or in the design calculations. The sheet also points to Building (Planning) Regulation 73 on elderly-friendly design, Building (Construction) Regulation 34 on a weatherproof roof, and the energy-efficiency regulation, the 1995 OTTV code, these residential energy guidelines, and PNAP APP-67, APP-156, and APP-2.

Slab opening, the comparison on the amendment sheet: an opening larger than 1 m² and not larger than 4.5 m² is distinguished from an opening not larger than 1 m². The opening area is the total of each new opening and any adjoining existing opening after the work. If the furthest two points of an opening are not more than 150 mm apart, it may be designated exempted works item 1. Restoring an opening follows the same 1 m² and 4.5 m² split, and restoration of an opening whose furthest two points are more than 150 mm apart is distinguished from the smaller case. The opening must not add load to a cantilevered slab. A new opening that leaves a level difference needs a protective barrier (item 1.6 and PNAP APP-110). The use of the opening must be stated on the plan.

**SD takeaway:** Size a roof finish to the original structural allowance, using the 23 MPa formula if the finish thickness is not dimensioned, and keep a new slab opening inside the 1 m² or 4.5 m² band that matches the item.

### 7. Validation schemes named in the guidelines (sections 1.4 and 7)
The Household Minor Works Validation Scheme and the Minor Amenity Facilities Validation Scheme are statutory routes for prescribed building or building works. The 2021 amendment added the Minor Amenity Facilities Validation Scheme for smaller, lower-risk unauthorised minor amenity facilities that had been erected before 1 September 2020. The Signboard Validation Scheme is in section 7.3 of the Chinese guidelines. Detailed signboard validation procedure in English is in the separate 2014 addendum in this folder; do not copy that English addendum into this Chinese-source summary.

**SD takeaway:** An existing unauthorised minor amenity feature can be considered for validation only if it was erected before 1 September 2020 and is one of the prescribed items.
