# Practice Guidebook for Adaptive Re-use of and Alteration and Addition Works to Heritage Buildings
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
2021 edition (January 2021). First issue June 2009. The cover also carries the 2012 title with the 2021 edition | Buildings Department

> Scope note: How alteration and addition, and adaptive re-use, of a heritage building can meet building safety and health under the Buildings Ordinance, including where an equivalent alternative is accepted. Prepared with the Antiquities and Monuments Office, the Fire Services Department, and the Architectural Services Department. Dedicated Heritage Units process the submissions.

## Regulatory Overview
Except for government buildings, buildings on land vested in or managed by the Housing Authority, and buildings on unleased government land, an alteration and addition proposal for a heritage building, with or without a material change in use, is submitted for approval and consent before work starts, with an authorized person and normally a registered structural engineer. Parts affected by the works or by a change in use are normally brought up to current standards, while an alternative may be accepted if it demonstrably reaches an equivalent level of safety and health.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the guidebook treats as heritage (section 2)
| Status | Effect printed in the guidebook |
| --- | --- |
| Monument or proposed monument | Declared under section 3 of the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance. The building is legally protected. Confirm the monument boundary with the Antiquities and Monuments Office |
| Grade 1 | Outstanding merit. Every effort should be made to preserve if possible. A pool for the Antiquities Advisory Board to consider for monument status |
| Grade 2 | Special merit. Selectively preserve |
| Grade 3 | Some merit. Preservation in some form is desirable. Alternative means may be considered if preservation is not practicable |
| Graded building | Unlike a declared monument, a graded building does not enjoy protection under the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance |
| Adaptive re-use | Modifying a building for a use other than its original use, such as a residence becoming an exhibition hall or a tea house open to the public |

The owner or agent should check the status before any works.

**SD takeaway:** Confirm monument or grade, and the monument boundary, before massing or opening-up, because a grade is not the same legal protection as a declaration.

### 2. When current standards apply (sections 3.5–3.7)
Buildings built before a new standard came into force are not, in principle, required to be upgraded, except for certain commercial, domestic, and composite buildings under the Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Ordinance (Cap. 502) and the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572). When there is a change in use or alteration and addition, the parts affected are normally required to comply with current standards. New structural elements in the alteration and addition should be designed to current structural standards. The age of the building is a reason to appoint a registered structural engineer for structural appraisal and integrity assessment. Comments from the Antiquities and Monuments Office are considered.

**SD takeaway:** Upgrade the parts the alteration or the new use actually touches, and commission the structural appraisal before the new use is locked.

### 3. Balanced approach and alternative design (section 4)
An alternative that departs from the prescriptive requirements will be favourably considered if an equivalent level of safety and health is explicitly demonstrated, case by case. The authorized person and registered structural engineer should liaise with the Buildings Department and the Antiquities and Monuments Office. From the conservation side, the Office may consider compatibility of the proposed use, reversibility of the works, authenticity, and the degree of disturbance.

| Route | When it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Fire engineering | Often used to reach equivalent fire safety. Compensatory measures may include automatic detection, smoke control, suppression such as sprinklers, and compartmentation. Part F and Part G of the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings apply |
| Management approach | Only if no alternative of equivalent standard is feasible and practicable. Acceptance is on the merits of the case. The authorized person formulates a feasible and enforceable management plan with the owner and, if already identified, the future operator. Appendix III is a sample management plan |

**SD takeaway:** Offer an equivalent-performance alternative, agreed with the Antiquities and Monuments Office, before relying on a management plan.

### 4. Existing protective barriers (section 5)
| Case | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Height less than 1100 mm but not less than 900 mm, at balconies and verandahs | May be favourably considered if the height is not less than 900 mm and the sum of the top-surface width measured from the centreline of the barrier (not less than 300 mm) and the barrier height (not less than 900 mm) is not less than 1220 mm |
| That lower barrier | The top is constructed so people cannot sit on it or place articles that could fall. Fixing and strength meet the minimum horizontal imposed load. Warning signs state the lower height and that people should not sit on the parapet. The floor in front is non-slip |
| Wider openings, and a barrier that does not fully meet the prescriptive function | Compensatory measures can include restricted use, warning signs, a management system that stops children reaching the barrier, and a management plan under Appendix III |
| Premises used primarily by children | These compensatory measures will not be accepted. Examples printed: kindergarten, nursery, learning centre, child care centre, primary school |

**SD takeaway:** An existing balcony parapet under 1100 mm can be argued only if it is at least 900 mm and the width-plus-height total reaches 1220 mm, and not where the use is primarily for children.
