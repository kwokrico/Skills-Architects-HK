# Guidelines on Design and Construction Requirements for Energy Efficiency of Residential Buildings 2014
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
September 2014 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Fabric rules for new residential buildings: residential thermal transfer value (RTTV) of walls and roofs, and natural ventilation for thermal comfort (NVTC) of habitable spaces. This summary covers the main guidelines, the Appendix VI sample layout, and the four calculation workbooks. It does not replace the Overall Thermal Transfer Value (OTTV) control for commercial buildings and hotels.

## Regulatory Overview
The overall RTTV of the external walls of a residential building shall not exceed 14 W/m², and the overall RTTV of the roof shall not exceed 4 W/m²; the two values are reported separately and are not combined. Habitable spaces are checked for NVTC by cross ventilation, single-sided ventilation, or an alternative simulation that reaches an area-weighted average wind velocity of not less than 0.4 m/s.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Where the guidelines apply (sections 1.3 and 1.4)
| Rule | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Residential building | Domestic premises in a domestic or composite building, including a hostel or dormitory for pupils out of school hours |
| Not a residential building for this guide | Hospital, residential care home for the elderly, residential care home for the disabled, hotel, and guesthouse under the Hotel and Guesthouse Accommodation Ordinance (Cap. 349) |
| Caretaker exemption | A place of residence of not more than 50 m² usable floor space, for caretakers or persons employed in connection with the building |
| RTTV scope | All enclosed spaces of residential units, except bathrooms and enclosed kitchens |
| NVTC scope | Habitable spaces (living, dining, and bedrooms). An open kitchen in the same enclosed space as the living or dining area is included |
| Above Regulation 30 | NVTC is above the prescribed-window minimum in Building (Planning) Regulation 30 |

**SD takeaway:** Confirm the building is a residential building under section 1.4, and keep any caretaker flat at or below 50 m² if it is to stay outside these guidelines.

### 2. RTTV caps, formula, and what does not count (sections 2.1–2.3 and 2.6)
| Item | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| RTTVwall | Not more than 14 W/m² on the overall external walls |
| RTTVroof | Not more than 4 W/m² on the roofs |
| Submission | Report both values on the Appendix III forms. Do not merge them into one figure |
| Wall formula | RTTVwall = 3.57(1−WWR)Uw α Gw + 0.64(WWR)Uf Gw + 41.75(WWR)(SC)(ESC) Gw |
| Roof formula | RTTVroof = 3.47(1−SRR)Ur αr Gs + 0.40(SRR)Usl Gs + 41.10(SRR)(SCr) Gs |
| Climate assumption | Units are treated as not mechanically cooled from November to March inclusive. OTTV assumes conditioning through the year |
| Not counted in RTTV | Internal blinds and draperies; shading or reflection from adjacent buildings; efficient lamps, luminaires, air-conditioners, and controls |
| Air-conditioner openings | An opening with an air-conditioner installed, or with an opaque infill panel if the unit is not installed, is not counted |
| Shaded roof | A performance model from April to October, 24 hours a day, with adjacent rooms cooled to 22°C, must beat a barely compliant unshaded roof. Only permanent architectural shading counts |

Wall orientation factors (Gw) from Table 9, April to October: North 0.790, NE 0.924, East 1.072, SE 1.051, South 0.975, SW 1.092, West 1.131, NW 0.965. Roof orientation factor (Gs) is 2.16. If a direction falls between cardinal points, use the closest. A sloped surface is a linear interpolation between Gs and the wall factor for the direction the slope faces.

**SD takeaway:** Freeze wall and roof RTTV as two separate submissions, both under the caps, and treat a west-facing glazed wall as the tightest orientation factor.

### 3. External shading that may be counted (section 2.5)
| Projection | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Overhang length A, or side-fin length C | Not more than 1.5 m from the external wall |
| Long projection, north facades (NNW to NNE) | If the projection is more than 500 mm, the overhang projection factor (OPF) or side-fin projection factor (SPF) shall not be less than 0.2, and the shade shall be solid or effectively opaque |
| Long projection, other orientations | If the projection is more than 750 mm, OPF or SPF shall not be less than 0.5 |
| Re-entrant narrower than 4.5 m | Projection length not more than 500 mm |
| Multiple fins | The smallest OPF or SPF must meet the single-fin rules, and the free area shall be at least 2/3 |
| Not effectively opaque | A quantitative check must match a solid shade with those minimum OPF or SPF values |
| Top of an overhang | Minimum drop of 150 mm from the adjacent internal space |
| Overhang plus side-fin | ESCtotal = ESCoverhang × ESCsidefin |

A projection is effectively opaque for an overhang when X and Y are vertically aligned and the angle is less than 90°. For a side-fin, U and V are vertically aligned, or P and Q are horizontally aligned, and the angle is less than 90°.

**SD takeaway:** Keep sunshades at or under 1.5 m, cut them to 500 mm inside a re-entrant narrower than 4.5 m, and do not count a see-through fin unless the opacity test is met.

### 4. Deemed-to-satisfy wall route, glare, and daylight (sections 2.7–2.9)
If, for each facade orientation, the window-to-wall ratio (WWR) and the facade shading coefficient (SCfacade = SCglass × ESCshading device) are not more than the values in Tables 15 to 20, the wall is deemed to meet the RTTVwall cap. The full RTTVwall calculation in section 2.2, and submission of that calculation, are then not required. RTTVroof is still a separate target. Method 1 checks eight orientations. Method 2 uses Category A (NNE to NNW, those two directions exclusive) and Category B (NNW to NNE, those two directions inclusive).

If one external finish is more than 60% of the gross wall area in one orientation, its absorptivity may be taken as the average for that orientation.

Tables 15 to 20 are image grids. Tables 15 (absorptivity ≤ 0.4) and 20 (absorptivity ≤ 0.9) were read. Tables 16 to 19 use the same WWR rows (0.70 down to 0.20) and the same orientation columns for absorptivity ≤ 0.5, ≤ 0.6, ≤ 0.7, and ≤ 0.8. Selected maximum SCfacade values:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.70 | ≤ 0.4 (Table 15) | 0.46 | 0.30 | 0.35 | 0.28 |
| 0.40 | ≤ 0.4 (Table 15) | 0.78 | 0.50 | 0.58 | 0.46 |
| 0.70 | ≤ 0.9 (Table 20) | 0.39 | 0.24 | 0.28 | 0.21 |
| 0.40 | ≤ 0.9 (Table 20) | 0.54 | 0.26 | 0.34 | 0.22 |

| Glass | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| External reflectance | Glass with external reflectance exceeding 20% on any external surface of any window, door, wall, or roof is not accepted for RTTV compliance |
| Visible light transmittance | Glazing with visible light transmittance of less than 50% should not be used for prescribed windows. Glass that is not a prescribed window is not subject to this limit |

**SD takeaway:** Either stay inside the Table 15–20 shading-coefficient limits for the facade absorptivity, or submit the full wall calculation, and keep prescribed-window glass at 50% visible light transmittance or more and at 20% external reflectance or less.

### 5. Cross ventilation (section 3.2)
| Rule | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Path | Not more than two straight lines (one turn only), from the middle of one window to the other. The turn is not greater than 90° |
| Length | The cross-ventilation path must be less than 12 m for each habitable space |
| Room depth to a secondary window plane | Not more than 12 m from the primary opening to the secondary opening. Any notional plane between them is added, and the total shall not exceed 12 m |
| Window positions | In different halves of the smallest rectangle around the assessed area, split on the longer side |
| Complicated rooms | Split into notional rooms. Every notional room must comply |
| Corridor path | Include a notional strip 750 mm wide in the floor area used for the openable-window check |
| Openable area | Aggregate openable windows not less than 1/8 of the usable floor area, including the notional plane and, where used, the bathroom or toilet and the 750 mm corridor strip |
| Secondary opening | Preferably not less than 1/16 of the usable floor area, and not less than 1/20 of the usable floor area of the rooms and any notional corridor |
| Re-entrant | A re-entrant begins where a 4.5 m wide external plane can no longer pass. A secondary opening is acceptable if a secondary window plane 2.3 m wide and 4.5 m long can connect it to that external plane |
| Re-entrant of not more than 4 storeys | Deemed to meet the secondary window plane regardless of width and length |
| Notional plane | Width of 1 m or the width of the secondary opening, whichever is larger. If a portion is narrower than 1 m, its depth shall not be more than its width |
| What may cross the planes | Effective sunshades under section 2.5, and amenity features (drying racks, small air-conditioner platforms or hoods, and window eaves) if not excessive. The planes are otherwise vertically uncovered |

A bathroom or toilet door may be treated as open for the check only if every sanitary fitment and floor drain has an effective water seal (floor drains as in paragraph 2 of PNAP ADV-24), the bathroom windows meet the cross-ventilation rules, and the secondary opening is at least 1/20 of the aggregate floor area of the habitable spaces and the bathroom or toilet. Kitchen doors and entrance doors to common areas are treated as closed. Other internal doors are treated as open.

**SD takeaway:** Pair openings on opposite halves of the room, keep the path under 12 m and to one turn of not more than 90°, and size the openable area to at least one-eighth of the floor area being counted.

### 6. Single-sided ventilation and air-conditioner separation (sections 3.3 and 3.4)
| Rule | Lock |
| --- | --- |
| Room depth | Not more than 4.5 m, measured from the centre of the openable window. Only windows inside that depth count toward the openable area |
| Where the window faces | A primary ventilation opening facing an external plane, or connected to one by a notional plane |
| Notional plane | Width equal to the opening or not less than 1 m, whichever is larger. Depth is then measured from where the external plane meets the notional plane to the furthest corner. One turn only, not greater than 90° |
| Openable area | Not less than 1/5 of the usable floor area, including the notional plane |
| Heat from another unit’s air-conditioner | At least 1.5 m below, or horizontally apart from, the ventilation openings of an adjacent unit, openings and notional planes in a re-entrant less than 4.5 m wide, and the common boundary of an adjoining site |
| Measurement at occupation permit | If the unit is installed, measure to the closest outer edge of the unit or of the platform, whichever is closer. If it is not installed, measure to the closest outer edge of the window opening for the unit or of the platform, whichever is closer |

**SD takeaway:** Keep a single-sided room within 4.5 m of its window and give it openable area of at least one-fifth, and place air-conditioners at least 1.5 m clear of a neighbour’s ventilation opening.

### 7. Simulation alternative and the companion files (section 3.5, Appendix VI, and the workbooks)
The simplified simulation uses a wind speed of 3.0 m/s, any wind direction the designer chooses, and an area-weighted average wind velocity calculated on a plane 1.2 m above the average finished floor. A habitable space passes if that velocity is not less than 0.4 m/s. The site-specific method uses an urban model to 2 times the height of the tallest building, topography to 5 times that height, and enough wind directions to cover more than 75% of the probable winds in a typical reference year. Typical urban mesh is on the order of 0.5–1.0 m, and typical internal mesh is on the order of 0.1–0.2 m. A space passes if the velocity is not less than 0.4 m/s for any wind direction. The submission pack is an executive summary, methodology, model images, settings, results, and a conclusion.

Appendix VI is one sample floor. Its percentages (including an overall NVTC of 82%) are that sample, not a general pass mark. The notes on the sheet are rules: the secondary openings must together meet the areas required for the living and dining space and the adjacent bedroom; a cross-ventilation path across a corridor, or through a bathroom, toilet, or store that has secondary openings, counts toward openable window area but is excluded from the habitable area in the NVTC percentage; and a space that passes must still be clear of heat from another unit’s air-conditioner under section 3.4.
