# Extract on Prioritisation of Actionable Unauthorised Building Works
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
April 2016 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: An extract of internal guidelines on how the Department queues “actionable” unauthorised building works on rooftops and flat roofs, and in yards, lanes, and basements. It is an enforcement-priority note, not a toleration of those works and not a design standard.

## Regulatory Overview
Unauthorised structures on rooftops and flat roofs, and in yards, lanes, and basements, are actionable whether or not they are dangerous and whether or not they are new, and the Department calls this group “Top, Middle, Bottom”. New works, works in progress, and works with obvious danger to life and property are enforced at once, together with other such works at the same level, and the remaining cases are queued for large-scale operations.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is in the queue
| Place | How the extract defines it |
| --- | --- |
| Yard | A lightwell is regarded as a yard |
| Lane | A structure on a private lane, such as a horizontal extension, or projecting over a lane, such as a retractable awning, which is actionable under the Buildings Ordinance |
| Basement | An unauthorised extension below the lowest storey, or onto adjoining unexcavated backfilled areas. These are treated as unauthorised building works at the exterior |
| Same level | Enforcement against a new or dangerous case at a level includes other such works at that same level. Works in a single-family house are regarded as at the same level |
| Annual list | Reports received in a year form that year’s queue, used to select target buildings for large-scale operations |

**SD takeaway:** A rooftop structure, a lane awning, a lightwell enclosure, and a basement extension are all in the same actionable group.

### 2. How priority is ranked
| Queue | Ranking |
| --- | --- |
| Queues compiled in 2011 and 2012 | The earliest date the case was known to the Department, taking account of the date of warning notices or letters. Earlier cases rank ahead of later ones |
| Queues after 2012 | The current report date only |

The extract says prioritisation is needed because of the number of reports and the Department’s capacity to attend to reports, issue orders, and enforce compliance. It does not set a size below which a rooftop structure is left in place.

**SD takeaway:** Do not treat silence after a report as acceptance. After 2012 the queue follows the report date, and a new or dangerous case is taken outside that queue.
