# General Guidelines on Minor Works Control System
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
October 2010 (printed 2010/10) | Buildings Department

> Scope note: The 2010 English introduction to the Minor Works Control System, written when the system had 118 items in seven types. Later expansion to 187 items and eight types is in the 2024 Chinese technical guidelines, which are summarised separately. Do not treat the item counts below as the current schedule.

## Regulatory Overview
The 2010 guidelines introduce a simplified route for specified minor works so that prior approval of plans and consent to commence are not required, provided the Building (Minor Works) Regulation is followed. Class I and Class II still need a commencement notice at least 7 days before work starts, and every class needs a completion submission within 14 days.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Three classes, seven types, 118 items (sections 3.1–3.3)
Works are classified by nature, scale, complexity, and risk.

| Class | Items in this edition | Character printed in the guidelines | Examples printed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 40 | Relatively more complicated, higher expertise and stricter supervision | Internal staircase connecting two floors; projecting signboard with display area not more than 20 m²; removal of an unauthorised floor slab |
| II | 40 | Comparatively less complex | Repair of an external wall; repair or replacement of a protective barrier; construction, alteration, repair, or removal of a window or window wall |
| III | 38 | Small-scale and common household works | Supporting frame for an air-conditioner; drying rack; lightweight canopy |

Seven types: A alteration and addition, B repair, C signboards, D drainage, E structures for amenities, F finishes, G demolition. The first digit of the item number is the class. Item 1.1 is erection or alteration of an internal staircase and is Class I. Specifications are in Part 3 of Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation, copied in Appendix II of these guidelines.

**SD takeaway:** Use this 2010 booklet for the original class logic and the 20 m² projecting-signboard example, and check the current schedule before naming an item number.

### 2. Simplified requirements against approval and consent (section 2.4)
| Step | Approval and consent | Simplified requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan fee | Minimum $11,200 | Not applicable |
| Statutory processing time | Minimum 30 days | Nil |
| Commencement notice | 7 days before | 7 days before for Class I and Class II. Not required for Class III |
| Prescribed building professional | Required | Required only for Class I |
| Prescribed registered contractor | Required | Required |
| Completion notice | Within 14 days after completion | Within 14 days after completion |

The prescribed building professional is an authorized person, and a registered structural engineer and/or registered geotechnical engineer if necessary. The prescribed registered contractor is a registered general building contractor, a registered specialist contractor, or a registered minor works contractor. For Class III, the Building Authority accepts individual workers who are not companies, after a mandatory training course, registering as Class III minor works contractors.

If an authorized person is appointed, that authorized person submits the notifications. If no Class I item is involved and no prescribed building professional is appointed, the prescribed registered contractor submits them. Completion for Class I and Class II includes the submission number, record plans, supporting documents, and record photographs within 14 days. Class III completion documents are also within 14 days.

**SD takeaway:** A Class II or Class III package under the simplified route has no plan-processing period, but the 14-day completion lodgement still applies.

### 3. What the booklet is for
The guidelines group the 118 items by similar substance (for example supporting frames for air-conditioners and water cooling towers) and point to the schedules for the full specification. They also describe the household minor works validation scheme then in force, under which validated structures are not enforced against solely because they were completed without prior approval and consent.

**SD takeaway:** Treat this booklet as the 2010 map of classes and procedures, and take current dimensions from the regulation and from the 2024 technical guidelines.
