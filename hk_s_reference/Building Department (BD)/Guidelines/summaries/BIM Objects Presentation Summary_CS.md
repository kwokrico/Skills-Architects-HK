# BIM Objects Presentation Summary
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
The text layer does not print an edition or a cover title. Sheet headings are “BIM OBJECT” and “DRAWING TITLE” | Buildings Department

> Scope note: The object-presentation appendix for the eight statutory plan types other than general building plans. It shows how major objects are drawn. It is not a dimensional design code. Figures on a sample sheet, including the curtain-wall extrusion properties and the 150 mm and 225 mm drain labels, are annotations on that sheet.

## Regulatory Overview
Each structural object sheet sets out a plan, a plan with annotation, a section, a section with annotation, a three-dimensional view marked for reference only, and, where the object needs it, a two-dimensional detail, a schedule, graphical information, or a schedule formula. Drainage sheets use a plan, a schematic diagram, and a three-dimensional view marked for reference only, with a schedule on the chambers and interceptors.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Views that repeat (object sheets)
Reinforced-concrete slabs, walls, beams, columns, stairs, and water tanks add a view of the object with reinforcement. The slab sheet also has a loading key plan. The beam sheet has a reinforced-concrete detail in schedule form. The steelwork sheet has a connection detail. The embed sheet has a loading schedule. The pile, tie-beam, screen-wall, sheet-pile, and soil-nail sheets carry a schedule or a schedule formula. The three-dimensional view, where the sheet labels it, is for reference only.

**SD takeaway:** Build the statutory sheets from the plan, the annotated section, and the schedule, and do not treat the three-dimensional view as the approval drawing.

### 2. Objects named on the sheets
| Group as titled on the sheets | Objects |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation | Bored pile, socket H-pile, pile cap, tie beam, screen wall, rock-core sample, monitoring instruments |
| Superstructure | Slab, structural wall, beam, structural column, staircase, water tank, steel structure, curtain wall, embed, railing |
| Drainage, by drawing title | Drainage pipe, drain, sanitary fitment, manhole, terminal manhole, sump pit, petrol interceptor, open trapped gully and seal trapped gully, grease trap, fresh-air inlet, wire balloon, surface channel, septic tank, soakaway pit, back-inlet gully trap |
| Demolition and temporary works | Propping, debris chute, video camera, hoarding with gantry and concrete footing |
| Excavation and lateral support | Sheet pile, strut, waling and tie, rock profile, soil profile |
| Site formation | Soil nail, slope drainage, U-channel, catchpit without cover |
| Other | Street furniture |

Sanitary fitments named on that sheet are a sink, a wash basin, a water closet, and a shower. Manhole and terminal-manhole sheets distinguish a soil-and-waste manhole from a stormwater manhole. The drain sheet labels FD, TFD, TSD, VRWO, and VFD. The gully sheet labels STG and OTG.

**SD takeaway:** Name the model object with the sheet title for that plan type so a pile, a manhole, a slab, and a hoarding are not drawn as a generic solid.

### 3. What the sample annotations are
The curtain-wall sheet is one sample: a fifth-floor cast-in layout and a section marked 1:25, transom and mullion details marked 1:2, and a typical transom fixing marked 1:10. It labels alloy 6063-T6 extrusions, a 3 mm aluminium panel, smoke seal, fire stop, structural sealant, and a non-structural reinforced-concrete curb marked “NOT FOR APPROVAL”. The tie-beam sheet includes a section labelled 1000 x 800. The surface-channel sheet includes the label “225mmW covered channel”. The septic-tank and soakaway sheets include a 150 mm label, a covered access opening, a vent pipe, and the words “to soakaway”. These are labels on the sample sheets. The appendix does not state them as a limit for every project.

**SD takeaway:** Copy the view set and the object name, and do not copy the sample curtain-wall sizes or the sample drain labels into the project as if they were rules.
