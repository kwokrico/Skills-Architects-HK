# Guidelines on Maintenance and Repair of Drainage System and Sanitary Fitments
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
The file does not print an edition or effective date | Buildings Department

> Scope note: A one-page maintenance note for existing drains and sanitary fitments. It is not a design code for new above-ground or underground drainage.

## Regulatory Overview
Soil pipes, waste pipes, ventilating pipes, and underground drains are kept in working order, inspected regularly, and repaired as soon as leakage, blockage, or a defect is found. Sanitary fitments should be inspected preferably once every three months, and a serious or unexplained leak is referred to an authorized person.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Drains and manholes
Minor blockage is cleared by high-pressure water jet or rodding. Serious blockage, for example by cement, may require the defective length to be exposed and replaced. Manholes are kept accessible. Floor finishes, planters, and furniture must not cover them. Foul air from a manhole is stopped with a double-seal cover, or by repairing the edge of the opening or cracks in the cover. Ventilating pipes and other lengths that do not carry water need a close inspection, including every branch. Any cracked or leaking section of a vertical stack, waste pipe, ventilating pipe, or branch is replaced.

**SD takeaway:** Keep every manhole cover reachable and specify a double-seal cover where foul air is already leaking.

### 2. Sanitary fitments
Inspect water closets, bathtubs, lavatory basins, shower trays, sinks, and floor drains, preferably once every three months. Repair or replace the fitment and its drain pipe as soon as leakage is found. Clean and disinfect as recommended by the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department. A smell of foul air means a fitment is not working and is investigated at once. If the leak or blockage is serious, or the cause is not obvious, appoint an authorized person. Enquiries go to the Buildings Department hotline on 2626 1616.

**SD takeaway:** Put a three-month inspection of every sanitary fitment into the maintenance schedule, and call an authorized person when the leak cannot be traced.
