# Amendment to the Technical Guidelines on the Minor Works Control System (August 2026)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
August 2026 revision of the 2024 edition | Buildings Department

> Scope note: A 12-page Chinese appendix that replaces selected comparison pages of the Technical Guidelines on the Minor Works Control System (2024 revision). The cover marks the pages as a revision. Each page points to section 3.32 of the 2024 guidelines, which this sheet does not reprint. Suggested details said to be in Appendix VII are not reproduced here.

## Regulatory Overview
The sheet revises the comparisons for roof finishes, protective barriers, slab openings and their reinstatement, non-loadbearing external walls, metal gates at a fence or a building entrance, subdivision of a domestic unit, internal stairs, partitions inside a unit, and thickening a slab with solid screed. The class, the appointed person, and the commencement and completion clocks are not restated on these pages.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Roof finishes (section 3.5.1)
A roof finish includes the tile layer, the screed, the insulation layer, and the related waterproofing layer. Types A and F are printed for the comparison. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-32.

| Item | Work printed in that column | Lock printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.62 | Laying or repair | Does not alter other structural members. Is not item 2.34. Is not designated exempted works item 7 |
| 2.34 | Laying, repair, or removal | Where the work is laying or repair, the thickness is not greater than the original design thickness |

The original design thickness is either the difference between the structural level and the finish level on the approved building plans, or the design superimposed load of the finish in kilopascals, multiplied by 1,000 and divided by 23 MPa. That load is the load shown on the approved structural plans or in the design calculations.

Other locks printed for the comparison: Building (Planning) Regulation 73 on mandatory elderly-friendly design; Building (Construction) Regulation 34, the roof constructed so as not to be affected by the weather; Building (Energy Efficiency) Regulation 4, the 1995 Code of Practice for Overall Thermal Transfer Value in Buildings, the 2014 residential energy-efficiency guidelines, and Practice Notes APP-67, APP-156, and APP-2; paragraph 5 of Practice Note APP-102; the bamboo-scaffold design guidelines and the Labour Department codes and guide for bamboo scaffolds, truss-out bamboo scaffolds, and metal scaffolds. Works in common parts need the consent of the owners’ corporation, the co-owners, or the management office, as applicable, and a notice posted in a conspicuous place at or near the works. Laying, repair, or removal of the finish of an open roof whose slope is less than 1 in 4 may be designated exempted works item 7.

**SD takeaway:** Do not thicken a roof finish beyond the original structural allowance, and use the 23 MPa formula only when the approved plans show the finish load and not a finish thickness.

### 2. Protective barriers (section 3.9.1)
The barrier in this comparison excludes a reinforced-concrete external wall and a brick external wall. Type A is printed for the comparison. No additional load may be placed on any cantilevered slab. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-64.

| Item | Work printed in that column | Level difference printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.6 | Erection, alteration, or removal | No separate figure in this column |
| 2.5 | Repair or replacement, to the original design | Greater than 2 m |
| 3.3 | No separate verb is printed in this column | Not greater than 2 m |

Other locks: a protective barrier where the level difference is greater than 600 mm, or at an opening in an external wall above ground level, under Building (Planning) Regulation 3A, Building (Construction) Regulations 37 and 38, and Practice Note APP-110; Building (Planning) Regulation 73; a fire-resisting spandrel under Building (Construction) Regulation 35 and clause C11.1 of the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings 2011; the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Glass 2018, and section 3.32.2 of the 2024 guidelines where tempered glass is used; Practice Note APP-166 for metal grilles and ventilation windows; Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 11 when steel is cut; the Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings 2004; Practice Note APP-21; Joint Practice Notes No. 1 and No. 2 where they apply. Stainless-steel drilled-in anchors should be used. A barrier used as a parapet on a roof must not exceed 2 m in height. If the lowest point of the main frame of a window or a glass wall is not more than 1.1 m above the adjacent floor, part of that window or glass wall forms part of the protective barrier, and altering or replacing it is an alteration of a protective barrier under item 1.6.

**SD takeaway:** Treat a window or glass-wall frame whose bottom is at or below 1.1 m above the floor as part of the barrier, and keep a roof parapet barrier at or under 2 m.

### 3. Forming an opening in a slab (section 3.10.1)
The opening must not add load to any cantilevered slab, and must not alter any other structural member except a simply supported beam that is not prestressed and that does not support a column, a flat slab, or a ribbed beam. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-66.

| Item | Type | Opening area printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.2 | A | Greater than 1 m² and not greater than 4.5 m² |
| 2.1 | A and D | Not greater than 1 m², and not designated exempted works item 1 |

The opening area is the total, after the works, of each new opening and any adjoining existing opening. An opening whose furthest two points are not more than 150 mm apart may be designated exempted works item 1. The use of the opening must be stated on the plan, for example a route for building services, a drain, a lift, or a stair. A level difference left by the opening needs a protective barrier; the sheet points to item 1.6 and Practice Note APP-110. Also printed: fire-compartment volume and fire-resisting construction under Building (Construction) Regulation 35 and sections C3 and C4 of the 2011 fire-safety code; a vertical barrier or a smoke curtain under the slab around the opening under section C10 of that code; Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 10, the floor must not be overloaded; the 2004 demolition code; and, where the opening combines two domestic units, the environmental facilities under Joint Practice Notes No. 1 and No. 2 must not be contravened. Suggested details for item 2.1 are in Appendix VII.

**SD takeaway:** Add every new opening to any adjoining existing opening before choosing item 1.2 or item 2.1, and state the use of the hole on the plan.

### 4. Restoring an opening in a slab (section 3.10.2)
Restoration follows the original design, adds no load to any cantilevered slab, and does not alter other structural members. Type A is printed in the item 1.35 column. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-68. Both captions on the sheet read as restoring the slab.

| Item | Opening area and size printed in that column |
| --- | --- |
| 1.35 | Greater than 1 m² and not greater than 4.5 m² |
| 2.35 | Not greater than 1 m², and the furthest two points of the opening are more than 150 mm apart |

The restored area is the total area of slab restored inside the same opening after the works. Restoring an opening whose furthest two points are not more than 150 mm apart may be designated exempted works item 2. Some floors must be impervious under Building (Construction) Regulation 33. Unless the method statement and the details are already on the approved plans, existing and new reinforcement must not be connected by welding, by drilling and chemical grout, by couplers, or by any method other than lapping. Suggested details for item 2.35 are in Appendix VII. Building (Planning) Regulation 73 applies.

**SD takeaway:** Lap new bars to existing bars unless the approved plans already show another connection, and keep a restored opening inside the 1 m² or 4.5 m² band of the item used.

### 5. Non-loadbearing reinforced-concrete external walls (section 3.14.1)
The wall must not alter other structural members and must not add load to any cantilevered slab. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-79.

| Item | Type | Work printed in that column | Height and projection printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.15 | A | Erection or alteration | Greater than 1.1 m and not greater than 3.5 m, and not item 2.15, 3.45, or 3.46 |
| No item number is printed in the middle cell | — | Repair or removal, including a concrete projection | No separate height in this cell |
| 2.15 | A and B | Repair | Height not greater than 3.5 m. A concrete projection in this column is not thicker than 125 mm and does not project more than 150 mm from the external wall |
| 2.13 | A | Erection, alteration, or removal | Height not greater than 1.1 m, and not item 3.45 or 3.46 |

Other locks, some marked (1) on the sheet without a footnote legend on this page: fire-resisting walls and spandrels under Building (Construction) Regulation 35 and sections C5 and C11 of the 2011 fire-safety code; separation of an exit route under Building (Planning) Regulation 41 and clause B5.7 of that code; natural lighting and ventilation of a room used for habitation, an office, a kitchen, and a latrine under Building (Planning) Regulations 29, 30, 33, and 36; Building (Planning) Regulation 73; Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 10; a protective barrier at an opening in the external wall under Building (Planning) Regulation 3A, Building (Construction) Regulations 37 and 38, and Practice Note APP-110; protection against moisture under Building (Construction) Regulation 32; the 2004 demolition code; no opening within 5 m of an opening of an MTR ventilation shaft under Appendix B of Practice Note APP-24 and Appendix B of Practice Note for Registered Contractors 14; Practice Note APP-21 for demolition.

**SD takeaway:** Place a new or altered non-loadbearing reinforced-concrete external wall by its height against 1.1 m and 3.5 m, and keep a projection on a repair at or under 125 mm thick and 150 mm deep.

### 6. Non-loadbearing brick external walls (section 3.14.2)
The wall must not alter other structural members and must not add load to any cantilevered slab. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-82. Note (1) on this page applies only to items 2.14 and 3.11. Suggested details for items 2.14, 3.11, and 3.12 are in Appendix VII.

| Item | Type printed | Work printed in that column | Height printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.14 | A | Erection, alteration, or removal | Greater than 1.1 m and not greater than 3.5 m, and not item 3.45 or 3.46 |
| 3.11 | No separate type is printed in this column | No separate verb is printed in this column | Not greater than 1.1 m |
| 3.12 | A and B | Repair | Not greater than 3.5 m |

Other locks follow the reinforced-concrete wall list for fire separation, lighting and ventilation, barriers, moisture, demolition loading, and the 5 m MTR shaft clearance, with the (1) bullets limited to items 2.14 and 3.11. Works in common parts need the consent of the owners’ corporation, the co-owners, or the management office, and a posted notice. An opening in a non-loadbearing brick external wall whose furthest two points are not more than 150 mm apart, and the corresponding reinstatement, may be items 3.45 and 3.46 respectively. Existing environmental facilities under Joint Practice Notes No. 1 and No. 2 must not be contravened.

**SD takeaway:** Use the 1.1 m and 3.5 m wall heights to separate items 2.14, 3.11, and 3.12, and treat a hole not wider than 150 mm between its furthest points as a possible item 3.45.

### 7. Metal gates at a fence or a building entrance (section 3.16.1)
The gate must not add load to any cantilevered slab and must not alter other structural members. The comparison prints a gate height of not more than 3.2 m in the left column, and no different height in the other columns. Type A is printed for the comparison. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-117.

| Item | Work printed in that column | Leaf weight printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.16 | Erection, alteration, or repair | At least one leaf heavier than 300 kg |
| 2.16 | No separate verb is printed in this column | At least one leaf heavier than 200 kg, and every leaf not heavier than 300 kg |
| 3.13 | Erection, alteration, repair, or removal | Every leaf not heavier than 200 kg, and not designated exempted works item 8 |

Other locks: exit routes under Building (Planning) Regulation 41 and section B5 of the 2011 fire-safety code; where a gate is installed at a building entrance or at a required stair exit, the lock must be readily openable from the inside without a key, and the minimum width in sections B5, B7, B8, B9, and B13 of that code applies; the gate must not open over a street under Building (Planning) Regulation 15; Building (Planning) Regulation 73; Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 11; the 2004 demolition code; Practice Note APP-146 and Practice Note for Registered Contractors 68; Practice Note APP-21. A powered metal gate must follow the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department code on electric gates, electric glass doors, and electric rolling shutters. The Labour Department guide on gate work applies. Where the aspect ratio is high, the dynamic effect of wind should be considered, and the sheet points to BS EN 40-3-1:2013 for the characteristic loads of lighting columns.

**SD takeaway:** Weigh each leaf before choosing the item: above 300 kg is item 1.16, above 200 kg and at or under 300 kg is item 2.16, and at or under 200 kg is item 3.13.

### 8. Subdivision of a domestic unit (section 3.24.1, item 1.41)
Item 1.41, type A, is erection of a non-loadbearing brick wall, laying of a solid floor screed, and erection or alteration of an above-ground drain, inside a domestic unit, with no additional load on any cantilevered slab. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-143. The unit is subdivided into three or more rooms, of which at least three rooms are formed by or affected by the layout change, are provided with a wash basin, a sink, a water-closet fitment, a shower, or a bath, and are intended, or having regard to size and layout are likely, to be used for sleeping. The number of rooms that both have such a fitment and are for sleeping is greater than the number shown on the approved plans, or will become greater because of the works.

Other locks: do not obstruct natural lighting and ventilation of windows under Building (Planning) Regulations 30, 31, and 36; exit routes and exit doors under Building (Planning) Regulation 41 and sections B5, B7, B8, B10, and B13 of the 2011 fire-safety code; the distance measured along the actual route from any part of the storey to the fire door of a fireman’s-lift lobby is not more than 60 m, under Building (Planning) Regulations 41A, 41B, 41C, and 41D and clause D8.4 of that code; fire-resisting construction under Building (Construction) Regulation 35 and sections C6 to C7 and E4 to E9 of that code; check the existing slab against Part 3 of the Building (Construction) Regulations and the 2011 Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads; kitchens under Building (Planning) Regulation 45; a room-sealed gas water heater in a bathroom under Building (Planning) Regulation 35A; Building (Planning) Regulation 73; sanitary fitments in a domestic building under regulation 4 of the Building (Standards of Sanitary Fitments, Plumbing, Drainage Works and Latrines) Regulations.

**SD takeaway:** If the subdivision creates three or more rooms and at least three of them combine a sanitary fitment with sleeping use, and that number exceeds the approved plans, treat the brick walls, solid screed, and above-ground drains as item 1.41.

### 9. Internal stairs (section 3.25.1)
The stair is not a means of escape or a means of access. It does not alter other structural members except a simply supported beam that is not prestressed and that does not support a column, a flat slab, or a ribbed beam. It does not add load to any cantilevered slab. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-145.

| Item | Type | Work printed in that column | Further lock printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1 | A | Erection or alteration of an internal stair | The erection or alteration may include altering the stair opening of the original design |
| 1.32 | A and G | Removal | Not item 3.1 |
| 3.1 | No separate type is printed in this column | Removal of the whole internal stair on the lowest floor of the building | Height of the stair not greater than 1.5 m |

If the finished stair leaves a level difference greater than 600 mm, a protective barrier is required under Building (Construction) Regulations 37 and 38 and Practice Note APP-110, which the sheet ties to item 1.6. Also printed: fire-compartment volume and fire-resisting construction under sections C3 and C4 of the 2011 fire-safety code; stairs and handrails under Building (Planning) Regulation 72, Practice Note APP-41, and Divisions 7 and 8 of the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008; Building (Planning) Regulation 73; a vertical barrier or smoke curtain under the slab around the floor opening under section C10 of the fire-safety code; Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 10; the 2004 demolition code. The stair is not a maintenance access to a roof, a podium, or a canopy. An opening formed in the slab may be item 1.2 where the area is greater than 1 m² and not greater than 4.5 m², or item 2.1 where the area is not greater than 1 m². A new floor built in an original stair opening must follow the original design of the adjoining floor, and the new floor area must not exceed 4.5 m².

**SD takeaway:** Keep a new internal stair off the escape and access route, and if it fills an existing stair opening, match the adjoining floor and stay at or under 4.5 m².

### 10. Partitions inside a unit (section 3.27.1)
The work is erection of a non-loadbearing brick wall, with no additional load on any cantilevered slab and no alteration of other structural members. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-148. Lengths are the total length of added wall per square metre of the floor area of the unit.

| Item | Unit printed in that column | Added-wall length printed in that column | Wall size printed in that column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.43, type A | Domestic or non-domestic | Domestic greater than 0.1 m; non-domestic greater than 0.2 m. Not item 3.39 or 3.40 | No separate size in this column |
| 3.39 | Domestic | Greater than 0.1 m and not greater than 0.3 m | Height not greater than 3 m. Thickness not greater than 75 mm |
| 3.40 | Non-domestic | Greater than 0.2 m and not greater than 0.4 m | Height not greater than 3.5 m. Thickness not greater than 100 mm |

The Class III columns also print a wall density of not more than 650 kg/m³, a supporting slab not thinner than 125 mm, and a screed on that slab not thicker than 25 mm. Other locks: natural lighting and ventilation under Building (Planning) Regulations 30, 31, and 36; exit routes and exit doors under Building (Planning) Regulation 41 and sections B5 and B8 of the 2011 fire-safety code; not more than 60 m along the actual route to the fire door of a fireman’s-lift lobby under Building (Planning) Regulations 41A to 41D and clause D8.4 of that code; Building (Planning) Regulation 73; fire-resisting construction under Building (Construction) Regulation 35 and sections C4 to C7, C9, and E4 to E9 of that code.

**SD takeaway:** Measure added brick-wall length against the floor area of the unit, and use the 0.3 m and 0.4 m caps, with the 650 kg/m³ density, only for the Class III items.

### 11. Thickening a slab with solid screed (items 1.44, 3.41, and 3.42)
The sheet heads this page with items 1.44, 3.41, and 3.42, prints type A under item 1.44, and captions item 1.44 as thickening the slab with a solid screed. It does not print a dimensional comparison among the three items. Suggested details for items 3.41 and 3.42 are in Appendix VII. The page number printed on the sheet is 3-152.

Locks printed under these items: minimum clear headroom of an exit route of 2 m under Building (Planning) Regulation 41 and section B5 of the 2011 fire-safety code; storey height under Building (Planning) Regulation 24; check the existing slab under Part 3 of the Building (Construction) Regulations and the 2011 Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads, and under Practice Note APP-117 for the capacity and the details of the existing structure; after completion the protective barrier is not lower than 1.1 m under Building (Construction) Regulations 37 and 38, Building (Planning) Regulation 3A, and Practice Note APP-110; barrier-free access under Building (Planning) Regulation 72 and the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008 where it applies; Building (Planning) Regulation 73. A drain embedded in the floor screed is item 2.30. Repair or laying of the screed of a unit, including the waterproofing layer, to the original design and without thickening the slab, is building works exempt from approval under the Buildings Ordinance.

**SD takeaway:** A screed that thickens the slab is item 1.44, 3.41, or 3.42, while a screed relayed to the original design without thickening the slab is the exempted case printed on this page.
