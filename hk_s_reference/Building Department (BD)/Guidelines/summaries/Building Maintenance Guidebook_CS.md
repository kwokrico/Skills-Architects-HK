# Building Maintenance Guidebook
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
June 2002 (message of the Director of Buildings) | Buildings Department

> Scope note: The text layer does not print a cover title. The document calls itself this Guidebook and is written for owners, owners’ corporations, and people who maintain buildings. It consolidates guidance from the Buildings Department and other departments. It is a 2002 owner’s manual, not a current submission checklist.

## Regulatory Overview
Owners are responsible for keeping buildings safe and sanitary, and statutory orders may require repair, investigation, removal of unauthorised building works, or work to slopes, drains, and fire hazards. Fire service installations are inspected and maintained by a registered fire service installation contractor at least once a year, and a registered geotechnical engineer inspects slopes and retaining walls at least once every five years.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Orders and unauthorised works (chapters 2 and 3)
The guidebook lists orders and notices from the Buildings Department, including a removal order for unauthorised building works under section 24 of the Buildings Ordinance, an investigation order, a repair order, a dangerous hillside order, an investigation and repair order for water pipes, drains, or sewers in slopes, a drainage repair order, and a fire safety improvement direction. Other departments may serve notices: the Water Supplies Department, the Environmental Protection Department, the Fire Services Department, the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department, and the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department. Failure to comply can lead to prosecution, including a fine or imprisonment, and orders may be registered against the title. Areas called out for special attention include storage of dangerous goods, buildings with a single staircase, misuse and change in use, swimming pools, private lanes, unauthorised building works, advertisement signboards, asbestos, noise, canopies and balconies, and defects caused by adjoining works.

**SD takeaway:** Treat a section 24 removal order, a repair order, and a hillside order as separate instructions, and do not assume a signboard or a canopy on an existing building is approved.

### 2. Inspection cycles printed in the guidebook (chapters 3 and 4)
| Item | Cycle |
| --- | --- |
| Fire service installations | At least once every year, by a registered fire service installation contractor. Repair at once if an installation is not working |
| Lift and escalator | A registered contractor inspects, cleans, lubricates, and adjusts at least once a month; tests and examines the safety equipment annually; and tests the full load, the overload device, and the brake once every five years |
| Electrical installation with an approved loading exceeding 100 A, in a residential apartment, shop, office, or communal area | Inspect, test, and certify on Form WR2 at least once every five years. Additions and alterations follow the Electricity Ordinance and are certified on Form WR1 |
| Slopes and retaining walls | Routine maintenance inspection, and inspection by a qualified geotechnical engineer at least once every five years. Management staff inspect surface drainage channels and surface protection at least once a year before the wet season, and after a heavy rainstorm or a typhoon |
| Play equipment and similar items in the maintenance-cycle table | Inspection by management staff on a short cycle, and by mechanics or a specialist yearly, as set out in the table in section 4.4 |

New electrical work needs a feasibility study, a check of future load, and consent of the electricity supplier and the owners’ corporation. Concealed wiring of a new installation should have mechanical protection, and installations should be earthed.

**SD takeaway:** Put the annual fire-service inspection, the monthly lift visit, the five-year electrical WR2 for loads over 100 A, and the five-year geotechnical inspection of slopes into the maintenance plan.
