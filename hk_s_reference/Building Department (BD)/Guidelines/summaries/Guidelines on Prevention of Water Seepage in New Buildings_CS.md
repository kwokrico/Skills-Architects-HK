# Guidelines on Prevention of Water Seepage in New Buildings
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
The file does not print an edition or effective date | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Design recommendations for designers and contractors to reduce water seepage in new buildings, area by area. The guidelines are recommendations, not a stated statutory minimum, except where they point to good practice that protects the waterproofing.

## Regulatory Overview
Roofs and basements should be designed structurally as water-retaining structures, with falls, overflows, and kickers or waterstops at the joints that leak. In a bathroom the waterproofing membrane should cover the whole floor and turn up every wall at least 300 mm, and at least 1800 mm on the walls around a bathtub and a shower tray.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Roof and flat roof
Design the roof so that it does not crack or deform excessively in service. Provide a monolithic concrete kicker of sufficient height, or for the whole parapet, at the perimeter. Provide falls and outlets so that water does not pond, and an overflow for a blocked outlet. At expansion joints, use monolithic kickers, flashings or copings, and a back-up drip tray. Where plant sits on the roof, fix the plant before the membrane is dressed to the plinth, and use dampers so that vibration does not break the membrane. Do not embed conduits and pipes in the roof slab if it can be avoided, and enclose electrical and mechanical services. Shield openings in plant-room walls from the weather.

**SD takeaway:** Set the roof falls, the overflow, and a monolithic perimeter kicker before the waterproofing specification is written.

### 2. External walls, windows, bathrooms, and kitchens
| Location | Recommendation printed in the guidelines |
| --- | --- |
| External wall | A kicker is recommended. Clean kickers, fully grouted tie holes, and careful junctions |
| Window | Fill the gap between frame and opening with non-shrink waterproof grout. Lap the wall finish onto the frame and seal it. Specify a field water test. A window hood with sufficient fall avoids ponding |
| Bathroom membrane | Entire floor, turned up every wall at least 300 mm. Walls around the bathtub and shower tray at least 1800 mm |
| Bathroom construction | Concrete kicker as the base for block walls. Galvanised bars where corrosion could crack the slab. Floor drain placed to keep the fall short. Access under the bathtub. An under-tray or waterproofing underlay if the shower tray is not a proprietary product |
| Tiling | Enough joint width. Cement-based waterproofing if tile or granite is fixed on the membrane. Do not damage the waterproofing with later decoration |
| Kitchen | Waterproof the floor and walls. Monolithic worktop with a splashback and sealed junctions. Expose water and drainage pipes for maintenance |

**SD takeaway:** Draw the bathroom membrane to 300 mm on every wall and to 1800 mm at the bath and shower before the wall build-up is frozen.

### 3. Kitchens services, car park, and basement
Avoid embedding pipes in common walls between units. Provide for water hammer and pipe vibration. A car-park floor should take the imposed load, receive a proprietary coating where recommended, and fall to drainage. If cars are washed, give that area a waterproof floor, a fall, and outlets. Prefer a structural fall. Keep movement joints off the low points, and finish them with waterstops and sealant where a kicker cannot close the membrane. Test by ponding where the floor will be wet.

Design the basement, excluding pile caps, thick footings, and diaphragm-wall panels, structurally as a water-retaining structure, and allow a path for ground water if the tanking leaks. Sequence the work so that tanking is not broken at construction joints or temporary supports, and put waterstops at those joints. A cavity wall with surface channels may divert penetration water to the drainage system. Select the waterproofing for the location and the topography.

**SD takeaway:** Treat the basement as a water-retaining structure and put waterstops on every construction joint and temporary-support location.
