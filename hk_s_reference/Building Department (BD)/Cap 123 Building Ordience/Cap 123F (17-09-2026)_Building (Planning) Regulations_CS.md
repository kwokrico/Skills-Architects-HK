# Cap 123F — Building (Planning) Regulations
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Consolidated version: 17 September 2026 | Cap. 123 sub. leg. F | Building Authority

> Scope note: planning and design geometry of buildings (projections, site coverage, plot ratio, open space, lanes, light and ventilation, sanitation, access). Not the construction regulation (Cap. 123Q), not the fire code, not the structural codes.

This summary is of the 17 September 2026 consolidated text. The contents pages print “Last updated date 17.9.2026”. Individual regulation pages also print their own “Last updated date” (examples in the extract: 10.9.2020, 1.12.2020, 16.6.2024, 1.8.2026, and 17.9.2026 on regulations 72 and 73).

## Regulatory Overview
These regulations set the planning geometry of buildings under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), for domestic buildings, non-domestic buildings and composite buildings, and they classify a site as a class A site, a class B site or a class C site by the number and width of specified streets on which it abuts. The controls are projections over streets, site coverage (SC), plot ratio (PR), open space, service lanes, storey height, lighting and ventilation, staircases and firefighting access, and access for persons with a disability and for elderly persons.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Interpretation and site classification (regulations 2, 18A, 19)
A site that is not a class A, class B or class C site has its height, maximum SC and maximum PR determined by the Building Authority (regulation 19(3)). Street width, where these regulations require it, is determined by the Building Authority (regulation 6).

| Class | Test as printed (regulation 18A) |
| --- | --- |
| Class A site | Not a class B or class C site, and it abuts on one specified street not less than 4.5 m wide, or on more than one such street. |
| Class B site | A corner site that abuts on 2 specified streets, neither less than 4.5 m wide. At least 40 per cent of the site boundary must abut on the streets, or it is not treated as abutting on 2 specified streets. |
| Class C site | A corner site that abuts on 3 specified streets, none less than 4.5 m wide. At least 60 per cent of the site boundary must abut on the streets, or it is not treated as abutting on 3 specified streets. |

A street is a specified street only in the cases in regulation 18A(3): vested in the Government and maintained by the Highways Department; a private street on land held under the same Government lease as the site which the lessee must surrender when required; a street the lessee must construct on unleased Government land under that lease; land over which the owner has an express right of way exercisable at all times; land held under a Government lease by the owner of the site; or parts of the street which together meet those cases.

| Term | Spatial test in regulation 2 |
| --- | --- |
| Detached building | Not connected to any other building, with clear unobstructed open space of not less than 2.3 m for the entire depth, measured at right angles to the external surface, and a rear open space 2.3 m deep for the full width of the site. |
| Semi-detached building | One of a pair connected by a party wall, with the same 2.3 m side and 2.3 m rear open-space tests. |
| Open air | Vertically uncovered and unobstructed; not less than 1.5 m in any horizontal dimension; if enclosed on 4 sides, horizontal area not less than 1 m² for every 6 m of the mean height of the enclosing walls. |
| Canopy | Projects more than 500 mm from a wall, at a height of not more than 7.5 m above ground, carries no floor load. |
| Balcony | Projects from a wall to carry a floor or roof load. |
| Verandah | Projects from a wall and is supported by piers or columns. |
| Road | A street not including any footpath. Street includes any footpath and any private or public street. |
| Usable floor space | Floor space other than staircases, staircase halls, lift landings, space used for water-closet fitments, urinals and lavatory basins, and space occupied by machinery for any lift, air-conditioning system or similar service. |
| Plot ratio (regulation 21(3)) | Gross floor area of the building divided by the area of the site. The regulations use the words “gross floor area”; this summary uses GFA for that quantity after this point. |
| Storey | Space between the upper surface of a floor and the upper surface of the floor next above, or, for a top storey, up to the mean height of the ceiling or roof. |

**L.N. 52 of 2026 (printed on the new definitions).** A single-household building is intended for habitation by a single household and does not include a hotel, guest-house, boarding-house, hostel, dormitory or similar accommodation. A residential unit is a separate unit that would have been a residential building had it been a whole building, and it does not include a unit under a specified public housing scheme. A specified building is any building that is not a single-household building, not a building the whole of which is under a specified public housing scheme, and not a temporary building or contractor’s shed in Part VII. Specified public housing scheme means the scheme known as Transitional Housing or the scheme known as Light Public Housing.

“Residential building”, “commercial building” and “industrial building” take their meanings from Cap. 123 sub. leg. I regulation 3, Cap. 123 sub. leg. M section 2, and Cap. 123 sub. leg. H regulation 2. Those instruments are named only as definition sources.

**SD takeaway:** Lock class A, B or C only after the 4.5 m specified-street test and the 40 per cent or 60 per cent boundary test; extra frontage does not by itself create class B or class C, and a site outside those classes has SC, PR and height set by the Building Authority.

**SD takeaway:** Keep street projections inside the 2.5 m height line, and size a canopy to the lesser of one-tenth of the street or 3 m, clear of the 4.5 m centre-line offset, with 5.5 m clear over a road and 3.3 m clear over a footpath.

### 3. Buildings not to obstruct, access, and the 4.5 m street setback (regulations 4, 5, 6, 26)
The 4.5 m figure is in the regulation text, not only in the contents heading.

| Rule | Printed limit |
| --- | --- |
| Obstruction and nuisance (reg. 4) | No building or fixture may obstruct or endanger users of an adjacent footpath or street, or create a nuisance. |
| Exhaust (reg. 4(c)) | No escape into or over an adjacent footpath or street, at a height of less than 2.5 m, of noxious gases or exhaust from a ventilating system. |
| Access (reg. 5) | Every building must have means of obtaining access from a street. The Building Authority may require an access lane or access road within the site of a new building. |
| Street less than 4.5 m (reg. 26) | Where the existing street in front of a new building is less than 4.5 m wide, no part of the building shall be nearer to the centre line of the street than 2.25 m. |

**SD takeaway:** If the existing front street is under 4.5 m, set the new building so that no part is closer than 2.25 m to the centre line, and keep exhaust outlets at or above 2.5 m over the footpath or street.

### 4. Service lanes (regulation 28)
Every domestic building must have a service lane at the rear or side, in addition to the open space under regulation 25, unless one of these applies: a public lane not less than 3 m wide, or a street, already exists; the building is detached or semi-detached; or the Building Authority exempts it.

The lane must be accessible from an existing street. If that access is not immediately possible, the regulation is complied with if access would be obtained on future development or redevelopment of other lots in the block. Alignment, width and levels are decided by the Building Authority, who may grant a modification of regulation 25 when a lane exceeding 1.5 m in width is required.

The regulation does not print a minimum width for the new service lane itself. The 3 m figure applies to an existing public lane that removes the need for a new lane.

**SD takeaway:** Plan a rear or side service lane for a domestic building that is not detached or semi-detached, unless a street or a public lane of at least 3 m already exists, and treat the new lane’s width as a Building Authority decision.

### 5. Permitted site coverage (regulation 20 and the First Schedule)
Subject to regulation 22, and depending on the height of the building, domestic SC, and SC of the domestic part of a composite building, must not exceed the percentage in the second, third or fourth column of the First Schedule for a class A, class B or class C site. Non-domestic SC uses the eighth, ninth and tenth columns.

Height used to enter the table is measured under regulation 23(1): from the mean level of the specified street or streets not less than 4.5 m wide on which the building fronts or abuts, or, where those streets have different levels, from the mean level of the lower or lowest street, to the mean height of the roof over the highest usable floor space.

Whatever the height of the building, non-domestic SC, or SC of the non-domestic part of a composite building, on a class A, B or C site may exceed the permitted percentage up to a height not exceeding 15 m above ground level (regulation 20(3)). Above the level to which that excess is taken, SC must not exceed the SC permitted under regulation 20(1) or (2), subject to regulation 22, and the PR of the whole building must not exceed the permitted PR, subject to regulation 22.

### 6. Permitted plot ratio (regulation 21 and the First Schedule)
Domestic PR must not exceed the fifth, sixth or seventh column of the First Schedule for a class A, class B or class C site. Non-domestic PR must not exceed the eleventh, twelfth or thirteenth column. Those figures are the PR columns of the table in topic 5.

For a composite building, regulation 21(2) states that the PR for the domestic part shall not exceed the product of the difference between the permitted PR for the building if it were a non-domestic building and the actual PR of the non-domestic part, and the permitted PR for the building if it were a domestic building, divided by the permitted PR for the building if it were a non-domestic building. Read as arithmetic, that is:

domestic PR ≤ [(permitted non-domestic PR − actual non-domestic PR) × permitted domestic PR] / permitted non-domestic PR

PR is GFA divided by site area (regulation 21(3)). GFA rules are in topic 7.

**SD takeaway:** Take domestic and non-domestic PR from the height band in topic 5, and cap the domestic share of a composite building with the regulation 21(2) product so that non-domestic PR already used reduces the domestic allowance.

### 7. Exceeding SC and PR, and hotels (regulations 22, 23, 23A)
**Dedication for passage (regulation 22(1)).** Where, between ground level and a height of not less than 5.5 m, or 3.3 m where the Building Authority is satisfied there will be no obstruction to vehicular traffic, a building on a class A, B or C site is set back from a lot boundary that abuts on a street, and, with the consent of the Government, that part of the lot is dedicated to the public for passage:

| Quantity | How far it may exceed the permitted figure |
| --- | --- |
| SC | Permitted percentage SC, plus the figure obtained by dividing the product of 1 500 and the dedicated area by the product of the site area and the height of the building. |
| PR | The lesser of: not greater than the permitted PR by more than 20 per centum; or the permitted PR plus the figure obtained by dividing the product of 5 and the dedicated area by the site area. |

**Street widening (regulation 22(2)).** Where part of a lot that abuts on a street is acquired by the Government, by agreement or under the Lands Resumption Ordinance (Cap. 124), for street widening, the Building Authority may permit the same form of excess. The SC addition uses the product of 1500 (printed without a space in paragraph (2)) and the acquired area, divided by the product of the site area and the height. The PR cap is again the lesser of 20 per centum and the addition of (5 × acquired area / site area). The paragraph (2) PR concession is for the building or, if composite, for the domestic part.

If the permitted percentage SC is exceeded for one part of a building, any other part must not exceed the permitted percentage SC. Regulation 22 does not reduce the open space required by regulation 25.

**Site area and GFA (regulation 23).**

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Site area | Ignore any part of any street or service lane. Include any area dedicated to the public for passage. |
| GFA | Area contained within the external walls at each floor level, including any floor below ground, together with each balcony measured to its overall dimensions (including the thickness of its sides), and the thickness of the external walls. |
| Floor space the Building Authority may disregard | Floor space he is satisfied is constructed or intended solely for parking motor vehicles, loading or unloading of motor vehicles, or for refuse storage chambers, refuse storage and material recovery chambers, material recovery chambers, refuse storage and material recovery rooms, refuse chutes, refuse hopper rooms and other facilities for separation of refuse to his satisfaction, or for access facilities for telecommunications and broadcasting services, or occupied solely by machinery or equipment for any lift, air-conditioning or heating system or any similar service. |
| Caretaker or similar residence | The Building Authority may treat as a non-domestic building a composite building whose only domestic part is a place of residence of not more than 50 m² of usable floor space for a caretaker or other person employed in connexion with the building or a service provided for it, or a residence comprising the top storey, or both. |

**Hotels (regulation 23A).** A hotel means premises held out as providing sleeping accommodation to any persons presenting themselves who are willing to pay a fee. The Building Authority may treat a hotel building, or the hotel part of a composite building, as non-domestic. In GFA he may disregard floor space constructed or intended solely for: a place for picking up and setting down persons arriving or departing by vehicle; a laundry, carpentry workshop, or mechanical or electrical workshop; an area for storing dry goods, food, beverages, linen or furniture; staff welfare facilities including a staff canteen, changing room and rest room; or other supporting facilities he approves.

That concession is tied to continued hotel use. A change of use of the hotel building or hotel part, or of disregarded floor space, needs prior approval of the Building Authority. If no licence under the Hotel and Guesthouse Accommodation Ordinance (Cap. 349) is in effect, or an order of exclusion under section 3 of that Ordinance is in effect, use is deemed a change away from hotel use. Contravention is an offence liable to a fine at level 6 and imprisonment for 2 years. Failure to comply with a discontinuance order is liable to a fine at level 5 and imprisonment for 1 year, and a further fine of $5,000 for each day the failure continues.

**SD takeaway:** A street setback dedicated for passage can add SC by (1 500 × dedicated area) / (site area × height) and PR by the lesser of 20 per cent and (5 × dedicated area / site area), and a hotel concession is a non-domestic GFA treatment that stays only while the hotel use, and any disregarded support space, remains as approved.

### 9. Lighting and ventilation (regulations 29–35, 37, 71)
Every storey used for an office or for habitation must have effectual lighting and ventilation (regulation 29). Every room used for habitation, as an office, or as a kitchen must have natural lighting and ventilation (regulation 30). A laundry that is part of, or used in connexion with, a building is deemed used for habitation for regulations 29 to 35.

| Window rule (reg. 30) | Printed limit |
| --- | --- |
| Glass | Aggregate superficial area of glass not less than one-tenth of the floor area of the room. |
| Opening | Openable area, in aggregate, at least equal to one-sixteenth of the floor area. |
| Height of the opening | Top of the opening of each window at least 2 m above the floor, or at least 1.9 m in a detached or semi-detached building. |
| Prescribed window | Not less than the one-tenth glass area must face directly into the external air. |

A prescribed window is not deemed to face external air unless it faces a street not less than 4.5 m wide, or it faces a space uncovered and unobstructed above the rectangular horizontal plane and satisfies the inclined-plane tests in regulation 31.

| Rectangular horizontal plane (reg. 31(2)) | Printed minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Area | Not less than 21 m². |
| Base (the side common with the line of the window sill) | Not less than 2.3 m. |
| Sides, to an opposite wall or building within the site | Not less than 4.5 m. |
| Sides, where the opposite side is a site boundary | Not less than 2.3 m, measured to that boundary. |
| Sides, where a service lane or street less than 4.5 m wide is adjacent and parallel to that boundary | Not less than 2.3 m, measured to a line 1.5 m beyond the boundary, or, if that lane or street is less than 3 m wide, to the centre line of the lane or street. |
| Deemed sill (reg. 31(3)) | 1 m above the floor of the room, whether or not the sill is at that level. |

Inclined planes, with no part of a building on the site protruding above the plane:

| Case | Habitation | Office or kitchen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plane inclined above the rectangular horizontal plane (reg. 31(1)(c)) | 71 1/2° from the horizontal | 76° from the horizontal |
| Window opposite a site boundary; inclined plane from the first intersection with the boundary (reg. 31(1)(d)) | 80 1/2° from the horizontal | 83° from the horizontal |

Where a service lane or street less than 4.5 m wide is adjacent and parallel to that boundary, the boundary is deemed, for regulation 31(1)(d), to be 1.5 m beyond the boundary.

No part of a room used for habitation shall be more than 9 m, measured within the room, from a prescribed window that faces directly into the external air. Where regulation 33 or 71 applies, measure from the outer edge of the enclosed or unenclosed verandah, balcony, conservatory or similar place (regulation 32).

An enclosed verandah, balcony, conservatory or similar enclosed place within the site may be used under regulation 33 if its own window would satisfy regulations 30 and 31 for a room whose floor area is the aggregate of the room and that enclosed place, and the room window’s glass and opening would likewise satisfy regulation 30(2)(a) for that aggregate area.

Where an office cannot comply with regulation 31 to the satisfaction of the Building Authority, provide mechanical ventilation capable of supplying fresh air to all parts of the room at not less than 5 changes of air per hour, and artificial lighting he may approve (regulation 34). He may also require an additional aperture or airshaft to the open air of unobstructed sectional area not less than 0.015 m², a fanlight to a ventilated lobby or corridor, or other approved ventilation (regulation 35).

Regulation 71 allows the Building Authority to permit a required window, vent or airshaft to face or communicate with an unenclosed verandah, balcony or other unenclosed place, only if that will not prejudice the lighting or ventilation standards or the health of the occupiers. Regulation 37 prohibits a new building from reducing light and air to another building, erected in accordance with these regulations, below what these regulations require.

**Tenement house kitchens and rear windows (regulation 46), because they set room size.** A tenement house is a building in the domestic part of which any living room is intended or adapted for more than one tenant or sub-tenant. Living room means any room intended or adapted for cooking or sleeping. Depth from the front main wall, or from the front of a balcony on that wall, to the nearest rear main wall must not exceed 10 m unless the Building Authority exempts it. Every storey needs a window in the rear main wall with glass of at least 1.5 m² and an opening of at least 1.5 m² into the open air, with the top of the opening at least 2 m above the floor, unless exempted. Required windows must not be obstructed inside or outside.

| Tenement kitchen, total area of the domestic premises | Minimum internal kitchen area | Smaller dimension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does not exceed 45 m² | 3.75 m² | Not less than 1.5 m in every case |
| Exceeds 45 m² but does not exceed 70 m² | 4.5 m² | Not less than 1.5 m |
| Exceeds 70 m² | 5.5 m² | Not less than 1.5 m |

Every domestic building, and unless exempted any separately let dwelling part, must have kitchen accommodation (regulation 45). The internal kitchen surface to a height of at least 1.2 m must be tiled, or rendered in cement mortar not less than 12.5 mm thick, or finished in other non-absorbent material, and the kitchen must have a sink and water fittings, and a fireplace or cooking slab unless cooking is by gas, oil or electricity.

**SD takeaway:** Size every habitation, office and kitchen window to one-tenth glass and one-sixteenth opening, facing a 4.5 m street or a 21 m² light plane with a 2.3 m base, and keep every part of a habitation room within 9 m of that prescribed window.

### 10. Rooms containing soil fitments (regulation 36)
These regulations do not set population ratios of water closets, urinals or lavatory basins. Cap. 123 sub. leg. I is named only as the definition source for “residential building”. The sanitary geometry printed in the body of Cap. 123F is regulation 36. Accessible toilet sizes are in the Third Schedule (topic 12), not as a population ratio.

| Rule | Printed limit |
| --- | --- |
| Window or lantern light | Required in every room containing a soil fitment or waste fitment. |
| Glass | Aggregate superficial area not less than one-tenth of the floor area of the room. |
| Opening | A part not less than one-tenth of the floor area must open directly into the open air. |
| Height of the opening | Top of the openable part not less than 2 m above the floor. |
| Soil fitment | A water-closet fitment, a trough water-closet, or a urinal. |

**SD takeaway:** Give every soil-fitment room its own one-tenth openable window to the open air, with the opening starting by 2 m above the floor, and do not plan that room opening directly into a food room or a place-of-public-entertainment auditorium.

### 11. Staircases, means of escape, and firefighting access (regulations 39–44 and 41A–41D)

| Retaining wall (reg. 47) | No domestic building against a retaining wall which exceeds 4.5 m in height. Leave a space not less than 1.5 m between any domestic building and the bottom of any retaining wall exceeding 4.5 m. A massive rock face is deemed a retaining wall. |
| Retaining wall forming part of a domestic building (reg. 48) | Subject to regulation 27, waterproof it to prevent dampness, and insulate it to prevent condensation on the internal face of any room intended for habitation. |
| Dangerous trade and domestic use (reg. 49) | A building used or designed for domestic purposes or intended for habitation must not also be used for manufacture of dangerous goods under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295), storage of dangerous goods to which section 6 of that Ordinance applies, a motor repair shop, a vulcanizing shop, automobile or carriage painting, a paint shop where paint or varnish is manufactured or mixed, or dry-cleaning, unless the Building Authority exempts it. Notwithstanding that bar, a part not exceeding 50 m² may be used as a residence for a caretaker or other person employed in connexion with the maintenance of, or services for, the building. |

**SD takeaway:** Do not place a domestic building against a retaining wall or rock face over 4.5 m; if the wall is over that height, keep a 1.5 m space at its bottom.

### 16. Places of public entertainment (regulations 49A–49E)
A place of public entertainment must not be situated in a building used for any other purpose (regulation 49A). A cinema may be in a building used for other purposes, but only in a non-domestic building or the non-domestic part of a composite building (regulation 49B).

| Cinema rule | Printed limit |
| --- | --- |
| Capacity | One cinema, or two or more cinemas in the same building, must not provide accommodation for more than 2 000 persons in aggregate. |
| Incompatible operations in the same building | Motor vehicle repair workshop; petrol filling station; godown or warehouse; factory or industrial undertaking; school; child care centre; home for elderly persons (a residential care home under Cap. 459); home for persons with disabilities (a residential care home for persons with disabilities under Cap. 613). |
| Shared facilities | A cinema may share a means of escape, an entrance, a foyer and a waiting space with another cinema or place of public entertainment in the same building only if each shared facility is to the satisfaction of the Building Authority. |
| Domestic accommodation (reg. 49C) | Must not be provided in a place of public entertainment. |
| Floors and tiers (reg. 49D) | Gradient not steeper than 35° to the horizontal. Height between the floor or tier and the underside of the tier or ceiling above, in every part, at least 3 m. |
| Waiting (reg. 49E) | Adequate and suitable accommodation for persons awaiting admission. |

**SD takeaway:** Keep a cinema in the non-domestic part, cap the cinema accommodation in the building at 2 000 persons, and set each auditorium floor or tier to a rise of at least 3 m and a slope no steeper than 35°.

### 17. Temporary buildings that affect a permanent layout (regulations 50, 52, 53)
A temporary building of readily combustible materials must not be erected within 3 m of any other building or of the site boundary, and that 3 m must be kept clear (regulation 52). The floor of a contractor’s shed intended for habitation must be properly concreted or raised at least 1 m above ground level (regulation 53(3)).

Short-lived materials, so far as used wholly or principally for the weather-resisting part of a roof or external wall, include any boarding less than 16 mm in thickness (feather-edge boarding less than 16 mm at the thicker edge) and cement plaster not exceeding 40 mm in thickness on wood or metal lath, among the longer list in regulation 50(2).

Part VIII (regulations 59–63) is repealed. Parts VII and IX also contain permit, hoarding and deposit provisions. The deposit ceilings printed there are a sum not exceeding $500 for each contractor’s shed and a sum not exceeding $500 for a hoarding, covered walkway or gantry on Government land. Those are permit securities, not building geometry.

**SD takeaway:** Keep a combustible temporary building 3 m off other buildings and off the site boundary, and raise or concrete a habitable contractor’s shed floor to at least 1 m above ground.
