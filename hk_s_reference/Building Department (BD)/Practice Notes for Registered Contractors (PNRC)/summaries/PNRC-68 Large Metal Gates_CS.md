# PNRC-68 Large Metal Gates
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue December 2008; last revision December 2010; this revision April 2018 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs design, installation and maintenance of large metal gates, manually or electrically operated, at fence walls or at the entrance of a building. It is not a structural steel code: performance is Building (Construction) Regulation 3 and the standards in Appendix B, and installation of metal gates in an existing building may instead be minor works under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNRC 71.

## Regulatory Overview
Installation of a metal gate is building works under the Buildings Ordinance. For a new building the gate is shown on the building plans submitted for approval and consent, and a gate whose height exceeds 3.2 m also requires structural details and design calculations; a new gate exceeding 3.2 m in an existing building requires both building and structural plans before installation.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When plans are required (para 2)

| Situation | Submission |
|---|---|
| New building | Metal gates shown on the building plans for prior approval and consent |
| Large metal gate, height exceeding 3.2 m | Plans of structural details, including design calculations, submitted for approval |
| New metal gate in an existing building, height exceeding 3.2 m | Building plans and structural plans submitted for approval before installation |
| Existing building, minor-works route | Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNRC 71, as an alternative to prior approval and consent |

**SD takeaway:** Any entrance or fence gate higher than 3.2 m is a structural submission, on the new-building plans or, for an existing building, before it is installed.

### 2. Hinges, stops and drilled anchors (paras 4–8 and App. A)

All parts, fixed or movable, including fixings, are of sound construction, adequate strength and free from obvious defects. Erection checks the gate framework, post support and foundation; the connection of the frame to the surrounding structure; hinge pins on a swing gate; end stoppers on a sliding gate at both ends, and the equivalent connections on a multi-passing gate; and measures against lift-off or derailment.

| Type | Rule |
|---|---|
| Swing gate | Hinge fittings welded all round to the frame, able to take the dead weight and wind loads in normal operation |
| Hinge number | A minimum of 3 hinges between the gate and each supporting post |
| Lift-off | One hinge fitting with the hinge pin pointing in the reverse direction, or an equivalent restraint against vertical movement |
| Sliding gate | Leaf and moving parts prevented from falling, collapsing or derailment in normal operation or on contact with a stationary obstacle; guide and stopper of adequate size and strength for lateral stability, imposed loads and impact |
| Folding gate | Paragraphs 1 to 3 of Appendix A apply to two or more hinged leaves guided and/or supported at the top or bottom; hanging fittings between leaves also restrained against lift-off |
| Drilled-in anchors | At least 5 of each type and size tested in pull-out; pull-out capacity not less than 1.5 times the recommended tensile load specified by the manufacturer |
| Satisfactory anchor test | No sign of separation, plastic deformation or deleterious effect |
| Who directs the anchor tests | AP/RSE where plans are approved by the Building Authority or submitted as minor works with a Prescribed Building Professional; otherwise the registered general building contractor or registered minor works contractor |
| Electric operation | Also the Code of Practice for Installation of Electrically Operated Sliding Gates, Sliding Glass Doors and Rolling Shutters published by the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department |

**SD takeaway:** A swing gate needs at least three hinges to each post, one arranged against lift-off, and any drilled anchor is pull-tested at five samples of each type and size to 1.5 times the manufacturer’s recommended tensile load.

### 3. Inspection interval and reference standards (App. A paras 6–7 and App. B)

The AP coordinates with the registered general building contractor or registered minor works contractor to prepare safe-operation and maintenance documentation for the incorporated owners or management company. Routine work, including cleaning dirt and obstacles from the track and guide rail and lubrication, is carried out every three months. Appendix B lists reference standards, which are not exhaustive; other national standards may be used if equivalent performance is demonstrated. The list includes BS 4092:Part 1:1996; BS EN 12433-1:2000 and BS EN 12433-2:2000; BS EN 12604:2000; BS EN 12605:2000; BS EN 12635:2002+A1:2008; BS EN 13241-1:2003+A2:2016; BS EN ISO 1461:2009; BS EN ISO 12944-1 to 4:1998, BS EN ISO 12944-5:2007 and BS EN ISO 1294-6 to 8:1998; BS EN ISO 14713-1:2017, BS EN ISO 14713-2:2009 and BS EN ISO 14713-3:2017; and BS EN 1176-1:2017.

**SD takeaway:** Hand the owners a maintenance document that sets inspection, track cleaning and lubrication at every three months.
