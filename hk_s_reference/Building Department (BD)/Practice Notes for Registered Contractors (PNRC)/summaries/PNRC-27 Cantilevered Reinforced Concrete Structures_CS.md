# PNRC-27 Cantilevered Reinforced Concrete Structures
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue December 1994; last revision November 2005; this revision September 2012 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs design, construction, drainage and completion records for cantilevered reinforced concrete structures such as canopies, balconies, projecting windows, air-conditioner hoods and platforms, and flowerbeds. It is not a waiver of the Concrete Code, and paragraph 6 does not apply to in-fill slabs enclosed by edge beams or to slabs that do not behave as cantilevers.

## Regulatory Overview
Cantilevered reinforced concrete members, especially pure slabs exposed to weathering, depend on design, monolithic construction and maintenance, because weathering can degrade strength early and collapse has serious consequences. A cantilever under a permanent solid cover located at not greater than one storey height, and of at least the same plan area as that cover, may be treated as not exposed to weathering.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Span type, projection and loads (App. A paras 1 and 7)

| Rule | Figure in the note |
|---|---|
| Clear span more than 1000 mm | Use a beam-and-slab arrangement instead of a pure slab cantilever where practicable |
| Span-to-effective-depth | Comply with clause 7.3.4.2 of the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Concrete 2004 (Second Edition) |
| Loads to allow | Dead load of finishes, parapets and waterproofing; imposed load from maintenance; possible ponding if drainage fails |
| Future removal | Detail so the projection can be demolished or replaced without affecting the safety and integrity of the main structure, especially over streets |
| Projection and headroom | Extent of projection and clear space above ground to comply with Building (Planning) Regulations 7 and 10 and PNAP APP-19 as appropriate |
| Not cantilever behaviour | Paragraph 6 does not apply to in-fill slabs enclosed by edge beams, or slabs supported so they do not behave like cantilevers |

**SD takeaway:** Projections with a clear span over 1000 mm should be drawn as beam-and-slab where practicable, and the plan extent must already satisfy Building (Planning) Regulations 7 and 10 and PNAP APP-19.

### 2. Cantilevered beams (App. A para 5)

| Item | Minimum / rule |
|---|---|
| Overall depth at the support | At least 300 mm |
| Bars | Top and bottom bars held by stirrups; top bars anchored to clause 9.4.3 of the Concrete Code; top bars extended for support moments also follow the Concrete Code curtailment rules |
| Support by a cross beam | Supporting beam and adjacent internal slabs designed and detailed for the internal moment, torsion, shear and axial force |
| Exposure | External cantilevered beams designed for exposure condition 2 or higher if appropriate, to the Concrete Code |

**SD takeaway:** An external cantilever beam is at least 300 mm deep at the support and is specified for exposure condition 2 or higher.

### 3. Cantilevered slab thickness and reinforcement (App. A para 6)

| Clear span | Minimum overall thickness |
|---|---|
| Not greater than 500 mm | 110 mm |
| Greater than 500 mm but not greater than 750 mm | 125 mm |
| Greater than 750 mm | 150 mm |

| Reinforcement | Rule |
|---|---|
| Both faces, both directions | High yield steel |
| Main bars | At least 10 mm diameter; spacing not greater than 150 mm |
| Steel area | Not less than 0.25% of the cross-sectional area of the structural concrete |
| Anchorage | Clause 9.4.3 of the Concrete Code; curtailment rules apply to top bars extended into the adjacent span |
| Drop at the supporting end | Top main bars not greater than 16 mm diameter, so anchorage into the supporting beams and internal slab can be developed; details drawn at large scale |
| Supporting wall | Sufficient thickness for rigidity and for anchorage of the main bars, except for very small spans such as air-conditioner hoods |
| Corners and return parapets | Specific details where the slab continues around a corner, and at a side edge carrying a return parapet parallel to the span |

**SD takeaway:** Size the slab at 110 mm, 125 mm or 150 mm from the 500 mm and 750 mm span breaks, and reinforce both faces both ways with main bars at least 10 mm at not more than 150 mm centres and at least 0.25% steel.

### 4. External slabs, and the extra rules past 750 mm (App. A paras 8–9)

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| All external cantilevered slabs | Maximum crack width at the tension face 0.1 mm under the serviceability limit state, or stress in deformed high yield bars not exceeding 100 N/mm² under working-load flexural tension |
| Cover | Exposure condition 2 or higher if appropriate, to the Concrete Code |
| Span exceeding 750 mm and exposed to weathering | Construction report on completion, and Form BA14 confirming the works follow the approved plans and are structurally safe |
| That same case — concrete | Waterproof concrete of characteristic compressive strength not less than 35 MPa at 28 days |
| That same case — main bars | Hot-dip galvanized to BS EN ISO 1461 |
| That same case — membrane | Waterproof membrane or tanking, protected by 1:3 cement-sand mortar of 0.65 maximum free water/cement ratio, or other equivalent means |

**SD takeaway:** An exposed cantilever slab longer than 750 mm is a 35 MPa waterproof, hot-dip-galvanized-bar element with a completion report and BA14, and every external cantilever slab is checked to 0.1 mm crack width or 100 N/mm² bar stress.

### 5. Construction joint, cover, drainage and pipes (App. A paras 2 and 4; App. B)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Cast | Monolithic with, and at the same time as, the directly supporting members |
| Construction joint | Not along the external edge of the supporting members; any unavoidable alternative needs prior approval, strength no less than monolithic construction, and no water ingress through the joint |
| Appendix B | Recommended construction-joint locations for an exposed cantilevered slab: (i) continuous slab, (ii) drop slab, (iii) slab supported at beam top; joints marked at 45°, with galvanized steel reinforcing bars and waterproof concrete |
| Bar position | Adequate spacers; placing and compacting to clause 10.3.5 of the Concrete Code; avoid displacing or depressing bars |
| Formwork removal | Clause 10.3.8.2 of the Concrete Code |
| Supervision | Table 9.1 of the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 |
| Waterproofing and falls | Effective waterproofing; membrane or tanking protected by 1:3 cement-sand mortar laid to a fall of not less than 1:75 |
| Drainage | Drain surface water away where practicable; if water is to cascade from the edge (for example a projecting window), no nuisance under Building (Planning) Regulation 4(b); continuous drip or groove at the bottom edge |
| Canopies | Adequate surface-water drainage into the building’s surface-water disposal system, whether or not the canopy projects over a street; falls and drainage shown on general building plans and drainage plans |
| Inaccessible cantilevers | Maximum distance between drainage outlets, if provided, not more than 5 m |
| Pipes in the cantilever | Avoid penetration or embedment, especially in slabs; if unavoidable, allow for local loss of strength; PNAP APP-105 for water-borne pipes inside reinforced concrete beams and slabs |

**SD takeaway:** Cast the cantilever with its support, keep the construction joint off the external edge, fall the protection mortar at not less than 1:75, and space outlets at not more than 5 m where the slab cannot be reached for cleaning.

### 6. Construction report and canopy maintenance (App. A paras 10–12)

The report for a paragraph 9(a) slab includes an as-built plan of member location and dimensions, fall direction and gradient, drainage outlets, screed and finish thickness, membrane or tanking, construction joints, concrete and bar grades, bar diameter and spacing, cast date, and cover at critical positions measured by covermeter, plus record photographs of the bars before concreting and of the slab after concreting but before finishes and waterproofing. On completion of a cantilevered slab canopy, the AP coordinates with the RSE and the registered general building contractor to prepare inspection and maintenance documentation for the incorporated owners or management company, including cleaning of clogged drains and inspection of waterproofing and finishes, regularly and particularly in the rainy seasons.

**SD takeaway:** Exposed canopies over 750 mm need an as-built record of falls, outlets, cover and galvanized bars, and a maintenance document handed to the owners at completion.
