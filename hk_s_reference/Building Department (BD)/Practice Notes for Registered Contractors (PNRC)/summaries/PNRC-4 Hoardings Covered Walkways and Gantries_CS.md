# PNRC-4 Hoardings, Covered Walkways and Gantries
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue August 1965; last revision February 2009; this revision April 2025 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs hoardings, covered walkways and gantries — including the catch platforms erected on them — under Part IX of the Building (Planning) Regulations, and temporary suspension of public on-street parking for those works. It is not the Building Authority’s minimum construction standard (that standard is obtained from the authorized person) and it is not a permanent footpath or run-in design note.

## Regulatory Overview

Hoardings, covered walkways and gantries must keep a clear pedestrian route, resist demolition debris in urban areas, and stay safe, or the main works can be stopped. A hoarding permit can be self-certified under PNAP APP-23, and any suspension of on-street parking is a Transport Department application with fixed lead times.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Clear width, demolition construction and lighting

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Pedestrian clear width | Minimum **1.1 m** in every case; width is otherwise related to location |
| Urban demolition | Hoardings and covered walkways in **steel frames and steel plates**, to withstand debris impact |
| Covered-walkway lighting | Recommended layout for a typical walkway **2 m** wide and **2.5 m** high: luminaires with **18 W or 20 W**, **600 mm** tubular fluorescent lamps at **3 m** spacing |

Low scaffolding timbers or cross-pieces in the walkway are treated as a danger, particularly to people with weak sight. The installation must be maintained. If it is not kept safe, a cease works order is imposed on the main works until the remedial work to the hoarding, covered walkway or gantry is completed.

**SD takeaway:** The site logistics plan holds a **1.1 m** clear pedestrian route and, for urban demolition, a steel-plate hoarding with a lit covered walkway.

### 2. Hoarding permit by self-certification (PNAP APP-23)

| Application | Processing rule in this note |
|---|---|
| Permit to erect | Building Authority processes it within **30 days** if paragraph 19 of PNAP APP-23 is met |
| Renewal | Apply at least **30 days** before expiry (PNAP APP-23 paragraph 25) |
| Minor amendments | May be carried out without prior agreement of the Building Authority if paragraph 26 of PNAP APP-23 is met |

**SD takeaway:** Programme the hoarding permit and each renewal on the 30-day clocks in PNAP APP-23.

### 3. Suspending public on-street parking (Appendix A)

Apply to the Commissioner for Transport (regional Chief Transport Officer) and copy the Commissioner of Police (regional Senior Staff Officer/Traffic). State the period, when and where, and the reason. Keep the number of spaces to a minimum and stage the work if too many would be taken. No work starts on the spaces before suspension. Signs, meter heads, posts, studs and carriageway markings stay unless the Commissioner for Transport authorizes removal.

| Case | Notice |
|---|---|
| Less than three months | Written application with sketches, reaching the District Senior Transport Officer / Transport Officer at least **seven working days** before |
| Not more than two spaces, for no more than one day, for road works | Telephone **three working days** ahead to Transport Department and the Police, with written confirmation the same day |
| More than three months | Written application with sketches at least **30 days** before; longest approval is **one year**, then a fresh application; removal and reinstatement costs paid in advance to the Highways Department; tell Transport Department and Highways Department at least **one week** before project completion |
| Emergency | Short-notice request to the District Senior Transport Officer / Transport Officer and the Police; if advance notice is impracticable, notify the Police immediately and Transport Department as soon as possible |

Finish early: telephone Transport Department for reinstatement and confirm in writing. Overrun: telephone as soon as it is apparent, then confirm in writing. Transport Department, with the Police, may reinstate spaces once the works are observed complete or the area is no longer in use. Accidental damage to traffic aids is reported at once to the Highways Department, Transport Department and the Police; the applicant bears the cost.

**SD takeaway:** Any frontage that takes metered or non-metered bays needs a staged suspension sketch, with a seven-working-day or 30-day Transport Department lead time matched to the duration.
