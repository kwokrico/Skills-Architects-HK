# PNRC-5 Concrete against Adjoining Walls
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued February 1982; this revision May 1994 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs use of an adjoining building’s wall as permanent shuttering while pouring concrete. It is not a structural design method for the new member, and it is not a waiver of Buildings Ordinance section 40 if that wall fails.

## Regulatory Overview
Using the wall of an adjoining building as permanent shuttering is not acceptable unless a physical check has confirmed the wall’s thickness, condition and structural capability to take the load from wet concrete and tamping. Where stability is in question or cannot be verified, independent shuttering must keep load off the wall, or the works must support the wall and minimise the load imposed on it.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Check before the wall is used as shutter (paras 2–3)

| Condition | What the note requires |
|---|---|
| Wall proposed as permanent shutter | Physical check of thickness, condition, and capacity for wet-concrete and tamping loads |
| Stability in question, or not verifiable | Independent shuttering so no load is imposed on the wall; or support plus measures to minimise the load |

**SD takeaway:** Do not draw a new concrete face tight against a neighbour’s wall unless the existing wall has been checked as a loaded shutter, or the detail provides independent formwork.

### 2. Failure is treated as negligence (para 4)

Unless evidence clearly indicates otherwise, total or partial failure of the adjoining wall in these circumstances is taken as negligence in the checks or the precautions, and as an offence under Buildings Ordinance section 40.

**SD takeaway:** Party-wall and basement details must show either a verified existing wall or a separate shutter, because collapse is read as a section 40 offence.
