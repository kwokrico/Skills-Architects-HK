# PNRC-3 Cease Works Orders
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued June 1973 / This update January 1990 (GBS/C) | Buildings Department

> Scope note: This note tells a registered contractor when a stop, demolition, or alteration instruction under the Buildings Ordinance is a valid order. It does not define which works are dangerous, and it does not set a design standard.

## Regulatory Overview
An instruction or order under the Buildings Ordinance to stop building works, street works, lift works or escalator works in progress, or to demolish or alter any part of such works under construction or completed, must be in writing and signed on behalf of the Building Authority by an officer in one of the posts listed below. No member of the staff of the Buildings Ordinance Office has power under the Buildings Ordinance to issue such an instruction or order verbally.

## Critical main topics and subtopics
### 1. A statutory stop, demolition, or alteration order is written and signed
The order must be signed on behalf of the Building Authority by an officer holding one of these posts:

- Principal Government Building Surveyor
- Government Building Surveyor
- Government Structural Engineer
- Chief Building Surveyor
- Chief Structural Engineer
- Senior Building Surveyor
- Senior Structural Engineer

**SD takeaway:** The schematic site-instruction protocol should treat only a written order signed by one of those posts as authority to stop, demolish, or alter building works, street works, lift works, or escalator works.

### 2. A verbal order is reported, not obeyed as a Buildings Ordinance order
If any person claiming to be a government official acting under the Buildings Ordinance gives such an instruction or order verbally, the registered contractor is advised to ask to see the officer's Warrant Card and, having obtained information on his identity, to report the matter to the Building Authority without delay.

**SD takeaway:** Brief the contractor that a spoken stop, demolition, or alteration demand is reported to the Building Authority after the Warrant Card is seen, and is not itself the written order.
