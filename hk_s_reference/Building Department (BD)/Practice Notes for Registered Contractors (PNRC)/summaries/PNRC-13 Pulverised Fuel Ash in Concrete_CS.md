# PNRC-13 Pulverised Fuel Ash in Concrete
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued December 1982; last revision May 1994; this revision September 2001 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs pulverised fuel ash (PFA) as a partial replacement for ordinary Portland cement (OPC) in concrete. It is not a general permission to add PFA on top of a PFA blended cement, and it is not a cold-weather curing specification.

## Regulatory Overview
PFA as a partial cement replacement is permitted only on the conditions in paragraph 2, including the mass limits, the British Standards named there, and disclosure of the PFA content in the structural submission. Effective curing is required, and in cold weather very early strengths may be lower than equivalent OPC mixes.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. How much PFA is permitted (para 2(a)–(c))

| Form | Limit and standard |
|---|---|
| PFA as a separate constituent | Only with OPC; comply with BS 3892: Part 1:1982, except the maximum water-requirement criterion may not apply |
| Blended cement containing PFA | BS 6588:1985; nominal PFA content not exceeding 25% |
| PFA in addition to blended cement | Not to be used as a further partial cement replacement |
| PFA in the concrete | Not exceed 35% by mass of the cementitious content (OPC plus PFA) |
| 25% replacement | Usually only used in normal construction |
| Replacement exceeding 25% | Special applications such as marine construction and massive pours; expert advice and stringent site control |

**SD takeaway:** Specify the PFA percentage in the structural notes, and keep ordinary work at or below 25% unless the pour is a special application that can carry expert control up to the 35% ceiling.

### 2. Who must state the content, and what changes above 25% (para 2(d)–(e))

The AP/RSE specifies the PFA content and the extent of its use in the structural submissions. The registered contractor must be satisfied that the supplier’s quality control will make the finished concrete meet the specification and the statutory requirements, and must inform the AP/RSE of deviations. Where replacement exceeds 25%, the AP/RSE must also be satisfied that formwork removal, creep and long-term deflection will not adversely affect the structure.

**SD takeaway:** Any specification above 25% PFA has to be justified for striking time, creep and long-term deflection before the structural submission is frozen.
