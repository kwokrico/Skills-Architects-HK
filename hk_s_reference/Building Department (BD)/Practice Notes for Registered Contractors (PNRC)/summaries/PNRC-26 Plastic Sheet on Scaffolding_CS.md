# PNRC-26 Plastic Sheet on Scaffolding
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued May 1995 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs the use of plastic sheet over scaffolding outside buildings during demolition, construction, repair or renovation, alongside the statutory duty to provide screens, fans or catch platforms. It is not a fire-test specification for scaffold nets and it does not set sampling or laboratory rules.

## Regulatory Overview

Screens, fans, catch platforms or similar measures are obligatory so that objects do not fall outside the building boundary and so that dust is alleviated. Where polyvinyl sheet is nevertheless used to cover the scaffold, it must be flame-retardant, the scaffold must be reinforced for wind, and ventilation and light to occupied floors must be kept as far as practicable.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Screens, fans and catch platforms are already required

The note lists the duties as:

| Instrument | Provision cited |
|---|---|
| Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations (Cap. 59) | Regulation 49(1) |
| Building (Demolition Works) Regulations (Cap. 123) | Regulation 3(2)(a) |
| Summary Offences Ordinance (Cap. 228) | Section 4B(2) |

**SD takeaway:** Façade and demolition scaffolds are drawn with screens, fans or catch platforms as the protective system, whether or not a sheet is added.

### 2. Precautions when plastic sheet is used

Contractors often cover the whole scaffold of a vacant building under demolition or construction with polyvinyl sheet, and sometimes do the same on occupied or partly occupied buildings under repair or renovation. The Director of Fire Services is concerned that polyvinyl sheet aggravates fire spread. The Commissioner for Labour advises that sheeting which is not properly fixed can affect the stability of bamboo scaffolding in strong winds, and there have been cases where the sheet obstructed natural ventilation and lighting of occupied buildings under renovation.

When plastic sheeting covers scaffolding around the walls:

| Measure | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Fire | Use flame-retardant sheeting |
| Wind | Reinforce the scaffolding to withstand strong winds |
| Occupied renovation | Avoid obstructing natural ventilation and lighting as far as practicable |

**SD takeaway:** An occupied renovation that needs a sheeted scaffold must keep window ventilation and daylight in the phasing, and the sheet itself is specified flame-retardant.
