# PNRC-79 Conduct of Registered Contractors
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue May 2018 (AD/CS) | Buildings Department

> Scope note: This note states the statutory conduct expected of registered contractors, and gives examples of negligence and misconduct the Building Authority may put before a disciplinary board. In this note, "registered contractors" means registered general building contractors, registered specialist contractors, registered minor works contractors, and Qualified Persons. It does not itself impose a fine or a period of removal from the register.

## Regulatory Overview
The Buildings Ordinance provides for the planning, design, and construction of buildings and associated works by setting the building safety and health standards, and building owners are required to appoint and seek advice from registered contractors if they wish to carry out building works or street works. The registered contractor's statutory duties are to supervise the carrying out of the building works or street works, to notify the Buildings Department of any contravention of the regulations, and to comply generally with the Buildings Ordinance, and it is the contractor's responsibility that the works fully comply with the Buildings Ordinance, its subsidiary legislation, and any other relevant laws, codes of practice, and practice notes.

## Critical main topics and subtopics
### 1. Negligence or misconduct can be referred to a disciplinary board
The Buildings Department carries out curtailed and audit checks on submissions, and audit checks on completed building works and street works. The Buildings Ordinance and its subsidiary legislation provide for regulation of contractors through registration systems, prosecution, and disciplinary actions. Under section 13(2)(b), if a registered contractor has been negligent or has misconducted himself in building works or street works, the Building Authority may bring the matter to a disciplinary board appointed under section 11. Before that referral, the Building Authority considers the particulars, including the seriousness of the negligence or misconduct, to determine whether the registered contractor deserves censure under section 13(1).

Registered contractors are encouraged to report irregularities observed in the course of their work to the Chief Officer/Technical Services. Such reports are treated in the strictest confidence.

**SD takeaway:** The approved plan, or the plan relating to the minor works, is the line the contractor must not materially leave; a schematic change that will be built has to be on that plan before construction, because a material divergence is listed as non-compliance.

### 2. Examples of negligence and misconduct in Appendix A
Due diligence to comply with the building laws is required. Integrity is described as a fundamental quality. The Buildings Department expects full co-operation so that building safety, health, and environment standards are met. Registered contractors should comply with all relevant laws and regulations when carrying building works or street works.

| Head | Typical examples the appendix gives |
|---|---|
| 1. Buildings Ordinance and subsidiary legislation | Diverges or deviates in any material way from any work shown in a plan approved by the Building Authority, or in a plan relating to minor works. Knowingly misrepresents a material fact in any document given to the Building Authority. Undertakes construction work but is not qualified to discharge it. |
| 2. Integrity | Abets building owners and other persons to carry out unauthorised building works, or to disregard the Minor Works Control System. Falsifies information, documents, or records relating to building works. Advertises skills and knowledge in carrying out unauthorised building works. Has a financial or personal interest in any business related to the building works undertaken with a particular client, without declaring that interest to the client. |
| 3. Co-operation with the Buildings Department | Repeatedly fails, without a reasonable explanation, to provide factual information, inspection reports, or site records that the Buildings Department requires. Obstructs an investigation with a malicious intent to cover up. Repeatedly fails, without a reasonable explanation, to respond to a written warning on deficiencies or non-compliances. |
| 4. Other laws | Fails to observe site safety under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Chapter 59). Uses the Buildings Department logo on a name card, letterhead, or publicity material, which violates the Department's intellectual property rights. |

**SD takeaway:** Appoint a contractor who is qualified for the works, require any financial or personal interest in a related business to be declared to the client, and keep unauthorised building works and any disregard of the Minor Works Control System out of the schematic scope.
