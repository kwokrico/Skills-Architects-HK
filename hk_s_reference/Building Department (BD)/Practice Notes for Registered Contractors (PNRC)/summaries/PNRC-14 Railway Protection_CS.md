# PNRC-14 Railway Protection
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue April 1987; last revision September 2020; this revision December 2022 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Railway protection under the Railways Ordinance (Cap. 519), the Mass Transit Railway (Land Resumption and Related Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 276), and Area Number 3 of the scheduled areas in Schedule 5 to the Buildings Ordinance. Appendices B and C apply to building works, ground investigation, and underground drainage inside railway protection areas. On the part of a lot outside those areas, the registered contractor must still keep building works from adversely affecting railway structures or facilities or their factor of safety.

## Regulatory Overview

Since the 2 December 2007 merger, MTR Corporation Limited operates the railway lines listed in Appendix A, and the railway protection plans can be viewed by appointment at MTRCL. Area Number 3 boundaries are the Schedule 5 plans (PNAP APP-39), deposited in the Land Registry and viewable at the Buildings Department.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Protection-area boundary

As a general guide, the boundary is about **30 m** outside the outer surface of the railway structures and facilities, or the railway fence or wall, or from the nearest rail where there is no fence or wall. It may take in the whole of any lot where any part of the lot lies within that 30 m. At stations the enclosed area may be more extensive.

Appendix A (December 2022) lists lines in operation: Airport Express, Disneyland Resort Line, East Rail Line, High Speed Rail, Island Line, Kwun Tong Line, Light Rail, South Island Line, Tseung Kwan O Line, Tsuen Wan Line, Tuen Ma Line, and Tung Chung Line. Plans are updated from time to time.

**SD takeaway:** Treat any lot that touches the 30 m zone, and any larger station zone, as railway-protected from the first massing and foundation sketch.

### 2. Area Number 3 — approval, consent, and minor works

| Works | Control in this note |
|---|---|
| Ground investigation in Area Number 3 | Prior BA approval and consent. Plans under Building (Administration) Regulations regulation 8(1)(l). Proposals follow Appendix B. Concurrent approval and consent: PNAP ADM-16 |
| Underground drainage in Area Number 3 | Full Buildings Ordinance approval and consent, except underground drainage that may proceed under the simplified requirements of the Minor Works Control System as in paragraph 16 |
| GI or underground drainage in a railway protection area that is not Area Number 3 | Forward the proposal to MTRCL for comment before commencement |
| Class I and Class II minor works in Area Number 3 that involve spread footing, excavation, or underground drainage | BD copies the submission to MTRCL. The prescribed building professional or prescribed registered contractor obtains MTRCL’s agreement before commencement |

Section 27 of the Railways Ordinance or section 15 of Cap. 276 can be used to require protective measures, including where proposed minor works are incompatible with railway construction, maintenance, improvement, or operation.

**SD takeaway:** Ground investigation and underground drainage inside Area Number 3 stay on the approval-and-consent path unless the specific minor-works carve-out applies, and that carve-out still needs MTRCL agreement before start.

### 3. Ground investigation near underground railway structures

Ground investigation proposals must show exploration locations relative to railway structures and facilities inside or outside the lot, proposed depths, and method statements for sinking, excavation, backfilling, field testing, and instrumentation.

| Situation | Method-statement trigger |
|---|---|
| Borehole or drillhole within **10 m** on plan of any point of an underground railway structure or facility, and sunk to a depth of **3 m** from the highest point of those structures and facilities | Method statement for checking verticality |
| Borehole or drillhole within **3 m** on plan of any point of an underground railway structure or facility | Method statement for controlling depth |

**SD takeaway:** Place exploration holes on plan against the underground railway outline before tender, because holes inside 10 m and 3 m need verticality and depth controls.

### 4. Foundations, excavation, anchors, and the 20 kPa limit

Unless MTRCL has given prior special agreement:

| Element | Limit |
|---|---|
| Pile, foundation, borehole, drillhole, well, soil nail, horizontal drain, rock bolt, dowel, or other geotechnical installation | Not within **3 m** of any point of the underground railway structures and facilities |
| Piling | Not within **3 m** on plan of the railway fence or wall; where there is no fence or wall, not within **7 m** on plan of the centreline of the nearest track |
| Anchor | Any part more than **3 m** from any part of a railway structure or facility. The centroid of the fixed length more than **twice the fixed length** away from any railway structure or facility |
| Vertical or horizontal pressure change on underground railway structures and facilities | Not exceeding **20 kPa**, from excavation, filling, ground improvement, dewatering, field testing (plate loading, pressure-meter, packer), and from loads added or removed by foundations, including construction loads |

The 20 kPa limit may be reviewed case by case when an engineering approach is adopted, subject to acceptance by MTRCL and the Buildings Department.

Sinking of wells inside railway protection areas is building works under the Buildings Ordinance, including section 14(1): plan approval and consent before the works.

**SD takeaway:** Keep piles, bores, soil nails, and wells outside the 3 m underground exclusion, keep at-grade piling outside 3 m of the fence or 7 m of the track centreline, and hold underground railway pressure change to 20 kPa unless MTRCL and BD accept an engineering approach.

### 5. Openings, greening, and works above track

| Item | Distance |
|---|---|
| Openable or fixed window, doorway, or building ventilation intake or exhaust, relative to a railway ventilation opening (free-standing or inside a building) | Not closer than **5 m**. May be reduced to **2.5 m** if exhaust air from the railway ventilation facilities is directed away and is not likely to affect the opening by natural convection |
| Vertical greening | Not closer than **5 m** to the ventilation opening of any railway ventilation facilities |
| Scaffolding, working platforms, advertising signs, or projections at a level above the railway tracks | Not within **6 m** on plan of any point of the railway tracks without MTRCL agreement. Inside that limit, protect the railway; works may be required outside railway operation hours |
| Stationary lifting appliance erected adjacent to railway tracks and/or above-ground railway structures | Protective measures agreed with MTRCL |
| Mobile plant or lifting appliance with any part higher than railway track level | Protective measures agreed with MTRCL when operating within **6 m** on plan of railway structures and facilities |
| Jib swing | Arc not to encroach within **6 m** on plan of the railway tracks and/or above-ground structures and facilities, except with MTRCL agreement |
| Storage of materials, dangerous goods, other inflammable materials, containers, huts | Not within **6 m** on plan of railway structures, tracks, and ventilation openings without MTRCL agreement |
| Tree planting on an unfenced railway section | Not at a distance less than **2 m** from the nearest rail. Planting proposal, including estimated height and spread at maturity, to MTRCL so collapse of trunk or branches would not affect railway operation |
| Road-lamp maintenance plant | Not closer to the tracks than the nearest part of the lamp standard where the lamp is higher than the tracks. If the equipment operates higher than **1 m** above the tracks and closer than **6 m**, MTRCL-agreed protection is required; works may be outside operation hours |

A building that adjoins or is integrated with railway structures must have drainage that prevents flooding from drain blockage or a burst water pipe from entering the railway. Sites adjacent to railway structures need site drainage, agreed with MTRCL, that keeps foul and storm water out of the railway.

Open at-grade track is protected from fallen objects by hoardings, covered walkways, gantries, and catch platforms (PNAP APP-102). Demolition above or next to above-ground railway structures (entrances, vent shafts, substations, plant rooms, overhead structures, at-grade tracks), including scaffolding, signs, and container offices, needs effective protection.

Reclamation, dredging, submarine pipes, or other marine works near a railway immersed tunnel or marine railway structures are submitted for approval in consultation with MTRCL.

**SD takeaway:** Set façades, vents, greening, crane arcs, and storage so openings and greening stay 5 m from railway vents (2.5 m only for the exhaust-directed opening case) and so anything above track stays outside 6 m unless MTRCL has agreed.

### 6. Empirical Alert, Alarm, and Action triggers

Where MTRCL has not agreed an engineering analysis, building works must stay within these empirical limits: angular distortion not exceeding **1 in 1 000** in any plane; total movement not exceeding **20 mm** in any plane; induced level difference between rails of a track in the perpendicular plane not exceeding **5 mm**; peak particle velocity from blasting not exceeding **25 mm/s** and from prolonged vibration (pile driving or withdrawal, or similar) not exceeding **15 mm/s**; vibration on an overhead power-line mast or on signalling and telecommunication furniture not exceeding **10 mm/s** peak particle velocity and **80 µm** amplitude. A more stringent set may be required, for example where railway structures have previous settlement records. Vibration and settlement control in PNAP APP-137 still applies.

Alert, Alarm, and Action are about **50%, 75%, and 100%** of the empirical movement limits, and about **60%, 80%, and 100%** of the empirical vibration limits:

| Criterion | Alert | Alarm | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Angular distortion in any plane | 1:2 000 | 1:1 350 | 1:1 000 |
| Total movement in any plane | 10 mm | 15 mm | 20 mm |
| Level difference between rails of a track in the perpendicular plane | 2.5 mm | 3.8 mm | 5 mm |
| Peak particle velocity, blasting | 15 mm/s | 20 mm/s | 25 mm/s |
| Peak particle velocity, prolonged vibration | 9 mm/s | 12 mm/s | 15 mm/s |
| Peak particle velocity on overhead power-line mast, or signalling and telecommunication furniture | 6 mm/s | 8 mm/s | 10 mm/s |
| Vibration amplitude on that mast or furniture | 48 µm | 64 µm | 80 µm |

In principle, works that would induce an adverse impact on the affected railway structures or facilities are suspended once the pre-set Action limit is reached. The extent of suspension may be set in advance on the prescribed plans. If a checkpoint reaches the pre-set suspension limit, or BD, EMSD, or MTRCL considers railway safety so affected that suspension is warranted, the three parties notify each other immediately. A joint BD and EMSD press release is issued within **48 hours** of suspension, and again within **48 hours** after an agreed resumption. Partial resumption may be allowed with sufficient justification.

The registered contractor monitors movement, vibration, and other effects to the approved monitoring plan and sends records to MTRCL at **bi-weekly** intervals, or at a frequency MTRCL agrees, for the whole of the works. Piezometric change and ground movement on and near the site are monitored under the instruction of the AP, RSE, or RGE.

**SD takeaway:** Design so predicted movement stays inside Alert (1:2 000, 10 mm, 2.5 mm rail difference) and so vibration stays inside the Alert peak particle velocities, because Action is a suspension trigger.

### 7. Engineering-approach triggers

An engineering approach may set site-specific limits, accepted case by case by MTRCL and BD. The prescribed plans must include the preventive and mitigation measures. Appendix C requires existing ground and groundwater data, as-built railway information and design assumptions, historical movement records from MTRCL if available, the preventive measures and their limits, and a structural appraisal of the affected railway structures. A ground-movement analysis for each type and stage of work is submitted with the prescribed plans. An engineering appraisal of railway equipment (track, power supply, signalling) inside the affected zone is submitted to MTRCL.

Precautionary measures in the note include an alternative foundation or a setback of foundations or excavation, ground improvement, pre-boring, permanent casings, limits on concurrent rigs, staged excavation and de-propping, recharge wells, and deeper cut-off.

The works are split into critical stages. Estimated movement in each critical stage is **not less than 10%** and **not more than 50%** of the estimated total movement of those works. For each stage, Alert is 50% of that stage’s estimated movement plus the total estimated movement of previous stages. Alarm, except at the last stage, is 100% of that stage’s estimated movement plus previous stages; at the last stage, Alarm is the mean of Alert and Action. Action is 100% of the estimated total movement on completion. The breakdown is shown on the monitoring plan in the prescribed plans, for BA approval and MTRCL agreement.

Initial readings are taken by an independent qualified survey team. A report with photographs and the initial readings goes to BD and MTRCL before the works start. Consent may be given stage by stage after review of recorded movement.

**SD takeaway:** If the empirical limits cannot be met, the monitoring plan must split the works so each stage is between 10% and 50% of total predicted movement and must show Alert, Alarm, and Action for every stage.
