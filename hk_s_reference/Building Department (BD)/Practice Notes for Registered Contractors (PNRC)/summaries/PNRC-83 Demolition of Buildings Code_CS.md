# PNRC-83 Demolition of Buildings Code
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue October 2023 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Promulgates the Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings 2004 as amended by Appendix A (September 2016) and Appendix B (October 2023), both uploaded to the Buildings Department website. It is not a fresh demolition code, and this summary does not restate the code clause by clause; it records what the note and the September 2016 amendments in the extract require of the project.

## Regulatory Overview

Registered specialist contractors follow the Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings 2004 together with the amendments the note promulgates. The September 2016 amendments bring in the registered geotechnical engineer and the Minor Works Control System, tighten scaffold and catchfan stability, add a further debris net, and give further guidance for mechanical demolition, buildings designed to the LCC codes, and debris handling.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the note puts into force

The Buildings Department’s Technical Committee collects industry feedback on the 2004 code. Amendments promulgated on the department website are Appendix A (September 2016) and Appendix B (October 2023). A similar note went to authorized persons, registered structural engineers and registered geotechnical engineers. The extract contains the cover, the September 2016 amendment summary and table, and the last page of the 41-page note. Appendix B is named and promulgated; its body is not in the pages read, apart from that last page.

**SD takeaway:** Demolition drawings and the contractor’s method are prepared to the 2004 code plus the 2016 and 2023 amendments, not to the 2004 text alone.

### 2. Scope, survey and who signs the safety line

The amended clause 1.1 states that the code is not intended to cover demolition under the Minor Works Control System (Type G) or major civil engineering works such as underpinning, excavation, highway or railway bridges and dams. Minor-works demolition is referred to the Technical Guidelines on the Minor Works Control System. Removal of certain building works, including unauthorised building works, can be designated minor works, in which case the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and its precautionary measures apply, and prior approval and consent are not required for that minor-works stream. The old guidelines on removal of typical unauthorised building works are treated as obsolete.

Other demolition methods may be used on the recommendation of the authorized person, registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer and registered specialist contractor (demolition), or their consultants, based on scientific research and engineering assessment.

If record plans are not available, the building survey includes an on-site survey and, if necessary, material testing. The survey covers the age of the building, construction and structural materials, dilapidation and deterioration of any external façade facing a street, and structures projecting over a street. Wall types include blockwork, reinforced concrete, load-bearing, partition, screen and retaining walls. Site safety features, hoardings and covered walkways are designed by the authorized person, registered structural engineer and, where the geotechnical brief applies, the registered geotechnical engineer. The demolition contractor carries out the works and the precautionary measures to the approved plans and gives continuous supervision.

**SD takeaway:** Type G minor-works demolition stays on the Minor Works Control System guidelines; full demolition with a slope, retaining wall or street projection brings the registered geotechnical engineer onto the safety design.

### 3. Scaffold, nets, tarpaulin and catch platforms

These are the public-edge rules the September 2016 amendments write into the code. They are construction controls the demolition plan has to allow for.

| Item | Requirement in the amendment |
|---|---|
| Bamboo scaffold | Comply with the Code of Practice for Bamboo Scaffolding Safety and the Guidelines on the Design and Construction of Bamboo Scaffolds |
| Ties | Structural ties and struts to the building; bamboo tied to sound anchors at intervals of not more than **4 m** horizontally and vertically; ties and struts to the cantilevered portion above the topmost floor, and again above the floor being demolished |
| Tall scaffold | If higher than **15 m**, steel brackets or another support at intervals of not more than **15 m** |
| Striking | Scaffold comes down with the demolition; when ties and struts are released, the unsecured length is removed; unbraced sections not higher than **2 m** from the nearest anchor |
| Polyethylene net | Minimum specification in Table 3.3, or an approved equivalent: polyethylene, string diameter **1 mm**, **16** ply, mesh opening **20 mm**, weight **2130 g/m²** |
| Extra net | Where appropriate, a further heavy-duty nylon net of minimum **3.5 mm** diameter and mesh opening **50 mm** maximum, in addition to the tarpaulin and the polyethylene net, to catch larger falling debris |
| Tarpaulin | Lightweight and fire-retardant: Type B performance in BS 5867, or the flame-retardant test for lightweight cloths in the Japan Ministerial Ordinance on protective canvas for construction, or an equivalent |
| Catchfan | An extra bamboo layer with ties is recommended on top of the **0.5 mm** metal sheet so the sheet is not ripped off in strong wind or typhoon; through-bolts as well as anchor bolts are noted as industry practice for fixing catchfan supports |
| Catch platform | On the covered walkway to clause 3.2, and also under elements that can shed debris, including canopies and balconies and in particular any projection over a street, and where the process requires it under architectural features and prestressed elements; installed before demolition starts and designed for the anticipated load |

**SD takeaway:** Street frontage during demolition needs a tied scaffold (4 m anchors, 15 m brackets above 15 m), a double net where debris may be large, a fire-retardant tarpaulin, and catch platforms under every projection over the street before demolition starts.

### 4. Propping, vibration, debris paths and cantilevers

| Item | Requirement in the amendment |
|---|---|
| Propping differing from Table 3.4 | Full structural substantiation by the registered structural engineer |
| LCC-era buildings | Special attention where the building was designed to the LCC design codes of **1952**, with lower permissible material stress; clause 3.5.3(C)(4) allows slab capacity to be increased by spreading load through sleepers and base plates |
| Props | Lateral restraint in at least two directions, with cross-bracing at the perimeter and end bays |
| Vibration | Registered specialist contractor (demolition) is advised to monitor; peak particle velocity at any adjoining structure should not exceed **15 mm/s** for transient vibration and **7.5 mm/s** for prolonged and continuous vibration from mechanical demolition (aligned with PNAP APP-137) |
| Chutes | Adequate strength and support; lift shafts, light wells and floor openings may be used; barricade and warning signs when not in use; plastic chutes inside those openings are an option to limit noise and confine debris |
| Debris on floors | Not allowed unless justified by engineering calculation; not against the hoarding or external wall, and not behind or on top of a remaining wall or slope; propping design includes debris load |
| Temporary supports left in place | Owner, authorized person, registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer and registered specialist contractor (demolition) inspect and maintain them until removal |
| Cantilevered structures | The last page, on Appendix G Figure G.5, requires a temporary platform unless the cantilever is demolished by cut-and-lift or a similar technique under paragraph 3.5.1(B) |

**SD takeaway:** A building designed to the LCC design codes of 1952, and any cantilever over a street, needs a propping and platform strategy, with the 7.5 mm/s limit used where mechanical demolition will cause prolonged vibration at a neighbour.

### 5. Daily control of the works and of waste

Inspection of temporary structures, catchfans, catch platforms and other precautions is at frequencies not less than those in the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans and the Code of Practice for Site Safety Supervision, by the authorized person, registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer (or their experienced representatives) and the registered specialist contractor (demolition). The contractor inspects daily and clears debris off catchfans and catch platforms, and gives full-time continuous supervision so that unstable or partly demolished structure is checked safe before the site is left each day. A departure from the method statement stops further demolition until it is rectified and a written instruction to resume is issued. The authorized person, registered structural engineer and registered geotechnical engineer see that the contractor prepares and implements a debris disposal and management system. The contractor is advised to submit a waste management plan for sorting, processing and disposal of construction and demolition materials to those three professionals before works begin. Broken concrete is sorted for recycling at a construction and demolition materials recycling facility; advice may be sought from the Civil Engineering and Development Department at planning stage.

**SD takeaway:** The demolition programme includes a debris-management system and a waste plan approved before start, plus a daily clear of catch platforms, because debris left on a floor or against a remaining wall is an overload case.
