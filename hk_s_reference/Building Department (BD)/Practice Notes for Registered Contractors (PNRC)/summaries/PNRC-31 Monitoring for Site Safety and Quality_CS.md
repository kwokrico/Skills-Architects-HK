# PNRC-31 Monitoring for Site Safety and Quality
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue September 1995; last revision February 2006; this revision December 2010 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs how the Buildings Department Site Monitoring Section inspects construction and demolition sites, and how it samples minor works, for safety and quality. It is not the approval, consent, licence or occupation-permit inspection carried out by the New Buildings Divisions, and it is not the site-audit strategy in PNRC 49.

## Regulatory Overview

The Site Monitoring Section runs systematic inspections aimed at public safety and quality, and visits higher-risk operations more often. The contractor is expected to give access, keep the approved documents on site, follow the agreed sequence and Building Authority conditions, and put right unsatisfactory work without delay.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the inspection is looking for

Staff focus on unsatisfactory matters that bear on public safety and quality:

| Concern | Examples in the note |
|---|---|
| Plans | Divergence or deviation in a material way from approved plans |
| Materials | Use of defective materials |
| Precautions | Absence of adequate precautionary measures |
| People | Lack of supervision; workmen or plant operators without adequate experience for specially hazardous operations |
| Sequence | Departure from the approved or agreed work sequence or procedures |
| Orders | Non-compliance with conditions or orders of the Building Authority |
| Statute | Contravention of the Buildings Ordinance or its subsidiary legislation |

Where needed, the Section advises the authorized person, registered structural engineer or registered geotechnical engineer of corrective action with safety consequences. Orders under sections 23 and 24A of the Buildings Ordinance, prosecution and disciplinary action are considered case by case. Inspections are more frequent for demolition, deep excavations, percussion piling, formation of steep slopes, construction of high retaining walls, works projecting over public thoroughfares, and operation of heavy powered mechanical plant.

Inspecting officers carry a warrant card authorising them to enter premises and land, to check compliance with the Buildings Ordinance, and to carry out tests, make openings and take reasonable samples.

**SD takeaway:** Demolition, deep excavation, percussion piling, steep slopes, high retaining walls, projections over streets and heavy plant are the operations that draw more frequent Site Monitoring Section visits, so those sequences need precautionary measures on the drawings.

### 2. What must be on site, and what the contractor must do

| Expectation | Content |
|---|---|
| Access | Ready access for Site Monitoring Section staff |
| Approved-works file | Copies of all approved plans, agreed sequences and procedures, and all Building Authority conditions issued to the contractor by the authorized person, registered structural engineer and registered geotechnical engineer |
| Minor works file | Copies of prescribed plans, details and supervision plans (as required) issued by the authorized person |
| Compliance | Agreed sequences, conditions and orders adhered to; supervision requirements for building and demolition works met; adequate precautions, particularly against falling objects |
| Reporting | Any contravention from the carrying out of the works notified to the Building Authority as soon as possible; unsatisfactory aspects rectified without delay |

Approval of plans, consent, permits, licences, occupation permits, and performance tests of materials, structural elements and systems stay with the New Buildings Divisions. If the Section finds a safety or quality breach outside the Buildings Ordinance, it refers the case to the appropriate department.

Minor works under the simplified requirements (submitted plans rather than approved plans) are small and short. Selected cases under construction receive audit site-safety inspections instead of the systematic programme used for sites with approved plans. The same concerns, remedies and co-operation duties apply to the extent the individual minor works justify.

**SD takeaway:** The site set for both full-consent works and minor works includes the plans, the agreed sequence and the supervision plan, because an inspector can ask for them on arrival.
