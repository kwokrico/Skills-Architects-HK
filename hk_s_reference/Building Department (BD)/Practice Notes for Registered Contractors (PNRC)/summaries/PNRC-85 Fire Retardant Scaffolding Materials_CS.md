# PNRC-85 Fire Retardant Scaffolding Materials
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue December 2025 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs fire-retardant performance of protective nets, screens, tarpaulins and plastic sheeting on external-wall scaffolding for construction, demolition, alteration and addition, repair and minor works. It is not a scaffold structural design note, and domestic buildings not exceeding three storeys are outside the sampling rules.

## Regulatory Overview

Registered general building contractors, registered specialist contractors and registered minor works contractors may install these protective materials only when the lot meets a recognised fire-retardant standard and the sampling tests in this note. The Buildings Department audits by elevation after notification, and a failed sample clears the whole lot or the whole elevation.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Recognised standards and the report kept on site

Before delivery to any site, the contractor obtains the fire-retardant test report for that lot from the supplier, keeps it on site, and displays it in a conspicuous place for the authorized person, registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer, registered inspector, owners or occupants, and law-enforcement officers. The report shows manufacturer, place of origin, performance standard, test method and result.

| Standard | Applies to |
|---|---|
| **GB 5725-2009** “Safety nets” | Protective nets. **GB 5725-2025** “Fall protection — Safety nets” takes effect on **1 September 2026** and supersedes GB 5725-2009 |
| **BS 5867-2:2008** Type B | Protective nets, screens, tarpaulins and plastic sheeting |
| **NFPA 701:2023** Test Method 2 | Protective nets, screens, tarpaulins and plastic sheeting |

An equivalent standard with documentary proof may be considered if the contractor consults the Buildings Department in advance. Minor-works repairs of external rendering, wall tiles or cladding commonly fall under this note.

**SD takeaway:** Façade protection is specified to one of the three named standards, with GB 5725-2025 written in for any net that will still be on the scaffold on or after 1 September 2026.

### 2. Which scaffolds are sampled

| Building | Sampling trigger |
|---|---|
| Existing building undergoing alteration and addition, repair or minor works | Scaffolding of **three or more consecutive storeys**, including a scaffold over the whole elevation of those storeys even if only one elevation is covered, and a scaffold over the full height of a re-entrant or light well. Truss-out scaffolding for repair of an individual unit is excluded |
| Building under construction or demolition | The paragraph 6 sampling rules apply. The contractor may install first and then sample, or sample before installation |
| Either case | Domestic buildings not exceeding **three storeys** are excepted |

**SD takeaway:** A repair scaffold of three or more storeys on an existing building, or a full-height scaffold in a re-entrant, is in the sampling regime; a single-unit truss-out repair is not.

### 3. How a lot is sampled

A third party specifies the random method. The note gives as examples a representative of the owners, owners’ corporation or property manager, a notary, or a conformity-assessment body accredited by the Hong Kong Accreditation Service. The contractor or its authorised representative then samples, on site or off site. Each sample is at least **1.8 m × 2 m**, with longitudinal and transverse directions marked. Not more than one sample is taken from each roll or sheet. The whole sampling process is recorded on video. A Sample Information Sheet is signed by the contractor or its representative and endorsed by the authorized signatory (Part D is unnecessary if the authorized signatory signs Part C). Each sample goes to the laboratory in a transparent sealed envelope with that sheet.

Number of samples from each lot per order, adopted from **ISO 2859-1**:

| Rolls or sheets in the lot | Samples |
|---|---|
| 2 to 15 | 2 |
| 16 to 25 | 3 |
| 26 to 90 | 5 |
| 91 to 150 | 8 |
| 151 to 280 | 13 |
| 281 to 500 | 20 |
| 501 to 1 200 | 32 |
| 1 201 to 3 200 | 50 |
| 3 201 to 10 000 | 80 |

Tests are by a laboratory designated by the Government and accredited under the Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme, or by a body with a mutual recognition arrangement with that scheme. The report carries the accreditation symbol, the information in Parts A to C of the Sample Information Sheet, and photographs before and after the test. It is electronic and digitally signed with a recognised certificate under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance (Cap. 553). The designated-laboratory list is on the Buildings Department website. Every electronic report, pass or fail, is sent to the Buildings Department. Off-site sampling uses a tracking system such as QR codes, near-field communication or radio-frequency identification from warehouse delivery through sampling to site installation, and the purchase order, delivery record, sampling record and test report are bound together.

If any sample in a lot fails, the entire lot is not used. On an existing building, material is installed only when every sample in the lot has passed.

**SD takeaway:** Protective sheet and net are a tested lot, with at least a 1.8 m by 2 m sample and a video of the sampling, before they are relied on as the fire-retardant layer.

### 4. Construction and demolition sites, notification and elevation audit

On a construction or demolition site the same sampling rules apply, but the contractor may install the materials and start work first, then treat every **four storeys** on an elevation as one lot, take the paragraph 6 samples, and send them for test. A failed sample means that lot is replaced immediately. The contractor may instead sample and pass the tests before installation.

Within **seven days** after protective materials have been installed on the scaffolding of the entire building, the contractor notifies the Buildings Department on the form at Appendix IV, with a copy to the authorized person or registered inspector where applicable. The same notice covers replacement. The department randomly selects projects. Its officers require the contractor to sample on site at locations and storeys chosen at random; samples are tested by the Public Works Laboratories of the Civil Engineering and Development Department or by the Government Laboratory. If any sample from an elevation fails, the protective materials on that entire elevation are non-compliant and are completely removed. Test reports, purchase orders, delivery records, sampling records, Sample Information Sheets and results are kept on site.

Fire-retardant performance can fall off with time and site operations. The contractor monitors condition and samples again at intervals not exceeding **12 months**, using the paragraph 8 method, and submits new results to the Buildings Department. Replacement material is sampled under paragraph 6, 8 or 9 before it is treated as compliant, and replacement is also subject to site audit.

If sampling and testing are not done, or a sample taken by the department fails, the Buildings Department orders immediate cessation of the works and removal of the non-compliant materials under the Buildings Ordinance, and may prosecute, take disciplinary action, or refer the case to another enforcement department.

**SD takeaway:** Budget a seven-day notice when the full scaffold is sheeted, a retest at least every 12 months, and replacement of a whole elevation if one audit sample on that elevation fails.
