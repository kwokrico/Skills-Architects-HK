# PNRC-64 Site Safety Enhancement
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue August 2006 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs coordination between the Site Supervision Plan System under the Buildings Ordinance and the contractor’s Safety Management System under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings (Safety Management) Regulation. It is not a new technically-competent-person grade table and it is not itself the Safety Management Regulation.

## Regulatory Overview

The Site Supervision Plan System, under section 39A of the Buildings Ordinance and in force since 1997, protects occupants, the public, people on and around the site, and adjoining buildings, structures and land. The Safety Management System protects workers; from 1 April 2002 its depth depends on contract value and workforce size, and this note asks the registered contractor’s authorized signatory to knit the two systems together.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which Safety Management System tier applies

Under the Site Supervision Plan System the authorized person, registered structural engineer, registered geotechnical engineer and registered contractor each engage technically competent persons. Supervision requirements follow the type and scale of the building works or street works. The Safety Management Regulation is aimed at workers’ safety and health. Its initial stage, from **1 April 2002**, is:

| Contractor | Safety Management System duty |
|---|---|
| Contract value of **$100 million** or more, or an aggregate of **100 or more** workers in a day on one or more construction sites | The **8** elements in Part 1 and the **2** elements in Part 2 of Schedule 4, and a safety audit every **6 months** |
| An aggregate of **50 or more but less than 100** workers in a day on a single site, or working on **2 or more** construction sites | The **8** elements in Part 1 of Schedule 4, and a safety review every **6 months** |
| All contractors | The **4** elements in Part 3 of Schedule 4 were not in operation when this note was issued |

Duty holders mostly differ: the project team and their technically competent persons under the supervision plan, and the principal contractor, subcontractors and sub-subcontractors under the safety management system. The common duty holder is the main contractor, who is the registered contractor on one system and the principal contractor on the other.

**SD takeaway:** A contract at or above $100 million, or a site that will field 100 or more workers, carries a six-monthly safety audit; that cost and attendance belong in the procurement assumption.

### 2. Coordination measures the contractor is asked to adopt

The Buildings Department and the Labour Department recommend immediate implementation. The authorized signatory is expected to coordinate. The measures are:

| Measure | What the contractor does |
|---|---|
| Safety committees | Include the authorized signatory, the authorized signatory’s representative and some technically competent persons in the Safety Management System committees; the authorized signatory may chair at corporate level and the representative at site level |
| Inspection programme | In the Safety Management System element “programme of inspection”, give priority to high-risk processes and critical stages under the supervision plan |
| Method statements | When the element “evaluation of job related hazards and development of safety procedures” comes into effect, the authorized signatory covers it as well as the sequence on the approved plans, taking advice from technically competent persons, registered safety auditors and registered safety officers |
| Critical stages | Include the supervision plan’s critical stages in that same element’s programme when the element comes into effect |
| Training | Authorized signatory, representative, and technically competent persons T1 to T3 attend safety training; safety officers and safety supervisors attend technical training in building construction (Construction Industry Training Authority, Institutes of Vocational Education and professional bodies) |
| Audits and reviews | Registered safety auditors and safety review officers use the approved plans and the supervision plan when assessing the Safety Management System |

**SD takeaway:** Critical stages on the approved plans should be written so they can be dropped straight into the contractor’s inspection programme and method statements.
