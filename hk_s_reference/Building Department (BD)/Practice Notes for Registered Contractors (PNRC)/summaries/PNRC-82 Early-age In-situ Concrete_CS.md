# PNRC-82 Early-age In-situ Concrete
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue March 2022 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: governs early-age rebound hammer tests on in-situ reinforced concrete in superstructures that use different concrete grades, and the maturity-method alternative. It is not the general site-supervision list, which is Practice Note for Registered Contractors 77, and it is not imposed by this note on a structure that uses a single concrete grade.

## Regulatory Overview
Where the first approval of superstructure plans involves different concrete grades in the structure, a condition under item 6 of section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance requires rebound hammer tests on the higher-grade structural elements at an early age. For other projects already under way, the RSE and the registered contractor should advise their clients to consider the same enhanced supervision.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When the test is done, and who witnesses it (App. A paras 1–4)

Tests on the higher-grade elements are carried out between the seventh and tenth day after casting, to BS EN 12504-2:2021. If a public holiday prevents a test in that window, it is carried out on the following working day. The RSE selects the elements at random and sets the frequency at or above the table in paragraph 5. A grade T3 technically competent person in the RSE’s stream witnesses at least 10% of the tests conducted by the registered contractor. Every test is carried out by a grade T1 or above technically competent person in the contractor’s stream, trained in the use of rebound hammers, or the contractor may employ a laboratory accredited under the Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme. Records follow the Annex and are kept on site for inspection by the RSE’s technically competent person. Where vertical elements have kickers, the kickers are tested as well as the vertical elements.

**SD takeaway:** A two-grade superstructure needs rebound hammer tests on the higher-grade elements between day 7 and day 10, with the RSE’s T3 witnessing at least 10% of them.

### 2. Which elements, and the minimum frequency (App. A para 5)

| Structural concrete element | Minimum testing frequency |
|---|---|
| Vertical elements of higher concrete grade than the immediate supports at the base (pile cap, footing, raft foundation, transfer plate or transfer beam) | 50% |
| Vertical elements of higher concrete grade than the adjoining beams or slabs at the top | 10% |
| Transfer beams | 50% |
| Beams of higher concrete grade than adjoining beams or slabs on the same floor | 10% |

**SD takeaway:** Show the grade change on the structural zones: half of the higher-grade verticals at the base, half of the transfer beams, and one tenth of the higher-grade verticals and beams at the upper floor levels, are the minimum early-age test sets.

### 3. Alert levels and the maturity alternative (App. A paras 6–7)

In the absence of an engineering assessment, the empirical alert level is:

| Concrete | Alert level |
|---|---|
| With pulverized-fuel ash or ground granulated blastfurnace slag | Rebound hammer reading below 55% of the minimum characteristic strength of the concrete |
| Without pulverized-fuel ash or ground granulated blastfurnace slag | Rebound hammer reading below 65% of the minimum characteristic strength of the concrete |

Follow-up is to examine the approved plans, concrete order and delivery records, concreting and curing method, and site supervision records; to ascertain in-situ quality as early as practicable, for example by additional rebound hammer tests or concrete cores; to tighten supervision of similar elements on other floors; and, if necessary, to report to Buildings Department and suspend concreting until the follow-up is complete.

As an alternative to rebound hammer tests, early-age quality may be assessed by the maturity method on the seventh day after casting, to clause 11.7.5.4 of the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Concrete 2013. The number of measuring points and the alert level follow paragraphs 5 and 6.

**SD takeaway:** Set the early-age alert at 55% of the minimum characteristic strength where the mix contains pulverized-fuel ash or ground granulated blastfurnace slag, and at 65% where it does not, or use the maturity method on day 7 with the same sampling and the same alert.
