# PNRC-30 Suspended Working Platforms
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue October 1995 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Governs suspended working platforms — scaffolds or platforms hung from a building by lifting gear and raised or lowered by lifting appliances — including ropes, counterweights, outriggers and the mechanical and electrical gear. It is not the structural design of permanent building-maintenance-unit anchors, and the Factories and Industrial Undertakings (Suspended Working Platforms) Regulation remains the binding instrument.

## Regulatory Overview

The Factories and Industrial Undertakings (Suspended Working Platforms) Regulation applies to any industrial undertaking where a suspended working platform carries persons; except for section 17, it took effect on 1 July 1995, and section 17 took effect on 1 January 1996. Failures of suspension ropes and poor maintenance of operating mechanisms have caused serious and fatal accidents, so the note sets minimum deck geometry, rope and counterweight rules, and a test-and-marking regime.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Deck size and edge protection

The platform is of sound construction, adequately supported, properly maintained, of sufficient length, and at least **440 mm** wide. Sides have guard-rails between **900 mm and 1 150 mm** high and toe boards not less than **200 mm** high.

**SD takeaway:** A façade that will be maintained from a suspended platform needs a clear hanging zone wide enough for a deck of at least 440 mm that does not bear on the face.

### 2. Ropes, counterweights and who may ride

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Ropes | Only wire ropes for raising, lowering and suspension |
| Stand-off | Suspension points at an adequate horizontal distance from the face so the platform does not touch the building |
| Counterweight | On any outrigger, not less than **3 times** the weight needed to balance the load on the projecting part when the platform is fully loaded |
| Safety rope | Besides the two suspension ropes, a safety rope with an automatic safety device at each suspension point, to hold the platform if a suspension rope or the raise/lower mechanism fails |
| Erection, alteration, dismantling | Only under the supervision of a competent person |
| Workers | At least **18 years** old, and holding a certificate from training recognised by the Commissioner for Labour or provided by the manufacturer or its local agent |
| Harness | Every worker wears a safety harness with the lanyard secured to an independent lifeline or an anchorage and its fittings |

**SD takeaway:** Outrigger and counterweight space, plus an independent lifeline anchorage, are planned with the suspension points so the 3-times balance weight and the safety rope can actually be installed.

### 3. Test, display and safe working load

A competent person inspects the platform before use. A competent examiner carries out a load test and thorough examination before use, and again after substantial repair, re-erection, adjustment, failure, collapse, or weather likely to have affected stability. Periodic tests and examinations follow. The latest certificate or report is displayed prominently on the platform. The platform is marked with its safe working load, the maximum number of persons carried at one time, and a mark that distinguishes it from other similar platforms.

Further reading cited: Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance section 2 (industrial undertaking) and sections 6A and 6B (duties), and the Suspended Working Platforms Regulation.

**SD takeaway:** Each platform is a numbered, load-marked appliance with a current certificate on it; the maintenance strategy should assume re-test after every re-erection and after severe weather.
