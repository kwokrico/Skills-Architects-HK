# PNRC-17 Environmental Nuisance from Construction Sites
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue June 1991; last revision August 1997; this revision March 2026 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Checklist (Appendix A, revised March 2026) of practical controls for noise, air, site drainage, waste, roads, and hoardings, with the department that enforces each item. It is site-management advice tied to existing ordinances. If the project is a Schedule 2 designated project under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance, an Environmental Permit is obtained before construction and operation, and its conditions are followed. This note does not set a numeric footpath width or a hoarding width; those remain the hoarding plan and the excavation permit.

## Regulatory Overview

Registered contractors are urged to prevent or minimise environmental nuisance from construction activities to workers, adjoining occupants, and the public. Further guidance is from the departments named in the checklist, principally the Environmental Protection Department, Drainage Services Department, Food and Environmental Hygiene Department, Highways Department, Transport Department, and the Buildings Department.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Noise — permit hours and method choice

Prescribed construction works in this note are erection or dismantling of formwork or scaffolding; loading, unloading, or handling of rubble, wooden boards, steel bars, wood, or scaffolding material; and hammering.

| Activity | Control stated in the checklist |
|---|---|
| Percussive piling | A construction noise permit is required between **7:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m.** on any day other than a public holiday, and percussive piling is generally prohibited at other times |
| Powered mechanical equipment for general construction works, and/or prescribed construction works, in designated areas (predominantly built-up areas) | A construction noise permit is required during the restricted hours: **7:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.**, or at any time on a general holiday including Sunday |
| Offence | Carrying out those works without a valid permit, or breaching permit conditions, is an offence under the Noise Control Ordinance |

Planning advice in the checklist: use a quieter method (non-percussive piling instead of percussive piling) and quieter equipment; screen noisy machines; locate the noise source farther from noise-sensitive receivers; run machines only when necessary; schedule noisy work away from sensitive hours, including school examination periods; and limit concurrent noisy operations.

**SD takeaway:** Choose non-percussive piling and place noisy plant away from noise-sensitive receivers, because percussive piling needs a construction noise permit even in the daytime window and is generally prohibited outside 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m.

### 2. Dust, smoke, and notifiable works

Notify the Environmental Protection Department beforehand for site formation, reclamation, demolition of a building, tunnel works excluding any part beyond **100 metres** from the exit to the open air, construction of the foundation of a building, construction of the superstructure of a building, and road construction works. Carrying out those works without prior notification is actionable under the Air Pollution Control (Construction Dust) Regulation.

Dust reduction under the same Regulation is a separate duty. Open burning is actionable under the Air Pollution Control (Open Burning) Regulation. A specified process as defined in Schedule 1 of the Air Pollution Control Ordinance needs a licence. Demolition needs fans, catch platforms, and dust screens under the Building (Demolition Works) Regulations.

Site measures named for schematic layout: cover dusty materials and stockpiles; dampen them; hard-pave and water the main haul road; cover loads; control vehicle speed; wash wheels before vehicles leave; water the site; erect hoarding on the boundary; and, for concrete batching, locate it away from sensitive receivers and suppress dust.

**SD takeaway:** Allow haul-road paving, wheel washing, covered stockpiles, and boundary hoarding on the site plan, and notify the Environmental Protection Department before foundation, superstructure, demolition, or site formation, including tunnel works within 100 metres of the open-air exit.

### 3. Site water, drains, and toilets

| Risk | Measure in the checklist |
|---|---|
| Silt, mortar, or concrete blocking drains; muddy discharge; flooding | Temporary drainage with a de-silting facility before the main drainage system; sand trap, settling pit, or grease trap; perimeter channels or sandbags at the lower boundary; pH adjustment or a petrol interceptor as necessary; gravel on open site areas |
| Servicing and washdown | Vehicle and plant servicing, wash bays, and lubrication bays within roofed areas as far as possible; vehicle washdown on site |
| Stagnant water | Pump and drain it. Cover open stockpiles and temporarily exposed slopes, especially in the rainy season. Reuse water from the de-silting facility, for example for wheel washing |
| Old connections | Seal sewer and drainage connections before demolition or foundation works. Failure to seal them in demolition is actionable under the Building (Demolition Works) Regulations |
| Foundations | Monitor operations so drains and sewers are not damaged. Keep access to public manholes and drainage reserves clear of temporary structures |
| Surface water into foul sewers | Direct surface water to sand or silt removal before discharge to natural drainage channels or surface-water drains |
| Temporary toilets, and kitchens | Connect to a proper sewer. In an unsewered area, connect to a septic tank and soakaway, or to a sewage treatment plant for large flows |

Construction-site discharges are controlled under the Water Pollution Control Ordinance and must meet a valid licence. Discharge of waste or polluting matter into a water control zone, or into stormwater drains or watercourses, is an offence under that Ordinance.

**SD takeaway:** Set the site plan with de-silting before any connection to public drains, roofed wash and servicing bays, sealed abandoned connections before demolition or foundations, and toilets to a sewer or a septic tank rather than to a storm drain.

### 4. Public roads, footpaths, and hoardings

Excavation, including utility trenches, follows the excavation permit and the Excavation Permit Processing Manual. The checklist asks for the minimum footpath width for the pedestrian demand, and for lighting, signing, and guarding to the Code of Practice for the Lighting, Signing and Guarding of Road Works.

The construction or demolition vehicle run-in is to keep pedestrians safe, with Transport Department agreement to any temporary resiting or removal of traffic signs, and without an uneven walking surface where steel plates are used. Hoardings must not damage or obscure existing or proposed traffic signs and facilities. Footpath dumping is avoided by designating on-site areas for sorting and storage of construction waste.

Objects dropped from buildings: provide fans, catch platforms, and/or mesh screens, and clear them regularly.

Hoardings, covered walkways, and gantries also refer to PNAP APP-23. The checklist requires hoardings of the minimum width stipulated in the hoarding plan, a pedestrian passage that is not obstructed, and a site survey so taxi ranks, bus stops, pedestrian queuing areas, and crossings are considered in that plan and agreed by the Transport Department. Temporary pedestrian management is as agreed by the Transport Department and the Hong Kong Police Force.

**SD takeaway:** Fix the hoarding plan, including its stipulated width and the pedestrian provisions agreed with the Transport Department, together with a safe run-in and catch platforms, before the site boundary is locked.

### 5. Chemical waste

Where construction generates chemical waste, such as waste lubricating oil or waste lead-acid batteries, the checklist requires registration with the Environmental Protection Department as a chemical waste producer, proper packaging, labelling, and storage, and collection by a licensed chemical waste collector for disposal at a licensed facility. Improper handling is actionable under the Waste Disposal Ordinance and the Waste Disposal (Chemical Waste) (General) Regulation.

**SD takeaway:** Provide a labelled chemical-waste store and a licensed collector where the works will generate waste oil or waste batteries.
