# PNRC-21 Tropical Hardwood Timber
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued July 1992 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Buildings Ordinance Office encouragement to cut tropical hardwood timber in hoardings, covered walkways, temporary formwork, and internal finishes, while amendments to legislation and practice notes were still in preparation. This note records that lead and the specification options it asks contractors to adopt. It does not itself publish a banned-species list or a numeric timber cap.

## Regulatory Overview

The note states that Hong Kong is claimed to be one of the top ten importers of tropical timber, most of it used by the construction industry, and that a large proportion is discarded as construction waste. It asks the industry to change traditional methods that use hardwood, and says the Buildings Ordinance Office will consult the industry before finalising long-term changes to construction methods.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Hoardings and covered walkways

A large quantity of tropical timber is used for hoardings. Steel is identified as an alternative that can be reused; the Architectural Services Department and the Housing Department had already started to require steel hoardings on their sites, and private projects are encouraged to follow that example.

A covered walkway is described as not essential on every site, particularly when buildings are set back from the site boundary.

**SD takeaway:** Where the building is set back from the boundary, test whether a covered walkway is required at all, and specify steel hoardings where a hoarding is required.

### 2. Temporary formwork and the structural frame

Formwork is described as the largest single consumer of tropical timber on site. Before the next in-situ reinforced-concrete project, the note asks that these alternatives be considered:

| Alternative | Role named in the note |
|---|---|
| Profiled steel sheeting | Permanent formwork to support wet concrete, and reinforcement in the hardened concrete |
| Precast concrete units | In place of in-situ concrete |
| Structural steel | Building framework in place of reinforced concrete |
| Steel formwork | In place of timber formwork |
| Proprietary steel props | In place of timber props |

**SD takeaway:** At structural-system selection, prefer precast, structural steel, or steel formwork and steel props over timber formwork for in-situ reinforced concrete.

### 3. Finishes, waste, and renewable sources

Non-loadbearing built-in wall panels are often hardwood-framed; the note points to non-timber proprietary wall-panel systems. It also points to alternatives to hardwood for doors, door frames, skirtings, and architraves, especially where those fittings are painted.

Where hardwood is still used, contract specifications could require hardwood waste to be separated and reused rather than dumped with other rubbish. Where no alternative can be found, the specification could require hardwood timber to be obtained from renewable sources.

**SD takeaway:** Specify non-timber frames and painted-joinery alternatives, and where hardwood remains, require separation for reuse or supply from renewable sources.
