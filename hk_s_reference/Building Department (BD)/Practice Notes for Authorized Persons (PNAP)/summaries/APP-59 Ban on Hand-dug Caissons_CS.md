# APP-59 Ban on Hand-dug Caissons
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision February 1995 | Buildings Department (first issued May 1993; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Buildings Ordinance section 16(1A) refusal of plans that include a hand-dug caisson, and the only exemptions. A hand-dug caisson is any foundation or earth-retaining structure, or part of one, whose construction includes excavation of a shaft by a person digging inside the shaft, with or without machine tools.

## Regulatory Overview
The Buildings (Amendment) Ordinance 1995, enacted on 19 January 1995, bans hand-dug caissons, and from 1 February 1996 the Building Authority will not approve plans involving them unless the exceptional circumstances in section 16(1A) are shown to the Building Authority personally. Authorized persons and registered structural engineers are urged to avoid them.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. The only exemptions (paragraphs 3–5)
| Exemption | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | Depth does not exceed 3 metres, and the diameter of the inscribed circle is not less than 1.5 metres |
| (b) | For that site, a hand-dug caisson is the only practical construction method, or there is no other safe engineering alternative. Examples are narrow or steeply sloping sites where access or working space makes machine tools impossible or unsafe |
| If exempted | The method statement must specify stringent safety requirements, and the registered contractor must comply with them, including the Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations. See also the Hong Kong Institution of Engineers booklet "Guidance Notes on Hand-dug Caissons" and the Occupational Safety and Health Council "Code of Safe Working Practices for Hand-dug Caissons" |

**SD takeaway:** Do not design a hand-dug caisson unless it is no deeper than 3 m with an inscribed diameter of at least 1.5 m, or it is the only practical and safe method on that site and the Building Authority personally accepts it.
