# ADV-30

**Official title:** Provision of Mechanical Ventilation under Building (Planning) Regulation 34

**In plain words:** An office that cannot get adequate natural ventilation because of its position, level, or surroundings must have mechanical ventilation. This note adds a practical point: a large central plant serving many rooms should be designed with a building-services engineer. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Every office needs adequate lighting and ventilation under Building (Planning) Regulations 30 and 31.
- Where regulation 31 cannot be met because of position, level, or unsuitable surroundings, regulation 34(a) requires mechanical ventilation capable of supplying fresh air to all parts of the room at not less than 5 air changes per hour.
- For a large or complex central plant with ducts serving many rooms or floors, in order to comply with regulation 34(a), engage a registered professional engineer in the building-services discipline for design and installation advice.

**What to submit**
- No separate certificate in this note. For a complex central system, involve the building-services engineer, and be able to show at least 5 air changes per hour of fresh air to all parts of each office room that cannot meet regulation 31.

**Watch-outs**
- Claiming regulation 34 relief without showing 5 air changes per hour to all parts of the room.
- A complex ducted system with no building-services engineering input.
