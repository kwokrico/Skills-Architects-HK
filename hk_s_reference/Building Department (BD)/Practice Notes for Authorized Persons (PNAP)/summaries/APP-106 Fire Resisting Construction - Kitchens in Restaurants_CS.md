# APP-106 Fire Resisting Construction — Kitchens in Restaurants
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue October 1998; re-issued August 2009 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 231)

> Scope note: Fire resisting construction for a kitchen treated as ancillary to a restaurant, pending incorporation into the next edition of the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction. Paragraph 6 of that code may be read with this clarification. It is not a restaurant means-of-escape code.

## Regulatory Overview
A kitchen in a restaurant may be regarded as ancillary accommodation when assessing fire resisting construction under Part XV of the Building (Construction) Regulations. The enclosure, door, and lobby rules in paragraph 1 apply to that kitchen.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Kitchen enclosure (paragraph 1)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Walls | Fire resistance period of not less than 1 hour |
| Openings | Defended by a door having a fire resistance period of not less than 1/2 hour |
| Protected lobby | Between each kitchen door and any escape route from the main building |
| Large kitchen | Also a protected lobby between each kitchen door and the dining area where the kitchen usable floor area exceeds 45 m2 and the door opens onto an exit route of the dining area |

**SD takeaway:** Enclose the restaurant kitchen in 1-hour walls with 1/2-hour doors, and add a protected lobby once the kitchen exceeds 45 m2 and opens onto a dining exit route.

### 2. Hold-open doors (paragraph 2)
Hold-open devices for the kitchen doors may be installed subject to compliance with paragraph 17.1 of the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction.

**SD takeaway:** A hold-open kitchen door is only acceptable if it meets paragraph 17.1 of the Fire Resisting Construction code.
