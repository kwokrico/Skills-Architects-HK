# APP-110

**Official title:** Protective Barriers

**In plain words:** This note sets the height, solid toe, openings, and design information for protective barriers under Building (Construction) Regulations (B(C)R) sections 36, 37, 9, and 38, and Building (Planning) Regulation (B(P)R) 3A. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). Barrier shape still affects the edge of a balcony or roof.

**Numbers that matter**
- The barrier must be at least 1.1 m high, measured from the finished floor of any surface a person could step on. That surface is the adjoining floor level.
- The lowest 150 mm must be built solid, except on a staircase enclosed by walls and with no open stair-well. PNAP APP-119 is relevant to that exception.
- Openings must stop the passage of anything larger than 100 mm in its smallest dimension.
- The top of a curb does not count as the adjoining floor if the curb is higher than 500 mm. It also does not count, for something other than a railing, if the protruding width is less than 75 mm. For a railing with a curb under 500 mm, it does not count if the top of the lowest horizontal rail is 250 mm or less above the adjoining floor. It also does not count where there are only vertical balusters, the curb is 250 mm or less high, and the protruding width is less than 75 mm. Ignore any splay steeper than 45° when measuring protruding width.
- At the outer edge of an upper tier in a place of public entertainment, the horizontal surface must incline 30° toward the seating and be concave, or be formed in another way that lets only small articles through and stops a fall to the tiers below.
- Design loads are under B(C)R section 9. Glass barriers follow the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Glass 2018. Tempered glass, structural sealant, and spider fixings also follow PNAP APP-37.
- The barrier rules above do not apply to a roof or area that people enter only for maintenance. Follow the Access for External Maintenance 2021 Code of Practice and section 6 of the Occupational Safety and Health Regulation.
- Previously PNAP 235. First issued September 2000. Last revision June 2024. This revision January 2026.
- File reference: BD GR/1-55/71/2 (III).

**What to submit**
- Structural plans for barriers must show the frame and members, anchors and supports, design loads and standards, material specifications, workmanship for connections and protection, and calculations. The calculations must check the parent structure, adequacy and stability, member design, and deflection.
- As a shorter route, typical barrier details, layout, and general notes may go in the superstructure plans instead of a separate barrier submission. Location, layout, and elevation still go on the general building plans (GBP). The authorized person (AP) and registered structural engineer (RSE) must keep the two sets compatible. Use the checklist in Appendix B5 and the samples in Appendix B8 of PNAP ADV-33.
- A suitably qualified person must supervise the work on site.
- Some barrier minor works fall under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation (B(MW)R) and PNAP APP-147.

**Watch-outs**
- Measuring the 1.1 m from a curb that does not count as the adjoining floor leaves the barrier too low.
- A gap larger than 100 mm, or a missing solid 150 mm toe where the toe is required, does not comply.
- A glass barrier that skips the Glass Code or APP-37 does not comply.
- Barrier positions on the GBP must match the structural details.
