# APP-27

**Official title:** Gas Water Heaters — Regulation 35A of Building (Planning) Regulations

**In plain words:** Building (Planning) Regulations (B(P)R) regulation 35A requires a suitable opening for a room-sealed gas water heater in every bathroom, on a clear external wall or venting into a permanently open, well-ventilated balcony. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA); the openings do affect the facade, light wells, re-entrants, and bathroom layout, and an all-electric domestic building may omit them under the paragraph 12 undertakings.

**Numbers that matter**
- Usual opening: 320 mm wide by 420 mm high.
- Where the bathroom is only large enough for a shower: 240 mm by 240 mm.
- A non-standard opening is allowed only to suit a specific water-heater flue installed before completion. It must be possible to enlarge it later to the standard size, the provision must be clearly visible, and the enlargement must not cut reinforcing bars or move services.
- Clear space inside around the opening: 50 mm above, 100 mm on each side, and 150 mm below.
- Bottom of the opening at least 200 mm above the floor.
- At least 300 mm from any corner or other opening, including a window, vent, air-conditioning opening, or another aperture.
- Clear space in front at least 1500 mm to a facing wall, another part of the building, or the site boundary. If the boundary abuts a street, the opening may sit against that street.
- Do not put the opening directly under a fixed clothes-drying rack. Fit a flue-terminal guard if the heater has no overheating protector.
- Hot-water pipe lengths follow the Waterworks Regulations (Cap. 102A) regulation 19.
- In an enclosed space or light well, keep vertical airflow clear. Vents below the lowest opening: at least 0.05 m² per opening, and each vent at least 200 mm in each internal dimension. Plan dimension against height: at least 3 m for a building of 10 storeys or fewer, at least 4 m for 11 to 19 storeys, and at least 5 m for 20 storeys or more.
- Re-entrant: plan width at least 1.5 m. An obstruction at the open end, such as a tie beam, may cover at most 20% of the vertical open area. Keep vertical airflow clear.
- In a false ceiling or confined cavity: a quick-release access panel that needs no tools, non-adjustable louvres that are not easily blocked, and the manufacturer’s requirements.
- Seal an unused opening in easily removable plain concrete, brick, or block, and keep it clearly visible from inside, for example by a recess.
- Electrical and Mechanical Services Department (EMSD) code of practice GU03 covers domestic gas water heaters of 70 kW or less.

**What to submit**
- Show complying openings on the general building plans (GBPs), with the Appendix A location diagram.
- For an exemption, the authorized person (AP) gives the justifications, for example a design constraint, and the developer or owner gives a letter. The letter undertakes that (a) there will be no gas supply under the Gas Safety Ordinance (Cap. 51) to the domestic parts, (b) the deed of mutual covenant (DMC), or a sale and purchase agreement, assignment, or tenancy where there is no DMC, will effectively restrict gas supply and delivery of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) cylinders, other than disposable cassette-cooker cartridges carrying an EMSD GU mark, and (c) electric water heaters and electric or induction cookers will be installed in every domestic unit before the occupation permit (OP).

**Watch-outs**
- Openings too close to other openings or to corners.
- Light-well vents or plan width below the required size.
- A sealed opening that is not easy to see or to remove.
- An exemption without an enforceable no-gas control in the DMC or the sale documents, or without the electric appliances installed before the occupation permit.
