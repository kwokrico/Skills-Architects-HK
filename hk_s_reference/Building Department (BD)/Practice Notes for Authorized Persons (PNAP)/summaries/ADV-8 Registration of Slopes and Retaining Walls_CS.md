# ADV-8 Registration of Slopes and Retaining Walls
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision February 2002 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009; previously PNAP 168; first issue August 1994)

> Scope note: How completed permanent man-made slopes and retaining walls are entered in the Geotechnical Engineering Office Catalogue of Slopes. It does not set the structural design of the feature. Consequence-to-life categories are in PNAP 234, which this note cites.

## Regulatory Overview
The Geotechnical Engineering Office gives each sizable man-made slope and retaining wall a registration number and keeps the record in the Slope Information System. The authorized person or registered structural engineer responsible for site formation or feature upgrading submits the record of every completed permanent feature that is formed, modified, or changed in condition, within or next to the development, where the development owners have the maintenance responsibility.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is registrable (Appendices A and B)
| Type | Definition in this note |
| --- | --- |
| F | Fill slope or platform sloping at 15° or steeper, with a minimum fill thickness of 1 m, and with no associated retaining wall |
| C | Cut slope with no associated retaining wall |
| R | Retaining wall supporting a slope or platform whose surface slopes at less than 15°. Where a series of walls, each below registrable height, stands on an overall slope steeper than 45°, combine their heights |
| CR / FR | Cut or fill slope with an associated retaining wall at the crest, middle, or toe |
| DT | Disturbed terrain: repairs to landslide scars, or a series of composite cut and/or fill slopes, where the ground has been disturbed, the natural gradient is steeper than 15°, and the total height meets the registration criteria even if each piece does not |
| Height criteria | Cut slopes, including associated walls, and retaining walls, greater than 3 m high. Fill slopes, including associated walls, greater than 5 m high. Fill slopes, including associated walls, less than 5 m high that pose a direct risk to life, meaning Consequence-to-life Category 1 or 2 in PNAP 234 |

**SD takeaway:** A cut or a wall over 3 m, and a fill over 5 m, or a lower fill in Consequence-to-life Category 1 or 2, is a registrable feature and needs a maintenance owner.

### 2. What is not registered (Appendix B)
| Not registered | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Seawalls and marine structures | The water-facing side of a coastal structure |
| Dams and water-retaining structures | Fill embankments in front of a service reservoir or other water-retaining structure should still be registered |
| Basement walls and screen walls | Concrete walls that are part of a building, in contact with soil or rock, with the free-air side facing into the building |
| Tunnel and shaft linings | Free-standing tunnel portal slopes and retaining walls should be registered |
| Culverts and covered nullahs | Free-standing side walls of an open nullah, catchwater, or river bank that meet the height criteria are registrable |
| Bridge abutments | Fill embankments, reinforced fill, and slopes should be registered |
| Temporary features | Features under construction. An abandoned quarry face is registered as a cut, and a completed controlled tip as a fill |
| Walls monolithic with a building | Not registered. If such a wall is already in the catalogue, tell the Geotechnical Engineering Office |

**SD takeaway:** A basement wall that is part of the building is not a catalogue slope; a free-standing wall or a fill embankment beside it may still be.

### 3. What to submit, and when (paragraphs 3–6 and Appendices C and D)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| When | With the certificate of completion of the site formation works and/or the feature upgrading works |
| Drawings | A 1:1000 site plan, with a location plan, showing the boundary of each feature |
| Record | The Appendix C sheets: technical data, consequence-to-life category and the facilities at crest and toe with distances, ground-investigation and construction dates, designer, as-constructed drawing numbers, and dated photographs |
| Digital | A soft copy of the basic data in the Appendix D structure, and a digital copy of the photographs if possible. Map coordinates are on the 1980 datum |
| Return | The Geotechnical Engineering Office sends back a copy of the 1:1000 plan with the registered numbers of the newly formed or modified features |
| Owners | Advise the owners of those numbers with the maintenance details, and advise them to include the numbers in the Deed of Mutual Covenant |
| Maintenance responsibility | Normally from the Slope Maintenance Responsibility Information Centre of the Lands Department, or from the land title and lease clauses such as cutting-away clauses and green hatched black areas |

**SD takeaway:** Identify, on the site-formation drawings, every feature the owners will maintain, because the registration record and the Deed of Mutual Covenant both need its number.
