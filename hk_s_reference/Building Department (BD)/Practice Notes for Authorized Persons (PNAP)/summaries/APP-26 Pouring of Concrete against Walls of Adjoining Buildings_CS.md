# APP-26 Pouring of Concrete against Walls of Adjoining Buildings
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision May 1994 | Buildings Department (first issued February 1982; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Whether the wall of an adjoining building may be used as permanent shuttering. It does not permit that use as a default construction method.

## Regulatory Overview
Using the walls of adjoining buildings as permanent shuttering has caused portions of those walls to fail, with damage to property and injury to persons. The practice is not acceptable unless a physical check has confirmed that the wall is safe to be used that way.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Check, or independent shuttering (paragraphs 2–4)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Physical check | Confirm thickness, condition, and structural capability to withstand the load from the wet concrete and the tamping process |
| If stability is in question or cannot be verified | Provide independent shuttering so that no load is imposed on the wall. Alternatively, support the wall adequately and minimize the load |
| Failure | Unless evidence clearly indicates otherwise, total or partial failure is taken as negligence in the checks or the precautions, and as an offence under Buildings Ordinance section 40 |

**SD takeaway:** Detail independent shuttering against an adjoining wall unless a recorded check has proved that wall can take the wet-concrete and tamping load.
