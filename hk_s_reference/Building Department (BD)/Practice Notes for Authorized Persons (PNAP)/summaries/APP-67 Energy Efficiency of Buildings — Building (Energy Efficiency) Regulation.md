# APP-67

**Official title:** Energy Efficiency of Buildings — Building (Energy Efficiency) Regulation

**In plain words:** Commercial and hotel envelopes must meet overall thermal transfer value (OTTV) limits, and a real sunshade that helps meet those limits can be left out of gross floor area and, within a set projection, out of site coverage. Plans first submitted, or a major revision of general building plans, on or after 31 December 2025 must use the tighter limits.

**Numbers that matter**
- The OTTV Code of 1995 and the Energy Saving Plan 2015 to 2025 and beyond sit behind this note.
- Revised limits: tower not more than 20 W/m² (previously 21 W/m²); podium not more than 40 W/m² (previously 50 W/m²).
- Where site coverage varies under PNAP APP-132, the notional podium may be taken as 20 m above mean street level. You may set the line lower to simplify the calculation.
- Alteration and addition work is covered when (a) a horizontal or vertical extension is itself a new building, or (b) new external walls or facades cover the whole tower or the whole podium.
- An open-front shop on the ground floor may be left out of the OTTV if its usable floor area is 50 m² or less and its air-conditioning is separate from the main building system. If the usable floor area is over 50 m², a notional OTTV using a clear-glass shopfront may be used to show compliance.
- A genuine sunshade that helps reduce OTTV is not accountable for gross floor area. Under Building (Energy Efficiency) Regulation regulation 6, it is not included in site coverage if it projects not more than 1.5 m. If it projects more than 750 mm, provide a quantitative assessment. A projection of 750 mm or less is treated as not obstructing a prescribed window. Projection over a street is generally not allowed under Buildings Ordinance section 31(1). Exemptions are case by case.
- An innovative design that is as good as, or better than, the code on energy efficiency can be accepted. An effective innovative feature that reduces OTTV is not penalised in plot ratio or site coverage.
- The first general building plans need not include the OTTV information and calculations required by Building (Energy Efficiency) Regulation regulation 5.
- The revised 20 W/m² and 40 W/m² standards apply to a new general building plan, or a major revision of one, for a development or alteration and addition, submitted for approval on or after 31 December 2025.

**What to submit**
- After approval, and before consent, submit the OTTV Summary Sheet (Appendix A) under Building (Administration) Regulations regulation 10.
- Before the occupation permit, or with Form BA14, finalise the OTTV figures and the glass shading coefficient (SC) on the general building plans for record. Submit the OTTV Report under regulation 10: Forms OTTV1 to OTTV4, record plans, material test certificates and specifications, and the final Summary Sheet.
- If a material is not in the code, document its source and show it is suitable locally.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not use the old 21 W/m² and 50 W/m² limits on a submission on or after 31 December 2025.
- Do not claim the gross-floor-area or site-coverage exclusion for a sunshade unless it genuinely helps the OTTV. If it projects more than 750 mm, do not skip the quantitative assessment.
- Do not apply for an occupation permit without the final OTTV Report and record plans.
- Do not leave an open-front shop of more than 50 m² out of the calculation without the notional clear-glass check.
