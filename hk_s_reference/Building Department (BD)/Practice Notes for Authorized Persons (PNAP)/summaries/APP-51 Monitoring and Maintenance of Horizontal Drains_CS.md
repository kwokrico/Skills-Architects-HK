# APP-51 Monitoring and Maintenance of Horizontal Drains
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Issued November 1990 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Monitoring and maintenance of horizontal drains that are installed to lower groundwater so that a slope meets the factor of safety in Tables 5.1 and 5.4 of the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes, second edition. It amplifies Chapters 4 and 11 of that manual. Short drains that only tap individual rock joints are not the main subject, but they still need regular inspection. In Scheduled Area No. 1, sections 28A to 28D apply as well.

## Regulatory Overview
Outside Scheduled Area No. 1, horizontal drains that need specific long-term maintenance will be approved, where there is a significant risk to life, only if a positive undertaking is given that the maintenance will be carried out. Because ownership of a private development usually changes, such an undertaking is envisaged only in rare cases, such as a major private body developing for its own purposes where ownership will not change.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Monitoring before and after installation (Appendix A, paragraphs 5–13)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Instruments | The designer sets the type, number, and location of piezometers and the method of measuring drain flow, and allows for replacement of defective instruments. Install piezometers early, preferably with the ground investigation. Open hydraulic piezometers are commonly used for long-term observation (Geoguide 2, section 20.2.3). Halcrow buckets record the highest level since the last reading |
| Frequency | At least once every two months in the dry season and weekly in the wet season, the wet-season reading within two days of a heavy rainstorm, for example rainfall greater than 100 mm in 24 hours. All drains and piezometers on the site are monitored |
| Before design is fixed | Preferably two wet seasons. If that is not possible, use the results obtained while tender documents are prepared to review the design, and establish base groundwater levels in the wet and dry seasons before the drains are installed |
| What "effective" means, in descending order | Piezometric levels after installation do not rise above the design level in heavy rainstorms; the base groundwater level is lower than before installation; the storm-response range is lower; the level falls faster after a storm and returns to normal within a few days |
| When monitoring stops | Aim to assess effectiveness before the end of the contract maintenance period. Continue if the criteria are not met, and continue through any remedial works until the designer is satisfied |

**SD takeaway:** Do not adopt long horizontal drains as the way to reach the slope factor of safety unless two wet seasons of piezometer records can be programmed, and readings continue weekly in the wet season after rain over 100 mm in 24 hours.

### 2. Maintenance (Appendix A, paragraphs 14–17)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Cleaning | Flush with a clean water jet under controlled pressure, from the deep end toward the outlet, until the outflow is clean. Brush if dried soil deposits are present. Replace removable inner liners if blocked or if flows are substantially reduced |
| Programme | At least once during the contract maintenance period, then once a year: inspect drains and surroundings, remove weeds, clear outlets, and flush. The designer hands the programme and as-built details to the maintenance authority |
| Leak check | If flow increases sharply, test the discharge and inspect for leaking services, and notify the appropriate authority |
| Difficult access | Permanently installed piezometers may be used to decide when to flush, but outlets are still inspected and cleared regularly. A suitably experienced maintenance officer examines the system at the annual maintenance |

**SD takeaway:** Hand the maintenance authority an as-built drain plan and an annual flushing programme before relying on the drains for long-term slope stability.
