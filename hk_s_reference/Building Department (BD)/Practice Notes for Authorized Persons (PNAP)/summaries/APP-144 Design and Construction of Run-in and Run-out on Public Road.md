# APP-144

**Official title:** Design and Construction of Run-in and Run-out on Public Road

**In plain words:** This note sets Highways Department standards and a self-certification path for a vehicular run-in and run-out on a public road. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly PNAP 300. First issue September 2006. Effective 1 January 2007.
- Match the adjoining footpath: concrete with concrete; pavers with pavers, with a visual contrast or pattern change.
- Follow Highways Department standard drawings and the General Specification (sections 9 to 11 and related sections). Concrete drawings H1113 to H1116. Paver drawings H1103, H5101, H5102, and H5114 to H5116.
- Use the saw-cut method. Damage outside the run-in needs a separate Highways Department Excavation Permit and reinstatement.
- Protect utilities to the minimum cover in Highways Department Technical Circular 3/90, or its update.
- Before the occupation permit (early submission is encouraged): Certificate of Completion of Vehicular Run-in and Run-out (Appendix B). The authorized person signs Part A and the registered contractor signs Part B. Copy the Highways Department, with a block plan.
- The Buildings Department notifies the Highways Department for inspection and takeover. Defects may be grounds to refuse the occupation permit under Buildings Ordinance section 21(6)(a).
- For Highways Department entrusted works: an entrustment letter and a fee receipt. A temporary run-in may allow occupation permit processing before the permanent works are finished.

**What to submit**
- Design details on the general building plans for circulation to the Highways Department. Arrange utility diversion and the Excavation Permit early.
- Appendix B completion certificate (authorized person Part A, registered contractor Part B), copied to the Highways Department with a block plan, before the occupation permit.
- For entrusted works, the entrustment letter and fee receipt.

**Watch-outs**
- An incomplete or defective run-in at occupation permit stage can draw a Highways Department objection and a refusal of the occupation permit.
- Damaging the adjoining pavement without a separate excavation permit is not acceptable.
