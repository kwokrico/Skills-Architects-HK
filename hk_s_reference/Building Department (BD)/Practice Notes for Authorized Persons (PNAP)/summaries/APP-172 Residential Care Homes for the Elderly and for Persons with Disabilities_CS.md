# APP-172 Residential Care Homes for the Elderly and for Persons with Disabilities
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue April 2024 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Modifications under the Buildings Ordinance so that a residential care home for the elderly or for persons with disabilities may be treated as non-domestic for plot ratio, site coverage, and open space, and a time-limited higher plot ratio under two incentive schemes. The schemes themselves are Lands Administration Office Practice Notes Issue Nos. 5/2023 and 10/2023, and the Social Welfare Department guidance notes. Gross floor area calculation guidance is in the annexes of those notes. It is not a care-home licensing code.

## Regulatory Overview
These homes are domestic uses under the Buildings Ordinance because they are for habitation, but the Building Authority may modify that treatment for regulations 19, 20, 21, 22, and 25 of the Building (Planning) Regulations if the criteria in paragraph 2 are met. A higher plot ratio for eligible premises is available only during the pilot period of the two incentive schemes, and only in new developments.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Treating the premises as non-domestic (paragraph 2)
The modification covers a building or part constructed or intended as such a home, for regulations 19, 20, 21, and 22, and allows non-provision of open space under regulation 25, if all of the following are met:

| Criterion | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Social Welfare Department | No adverse comments, including on access for ambulant residents |
| Fire Services Department | No objection |
| Buildings Ordinance | Compliance on all aspects, including structural suitability, fire safety, adequate headroom, and natural lighting and ventilation |
| Building (Planning) Regulations 49(1) and 49B | Complied with. Genuine hardship may be considered on the special circumstances |
| Care-home regulations | Section 20 of the Residential Care Homes (Elderly Persons) Regulation (Cap. 459A) or section 21 of the Residential Care Homes (Persons with Disabilities) Regulation (Cap. 613A). Not located in the basement |
| Industrial building | The premises are not situated in an industrial building |

**SD takeaway:** A care home treated as non-domestic still needs natural light and ventilation, must not be in a basement or an industrial building, and needs no adverse comment from Social Welfare or Fire Services.

### 2. Higher plot ratio in the pilot schemes (paragraphs 3–6)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Accountability | The homes are accountable for gross floor area under the Building (Planning) Regulations |
| Higher plot ratio | During the pilot period, the Building Authority is prepared to permit a higher plot ratio to accommodate the additional gross floor area of eligible premises, taking account of Planning Department advice on planning intention and whether the Outline Zoning Plan or planning approval is complied with |
| Eligibility | The premises fulfil the parameters of the relevant incentive scheme and the criteria in paragraph 2 |
| Paragraph 2 measures | Apply to all new developments and to alteration and addition proposals for new or additional homes in an existing building |
| Paragraph 4 measure | Only for premises in new building developments eligible for the two incentive schemes |

**SD takeaway:** Use the non-domestic treatment for a new home in an existing building, and reserve the extra plot ratio for a new development inside the pilot schemes.
