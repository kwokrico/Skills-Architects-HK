# ADV-14

**Official title:** Facilities for External Inspection and Maintenance of Buildings

**In plain words:** Design safe permanent access to the outside walls before the building is built, so later cleaning, repainting, and repair do not depend on ad-hoc scaffolds. A gondola on the roof is strongly recommended, including on a residential building. A gondola enclosure may be left out of the building-height calculation if it is no bigger than it needs to be. That is a height point, under Planning Department and Lands Department practice. It is not an automatic disregard from gross floor area (GFA) under Building (Planning) Regulation 23(3). Check the lease and the outline zoning plan for the site.

**Numbers that matter**
- Provide permanent safe access to external walls for drainage, window cleaning, repainting, overhaul of finishes, and repair of the fabric.
- A permanent suspended-access system, or gondola, is strongly recommended at the roof of a new building, including residential buildings. Installation, use, and maintenance follow ADV-11. The Construction Industry Council has identified a gondola as a desirable safety feature.
- Planning Department: a gondola enclosure may be treated as a rooftop utility structure and exempted from the height-restriction calculation if its size is the minimum needed for the function. In general, a rooftop utility structure should not exceed 15 m above the main roof, or 10% of the building height, whichever is less. Also respect height limits on the statutory town plan, and the preservation of ridgeline views from vantage points.
- Lands Department: comply with any building-height restriction in the lease. A gondola enclosure may be exempted from the lease height calculation if its size is not excessive, as a rooftop utility structure.
- Buildings Department, structure: supporting elements comply with the Buildings Ordinance, the Building (Construction) Regulations, and the relevant codes of practice. Design every support, member, and fixing for all load combinations of the system, and obtain Building Authority approval. Use corrosion-resistant materials, or protect them against corrosion. Restrain the system against undue sway on every part of the facade exposed to wind. Provide convenient and safe access to the system.
- If there is no permanent suspended access, consider mechanical booms, or roof anchorages so a suspended working platform can be installed later, plus permanent hooks, bolts, holes, or platforms for temporary scaffolding. All of these need corrosion resistance or protection.
- Cast-in anchors for personal fall-protection are advised on new buildings. Follow the Construction Industry Council “Technical Guidelines on the Design, Installation and Maintenance of Cast-in Anchors at External Walls of New Buildings” (hkcic.org).
- Give special attention to lightwells and re-entrants where drainage and service pipes are placed. Access to common maintenance facilities should preferably be through common areas, or through areas the deed of mutual covenant designates for inspection and maintenance. The lowest level of a re-entrant or lightwell that houses a soil or waste pipe or stack should be a common area, with access, including a cat-ladder where that is appropriate, for maintenance and for clearing refuse.
- Roof tracks, davits, counterweight rooms, cast-in anchors, and cat-ladder access can affect roof-plant floor area, what is designated as common area, and the deed of mutual covenant. Making the lowest level of a re-entrant or lightwell a common area affects what can be sold and what stays common. That is an ownership split, not a GFA concession.

**What to submit**
- Structural supports and fixings of a gondola system need Building Authority approval under the Buildings Ordinance and the Building (Construction) Regulations.
- Check the outline zoning plan and any height limit with the Planning Department, and the lease height with the Lands Department, before you claim a rooftop-utility exemption.
- Show re-entrant and lightwell maintenance access on the general building plans, and as common areas in the deed of mutual covenant.

**Watch-outs**
- A residential tower with no gondola and no roof-anchorage strategy leaves later facade workers on ad-hoc scaffolds, with a higher fall risk.
- A gondola enclosure that fails the Planning Department test of 15 m or 10%, whichever is less, or the Lands Department “not excessive” test, is a height breach.
- Gondola supports installed without Building Authority approval, or without corrosion protection, or without restraint against facade sway.
- Soil and waste stacks in a private re-entrant, with no common access, create a maintenance deadlock and disputes about illegal entry. That is contrary to this note.
