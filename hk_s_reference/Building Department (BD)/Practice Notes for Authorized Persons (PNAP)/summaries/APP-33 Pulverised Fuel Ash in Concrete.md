# APP-33

**Official title:** Pulverised Fuel Ash in Concrete

**In plain words:** Pulverised fuel ash (PFA) may replace part of the cement in concrete when the mix standard, the percentage, and the site control all meet the conditions below. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Pulverised fuel ash added as a separate constituent is used only with ordinary Portland cement (OPC), and it complies with BS 3892: Part 1:1982. The maximum water-requirement criterion in that standard may not apply.
- Blended cement that already contains pulverised fuel ash complies with BS 6588:1985, and the nominal ash content is 25% or less. Further pulverised fuel ash is not added on top of that blended cement.
- Ash content at most 35% by mass of the cementitious content (ordinary Portland cement plus pulverised fuel ash). 25% is the usual figure for normal works. More than 25% is for special uses, such as marine work or a massive pour, and it needs expert advice and strict site control.
- If the ash content is more than 25%, the authorized person (AP) and the registered structural engineer (RSE) must be satisfied that there is no adverse effect from striking the formwork, from creep, or from long-term deflection.
- Early strength may be lower than an ordinary Portland cement mix. Effective curing is essential, especially in cold weather.

**What to submit**
- State the pulverised fuel ash content, and the extent of its use, on the structural plans and specifications.
- The authorized person, the registered structural engineer, and the registered contractor (RC) ensure the supplier’s quality control, and the authorized person and registered structural engineer are told of any deviation.

**Watch-outs**
- Ash content above 35%.
- Counting pulverised fuel ash twice, by adding it as well as using a blended cement that already contains it.
- Going above 25% without dealing with formwork striking, creep, and deflection.
- Curing that is too weak, so the concrete underperforms.
