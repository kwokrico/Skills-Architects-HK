# APP-51

**Official title:** Monitoring and Maintenance of Horizontal Drains

**In plain words:** Long horizontal drains that lower groundwater pressure, so a slope meets its required factor of safety, must be checked and cleaned for as long as the slope relies on them. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- These steps are for drains designed so the factor of safety in Tables 5.1 and 5.4 of the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes is achieved. Other drain systems still need regular inspection and maintenance, but they need not follow this programme.
- Measure piezometer water levels and drain flow rates for every drain and piezometer on the site: at least once every two months in the dry season, weekly in the wet season, and within two days of a heavy rainstorm (for example rainfall over 100 mm in 24 hours).
- Prefer monitoring that covers two wet seasons before the design is finished. Set wet-season and dry-season base groundwater levels before the drains are installed.
- Judge performance in this order. (a) After installation, piezometer levels must not rise above the design level in a heavy rainstorm. (b) The base groundwater level should be lower than before the drains were installed. (c) The up-and-down range in a storm should be smaller than before. (d) The level should fall faster after rain and return to normal within a few days.
- Maintain at least once during the Contract Maintenance period, then once a year. Flush with a clean water jet under controlled pressure, and use a brush if deposits have dried. Clean from the deep end toward the outlet until the outflow is clean. Inspect the surroundings, remove weeds, and clear the outlets. Replace a removable inner liner if it is blocked or flow has reduced.
- In Scheduled Area No. 1 (the Mid-levels), maintenance is also controlled under Buildings Ordinance (BO) sections 28A to 28D.

**What to submit**
- At design stage, set the instrument scheme: type, number and location of piezometers, and how flow will be measured. Open hydraulic piezometers are common. Halcrow buckets are often used. Allow for instrument maintenance and replacement.
- The designer prepares the maintenance programme and as-built details and hands them to the maintenance authority.
- Appoint a maintenance officer with suitable experience to keep inspection records.
- Outside Scheduled Area No. 1, drains that need specific long-term maintenance, where risk to life is significant, are approved only with a positive undertaking that maintenance will keep them effective. Acceptable undertakings are rare. One example is a major private body developing for its own use, where ownership is not expected to change.
- Start monitoring again if groundwater changes substantially (a large change in flow, or new seepage at the surface).

**Watch-outs**
- Do not rely on these drains without a credible long-term maintenance undertaking where risk to life is significant.
- Do not stop monitoring before effectiveness is fully assessed. Continue monitoring after remedial works.
- Nearby development or pumping can change the groundwater regime. Do not ignore it.
- If flow increases a lot and you cannot explain it, test the discharge and inspect the area for leaking services. Notify the appropriate authority.
- The designer must stay closely involved in monitoring. The maintenance authority must alert the designer if the system is not performing.
- Drains clog from plant roots, fungi and algae, fine soil, and precipitates of calcium, magnesium, iron and other compounds.
- On an extensive natural hillside where outlets are hard to reach, piezometer monitoring may be enough, with flushing only if the readings show it is needed. Routine outlet inspection and clearing is still required.
