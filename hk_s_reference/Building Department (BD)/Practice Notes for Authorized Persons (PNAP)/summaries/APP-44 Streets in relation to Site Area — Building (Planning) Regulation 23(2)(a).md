# APP-44

**Official title:** Streets in relation to Site Area — Building (Planning) Regulation 23(2)(a)

**In plain words:** Where new streets cut a large site into separate sites, and enforceable lease or planning controls keep the open space, the Building Authority (BA) may allow a higher plot ratio on another site in the same project. This is not a general gross floor area (GFA) concession; it is a redistribution of plot ratio across the sites, with consequential adjustments to the regulations.

**Numbers that matter**
- Building (Planning) Regulations (B(P)R) regulation 23(2)(a) can treat land divided by a new street as separate sites. That can prevent a consolidated layout, and plot ratio can be lost if one of the island sites is taken up by a large park.
- The Buildings Ordinance has no explicit mechanism for transferring plot ratio. An uncontrolled transfer would risk later redevelopment of the park site, overloading the neighbourhood and its infrastructure.
- Where lease controls, a master layout plan, and/or conditions of a permission under section 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance can be enforced, the Building Authority may, on Form 29 and on special circumstances, allow a higher plot ratio on another site within the large project, and the consequential regulation adjustments, if the overall planning is improved and the specific planning requirements are not significantly impaired.
- If that higher plot ratio is granted, the Building Authority will oppose a later change to the control drawings, the master layout plan, or the town-planning permission that would undo the benefit of the Form 30 permission.

**What to submit**
- Form 29, citing the special circumstances and enclosing the lease, master layout plan, or section 16 controls that can be enforced.
- Form 30 is the permission, where it is granted.

**Watch-outs**
- Seeking a plot-ratio transfer without enforceable planning controls outside the building plans.
- Later amending the master layout plan or the section 16 permission so that the park site is developed and the concession is negated.
