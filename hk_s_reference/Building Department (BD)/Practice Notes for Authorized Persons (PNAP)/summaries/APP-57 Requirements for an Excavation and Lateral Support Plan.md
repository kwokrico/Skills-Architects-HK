# APP-57

**Official title:** Requirements for an Excavation and Lateral Support Plan

**In plain words:** A deep excavation that can affect a road, building, steep slope or water main needs an approved excavation and lateral support (ELS) plan before consent, and it affects basement or podium feasibility and monitoring cost. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- Building (Administration) Regulations (B(A)R) regulation 8(1)(bc). Design notes are in Appendix A, August 2011.
- Plans are required when the excavation is deeper than 2.5 m and longer than 5 m, and it is liable to affect any road, building, structure, slope steeper than 30 degrees, or water main of 75 mm diameter or more, inside the area set by a 45-degree line drawn up from the base of the excavation to the ground surface.
- The registered structural engineer (RSE) prepares and signs the plans, the structural design, and the structural assessment of excavation and dewatering effects on adjoining structures. If the excavation is deeper than 4.5 m, the registered geotechnical engineer (RGE) prepares and signs the geotechnical assessment, details, calculations and ground-investigation reports that go with the submission.
- Use either a global factor of safety (unfactored soil strength and loads, checked against Building (Construction) Regulations regulation 15) or the limit-state partial-factor method in CIRIA C580 as modified by Appendix A. Do not mix the two methods. State the method on the plans and calculations.
- Both methods: check hydraulic failure to Geotechnical Control Office Publication 1/90 section 6.2.4, with a minimum factor of safety of 1.5.
- Limit-state partial factors do not apply if the wall embedment is entirely in soft clay. Use at least the partial factors and surcharge in Geoguide 1 (2nd edition). For the structure, use a minimum factor of 1.4, not 1.35, on the serviceability-limit-state forces and moments from C580. Check the ultimate limit state by limit equilibrium or soil-structure interaction. You do not need a separate global factor of safety of 2.0 for overturning or kick-out if you follow the ultimate-limit-state checks in the appendix. For a singly propped wall, test prop level by plus or minus 0.5 m, and test both full surcharge and no surcharge. Assess progressive failure and how you will limit it. On completion, carry out the performance review in PNAP APP-115.
- A computer program needs prior acceptance under PNAP ADM-6. The same software needs a separate acceptance for each design method.

**What to submit**
- Consent for the excavation and lateral support works is not given unless the required plans are submitted and approved.
- Stage 1: the lateral support system (for example sheet piles or a diaphragm wall), the strutting layout, the sequence, the geotechnical documents, and realistic ground-movement estimates for the whole of the works. Stage 2: structural details of the supports and struts, matching the approved Stage 1.
- A design report, certified by the RSE and the RGE, stating that there is an adequate margin against collapse and no unacceptable impact if the assumptions are verified and the works are built as specified. List the assumptions, the monitoring ranges and the critical procedures. Give a copy to the site supervision personnel.
- Minimum monitoring: ground settlement and heave, and piezometric pressures. Also monitor deflection of vertical members where there is a significant risk to life or property.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not start a deep excavation that meets these tests without an approved excavation and lateral support plan.
- Do not omit the RGE where the excavation is deeper than 4.5 m.
- Do not mix the global factor of safety and the limit-state partial-factor method in one design.
- Do not use a computer program without prior Buildings Department acceptance for the method you have chosen.
- Do not skip the progressive-failure check for a lost strut.
