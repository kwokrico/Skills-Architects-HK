# APP-29

**Official title:** Lift and Escalator Installations — Building Works Requirements

**In plain words:** Building works for lifts and escalators must follow the 2011 Lift Code, including strength of fixings, ventilation, pits, stairs, and partitions, before the installation is put into service. This note does not give a gross floor area (GFA) concession; it does affect lift-well ventilation, pit depth, machine-room stairs, partition walls, and lobby clearances.

**Numbers that matter**
- Primary standard: Code of Practice for the Design and Construction of Buildings and Building Works for the Installation and Safe Use of Lifts and Escalators 2011 (the Lift Code). Another standard is accepted only if it is shown to be equivalent to the satisfaction of the Building Authority (BA). Building (Construction) Regulations (B(C)R) regulation 9A applies. Electrical, mechanical, and operational rules are in the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department (EMSD) codes under Cap. 618.
- August 2015 amendments (Appendix A): inspection traps follow the dual-lock or single-lock rules. An emergency door has dual key-operated locks from the outside and can be opened from the inside without a key. A warning figure at least 100 mm high must be placed beside the keyholes. Exceptions: landing doors, and a sill at least 1 m above the adjoining floor, which may use a single designated key.
- September 2019 amendments (Appendix B), for plans on or after 1 December 2019. Ventilation at the top of the well: at least 1% of the horizontal cross-section, and at least 0.3 m² net free area per well. A common well serving 4, 5, or 6 lifts: 0.4 m², 0.5 m², or 0.6 m². Ferrules at least 50 mm above the slab. Full-height partitions between lifts through the whole well.
- A pit access door is mandatory if the pit is deeper than 2 m. The door is solid (imperforate), does not open into the well, is lockable, and carries a durable bilingual notice with lettering at least 25 mm high. If the pit is deeper than 1.6 m but not deeper than 2 m, provide that door where the layout allows.
- Permanent fall-arrest anchorages at the lowest landing, between 1.5 m and 1.8 m above finished floor level (AFFL). They are not required if there is a complying pit access door.
- Stairs where the difference in level is more than 0.6 m: width at least 900 mm, tread at least 225 mm, riser at most 175 mm, at most 16 steps in a flight, handrails on both sides, and edge barriers at least 1.1 m high.
- Metal supports or hooks for hoisting in the machine rooms and on the lift-well ceilings, and durable embossed notices.
- Works often still incomplete (Appendices C and D) include permanent doors and notices, lighting, partitions, pit waterproofing, ventilation, isolation switches, escalator headroom of at least 2.3 m, unrestricted landing areas, and obstruction guards.

**What to submit**
- Design the building and structural plans to B(C)R regulation 9A and the Lift Code. The authorized person (AP) and the registered structural engineer (RSE) must ensure adequate strength for guide-rail brackets, the lift machine, and deflector sheave brackets, and machine rooms, pulley rooms, and lift wells that can resist the normal loads and forces.
- Complete all of the associated building works before the application to the Director of Electrical and Mechanical Services (DEMS) under Cap. 618 to put the lifts or escalators into service.

**Watch-outs**
- A DEMS application to put lifts or escalators into service while building works on the Appendix C or D lists are still incomplete.
- Well vents that are too small.
- Missing full-height partitions between lifts.
- A pit deeper than 2 m without a complying access door.
- Durable bilingual notices not provided.
- Fixing details for rails, machines, or sheaves that are not strong enough.
