# APP-111

**Official title:** Design of Car Parks and Loading/Unloading Facilities

**In plain words:** This note sets stall sizes, aisles, driveways, ramps, and clearances so that a car park or loading bay is convenient and safe, with the Building Authority (BA) checking the layout and the Commissioner for Transport (C for T) advising on road safety, traffic management, and run-ins. Floor space is left out of gross floor area (GFA) under Building (Planning) Regulation (B(P)R) 23(3)(b) and PNAP APP-2 only if the BA is satisfied with the car park design, and this note does not itself change site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Stall sizes in Table 1, length by width by headroom, in metres. Motorcycles: 2.4 long, with a minimum of 2, by 1 wide, by 2.4 headroom. Private cars and taxis: 5 by 2.5 by 2.4. Coaches and buses: 12 by 3.5 by 3.8. Light goods vehicles: 7 by 3.5 by 3.6. Medium and heavy goods vehicles: 11 by 3.5 by 4.7. Containers: 16 by 3.5 by 4.7. Light buses: 8 by 3 by 3.3. Disability stalls: 5 by 3.5 by 2.4. Align these with the latest Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines, Chapter 8, Table 11.
- Desirable one-way aisle widths for private cars: 3 m at 0°, 3 m at 30°, 3.6 m at 45°, 4.2 m at 60°, 4.7 m at 70°, and 5.3 m at 80°. At 90°, 6 m is desirable, 5.5 m is the minimum, and 5.0 m is the absolute minimum.
- Two-way aisle at 90°: 7.5 m desirable, 6.0 m minimum, and 5.5 m absolute minimum.
- Residential driveway, one-way: 5.5 m desirable, 4.0 m minimum, and 3.0 m absolute minimum, and the absolute minimum is for cars only. Two-way: 7.3 m, 5.5 m, and 5.0 m.
- Industrial or mixed driveway, one-way: 6.0 m, 5.5 m, and 3.5 m. Two-way: 10.5 m, 7.3 m, and 6.0 m.
- Straight ramp gradient for cars: 1:10 desirable, 1:7 maximum, and 1:6 absolute. For goods vehicles: 1:10 and 1:8. Helical ramp for cars: 1:12.5 and 1:8. For goods vehicles: 1:12.5 and 1:10.
- Vertical clearance over a driveway, for all vehicles, is at least 5.1 m. It may be lower if the vehicles using it are limited. Horizontal clearance is 500 mm, or at least 200 mm from a railing or sign post if 500 mm is impracticable.
- Widen a bend where the table requires it. One stated case: a driveway not wider than 6 m, with a radius under 18 m, needs 1.2 m of extra width. The rest of the cases are in Table 2 of the note.
- For a multi-storey car park ramp used by cars or taxis: a straight one-way ramp is at least 3.0 m wide; a helical one-way ramp is 3.65 m; a two-way straight ramp is 6.0 m, or 5.5 m minimum, with no central kerb. Horizontal clearance is 300 mm on a straight ramp and 600 mm on a helical ramp. The minimum inner radius at a bend is 5.5 m. The outer radius of a one-way helical ramp is 9.0 m. Two-way ramps are not recommended.

**What to submit**
- Show stall sizes, loading and unloading bays, and run-ins and run-outs. Show ramp gradients, radii, swept paths, and turning circles. Note the standards used.
- Highlight every deviation, justify it with swept paths or other data, and put right anything the BA asks to be put right in a later submission. Use an absolute minimum only in exceptional circumstances, and give a full justification.
- The C for T still advises on run-ins and traffic. The BA checks the general car-park standard.

**Watch-outs**
- A poor layout can put the APP-2 car-park GFA disregard at risk.
- A deviation from Appendix A is generally not treated as a fundamental reason to disapprove the plans, but it must be justified and corrected later if the BA says so.
- A goods bay needs room to reverse and manoeuvre, about one vehicle length. A hammerhead is preferred. Use a turntable only where the site is tight.
- Do not let cycle or motorcycle parking conflict with the run-in, and keep those users segregated where the layout requires it.
