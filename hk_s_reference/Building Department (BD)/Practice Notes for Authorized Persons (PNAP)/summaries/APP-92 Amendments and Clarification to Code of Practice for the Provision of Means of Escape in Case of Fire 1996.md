# APP-92

**Official title:** Amendments and Clarification to Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire 1996

**In plain words:** This note replaces paragraph 14(3)(b) of the 1996 Means of Escape (MOE) Code on dead-end travel. It does not change gross floor area or site coverage, but the 18 m limit still shapes corridors and rooms.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issued August 1997. Re-issued August 2009. The change was to be included in the next reprint.
- Where travel from a room’s exit door to a staircase, or to a street or open area at ground level under paragraph 8.2, is possible in one direction only, no point in the room may be more than 18 m from an exit, or from a point where travel can go in different directions to 2 or more exits.
- The 18 m is the sum of direct distance and travel distance.
- Once a two-direction point is reached, the distance from there to one of the exits must still stay within the direct-distance and travel-distance limits in paragraph 14.

**What to submit**
- Use this amended paragraph 14(3)(b) in the general building plan (GBP) and MOE calculations for schemes under the 1996 MOE Code.

**Watch-outs**
- The 18 m is not travel distance alone. It is direct distance plus travel distance.
- Reaching a point with two ways out does not cancel the paragraph 14 limits on the remaining distance to an exit.
