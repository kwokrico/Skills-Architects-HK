# APP-159

**Official title:** Measures to Deter Misuse of Industrial Buildings for Residential Use

**In plain words:** New industrial building proposals, including alterations and additions (A&A), must be laid out for industrial use so they are harder to convert into homes without approval. Private toilet pipe ducts are not exempt from gross floor area (GFA), except communal toilets in the common parts, and an industrial building with small workshop units and curtain walls is measured to the outer face of the curtain wall for both GFA and site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issue October 2016.
- For a small workshop unit, a storey height of more than 3.5 m must be fully justified.
- A small workshop unit has a usable floor area under 80 m².

**What to submit**
- Show loading and unloading areas, cargo lifts, and how those areas are separated from the communal circulation used by occupiers and visitors. Show this on the general building plans (GBP).
- Justify a storey height above 3.5 m. Justify any design or facility that is uncommon in an industrial building, or that does not match industrial use. The Buildings Department may refuse it as an industrial building development.
- These checks are applied when the Buildings Department processes a new industrial building proposal, including A&A.

**Watch-outs**
- Layouts that make residential misuse easier: a private toilet with no natural lighting and ventilation, an excessive storey height with no justification, or domestic-type facilities with no industrial servicing. Unauthorised residential use also conflicts with dangerous or inflammable goods and breaches the Buildings Ordinance.
- A modification to omit natural lighting and ventilation to toilets is generally refused, except for communal toilets.
- Claiming a GFA exemption for a private toilet pipe duct.
- Measuring GFA or site coverage inside the curtain wall where small workshop units apply. Use the outer face.
