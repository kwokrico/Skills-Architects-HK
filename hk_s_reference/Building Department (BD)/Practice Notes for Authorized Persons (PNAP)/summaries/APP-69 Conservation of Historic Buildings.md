# APP-69

**Official title:** Conservation of Historic Buildings

**In plain words:** Monuments need a permit before any work, graded buildings are referred to the heritage authorities when you alter or demolish them, and a verandah or balcony kept over a street is counted in gross floor area. This note is about keeping heritage fabric while still meeting safety and health, not about a general site-coverage concession.

**Numbers that matter**
- Monuments and proposed monuments: Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance (Cap. 53) section 6. No works without a permit from the Antiquities Authority (the Secretary for Development). Without that permit, plans may be refused under Buildings Ordinance section 16(1)(d).
- Grades 1, 2 and 3 are administrative. Grade 1 is outstanding merit: every effort to preserve. Grade 2 is special merit: selective preservation. Grade 3 is some merit: alternative means if preservation is not practicable. Grading alone is not statutory protection under Cap. 53, but the Buildings Department refers alteration, addition and demolition to the Commissioner for Heritage’s Office and the Antiquities and Monuments Office.
- A performance-based alternative is acceptable if safety and health are equivalent. Example: Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads 2011, section 4.2, where strengthening up to the table of minimum imposed loads would harm the heritage fabric.
- Typical section 42 modifications and exemptions, each with the justification the appendix requires. Verandahs and balconies over a street (Buildings Ordinance section 31(1)): include the area in gross floor area, show the critical dimensions, and provide a letter from the Antiquities and Monuments Office. Fee exemption for the Revitalising Historic Buildings Through Partnership Scheme: Building (Administration) Regulations regulation 29(1A), with Development Bureau appointment documents. Other charitable fee exemptions follow PNAP APP-55.
- Protective barriers: reductions in height, gap or curb under Building (Construction) Regulations regulation 8. Floor imposed-load capacity under regulation 17(1), and horizontal imposed load on barriers under regulation 17(3). Window sill height under Building (Planning) Regulations regulation 3A. Lighting and ventilation under regulations 30 to 32, via PNAP APP-130. Emergency vehicular access shortfall under regulation 41D, plus a fire-safety assessment. Roof gutters under the Building (Standards of Sanitary Fitments, Plumbing, Drainage Works and Latrines) Regulations regulation 39. Deviations for lift pits and machine rooms.
- Timber staircases and roofs. Staircase width at least 860 mm, with population control if there is more than one stair. Winders. Flights of 17 to 18 risers. Stairs that continue down to a basement. Compensatory measures follow the relevant sections of the Buildings Department Practice Guidebook (2012), plus management plans, owner undertakings, Antiquities and Monuments Office letters and photos, as applicable.

**What to submit**
- Contact the Antiquities and Monuments Office early and identify the character-defining elements.
- The Buildings Department refers historic alteration, addition and demolition to the Commissioner for Heritage’s Office and the Antiquities and Monuments Office under centralised processing.
- Heritage units in the New Buildings Divisions process the proposals. A pre-submission enquiry follows PNAP ADM-19.
- For a section 42 application, use the Appendix A checklist: show on the plans what must be shown, and submit separately what must be submitted separately.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not carry out works to a monument or proposed monument without a Cap. 53 permit.
- Do not demolish or alter a graded building without engaging the Commissioner for Heritage’s Office and the Antiquities and Monuments Office early.
- Do not ask for a blanket exemption without the management plan, owner undertaking, compensatory fire or structural measures, or Antiquities and Monuments Office letter, where those are required.
