# ADV-31

**Official title:** Building External Finishes — Wet-fixed Tiles

**In plain words:** Wet-fixed ceramic and mosaic tiles on cement-sand rendering fall off when the render is too thick, too soon, or poorly bonded. This note sets joint widths, waiting times, tile weight, and pull-off tests. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). Horizontal fins that break up a panel, and mechanical fixings, do affect the facade. A similar note goes to registered contractors.

**Numbers that matter**
- Joints are usually 8 mm to 10 mm, and in any case not less than 3 mm, so grout can be inserted.
- Prefer a spatterdash within 24 hours of striking the formwork. Let the concrete dry and shrink for a recommended 6 weeks before rendering. Leave intermediate coats for several days, or as the manufacturer says, before the next coat. Leave the final coat to dry for at least 2 weeks before tiling. Each rendering coat is 8 mm to 16 mm thick. Total rendering is not more than 20 mm, unless a proprietary system says otherwise. If the substrate undulates, consider reinforcement or expanded metal lathing anchored to the concrete.
- Control the weight of the tiles. A tile larger than 0.1 m² is secured by mechanical means. A tile that absorbs water poorly needs a special polymer-modified adhesive, as the manufacturer specifies. Ceramic water absorption is not more than 0.5% up to first-floor height, and not more than 3% above first-floor height. For a cement-slurry ceramic tile, soak it for at least 30 minutes and drain it before fixing, and dampen the rendering.
- If reinforcement is used, prefer austenitic stainless-steel wire of 2.5 mm diameter, in a mesh of about 50 mm by 50 mm, with stainless fixings. Galvanised reinforcement is not recommended outside, or where it will get wet.
- Prefer grout of type CG2 to BS EN 13888:2009. Sealants must take up movement and resist water and ultraviolet light.
- Manufactured sand may replace river sand, as Development Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 8/2018 allows.
- Pull-off tests: at least 3 samples of each tile type for every 10 typical floors, or part of 10 floors, when tiles are fixed on rendering over concrete, directly on concrete, or by slurry on rendering. The authorized person (AP) or registered structural engineer (RSE) chooses the locations. Do not take samples from tiles over a waterproofing layer. Use a laboratory accredited by HOKLAS, and obtain a HOKLAS-endorsed certificate.
- Some external rendering, tiling, and roof finishes can use the simplified requirements in APP-147 and Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation.
- Inspect regularly. A periodic debonding survey, by hammer tap and, where useful, infrared thermography or other void detection, is recommended.
- The registered general building contractor or registered minor-works contractor prepares method statements, quality-assurance and testing procedures, and a pull-off proposal with acceptance and non-compliance plans, before tiling. The AP or RSE audits the contractor’s supervision. Off-site precast tiling is encouraged. Workers on a proprietary system should be trade-tested and trained by the manufacturer.

**What to submit**
- The method statement, the quality procedures, and the pull-off proposal before tiling starts. Minor works, where they apply, follow APP-147.

**Watch-outs**
- A tile larger than 0.1 m² with no mechanical fixing.
- Total rendering thicker than 20 mm, or uneven thickness, which debonds.
- Skipping pull-off samples, or taking them over a waterproofing layer.
- Spatterdash later than 24 hours, with no remedial bonding strategy.
- A tile falling from height is a liability.
