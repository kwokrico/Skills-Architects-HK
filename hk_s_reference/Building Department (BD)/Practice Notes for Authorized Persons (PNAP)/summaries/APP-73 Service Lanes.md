# APP-73

**Official title:** Service Lanes

**In plain words:** A private or partly private service lane can be used for lighting, ventilation, escape or access only if users have a permanent right of way and the lane is fit for that use. Lane area can be counted in site coverage and plot ratio in the cases below, and surrender of a lane that is not already an existing lane can earn plot ratio and site coverage concessions.

**Numbers that matter**
- Before you rely on a private or partly private lane for Buildings Ordinance purposes (lighting and ventilation, means of escape, or access), the authorized person must show a permanent or pertinent right of way, and that the lane works for all users.
- Discharge of an exit into a private or partly private lane is unacceptable unless an unfettered right of way is shown.
- A lane, public or private, is unacceptable as a means of escape or as access if it is obstructed, cannot be improved, or is decaying.
- The long-term aim, once all abutting sites are developed, is a lane that is direct and has an unobstructed width of at least 3 m.
- Building (Planning) Regulations (B(P)R) regulation 28: every domestic building generally needs a suitable service lane at the rear or the side, except in the circumstances the regulation prescribes.
- Except for B(P)R regulation 28(3), service-lane area is excluded from open space under B(P)R regulation 25, whether or not that area is included in site coverage or plot ratio.
- If the developer owns an abutting private lane that the scheme does not specifically need for Buildings Ordinance purposes, the Building Authority will favourably consider including that lane area in site coverage and plot ratio.
- Where a right of way is established, apply formally to modify B(P)R regulation 23(2)(a).
- Lane area included in site coverage or plot ratio must, on completion, be free of structures, except structures that are approved and already accounted for in the calculations.
- If a diverted lane is needed only to keep the neighbourhood pattern, and not specifically for the proposed development, the Building Authority will favourably consider including the relevant area in site coverage and plot ratio.
- If a lane improvement is in the public interest but is not specifically required for the scheme under the Buildings Ordinance, the Building Authority may grant plot ratio and site coverage concessions for surrender of that lane free of cost, if the Government accepts the surrender. The surrendered area must not form part of an existing lane.

**What to submit**
- Show the right-of-way evidence on the general building plans if you rely on the lane for lighting and ventilation, means of escape, or access.
- Apply formally for the B(P)R 23(2)(a) modification where it is needed.
- For a surrender: a plan, and a written undertaking to surrender free of cost and to pay for paving, channelling, sewering, drainage and, where appropriate, lighting. The area must be free of structures.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not count a lane in site coverage or plot ratio and leave structures on it.
- Do not rely on an obstructed or decaying lane as a means of escape.
- Do not claim a surrender concession for land that already forms part of an existing lane.
- Do not use a private lane for escape without an unfettered right of way for the users of the proposed building.
