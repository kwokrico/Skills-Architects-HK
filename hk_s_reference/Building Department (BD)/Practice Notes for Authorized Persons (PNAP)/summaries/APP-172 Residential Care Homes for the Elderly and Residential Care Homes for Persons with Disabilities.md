# APP-172

**Official title:** Residential Care Homes for the Elderly and Residential Care Homes for Persons with Disabilities

**In plain words:** Although habitation is domestic under the Buildings Ordinance, the Building Authority may treat a residential care home for the elderly (RCHE) or a residential care home for persons with disabilities (RCHD) as non-domestic for site coverage and plot ratio, and may allow no open space, if every criterion below is met, while the home itself stays accountable for gross floor area (GFA). A higher plot ratio is possible only for eligible new developments under the time-limited Lands Department and Social Welfare Department incentive schemes, and that higher ratio is not a blanket GFA exemption of the home.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issue April 2024.
- The non-domestic treatment is for Building (Planning) Regulations 19, 20, 21, and 22, including where the home is the non-domestic part of a composite building. Non-provision of open space is under regulation 25.
- All of the following must be met:
  - No adverse comment from the Social Welfare Department (SWD), including on access for ambulant residents.
  - No objection from the Fire Services Department (FSD).
  - Compliance with the Buildings Ordinance and regulations, including structure, fire safety, adequate headroom, and natural lighting and ventilation.
  - Compliance with Building (Planning) Regulations 49(1) and 49B. A modification for genuine hardship is possible case by case.
  - Compliance with Cap. 459A section 20 or Cap. 613A section 21. The home must not be in a basement.
  - The home must not be in an industrial building.
- Incentive schemes: Lands Department Practice Notes 5/2023 and 10/2023, and the SWD Guidance Notes. During the pilot period, the Building Authority is prepared to modify the plot ratio so the extra GFA of an eligible RCHE or RCHD can be accommodated, taking Planning Department advice on the outline zoning plan (OZP) or on planning approval. The paragraph 2 criteria must also be met. GFA is calculated under the Lands Department and SWD annexes.
- Paragraph 2 (the non-domestic treatment and open space) applies to new buildings and to alterations and additions (A&A) for a new or additional home. Paragraph 4 (the higher plot ratio) applies to new developments only, and only under those two incentive schemes.

**What to submit**
- Apply for the modifications with the SWD and FSD clearances, and with evidence of compliance with Cap. 459A or Cap. 613A.
- If a higher plot ratio is sought, align the general building plans with the OZP and any planning permission.
- Show compliance with regulations 49(1) and 49B, and with natural lighting and ventilation, fire safety, structure, and headroom, on the plans.

**Watch-outs**
- Seeking non-domestic treatment for an RCHE or RCHD in an industrial building or a basement.
- Claiming a higher plot ratio outside the incentive schemes, or without Planning Department and OZP alignment.
- Relying on the facilitation while SWD or FSD clearances are still incomplete.
