# ADM-18

**Official title:** Site Auditing for Building Works

**In plain words:** The Buildings Department audits foundations and superstructure under Buildings Ordinance section 22. Audits are performance-based, not on a fixed timetable you can predict, and the people who check submissions are not the people who audit the site. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC), except that a small two-storey warehouse of 1,500 m² or less is generally audited less often.

**Numbers that matter**
- The first visit rates site conditions, complexity, the contractor’s organisation, how often the authorized person (AP), registered structural engineer (RSE), registered geotechnical engineer (RGE), and registered contractor attend, how complete the papers are, and the quality of the work. That rating sets how often later audits happen. Anomalies or substandard work bring the next audit forward. The auditor decides what percentage, and which components, to inspect.
- As a baseline in Appendix II: the first foundation audit is 1 month after piling starts; the first superstructure audit is 3 months after superstructure starts. Satisfactory performance may stretch this, for example to 2 months for foundations and 4 months for superstructure. Unsatisfactory performance may tighten it, for example to 1 month for foundations and 2 months for superstructure. Audits continue until all structural components are complete.
- Appendix I covers, among other items: driven piles (mill certificates and proof of yield, spun-pile origin and dimensions, welding electrode grade, attendance under the site supervision plan, hammer final set and founding stratum, and whether the ground investigation matches the as-built); excavation piles (concrete, grout, and steel grades, depth, diameter, and bell-out, founding against the pre-drill); spread footings (bearing stratum and dimensions); and superstructure (hammer and tensile tests on reinforcement, coring of in-situ concrete, transfer-plate falsework acceptance and concreting sequence, prestress profile and grout vents, cantilever canopy construction joints and cover, and curtain or glass wall member size, grade, and cast-in anchors).
- For the two-storey warehouse fast track in ADM-19: the foundation bearing stratum is inspected within 1 working day (8 working hours) of notification. The scheduled quality inspection and the comprehensive superstructure-stage inspections are combined into one joint inspection. A small warehouse with GFA of 1,500 m² or less generally has one unscheduled audit only, unless irregularities justify more.
- There is no appointment in advance, and no fixed percentage of structural units is set in advance. Completed sites and sites still in progress can be audited.
- Anomaly investigations concentrate on structural safety and integrity, and on problems that keep recurring. The extent, seriousness, and whether the problem was inadvertent are all considered.
- Selected minor works under construction are also audited. Appendix I items such as spread footings, reinforcement, in-situ concrete, and cantilever canopies remain relevant.
- A similar note goes to registered contractors.

**What to submit**
- No audit application. Keep the site supervision plan, founding records, mill certificates, and falsework acceptances on site so they can be shown when the auditor arrives.

**Watch-outs**
- Gaps in attendance records, founding records, mill certificates, or transfer-plate falsework acceptance are treated as structural-integrity anomalies and bring more audits.
- One unscheduled audit of a small warehouse is not immunity. Irregularities trigger further audits.
- A rigorous audit is not interference with site management. Normal operations continue if the work is proper. Substandard work attracts scrutiny and sanctions.
