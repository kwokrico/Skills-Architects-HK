# APP-137 Ground-borne Vibration and Ground Settlement from Piling and Excavation
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision November 2024 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 289; first issue May 2004)

> Scope note: Control of vibration and settlement from pile installation, including driving, boring, withdrawal, and temporary pile walls in excavation and lateral support. Design and monitoring still follow clause 7.2 of the Code of Practice for Foundations 2017 and PNAP APP-18. Railway protection areas follow Appendix B of PNAP APP-24. It is not a pile-capacity code.

## Regulatory Overview
Pile foundation and excavation-and-lateral-support plans include a condition survey, a vibration and settlement assessment, control limits, and a monitoring proposal, and percussive or vibratory piling next to vibration-sensitive buildings is not normally accepted unless trial piles demonstrate it. If a measured peak particle velocity exceeds the allowable value, or damage is seen, installation stops until a review is accepted.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the plans contain (paragraphs 3–5 and 8–12)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Assessment | Pre-construction survey with photographs of external and common areas that are vulnerable, and internal areas if access is gained. Preliminary assessment of structural and non-structural elements for the whole piling sequence. Control limits, with Appendix A as the reference, and critical monitoring locations |
| Limits above Appendix A | A detailed vibration assessment, with reference to the Foundation Code, BS ISO 4866, and CIRIA Technical Note 142, including cumulative effects of all piles, plus engineering analysis of adjacent stability. Railway protection areas: Appendix B of PNAP APP-24 |
| Trial piles | Required if the site is close to buildings, structures, land, or services vulnerable to the works, and for monuments or masonry buildings, at each critical ground condition where the maximum vibration and settlement is expected. Two sets of the trial report (one set if submitted through the Electronic Submission Hub) before consent for the working piles. A condition survey after the trial piles |
| Test piles | If neighbours are not vulnerable, the assessed magnitudes may be verified on test piles |
| Monitoring during a test or trial | Peak particle velocity at every metre of penetration, at final set, and at obstructions, on three orthogonal axes at ground level, with a calibrated device |
| Sheet piles and similar | A site-formation plan with temporary pile walls includes the same monitoring details, and a trial-pile proposal if neighbours are vulnerable |

**SD takeaway:** Do not rely on percussive or vibratory piling beside a monument or a masonry building without an approved trial pile at the shallow obstruction or rock-and-soil horizon.

### 2. Five-tier movement limits (Appendix A, paragraph 3)
Where there are no particularly sensitive neighbours, provisional trigger values:

| Instrument | Alert | Alarm | Action 1 | Action 2 | Action 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground settlement | 12 mm | 18 mm | 20 mm | 22 mm | 25 mm |
| Services settlement or angular distortion | 12 mm or 1:600 | 18 mm or 1:450 | 20 mm or 1:400 | 22 mm or 1:350 | 25 mm or 1:300 |
| Building tilting, angular distortion | 1:1000 | 1:750 | 1:600 | 1:550 | 1:500 |

Alert increases monitoring. Alarm reviews the installation method. Action Level 1 is a joint inspection and remedial works. Action Level 2 is immediate notice, investigation, and a review of the method. Action Level 3 suspends the works until remedial and preventive measures are complete. Site-specific limits may be derived under Appendix C of PNAP APP-24, with acceptance by the relevant authorities and permission from the affected owners, before plan approval.

**SD takeaway:** Set the first ground-settlement alert at 12 mm and stop the works at the 25 mm Action Level 3 unless a site-specific limit has been accepted.

### 3. Vibration limits, peak particle velocity (Appendix A, paragraph 4)
| Building | Transient (for example drop hammer): Alert, Alarm, Action 1, 2, 3 | Continuous (for example vibratory hammer) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Robust and stable | 9, 12, 13, 14, 15 mm/s | 4.5, 6, 6.5, 7, 7.5 mm/s |
| Vibration-sensitive or dilapidated | 4.5, 6, 6.5, 7, 7.5 mm/s | 1.8, 2.4, 2.6, 2.8, 3 mm/s |

**SD takeaway:** A vibratory hammer beside a dilapidated building is watched from 1.8 mm/s and stopped at 3 mm/s under the provisional limits.
