# APP-123 Alternative Designs for External-Wall Fire Separation
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue October 2001 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 260)

> Scope note: Alternatives to the 900 mm spandrel in paragraph 12.3 of the Code of Practice for Fire Resistance Construction 1996. It is not a general external-wall fire code. The remainder of a unit with an open balcony still has to meet the full separation requirements of that paragraph.

## Regulatory Overview
Paragraph 12.3 of the 1996 fire-resistance code requires the external wall at any floor to be separated from the external wall at the floor next below by a spandrel not less than 900 mm in height. A 900 mm horizontal apron, or a waiver for certain open balconies, may replace that vertical spandrel.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Spandrel or apron (paragraphs 1–2)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Code | Spandrel not less than 900 mm in height between the external wall at a floor and the external wall at the floor next below |
| Alternative | Where 900 mm vertical separation cannot be provided due to design constraints, a horizontal apron with a projected width of 900 mm is accepted |

**SD takeaway:** If the facade cannot take a 900 mm spandrel, project a 900 mm horizontal apron instead.

### 2. Open balconies (paragraph 3)
The Building Authority is prepared to waive paragraph 12.3(a) of the code for balconies which are open on at least two sides, because of the relatively low fire risk of ventilated balconies. The remainder of the unit has to comply with the full requirements of the paragraph for fire separation.

**SD takeaway:** A balcony open on at least two sides can omit the 12.3(a) spandrel; the rest of the flat still needs the full fire separation.
