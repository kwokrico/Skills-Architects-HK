# APP-14 Cinemas and Other Places of Public Entertainment in Non-domestic Buildings
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision April 2010 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 54; first issue July 1978)

> Scope note: Extra means-of-escape and fire-separation rules for cinemas, and for other places of public entertainment treated as cinemas, in a non-domestic building or the non-domestic part of a composite building. Under regulation 49B of the Building (Planning) Regulations, cinemas accommodating not more than 2,000 people in aggregate may be on any storey or storeys and may share exits, foyers, waiting spaces, vestibules, and projection rooms. The means-of-escape, fire-resisting-construction, and means-of-access codes still apply.

## Regulatory Overview
Cinemas may share exit routes with other non-domestic accommodation if the conditions in paragraph 2 are met, including a dedicated smoke extraction system, fire-resisting separation, and discharge to streets not less than 4.5 m wide, and those cinemas are not at basement. In a composite building, cinema exits must be separated from the domestic exits.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Shared exits, widths, and horizontal exits (paragraphs 2–7)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Cinema box | Dedicated smoke extraction and other fire service installations to the satisfaction of the Director of Fire Services. Walls and floors with the fire resistance period in the fire-resisting-construction code. Every door not less than that period. A protected lobby is not required at doors to the foyer or adjoining non-domestic accommodation |
| Foyer | Same fire separation, unless the foyer also has smoke extraction to the Director's satisfaction, in which case separate the foyer from other accommodation and protect any opening with a door or fire shutter of the same fire resistance period as the wall |
| Shared exits | Accessible from the common circulation area without passing through other private premises. Widths based on the combined capacity. If the width required on a floor exceeds that of the floors below, the stair from that floor down to ground is not less than the width required for that floor |
| Stair entered directly from the cinema | The entire stair complies with paragraphs 17.2(b) and 26.2 of the means-of-escape code |
| Discharge | To streets not less than 4.5 m wide. Two exit routes from each floor discharge into different thoroughfares |
| Width standard | Not less than Table 2 of the means-of-escape code. If the cinema or part of it is 12 m or more above pavement, also paragraphs 25.1, 25.3, and 25.4 |
| Horizontal exit through adjoining accommodation | Not more than half the total exit width required for that cinema. The common circulation areas, including the foyer, have not less than 0.5 m2 per person for the sum of the pro-rata cinema population using those exits, the foyer population at one person per 0.5 m2, and the circulation population from Table 1 of the means-of-escape code |

**SD takeaway:** Do not put a cinema that shares exits in a basement; give every box its own smoke extract, and send two exit routes from each floor into different streets at least 4.5 m wide.

### 2. Seats, gangways, projection rooms, and foyers (paragraphs 8–15)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Row length | Not more than 12 m with a gangway on one side, or 24 m with gangways on both sides |
| Seats in a row, by seatway width | 300–324 mm: 7 / 14; 325–349 mm: 8 / 15; 350–374 mm: 9 / 18; 375–399 mm: 10 / 20; 400–424 mm: 11 / 22; 425–449 mm: 12 / 24; 450–474 mm: 12 / 26; 475–499 mm: 12 / 28; 500 mm or more: limited by the 12 m or 24 m row length. The first figure is one gangway, the second is two. Seatway is the clear distance to the seat behind; for tip-up seats, measure to the maximum projection in the tipped-up position |
| Gangway width | Fewer than 500 persons: 1,100 mm each; 501–1,000: 1,200 mm; 1,001–1,500: 1,350 mm; over 1,500: 1,500 mm |
| Projection room | Walls and floors not less than 1 hour. Every door not more than 3 m3/hour/m leakage (head and jambs only) at 25 Pa under BS 476 Section 31.1, and not less than 1 hour. Not required where no film projector, or electric-arc, xenon, or other source that generates hazardous gases, dust, or radiation, is used |
| One exit from a projection room | Only an elevated room serving one cinema box, if direct distance plus travel distance complies with paragraph 14(3)(b) of the means-of-escape code. That exit may pass through the seating of the box it serves |
| Ventilation of a projection suite | If not naturally ventilated, an independent mechanical system to the open air |
| Waiting area, regulation 49E | 0.5 m2 per person, for one in six of the seated capacity |
| Seats before occupation permit | Not required if gangways and seatways are demarcated on site and the developer or authorized person undertakes that seats will be installed before the cinema-licence application |

**SD takeaway:** Limit a row to 12 m or 24 m, size the gangway from 1,100 mm up to 1,500 mm by the number of persons, and give a projection room 1-hour enclosure unless no hazardous projector is used.

### 3. Basement cinemas, other entertainment, and small premises (paragraphs 14 and 16–18)
| Case | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Basement | Paragraph 23.3 of the means-of-escape code, plus a ground-floor foyer of 0.5 m2 per person for one in three of the seated capacity. Lifts to other floors open through a protected lobby. The fireman's lift opens into the ventilated lobby of the firefighting and rescue stairway, and that lobby gives access to the cinema boxes. Separate all parts of the cinema from the rest of the building by elements of not less than 4 hours. See also paragraphs 6.2 and 15 of the fire-resisting-construction code |
| Other places of public entertainment | The cinema rules may be applied if the cinema conditions are met |
| Aggregate not more than 500, no cinema or theatre, in a non-domestic building without polluting industrial undertakings, or the non-domestic part of a composite building | The site may abut one thoroughfare if that thoroughfare is an acceptable emergency vehicular access and the Director of Fire Services has no adverse comment. Modification of regulation 49A may be considered |
| Capacity not exceeding 50 | May share non-domestic exits. This note and Part III of the means-of-escape code do not apply if the aggregate of all such premises in the building is not more than 150, none is in a basement, any projection room still complies with paragraphs 10 to 12, and a foyer, where applicable, complies with paragraph 13 |

**SD takeaway:** A basement cinema needs a ground-floor foyer sized for one in three of the seats and 4-hour separation; a room of 50 or fewer may share ordinary exits only while the building total of such rooms stays at or below 150 and none is in a basement.
