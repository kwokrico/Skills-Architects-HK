# APP-21

**Official title:** Demolition Works — Measures for Public Safety

**In plain words:** This note sets the plans, supervision, site protection, and completion route for demolition, following the Code of Practice for Demolition of Buildings 2004. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Submit demolition proposals for formal approval under Building (Administration) Regulations 8(3) and 8(4), and follow the Demolition Code 2004. Consent will not be given without approved plans (Buildings Ordinance sections 16(3)(a) and 16(3)(b)).
- These protective items may be put up without consent if they do not affect the structure: screens and nets, dust sheets, scaffolds and catch fans, hoarding, covered walkway, gantry, catch platforms, and shoring or propping.
- A supervision plan is required before or with the consent, except where paragraph 11 of the Technical Memorandum applies. Prepare it to the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 (2024 edition). Consent may be refused under section 16(3)(bc) if the plan is missing.
- A complex structure needs a full-time site engineer under the Demolition Code, responsible to the registered specialist contractor. Report a change of that engineer to the authorized person, the registered structural engineer, the registered geotechnical engineer, and the Building Authority.
- Provide at least 1 video camera on site, at locations shown on the plan. Keep the recordings for at least 14 days.
- Operators of powered plant must meet Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 9(3) and hold the “Demolition of Building Course for Plant Operators” of the Hong Kong Institute of Construction, or an equivalent. Give their particulars with the consent application (Building (Administration) Regulation 31). Tell the Building Authority of a change within 7 days.
- A dangerous building under an order is secured after closure. Steel shores are Grade 250 or higher to the Steel Code. Agree party-wall shoring details with the Existing Buildings Division. Coordinate a joint demolition. Tell the Building Authority of any unexpected weakness.
- A fast-track BA14B is available only if the building is structurally independent; there is no basement or below-ground structure (filling debris to resist buoyancy is acceptable, but fill used to retain earth fails this test); nothing remains above the ground-floor slab or beam except those elements; no party wall or adjoining support is needed; and no slope, retaining-wall, stability, drainage, or stabilisation works are needed. The reply time is 14 days if not audited and 28 days if audited.
- Design hoarding and the covered walkway to Chapter 3 of the Demolition Code and to APP-23. Fire-retardant protective nets and screens must be evidenced on site. Flammable materials must not be left on site (Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 6).

**What to submit**
- Approval plans. With the consent: Form BA8, the technically competent person on Form BA20, the supervision plan, operator particulars, a debris-management proposal, and Chinese demolition plans, using the checklist in Appendix B.
- Completion on BA14A or BA14B. Notify the Highways Department about street name plates, and the Drainage Services Department about sealing drains.
- Minor works that are streamlined to run with the demolition follow Appendix C: MW01 with the demolition approval, and MW02 with BA14A or BA14B.

**Watch-outs**
- Consent without approved plans, a supervision plan, operator particulars, or the protective measures can be refused, including under section 16(3)(bb).
- Crane-and-hammer demolition in a dense urban area is called out as potentially unsafe.
- Do not claim the fast-track BA14B if there is a basement, a party wall, or slope works.
- Leaving flammable materials on site breaches Building (Demolition Works) Regulation 6.
