# ADV-13

**Official title:** Application for Excavation Permit for Works on Public Road — Circulation of Proposal to Utility Undertakers

**In plain words:** Digging in a public road that the Highways Department maintains needs an excavation permit, even when the digging is part of building works such as a hoarding, a gantry, or a trial pit. The Highways Department will not issue the permit unless you have already written to the utility companies and dealt with their replies. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). It does affect the programme for hoardings, gantries, and ground investigation on a public road.

**Numbers that matter**
- The permit comes from the Director of Highways under the Land (Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 28) section 10. Work without a permit is an offence.
- Typical building-related excavations include sheet piling, trial pits, ground investigation, and the erection or demolition of hoardings, covered walkways, and gantries.
- An application with no documentary evidence that advance notices went to all relevant utility undertakers, and that their requirements were properly answered, is rejected.
- The list of utility undertakers is Table 1 of Appendix A to the Conditions of Excavation Permit, in the Excavation Permit Processing Manual on the Highways Department website.
- Before heavy plant is used, confirm the exact position of utilities by a suitable investigation, for example hand-dug trial pits.

**What to submit**
- The excavation permit is a Highways Department permit, not a Building Authority approval. Circulate the intended works to the utility undertakers, keep their responses with the permit application, and carry out the hand-dug checks before heavy excavation.

**Watch-outs**
- Excavating on a public road without a Cap. 28 permit.
- A permit application with no evidence of utility circulation is refused, and the programme slips.
- Heavy plant without trial pits risks damaged utilities and a service outage.
