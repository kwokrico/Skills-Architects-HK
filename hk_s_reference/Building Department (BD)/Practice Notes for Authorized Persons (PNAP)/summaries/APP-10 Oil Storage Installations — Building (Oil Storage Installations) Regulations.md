# APP-10

**Official title:** Oil Storage Installations — Building (Oil Storage Installations) Regulations

**In plain words:** This note covers approval, licensing, inspection, and demolition of large above-ground oil storage tanks. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Under Buildings Ordinance section 2(1), an oil storage installation is a tank of at least 110,000 litres, or a group in the same cluster or bunded area in which any one tank is at least 110,000 litres, above ground, for petroleum products.
- Construction needs approval and consent under section 14(1). The installation is deemed to satisfy the Building (Oil Storage Installations) Regulations if it meets the Code of Practice for Oil Storage Installations 1992. It must not operate before a licence is issued. Alteration and addition, including a new tank, needs a licence endorsement.
- General inspection under regulation 8(1)(a): inspect the inside of every tank not later than the 10th year of the tank’s life, and thereafter in the 5th year after the year of the first inspection. Annual licence renewal needs external inspection certificates under regulation 8(1)(b) and associated-works certificates under regulation 8(1)(c).
- Repair or alteration: two copies of the proposals, signed by the registered structural engineer, for written authorization under regulation 10. Extensive repair, or building works that are not exempt, also need section 14(1) approval and consent.
- Demolition: agree a method statement with the Building Authority before or with the consent or authorization. Follow Appendix D and PNAP 71 (APP-21) for public safety. Partly built tanks can be damaged in a typhoon, so take weather precautions.

**What to submit**
- New licence: six English copies and one Chinese copy of the operation instructions; one set of general arrangement plans. The occupation permit and the general inspection certificate are required before the licence.
- Certificates: Appendix A (general inspection), Appendix B (external inspection), Appendix C (associated works).
- Demolition: a contamination assessment report (Environmental Protection Department guidance); gas-free certification by a competent person; sludge and chemical-waste disposal under the Waste Disposal (Chemical Waste) regulations; oil-interceptor control under the Water Pollution Control Ordinance; soil tests for hydrocarbons and lead after demolition.

**Watch-outs**
- Operating or altering the installation without a licence or an endorsement is not allowed.
- Incomplete bilingual operation instructions can hold up the licence.
- Demolition without gas-freeing, interceptor control, or a contamination assessment brings pollution and explosion risk, and the Building Authority can refuse.
