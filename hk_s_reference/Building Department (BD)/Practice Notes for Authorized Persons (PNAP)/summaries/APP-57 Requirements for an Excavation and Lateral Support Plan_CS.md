# APP-57 Requirements for an Excavation and Lateral Support Plan
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision February 2012 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 148; first issue October 1991)

> Scope note: When Building (Administration) Regulation 8(1)(bc) requires an excavation and lateral support plan, and the two design methods that may be used. A mixture of the global safety factor method and the limit-state partial-factor method is not allowed. Monitoring where piling is included is in PNAP APP-18 and PNAP APP-137.

## Regulatory Overview
An excavation and lateral support plan is required where the excavation is deeper than 2.5 m and longer than 5 m, and is liable to affect any road, building, structure, slope steeper than 30°, or water main of 75 mm diameter or greater, the affected area being within the 45° line drawn up from the base of the excavation to the ground surface. Consent will not be given unless the prescribed plans have been approved.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Who signs, and the two-stage submission (paragraphs 5–6 and 9–10)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Structural content | A registered structural engineer prepares and signs the plans, the structural design, and the assessment of excavation and dewatering effects on adjoining structures |
| Deeper than 4.5 m | A registered geotechnical engineer prepares and signs the geotechnical assessment, details, calculations, reports, and the site or ground investigation reports that accompany the plans |
| Two stages | Permitted if the first stage shows the whole scheme is feasible: lateral support such as sheet piles or a diaphragm wall, strutting layout, construction sequence, geotechnical documentation, and realistic ground-movement estimates for the entire works. The second stage is the structural detailing of the support, including struts for each stage of excavation |
| Method | State which method is used on the plans and calculations. Do not mix the two methods |
| Software | Prior acceptance under PNAP ADM-6. A program that can run both methods still needs a separate acceptance for each method |

**SD takeaway:** Submit an excavation and lateral support plan before consent if the dig is deeper than 2.5 m, longer than 5 m, and anything sensitive lies inside the 45° line from the base; add a geotechnical engineer if it is deeper than 4.5 m.

### 2. Factors that apply to both methods, and the partial-factor alternative (Appendix A)
| Check | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Hydraulic failure | Section 6.2.4 of GCO Publication No. 1/90, with a minimum factor of safety of 1.5 |
| Movement | GCO Publication No. 1/90 may be used for ground movement and lateral wall movement from wall installation, dewatering, and excavation |
| Monitoring as a minimum | Ground settlement or heave, and piezometric pressures. Also monitor deflection of the vertical member where failure would significantly risk life and property. The design report, certified by both engineers, is given to the qualified site supervision personnel |
| Global safety factor method | Unfactored soil strength and loads, for stability checks under Building (Construction) Regulation 15. Separate checks against moment instability use a global factor of 2.0 |
| Limit-state partial-factor method | An alternative, conforming to Appendix A and CIRIA Report No. C580 as modified there. Not applicable where the embedded depth of the wall is entirely supported by soft clays. May be used for walls socketed in rock. Minimum partial factors and surcharge from Geoguide 1, second edition. Apply a minimum factor of 1.4, instead of 1.35, to forces and bending moments from the serviceability calculations. If these ultimate checks are done, the separate global factor of 2.0 against moment instability is not required |
| Singly propped excavations | Sensitivity analyses for prop level plus or minus 0.5 m, and for full surcharge and no surcharge |
| Loss of one strut | Assess the risk. If it is evident, either check progressive failure or provide mitigation, such as sensors on plant and protective elements welded to the supports. Unfactored soil parameters and unity load factors may be used for the progressive-failure check |
| After the works | A performance review under PNAP APP-115 |

**SD takeaway:** Design the excavation against hydraulic failure at a factor of safety of at least 1.5, state one design method only, and check a singly propped wall for a prop moved by 0.5 m.
