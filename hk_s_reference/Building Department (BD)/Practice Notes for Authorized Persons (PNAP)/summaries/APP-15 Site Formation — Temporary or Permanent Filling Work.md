# APP-15

**Official title:** Site Formation — Temporary or Permanent Filling Work

**In plain words:** This note stops loosely placed earth fill from flowing like a slide, and it sets how densely that fill must be compacted where the public could be harmed. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Where a failure could endanger the public, the Building Authority requires drawings that specify the Appendix A standards, periodic supervision and inspections by a registered geotechnical engineer (RGE), and a supervision plan under Buildings Ordinance section 39A (the Technical Memorandum) and section 17(1)(6)(e), as in PNAP 83.
- Compaction (Appendix A): the outer portion of an earth-fill slope must reach an in-situ field dry density of at least 95% of the maximum dry density in Geospec 3. Maximum dry density and optimum moisture content are determined to Geospec 3 for each soil type at first use, and again with each set of field-density tests. Pair the maximum-dry-density sample with the in-situ test from the same batch, type, and source.
- Table 1 sets how often to test. Examples given: excavations and formations of 0–100 m² need 3 determinations; other areas of 0–1 hectare need 4 determinations per 1,000 m², with further bands in that table. A small fill, less than 1 m deep and less than 300 m³ in volume, may be tested less often if penetration tests are added. Tests are done only by laboratories accredited by HOKLAS for the relevant tests.

**What to submit**
- Tell the Building Authority the date filling will start. Each month, the RGE signs a compaction report (in-situ density summary, laboratory compaction, relative compaction, and any non-compliance with the corrective measures). Keep the records on site for the Buildings Department and the Geotechnical Engineering Office.
- The Building Authority may require GCO probes or trial pits. The authorized person or RGE arranges labourers. For loose fill, do the in-situ density tests with the Buildings Department or the Geotechnical Engineering Office present. If the risk is extensive, a cease-works order may be made under Buildings Ordinance section 23 or 24A.
- Before the site-formation BA14 or the occupation permit: as-built plans and sections, and the full compaction records. For structural works, the registered structural engineer signs the structural calculations or assessment and certifies structural completion.

**Watch-outs**
- Temporary fill left on site, or permanent fill that fails Appendix A, can lead to refusal of the occupation permit under Buildings Ordinance section 21(6)(a).
- Testing outside HOKLAS, or pairing a maximum-dry-density result with the wrong soil, is not acceptable.
- Missing monthly reports, or missing RGE involvement, is a refusal risk.
