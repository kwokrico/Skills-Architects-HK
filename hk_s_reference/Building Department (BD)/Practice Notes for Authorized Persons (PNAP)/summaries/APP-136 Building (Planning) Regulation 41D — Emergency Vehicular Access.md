# APP-136

**Official title:** Building (Planning) Regulation 41D — Emergency Vehicular Access

**In plain words:** This note explains emergency vehicular access (EVA, the route for fire engines) for new buildings under Building (Planning) Regulation 41D. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage, but the EVA layout affects site planning and the major façade.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly PNAP 288. First issue May 2005. Revision July 2006. Design standards are in Part VI of the Means of Access Code. Regulation 41D took effect on 31 December 2004.
- The rule does not apply to works already under way, or consented, before 31 December 2004. Foundation consent is often the relevant consent.
- An EVA that meets the Means of Access Code complies with Regulation 41D.
- For a simple outline, use the actual perimeter to measure the one-fourth major façade. A complex outline may use a notional building contour (Appendix A).
- Projections the Building Authority has accepted are ignored in the façade-length calculation, except balconies and utility platforms under Joint Practice Notes 1 and 2.
- Exemption under Regulation 41D(3): the EVA is impracticable because of topography, or the use has a low fire risk.
- Appendix B examples where a fire-safety report is generally not required: dead-end reverse distance 30 m or less; run-in width at least 4.5 m; horizontal distance 10 m to 30 m with a fire service installation enhancement (for example a hose reel); more than 30 m for a domestic or family building of 3 storeys or less with a fire service installation (for example a sprinkler); Class A, B, or C redevelopment with road width at least 4.5 m; Class A minimum major façade equal to the side-boundary distance within 10 m of the EVA.
- For an EVA on a public street, provide only items 4(a), 4(b), 4(e), and 4(f). Loading capacity is not required for the public-street portion.
- If there is no EVA, or it does not comply: Form BA16 exemption and justification. A fire-safety report under Means of Access Code paragraph 27.2 where appropriate. The Building Authority consults the Director of Fire Services.
- Consent more than 2 years after plan approval carries a risk under Buildings Ordinance section 16(3)(d) for related works.

**What to submit**
- A separate EVA plan on the general building plans showing extent and alignment (coloured); width, turning radii, loading, and gradient; signage and crash gates; a clear-headroom section if there is something overhead; a block plan of the major façade; the façade-length percentage calculation; and any paving that is not hard.
- Form BA16 where an exemption is sought.

**Watch-outs**
- An incomplete EVA plan, or a façade percentage that counts Joint Practice Note balconies or utility platforms, is wrong.
- Do not rely on Appendix B relaxations without the case-specific fire service installation enhancement when one is required.
- Consent more than 2 years after plan approval is a risk under Buildings Ordinance section 16(3)(d).
