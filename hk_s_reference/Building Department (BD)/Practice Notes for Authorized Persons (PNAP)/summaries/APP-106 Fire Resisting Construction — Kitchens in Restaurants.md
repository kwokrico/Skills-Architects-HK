# APP-106

**Official title:** Fire Resisting Construction — Kitchens in Restaurants

**In plain words:** For fire resisting construction (FRC), a restaurant kitchen is treated as ancillary accommodation to the restaurant, and this note is to be read with paragraph 6 of the FRC Code until the Code itself is updated. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). It does change how the kitchen is compartmented.

**Numbers that matter**
- Under Part XV of the Building (Construction) Regulations, the kitchen must be enclosed by walls with a fire resistance period (FRP) of at least 1 hour. Openings must be protected by doors with an FRP of at least ½ hour.
- Provide a protected lobby between each kitchen door and any escape route from the main building, and/or the dining area where the kitchen’s usable floor area is more than 45 m² and the door opens onto a dining exit route.
- Hold-open devices on kitchen doors may be used if they follow paragraph 17.1 of the FRC Code.
- Re-issued August 2009. First issued October 1998.
- File reference: BD GP/BREG/C/19/L (VII).

**What to submit**
- Show the wall and door fire ratings, and the protected lobbies, on the fire strategy and on the general building plans (GBP) as applicable. Refer to this practice note together with the FRC Code.

**Watch-outs**
- A kitchen of more than 45 m² that opens straight onto a dining exit route, without a protected lobby, does not comply.
- Treating the kitchen as an unrelated room, without the 1-hour enclosure, is wrong under this note.
