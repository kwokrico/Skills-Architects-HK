# ADV-17

**Official title:** Noise Annoyance Prevention — Design of Pump Room and Ventilation System

**In plain words:** Put pump rooms and noisy plant away from flats and schools, and design the walls, mounts, and fans so the noise stays below the legal limits with a margin. This note does not grant floor area. Thicker walls, buffer rooms, and acoustic enclosures do use space. If you later ask for that plant room to be disregarded from gross floor area (GFA), it still has to meet APP-2 and APP-151.

**Numbers that matter**
- Prefer to separate a pump room from residential flats by a non-sensitive buffer, such as a stairwell or a switch room. Deal with both airborne noise and structure-borne noise.
- Airborne, pumps: choose quieter pumps and motors. If the total sound power level of the installed pumps is above 100 dB(A), consider insulation better than the stated minimum. Walls, and the ceilings or floors that adjoin a noise-sensitive receiver such as a flat or a school, should be at least sound transmission class (STC) 50. Put wall openings, air grilles, and fan outlets away from sensitive areas, or treat them with an acoustic louvre or a silencer. A door that faces a sensitive area should be at least STC 25.
- Structure-borne, pumps: anti-vibration mounts sized to the load, and flexible joints (Appendix A). Hang pipes on spring or rubber isolators. Where a pipe passes through a wall, use a rubber sleeve and a gasket. Size pipes so the flow velocity does not make noise, especially through valves.
- Ventilation design target: a noise level 5 dB(A) below the relevant acceptable noise level in paragraph 2 of the Technical Memorandum for the Assessment of Noise from Places other than Domestic Premises, Public Places or Construction Sites, issued under the Noise Control Ordinance.
- For every ventilation system: enough capacity, and a quieter model, which is usually also more energy-efficient. Isolate rotating and vibrating parts with anti-vibration mounts, flexible connections, damping, and sound-absorbing lagging (Appendix B).
- Near a sensitive receiver: locate the noise source away from it. Assess large fans and air-cooled chillers. Add acoustic panels, enclosures, silencers, or louvres where the plant faces a sensitive receiver.
- The Environmental Protection Department’s practical guide and pamphlets, and the Noise Control Ordinance and its technical memoranda, set the maximum levels from pump rooms, ventilation, and the associated air-conditioning.

**What to submit**
- No dedicated acoustic certificate in this note. Show plant-room positions clearly on the general building plans where another department will review them.

**Watch-outs**
- Pumps hard against flats, with no STC 50 envelope and no anti-vibration mounts, produce complaints and later alteration work.
- A total pump sound power level above 100 dB(A) with only the standard insulation.
- Ventilation designed only to the acceptable noise level, and not to 5 dB(A) below it, misses this note’s target.
- An air-cooled chiller facing a school or homes, with no silencer or enclosure.
