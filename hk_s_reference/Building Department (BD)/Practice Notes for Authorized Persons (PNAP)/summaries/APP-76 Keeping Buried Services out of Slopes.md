# APP-76

**Official title:** Keeping Buried Services out of Slopes

**In plain words:** This note guides an Authorized Person (AP) and a Registered Structural Engineer (RSE) to keep buried pipes and cables out of slopes and retaining walls, or to limit harm if they leak. It does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly practice note (PNAP) 183. First issued June 1995. Re-issued August 2009.
- Buried services should not sit in a slope nearer the crest than a distance equal to the slope’s vertical height (Geotechnical Manual for Slopes section 9.7). Routing close to the crest is poor practice.
- If that crest setback is not practical, design the slope to the factors of safety in Geotechnical Manual Chapter 5, allowing for leakage, or use one of these: lay the services on or above ground, protected against corrosion and damage, with a treatment for appearance; house them in a duct, sealed trench, or sleeve that drains to the surface drainage or a natural stream, check that discharge regularly, and provide inspection chambers; or, for a water main in the crest area, fit stop valves on each side of the slope.
- Read with PNAP 168 on slope and retaining-wall record plans.

**What to submit**
- Apply the guidance in the site formation, drainage, and building-plan design.
- Update the statutory plans with accurate as-built locations of new buried services.
- Put those locations into the slope or retaining-wall record plans under PNAP 168.
- Give copies to the developer and to the owners or building managers who will maintain them.

**Watch-outs**
- Ignoring the crest setback, or the factor of safety for leakage, can lead to slope failure.
- A rigid pipeline in fill can break if it has no flexible joints and no non-brittle pipe.
- A duct, sealed trench, or sleeve needs a drain to the surface or a stream, regular checks of the discharge, and inspection chambers.
- Missing as-built records, or failing to hand them to owners or managers, leaves a maintenance and leakage risk.
- Telephone ducts, electric ducts, and disused pipes can still carry a lot of water.
