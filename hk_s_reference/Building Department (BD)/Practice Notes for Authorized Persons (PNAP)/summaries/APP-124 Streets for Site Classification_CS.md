# APP-124 Streets for Site Classification
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision November 2005 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 263; first issue October 2001)

> Scope note: What counts as a street, and as a specified street under Building (Planning) Regulation 18A, for class A, class B, and class C site classification. It does not set the site-coverage or plot-ratio tables. A specified street that has to be built must exist before the occupation permit and must remain a street for as long as the buildings rely on it.

## Regulatory Overview
Permitted site coverage and plot ratio depend on the class of the site, which depends on the number of streets not less than 4.5 m wide that the site abuts. From 31 December 2005, regulation 18A defines a class A, class B, or class C site by the number of specified streets of that width.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is a street (paragraph 2)
| Test | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Characteristics | More than a passageway. Access as a street, and factors such as street lighting, paving, and drainage, are taken into account |
| Not a street | Land designated as public amenity, such as open car parks and public open spaces, and topographical features such as nullahs, the sea, and the like |
| Intervening land | Whether the site abuts a street across an intervening strip depends on the function, status, and ownership of that strip |

**SD takeaway:** Do not count a car park, public open space, nullah, or the sea as a street for site class.

### 2. Specified street under regulation 18A(3) (paragraph 3)
A street is a specified street if any of these exist. Width is still not less than 4.5 m.

| Circumstance | Proof |
| --- | --- |
| Vested in the Government and maintained by the Highways Department (18A(3)(a)(i)) | Public streets. If in doubt, consult the Highways Department |
| On land held under the same Government lease as the site, and the lessee must surrender that street land when required (18A(3)(a)(ii)) | Copy of the land grant or lease |
| To be constructed on unleased Government land by the lessee under the lease (18A(3)(a)(iii)) | Copy of the land grant or lease |
| Land over which the owner is expressly granted a right of way exercisable at all times (18A(3)(a)(iv)) | A private street not owned by the site owner, or a public passage outside (a) and (c), is accepted only with an express right of way. Submit the instrument or deed |
| On land held by the owner of the site under a Government lease, whether or not the same lease (18A(3)(a)(v)) | Evidence the street remains a street for the life of the development, such as an undertaking registered in the Land Registry that it will be assigned as common area for all owners |
| Parts under different limbs that together constitute the street (18A(3)(b)) | If intervening strips meet none of the circumstances, each part is a separate specified street |

**SD takeaway:** A private street counts only with the lease, an all-times right of way, or a Land Registry undertaking that it stays common area.

### 3. Timing and later building over (paragraph 4)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Not yet built | The specified street should be completed before the occupation permit. If it is not, the Building Authority would refuse the permit under section 21(6)(a) of the Buildings Ordinance |
| Life of the buildings | The street remains a street insofar as the buildings relying on it for site coverage and plot ratio exist |
| Building over or extinguishing it | Would contravene the Buildings Ordinance and would be disapproved under section 16(1)(d) |

**SD takeaway:** Finish the specified street before the occupation permit, and do not build over it while the plot ratio depends on it.
