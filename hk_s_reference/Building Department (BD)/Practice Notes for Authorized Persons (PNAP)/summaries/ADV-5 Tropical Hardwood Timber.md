# ADV-5

**Official title:** Tropical Hardwood Timber

**In plain words:** Hong Kong imports a large amount of tropical timber, and construction often throws it away as waste. This note asks you to use alternatives, especially for hoardings and formwork. It does not set a gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC) rule, and it does not create a quantified offence under the Buildings Ordinance.

**Numbers that matter**
- Prefer a steel hoarding, which can be reused. The Architectural Services Department and the Housing Department already require steel hoardings. The industry is urged to follow.
- Question whether a covered walkway is necessary, especially where the building is set back from the boundary. It is not an essential precaution on every site.
- Formwork is the largest timber use. Consider profiled steel sheeting as permanent formwork or reinforcement, precast units, structural steel frames, steel formwork, and proprietary steel props instead of timber props.
- For internal finishes, consider non-timber proprietary wall panels, and alternatives for doors, frames, skirtings, and architraves, especially where they will be painted.
- Write the contract so hardwood waste is separated and reused, not dumped.
- Where there is no alternative, specify hardwood from a renewable source.

**What to submit**
- No certificate under this note. Material choices appear in the contract. A hoarding or walkway that is a temporary structure still follows the normal Building Authority path and APP-23 where that applies.

**Watch-outs**
- Defaulting to single-use tropical-timber hoarding or formwork, without looking at a reusable or permanent alternative, misses the point of the note.
- Building a timber covered walkway where the setback means the walkway is not needed.
