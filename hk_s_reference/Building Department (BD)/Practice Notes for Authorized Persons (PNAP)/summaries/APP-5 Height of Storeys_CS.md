# APP-5 Height of Storeys
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision May 2019 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP-27; first issue February 1976)

> Scope note: How storey height is measured under regulations 3(3) and 24 of the Building (Planning) Regulations, the heights the Buildings Department will accept, and when a transfer plate is not counted as gross floor area. It does not set plot ratio.

## Regulatory Overview
Storey heights under regulation 24, and the clear height of balconies under regulation 3(3), are measured between structural members, ignoring finishes such as false ceilings, screeding, plastering, and flooring. The minimum height of rooms for habitation or office, for health, is 2.5 m.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Local reductions and maximum storey heights (paragraphs 3–5)
| Location | Height |
| --- | --- |
| Beams projecting internally from the face of external walls | 2 m to the underside is permissible, where 2.3 m is otherwise difficult |
| Beams over doorways | At the required door height |
| Staircases | Minimum clear height 2 m |
| Beams across narrow corridors where a doorway could have been provided | At the appropriate door height (the "notional doorway") |
| Flat, topmost floor | Not exceeding 4 m. Where the topmost floor is duplex or triplex, that maximum applies to only one storey of the unit |
| Flat, typical floor | Not exceeding 3.5 m |
| House | Not exceeding 4.5 m |

The range is what the Buildings Department may accept for regulation 23(3)(a) of the Building (Planning) Regulations.

**SD takeaway:** Set domestic storeys at not more than 3.5 m typical and 4 m at the topmost floor (4.5 m for a house), measured structure to structure, and drop to 2 m only at internal edge beams, stairs, and notional doorways.

### 2. Transfer plate not counted as gross floor area (paragraphs 6–7)
| Parameter | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Domestic blocks of not more than 40 storeys | Transfer-plate thickness not greater than the typical-floor storey height of the blocks. Where several domestic blocks sit on one plate, the lowest domestic block is the reference |
| Domestic blocks of more than 40 storeys, and non-domestic blocks | Thickness not greater than 4.5 m |
| Area | Total area of the transfer plate not more than twice the total gross floor area of the lowest floor of the building blocks atop |
| Building-services zone | Not counted in the thickness, but a convenient and safe means of access is required for maintenance and repair |

**SD takeaway:** Keep a transfer plate within the thickness and area limits above if it is to stay out of gross floor area, and give the services zone its own maintenance access.
