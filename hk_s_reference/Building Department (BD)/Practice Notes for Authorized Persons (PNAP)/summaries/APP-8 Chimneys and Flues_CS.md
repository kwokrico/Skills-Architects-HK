# APP-8 Chimneys and Flues
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision May 2021 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 45; first issue September 1977)

> Scope note: Design and construction of a chimney and a flue under section 44 of the Building (Construction) Regulation. Licensing of flue-burning equipment is a separate Environmental Protection Department and food-business matter.

## Regulatory Overview
A chimney or flue must comply with section 44 of the Building (Construction) Regulation, including fire safety and the position where it terminates under section 44(2) to (4). It is built of non-combustible materials of a nature, quality, and thickness that will not be unduly affected by heat, condensation, or products of combustion.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Heat, structure, and corrosion (paragraphs 3–5)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Insulation | Depends on fuel type and rate, materials of nearby parts, and use of surrounding areas. Provide calculations and justification. BS 5854:1980 gives guidelines |
| Steel chimney | Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Steel 2011, in particular Clause 13.2. Analyse wind-excited oscillations by aerodynamic methods and provide control. Circular chimneys may use the simplified method in Clause 13.2.8 |
| Corrosion and bimetallic action | Clause 13.2.7 of the Steel Code. Protective treatments on interior and exterior surfaces follow the manufacturer's instructions and Annex A of the Steel Code |
| Construction | Relevant parts of the Steel Code, BS EN 1856-1:2009, and BS 5854:1980 |
| Penetration of wall or roof | Suitable flashing or weather hood, allowing for different coefficients of expansion and bimetallic action |

**SD takeaway:** Design the steel chimney to Steel Code Clause 13.2, including wind oscillation, and detail a flashing or weather hood wherever the flue passes through a wall or roof.

### 2. Environmental referral (paragraph 6)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Control | Installation or alteration of flue-burning equipment and its chimney is subject to the Air Pollution Control Ordinance. Prior approval from the Environmental Protection Department may be required |
| Plans | Under PNAP ADM-2, general building plans that include chimneys or flues are referred to the Environmental Protection Department |
| Food business or offensive trade | The installation or alteration must also meet the relevant licensing requirements |

**SD takeaway:** Show chimneys and flues on the general building plans so they are referred to the Environmental Protection Department before approval.
