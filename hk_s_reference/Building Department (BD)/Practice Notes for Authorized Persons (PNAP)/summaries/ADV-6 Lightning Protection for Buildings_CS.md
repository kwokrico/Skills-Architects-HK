# ADV-6 Lightning Protection for Buildings
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision September 2017 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 156; first issue March 1993)

> Scope note: Asks authorized persons to consider lightning protection on new buildings and names the standards for the risk assessment. It does not reproduce the calculation method in those standards.

## Regulatory Overview
Authorized persons are requested to consider a lightning protection system in the design of a new building so as to reduce physical damage and life hazard from lightning. The need for protection, and the choice of measures, may be based on a risk assessment to BS EN 62305, AS/NZS 1768, or IEC 62305, which also advise on design, construction, and maintenance.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Buildings that, as a general rule, need protection (paragraph 2)
| Case | Examples given |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | Very tall or isolated |
| (b) | Exhibition or assembly |
| (c) | Caring or education, including schools, hospitals, child care centres, and residential care homes for elderly persons or persons with disabilities |
| (d) | Public utilities or public services. Exposed supports for electric lines are also subject to regulation 11 of the Electricity Supply Regulations (Cap. 406A) |
| (e) | Open areas such as outdoor swimming pools and sports fields |

**SD takeaway:** Allow a lightning-protection system, assessed to BS EN 62305, AS/NZS 1768, or IEC 62305, on a new building that is tall or isolated, or that is used for assembly, care, education, utilities, or open-air sport.
