# APP-171

**Official title:** Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Glass 2018

**In plain words:** Curtain wall design, structural sealant glazing, and performance tests must follow the Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Glass 2018 as amended by Appendix A (July 2020) and Appendix B (February 2024). This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issue of this practice note: February 2024. The amendments are on the Buildings Department website.
- July 2020, clause 7.1.1, retaining devices for four-sided structural sealant glazing (SSG). The clause applies where any point of the glass pane is 5 m or more above the finished floor of an accessible area on either side. Design the devices so the glass does not fall if the bond fails. Provide retaining devices (feature capping, angle, bracket, insert, or similar) at any two opposing edges. The device and the associated glass must resist 37% of the design wind pressure multiplied by a load factor γ of 1.0. The design wind pressure is the Code of Practice on Wind Effects 2019 reference pressure, without adjustment factors. Self-weight is carried on the setting blocks.
- July 2020, Annex C clause C2. Torsional inertia J uses depth d and thickness t: J = d × t³ / 3 × (1 − 0.63 × t/d). For glass fins, shear modulus G = 28,700 N/mm² and elastic modulus E = 70,000 N/mm².
- Other July 2020 text changes: gaskets on both sides of the glass pane (a wording correction), and deglazing-test wording covering factory quality.
- February 2024, Figure 6.1: the 8 mm diameter weep holes for external application are deleted from the typical glass balustrade details.
- February 2024, clause 8.3.1(b): the repeated positive and negative pressure, p2, equals the net wind pressure P on the mullion of the representative performance-test portion under the Wind Code 2019, with at least 5 pulses. If the Wind Code 2004 was used for the curtain wall, p2 = cp × qz for that part.
- February 2024, Annex B: the failure-load symbol is revised to W_max.

**What to submit**
- Curtain wall and structural sealant designs, and the performance tests, must reflect the amended Glass Code clauses.
- The registered structural engineer (RSE) submission should show the retaining-device strength check and which wind reference pressure was used.

**Watch-outs**
- Four-sided SSG at 5 m or more, with no opposing-edge retaining devices sized for 37% of the wind reference pressure.
- A performance test that still uses an old definition of p2 on a Wind Code 2019 project.
- Relying on the Figure 6.1 weep-hole detail of 8 mm diameter, which was deleted.
