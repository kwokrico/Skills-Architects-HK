# APP-40 Hotel Development
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision July 2023 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 111; first issue February 1985)

> Scope note: When the Building Authority may treat a hotel, or the hotel part of a building, as non-domestic for site coverage and plot ratio under Regulation 23A of the Building (Planning) Regulations, and which back-of-house areas may be disregarded from gross floor area. The regulation applies to bona fide hotels, including extensions of existing hotels, and not to service apartments. An alteration that adds gross floor area or building bulk is reassessed under the current regulation.

## Regulatory Overview
The concession depends on a site suitable for a hotel, basic and ancillary hotel facilities, a central air-conditioning system and a centralised hot-water system, and evidence that the hotel will be licensed under the Hotel and Guesthouse Accommodation Ordinance, Cap. 349, and a letter from an appointed hotel operator may be accepted as that evidence. Plant rooms of reasonable size for those central systems may be disregarded under regulation 23(3)(b), subject to the prerequisites and the overall 10% gross-floor-area concession cap in PNAP APP-151.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Suitability and the 5% back-of-house cap (paragraphs 3–8 and Appendix A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Site | Generally suitable if a hotel is permitted in Column 1 of the outline zoning plan, or section 16 permission has been obtained. Scale is compared with the intensity permitted for surrounding developments under the plan, the lease, or the regulations. Adequate streets must serve the extra traffic. Transport, parking, and loading facilities are provided |
| 100 or more guest rooms | Parking and loading or unloading normally in accordance with the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines. The Transport Department's assessment of traffic impact, loading, and parking is taken into account for every site |
| Floor space disregarded under regulation 23A(3)(b) | Size reasonable, location practical, design genuine, accessible to hotel staff only, and abuse unlikely. Unless there are strong justifications, that gross floor area is not expected to exceed 5% of the total gross floor area of the hotel |
| Not disregarded as a unique supporting facility | Offices for administration and management of the hotel, under regulation 23A(3)(b)(iv) |
| Back-of-house that may be disregarded | Linen store; laundry; carpentry workshop; mechanical or electrical workshop; dry goods store; food store; beverage store; furniture store; staff canteen; staff changing rooms; staff rest room; staff toilet that does not form part of, and does not solely serve, the administration office |
| Required for every hotel | Linen store, staff toilet, and reception counter or lobby. The reception is accountable |
| Required where there are more than 10 guest rooms | Dry goods store, and an administration and management office, which is accountable |
| Front-of-house, accountable | Reception, shopping, dining, business centre, and function rooms |
| Combined back-of-house and front-of-house | Minimum 3% of the total gross floor area of the hotel |

**SD takeaway:** To use non-domestic site coverage and plot ratio, provide central air-conditioning and hot water, keep staff-only back-of-house within 5% of hotel gross floor area, and provide linen, staff toilets, and a reception in every hotel.

### 2. Change of use (paragraphs 9–10)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Existing building to hotel | Assessed on the same criteria |
| Unauthorised change of use | Regulations 23A(8) and (9). The Buildings Department monitors with advice from the Licensing Authority |

**SD takeaway:** Do not convert a building to hotel use, or enlarge an existing hotel, without re-checking regulation 23A, because an unauthorised change of use can be sanctioned under regulations 23A(8) and (9).
