# APP-156

**Official title:** Design and Construction Requirements for Energy Efficiency of Residential Buildings

**In plain words:** A new residential building, meaning a domestic building under the Buildings Ordinance and excluding hotels, guesthouses, residential care homes for the elderly (RCHE), residential care homes for persons with disabilities (RCHD), similar air-conditioned premises, and alterations and additions (A&A) or a change of use that does not create a new residential building, must meet wall, roof, and residents’ recreation heat limits, and glass light and reflectance limits, before the Buildings Department will grant the APP-151 gross floor area (GFA) concessions for green and amenity features and for non-mandatory plant rooms and services. Genuine sunshades that cut that heat gain are left out of GFA and site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issue September 2014. Last major content revision July 2024. This revision September 2025. Paragraphs 5, 6(b), and 17 were amended.
- Residential thermal transfer value of the walls (RTTVWall) must not exceed 12.5 W/m² (previously 14 W/m²).
- RTTV of the roof (RTTVRoof) must not exceed 3.5 W/m² (previously 4 W/m²).
- Overall thermal transfer value of residents’ recreational facilities (OTTVRRF) must not exceed 20 W/m² for a tower (previously 21 W/m²) or 40 W/m² for a podium (previously 50 W/m²). Calculate it under the Code of Practice for Overall Thermal Transfer Value in Buildings 1995.
- Visible light transmittance of glass (VLTGlass) must be at least 50% for glass in the building envelope (curtain wall, cladding, skylight, window, or door) of the residential building and the residents’ recreational facilities (RRF). The visible-light rule applies only to glass in prescribed windows under Building (Planning) Regulations 30 and 31.
- External reflectance of glass (ERGlass) must not be more than 20%.
- Further metrics are in the Buildings Department “Guidelines on Design and Construction Requirements for Energy Efficiency of Residential Buildings” and in the 1995 overall thermal transfer value code.
- A sunshade projection of more than 750 mm from the external wall needs a quantitative assessment. A projection of more than 1.5 m is generally not expected. A projection of 750 mm or less is treated as not blocking prescribed windows. Projection over a street under Buildings Ordinance section 31(1) is generally not allowed. A case exemption is possible.
- The revised OTTVRRF limit in paragraph 6(b) applies to a new general building plan (GBP), or a major revision of a GBP, submitted for approval on or after 31 December 2025.
- The natural ventilation for thermal comfort (NVTC) figure is the percentage of habitable space that meets the Guidelines. It is collected for research and benchmarking.

**What to submit**
- On the first GBP, include a statement that the design will meet RTTVWall, RTTVRoof, OTTVRRF, VLTGlass, and ERGlass. Full calculations need not be attached to those first plans.
- After GBP approval, and before consent to start building works, submit Summary Sheet Appendix A (RTTV) and Appendix B (OTTVRRF) showing paragraph 6(a), 6(b), and 6(d).
- Before the occupation permit (OP), put the final RTTVWall, RTTVRoof, OTTVRRF, VLTGlass, ERGlass, and glass shading coefficients into the GBP for record.
- With the OP application, submit an Energy Efficiency Report. Include the RTTV calculations on the Guidelines Appendix III forms. Include OTTVRRF Forms OTTV 1 to 4 where they apply. Include record plans, material test certificates or published specifications, the final Appendices A and B, and the NVTC compliance extent on Guidelines Appendix VI.
- For materials not on the list, take RTTV and OTTV properties from a reliable source. Give the source background and why it suits local use. That helps consent and OP processing.
- After the OP, the Buildings Department uploads the development’s RTTV and OTTVRRF to its website. It does not upload the NVTC extent.

**Watch-outs**
- Missing the RTTV, OTTV, visible-light, or reflectance limits puts the APP-151 GFA concessions at risk.
- Leaving the compliance statement off the first plans, or missing Appendix A or B before consent, can delay or refuse consent or the OP.
- A false or incomplete Energy Efficiency Report, or incomplete material certificates, at OP is a documentary liability for the authorized person (AP) and the registered structural engineer (RSE).
- A sunshade over a street, or a projection of more than 750 mm without an assessment, is a problem. Treat residents’ recreation facilities under the OTTV control, not under the residential wall and roof values.
- A hotel, guesthouse, or care home with a similar air-conditioning profile is outside this note’s “residential building” definition.
