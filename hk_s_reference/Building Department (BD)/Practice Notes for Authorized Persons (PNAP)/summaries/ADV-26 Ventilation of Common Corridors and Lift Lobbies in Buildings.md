# ADV-26

**Official title:** Ventilation of Common Corridors and Lift Lobbies in Buildings

**In plain words:** The Building (Planning) Regulations already require natural ventilation for rooms used for habitation, as an office, or as a kitchen. This note goes further and asks you to ventilate common corridors, lift lobbies, and entrance lobbies as well, for indoor hygiene. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). It can affect lobby openings and the space needed for ducts.

**Numbers that matter**
- Promote ventilation of common corridors, lift lobbies, and entrance lobbies at design stage. If natural ventilation is not feasible, provide mechanical ventilation.
- For a mechanical system: supply enough fresh air for the people you expect; design the intake and the exhaust so they do not contaminate each other or short-circuit; and make the system and the ductwork easy to reach for maintenance.
- The Buildings Department keeps these recommendations under review. There is no mandatory checklist in this note.

**What to submit**
- No dedicated form. Show the lobby and corridor ventilation on the design so it can be reviewed with the plans.

**Watch-outs**
- A fresh-air intake that draws in contaminated air, or a mechanical system that short-circuits, defeats the hygiene purpose of ventilating the corridor and the lobby.
