# ADV-31 Building External Finishes - Wet-fixed Tiles
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision July 2021 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 303; first issue July 2008)

> Scope note: Design and workmanship for ceramic or mosaic tiles wet-fixed on cement-sand rendering to an external wall, so that detachment is minimised. Certain external rendering, external wall tiles, and roof finishes on an existing building may be minor works under Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and PNAP APP-147. Alternative products may be used with professional judgement.

## Regulatory Overview
Wet-fixed tiles are adhered to the rendering by a proprietary adhesive or a cement-sand slurry, and the rendering is bonded to the wall by spatter dash and/or a bonding agent. Thermal movement, water penetration, and pollution govern the choice of tile and bedding and the spacing and width of movement joints.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Joints, tile size, and water absorption (Appendix A, paragraphs 2, 11–16, and 20–21)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Joints between tiles | Usually 8 mm to 10 mm, and in any case not less than 3 mm, so that grout can be inserted |
| Movement joints | Form them to the full depth of the rendering at the time of rendering, and seal them. Provide them at regular intervals between panels, where tiling abuts a restraining surface, and over construction, control, or movement joints in the substrate. Fix expanded metal lathing over cold joints before rendering |
| Sealant | Must take the anticipated movement without losing adhesion, and resist water and ultraviolet light |
| Grout | Preferably type CG2 to BS EN 13888:2009. Start grouting as soon as the adhesive bed has hardened sufficiently |
| Large tiles | Control the weight in the design. A tile larger than 0.1 m² is secured by mechanical means |
| Water absorption of ceramic tiles | Not more than 0.5% up to first-floor height, and not more than 3% above first-floor height |
| Poor water absorption | Not recommended for an external wall. If used, apply a special polymer-modified adhesive to the manufacturer’s recommendations, and test compatibility if in doubt |
| Cement slurry | Soak the tiles in water for a minimum of 30 minutes and stack them to drain surface water immediately before tiling. Dampen the rendering. When a proprietary adhesive is used, neither the tiles nor the rendering should be too wet |
| Mosaic | For pre-grouted paper-faced mosaic, the adhesive covers the entire area of each tessera |

**SD takeaway:** Joint the tiles at not less than 3 mm, mechanically fix any tile larger than 0.1 m², and keep ceramic water absorption to 0.5% up to first-floor height and 3% above it.

### 2. Substrate and rendering (Appendix A, paragraphs 3–10 and 23)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Substrate | Rigid and sound. Remove honeycombs, loose particles, dirt, oil, grease, dust, paint, and efflorescence. Dampen it, but do not leave it too wet |
| Spatter dash | Preferably within 24 hours after striking the formwork. If later, prepare remedial measures, including a bonding agent, with the authorized person and registered structural engineer |
| Waiting | About six weeks for the concrete to dry and shrink before rendering. Each intermediate coat: several days, or the manufacturer’s period. The final coat: cure and leave to dry for at least two weeks |
| Thickness | Each coat not less than 8 mm and not greater than 16 mm. Total thickness not more than 20 mm. A proprietary product follows the manufacturer’s thickness |
| Uneven substrate | Uneven or over-thick rendering can debond. Consider reinforcement or expanded metal lathing anchored to the concrete, or a proprietary adhesion system |
| Sequence | Tile downwards where practicable, after deformation from the storeys above has occurred. Tap the rendering and rectify debonding before the tiles go on |
| Keys | Spatter dash, or mechanical scratching or bush hammering. Projecting horizontal fins that divide the tiles into panels are encouraged, to carry the self-weight |
| Metal reinforcement | Austenitic stainless steel wire, preferably 2.5 mm diameter, welded to a mesh of about 50 mm by 50 mm, with austenitic stainless steel fixings. Galvanised reinforcement is only for limited sheltered areas and is not recommended externally or where constantly exposed to moisture |
| Manufactured sand | May replace river sand. For private projects, follow Technical Circular (Works) No. 8/2018 |

**SD takeaway:** Keep each render coat between 8 mm and 16 mm and the total at not more than 20 mm, after about six weeks of concrete drying, and do not use galvanised mesh on an exposed wall.

### 3. Pull-off tests and maintenance (paragraphs 5–6 and Appendix A, paragraphs 28–30)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Minor works | Designated external rendering, external wall tiles, and roof finishes may use the simplified requirements instead of prior approval and consent |
| Pull-off | At least three samples of each type of wall tile for every ten typical floors, or part, of a building block, for tiles on rendering over concrete, direct on concrete, or fixed with slurry on rendering over concrete. The authorized person or registered structural engineer selects the locations. Do not take samples from tiles over a waterproofing layer |
| Laboratory | A laboratory accredited by the Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme for that test, reported on a HOKLAS endorsed certificate |
| Before work starts | The contractor develops the pull-off proposal, acceptance criteria, and non-compliance action with the authorized person and registered structural engineer |
| Infra-red | Thermographic scanning by a specialist may supplement the tests to locate cavities |
| In use | Inspect joints and surfaces regularly and repair water paths early. Recommend that owners of a new building arrange periodic debonding surveys by hammer tapping, supplemented as appropriate by infra-red scanning or another void-detection method |

**SD takeaway:** Specify a pull-off test of at least three samples of each tile type on every ten typical floors, chosen by the authorized person or structural engineer and kept off the waterproofing.
