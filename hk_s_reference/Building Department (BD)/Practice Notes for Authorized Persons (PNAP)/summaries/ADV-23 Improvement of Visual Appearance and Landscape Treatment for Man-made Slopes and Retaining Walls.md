# ADV-23

**Official title:** Improvement of Visual Appearance and Landscape Treatment for Man-made Slopes and Retaining Walls

**In plain words:** The Building Authority strongly recommends that new slopes, upgraded slopes, and maintained slopes look like landscape, not like a sprayed concrete face. Shotcrete or chunam is a last resort. This note does not grant gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). A smaller slope, or a retaining structure that is not visually huge, is easier to blend in and can free usable site area.

**Numbers that matter**
- If shotcrete or chunam is used, avoid it over the bottom 3 m of the slope, which is the part people see. Provide toe-wall planters, planter boxes, tree pits, creeper holes, and colour or pigment. Tree rings must be large enough for existing trees.
- On a consequence-to-life Category 3 soil cut slope (see the former PNAP 234), generally do not use shotcrete or chunam. Prefer a vegetated cover in upgrading and maintenance where safety allows. Where a hard finish is needed for appearance, stone pitching is preferred.
- On rock of Grade I to III, generally do not use shotcrete or chunam where the joints are tight and the average spacing is more than 60 mm. It may be needed where joint spacing is less than 60 mm, apertures are open, or the rock weathers quickly. Confirm that with an experienced geotechnical engineer or engineering geologist. Provide weepholes as Figure 5.4 of the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes shows.
- To hold fractured rock, prefer wire mesh over shotcrete where you can. Use masonry facing or a surface finish on a large buttress or dentition, and break up a large uniform finish.
- Keep healthy trees. Structural support, if needed, should not be visually obtrusive. Felling or transplanting is a last resort. Have an unhealthy or decaying tree inspected by an experienced person, and submit the inspection report to the Building Authority for record before the certificate of completion of the site formation works.
- After hydroseeding, provide a biodegradable protective fabric, whatever the slope gradient. Prefer low-maintenance native species. Avoid species that need heavy pruning or watering.
- References: Geotechnical Manual for Slopes, 2nd edition; GEO Publication No. 1/2000; GEO Report No. 116; and the former PNAP 267 and Lands Administration Office Practice Note 8/2002 on tree felling.
- Specify maintenance requirements in the slope maintenance manual. Get the relevant authority’s approval before any tree is removed, where the lease or the legislation requires it.

**What to submit**
- Build the landscape measures into the formation, upgrading, or maintenance submission. Send the vegetation inspection report to the Building Authority before the site formation completion certificate.

**Watch-outs**
- Defaulting to shotcrete or chunam on a Category 3 soil cut, or on the visible bottom 3 m, without looking at alternatives.
- Felling a tree without prior government approval where the lease or the law requires it.
- Leaving an unhealthy tree unchecked, which is a fall hazard, or failing to lodge the inspection report before the formation completion certificate.
