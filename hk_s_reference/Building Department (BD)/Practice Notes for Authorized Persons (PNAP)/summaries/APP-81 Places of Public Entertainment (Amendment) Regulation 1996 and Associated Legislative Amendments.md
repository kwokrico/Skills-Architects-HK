# APP-81

**Official title:** Places of Public Entertainment (Amendment) Regulation 1996 and Associated Legislative Amendments

**In plain words:** From 28 June 1996, design and construction rules for a place of public entertainment (PPE) moved into the building regulations and Part III of the means-of-escape code, while licensing stayed under the PPE Regulation. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage, but the new planning rules, the cinema definition, and upper-tier barriers still shape the layout.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issued June 1996. Re-issued August 2009. Related to the content of practice note (PNAP) 54.
- The amendment regulation took effect on 28 June 1996 (28.6.96).
- Protective barriers at the outer edge of any upper tier, and the definition of “cinema”, are in the Building (Construction) (Amendment) Regulation 1996.
- Situation, planning, and design of these places are in new Part VIA of the Building (Planning) (Amendment) Regulation 1996.
- Means of escape are in Part III of the 1996 Means of Escape (MOE) Code, taken from the repealed PPE rules and part of PNAP 54.
- Under Buildings Ordinance section 39(2), the amendment regulations and MOE Code Part III do not apply to works already being carried out, or already consented to start, on or before 28 June 1996.

**What to submit**
- For PPE works consented after 28 June 1996, comply with the transferred regulations and MOE Part III.
- A separate practice note on the revised MOE Code follows (see APP-82).

**Watch-outs**
- Designing to the old PPE Regulation safety clauses misses the Building (Construction) Regulations, Building (Planning) Regulations Part VIA, and MOE Part III.
- Misreading the 28 June 1996 cut-off under section 39(2) applies the wrong rules.
- The cinema definition and the upper-tier protective barrier are easy to miss.
