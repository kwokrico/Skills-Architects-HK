# APP-22

**Official title:** Dewatering in Foundation and Basement Excavation Works

**In plain words:** This note requires the dewatering method, and the precautions against settlement of neighbouring buildings, streets, and land, to satisfy the Building Authority. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- The plans must show adjoining foundations and utilities; the dewatering proposal, including ground treatment such as grout or recharge; the method and sequence; and instrumentation that monitors the effects, including groundwater change. The monitoring scheme states (i) the limiting criteria for movement and groundwater pressure, and the action if they are exceeded, (ii) how often readings are taken, and (iii) how the records will be made available.
- Support the plans with a site-investigation report (groundwater, geology, permeability, compressibility, consolidation, and particle size), a condition report on adjoining buildings, shoring or underpinning if it is needed, and an assessment of the effects of excavation and dewatering.
- The authorized person and the registered structural engineer supervise the work to the code and the approved plans, and they check that the design assumptions still hold. A full-time experienced supervisor is used where necessary. Piezometer and settlement records are available at all times, and copies go to the Building Authority periodically. Also follow the groundwater and drawdown sections of the Civil Engineering General Specification, and BS 8004:1986.

**What to submit**
- Include the monitoring scheme and the supporting reports with the foundation or basement plans. During the works, send copies of the monitoring records to the Building Authority periodically.

**Watch-outs**
- Dewatering without an assessment of utilities and adjoining buildings, or without a monitoring scheme that sets a trigger and an action, does not satisfy the Building Authority.
- Exceeding a limit without taking the action set out in the scheme leaves you liable for settlement of adjoining land and buildings.
