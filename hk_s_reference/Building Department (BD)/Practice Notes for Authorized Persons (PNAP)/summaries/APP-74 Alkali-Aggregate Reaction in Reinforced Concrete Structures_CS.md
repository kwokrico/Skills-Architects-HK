# APP-74 Alkali-Aggregate Reaction in Reinforced Concrete Structures
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued April 1995 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: A specification limit to reduce the risk of alkali-aggregate reaction in concrete for private developments. The reaction needs reactive minerals in the aggregate, sufficient alkali, and sufficient moisture. It may be omitted where the authorized person or registered structural engineer is of the opinion that the element will not be subject to moisture ingress throughout its design life.

## Regulatory Overview
Authorized persons and registered structural engineers should consider incorporating the Appendix A clauses. Where they do, the plans for approval must state that the reactive alkali of the concrete, expressed as the equivalent sodium oxide per cubic metre, does not exceed 3.0 kg, determined in accordance with Appendix A.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. The 3.0 kg limit and how it is calculated (paragraphs 7–8 and Appendix A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Limit | Equivalent sodium oxide not exceeding 3.0 kg per cubic metre of concrete |
| Sum | Equivalent Na2O = A + B + C. A is the acid-soluble alkalis of cement, admixtures, and water. B is one-sixth of the total alkalis of pulverised fuel ash. C is 0.76 times the chloride ion of the aggregate |
| Cement | BS 4550: Part 2:1970, excluding amendment AMD 7285 of July 1992. Average of the latest 25 daily determinations plus twice the standard deviation |
| Admixtures | BS 1881: Part 124:1988 |
| Water | APHA, 17th edition, 1989, sections 3500-K and 3500-Na |
| Pulverised fuel ash | Same BS 4550 method. Average of 25 weekly determinations plus twice the standard deviation |
| Aggregates | Chloride ion to BS 812: Part 4:1976 |
| Certificates | HOKLAS-endorsed certificates not older than six months, the calculation of reactive alkali, and new certificates at quarterly intervals |

**SD takeaway:** If the concrete can get wet in service, state on the structural plans that reactive alkali does not exceed 3.0 kg of equivalent sodium oxide per cubic metre, and keep HOKLAS certificates current.
