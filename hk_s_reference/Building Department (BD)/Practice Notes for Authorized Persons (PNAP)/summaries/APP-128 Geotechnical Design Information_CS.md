# APP-128 Geotechnical Design Information
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision December 2005 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 274; first issue January 2003)

> Scope note: How geotechnical assessments, reports, plans, and computer-program output should be organised for submission. Design standards are in the documents listed in Technical Guidance Note No. 1 and in the practice notes cited for each work type. It is not a slope or foundation design code.

## Regulatory Overview
Geotechnical feasibility is to be examined at general building plan stage where Building (Administration) Regulation 8(1)(ba) requires a geotechnical assessment. The works must keep an adequate margin of safety for the site and must not impair adjacent ground and facilities.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Early assessment and ground model (paragraphs 2–5)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Standards | Technical Guidance Note No. 1, a list of guidance used by the Geotechnical Engineering Office as de facto standards |
| When | If the site meets one or more of the criteria for a geotechnical assessment under regulation 8(1)(ba). Criteria and contents are in PNAP 78 |
| Investigation | Start from a desk-study geological model, then fieldwork to confirm the model, the design parameters, and the uncertainties. Guidance in PNAP 132 |
| Safety | Adequate margin of safety during construction and thereafter, and no inadequate margin, impaired stability, or damage to adjacent ground and facilities |

**SD takeaway:** Test geotechnical feasibility at general building plan stage wherever regulation 8(1)(ba) applies, before the layout is fixed.

### 2. Reports, plans, and which note applies (paragraphs 6–13)
| Work | Where the requirements sit |
| --- | --- |
| Every geotechnical design report | An executive summary, and a schedule of geotechnical design assumptions and parameters, with justifications from investigation, laboratory tests, and groundwater monitoring |
| Design review during construction | Particularly for complex ground or unconventional schemes. Guidance in PNAP 246 |
| Plans | Differentiate proposed from existing works (regulation 14). Show mutual effects with existing site conditions. Avoid inaccurate survey, missing temporary support or heavy-rainfall precautions, unclear sequence, and discrepancies with the report |
| Site formation | Regulation 8(1)(bb). Filling guidance in PNAP 55 |
| Excavation and lateral support | Regulation 8(1)(bc) and PNAP 148. Dewatering information in PNAP 74 |
| Scheduled Area No. 1, and foundation works in Scheduled Area Nos. 2 and 4 | PNAP 85 and PNAP 161. Designated Area of Northshore Lantau in PNAP 283 |
| Foundations near geotechnical features or on sloping ground | Must not cause damming of groundwater, extra loading, or vibration, in compliance with Building (Construction) Regulation 23. Foundation-plan contents in regulation 8(1)(d) |
| Demolition of a building that supports adjacent ground | Stability of that ground. Do not stockpile debris near geotechnical features. Regulations 8(3) and 8(4) and PNAP 71 |

**SD takeaway:** Put a schedule of geotechnical assumptions in every design report, and do not stockpile demolition debris next to a slope or wall.

### 3. Computer programs (paragraphs 14–15 and Appendix A)
For a pre-accepted program (PNAP 79), include the program name, version, developer, Buildings Department reference, and validity period; the accepted scope and limitations; justification of the mathematical model; assumptions and parameters; how input was prepared from the ground; salient output such as factors of safety, design envelopes of moment, shear and displacement, and ground displacement; and how the output is applied. The registered geotechnical engineer completes the Appendix A statement and confirms responsibility for executing the programs.

**SD takeaway:** Name the pre-accepted program, its validity period, and the factor of safety or displacement the design uses.
