# APP-104

**Official title:** Exclusion of Floor Areas for Recreational Use

**In plain words:** This note sets when residential recreational floor space may be left out of gross floor area (GFA), by modifying Building (Planning) Regulation (B(P)R) 23(3)(a) under section 42 of the Buildings Ordinance (BO), for facilities that benefit owners and residents generally. The exclusion changes GFA, it is capped, and it must meet PNAP APP-151 except for covered landscaped and play areas under paragraph 8.

**Numbers that matter**
- The cap in Table 1 covers recreational GFA, including voids, plant rooms (including swimming-pool filtration plant), covered courts, and similar space that serves only recreation. Use whichever is greater: the percentage of total domestic GFA, or the absolute area where one is stated.
- Total domestic GFA up to 25,000 m²: 5%.
- More than 25,000 m² up to 50,000 m²: 4.5% or 1,250 m².
- More than 50,000 m² up to 75,000 m²: 4.0% or 2,250 m².
- More than 75,000 m² up to 100,000 m²: 3.5% or 3,000 m².
- More than 100,000 m² up to 125,000 m²: 3.0% or 3,500 m².
- More than 125,000 m²: 2.5% or 3,750 m².
- Covered landscaped areas and children’s play areas under the domestic tower footprint are outside Table 1. They are limited to 5% of total domestic GFA (PNAP APP-42) and still need the APP-151 pre-requisites.
- A snack bar, mini-bar, or pantry may total not more than 10 m². A kitchen may be not more than 15 m², and only on a site with no commercial floor space.
- From 31 August 2025, for a new general building plan (GBP) or a major revision, provide at least one elderly-friendly toilet and elderly fitness equipment under the Design Manual: Barrier Free Access. If that toilet is also the required accessible toilet, provide another elderly-friendly facility as well as the fitness equipment. Show them on the plans. The amount should match the recreational design. Chapter 6 of the Design Manual gives examples.
- Previously PNAP 229. First issued January 1999. Last major design-concession alignment January 2011. This revision July 2025.
- File reference: BD GP/BORD/48 (II).

**What to submit**
- Show why the facilities are needed, and show the overall size, headroom, mix of facilities, and how they will be run only for owners and residents.
- Before plans are approved, the developer or owner must give a letter of undertaking. It must designate the recreational facilities as common area in the deed of mutual covenant (DMC), and bind the parties on control, operation, money, and maintenance. A later change needs a revised undertaking and layout plan.
- Exemption is by modification of the relevant B(P)Rs. The undertaking must be registered in the Land Registry before the occupation permit (OP) application. The recreational area may be used only as shown on the approved plans. Any other use or user needs prior BA consent.
- The OP will not be issued without proof that the undertaking and layout are registered, and without the GFA concession being disclosed on the approved building plans as required by PNAP ADM-2.
- Commonly accepted active facilities in Appendix A include a swimming pool, multi-purpose or squash space, basketball or football, tennis or badminton, children’s play, a games room, indoor golf, table-tennis, fitness, a gymnasium, weight training, aerobic or exercise space, bowling, skating, volleyball, rock climbing, and dance or yoga.
- Commonly accepted passive facilities include sitting or lounge space, billiards, sauna, jacuzzi or spa, reading, study or library, a function room, computer or video space, music or karaoke, a club management office or reception, a staff room, the snack bar size above, first aid, the kitchen size above, changing and shower rooms, lavatories, and stores.

**Watch-outs**
- A luxury club, or a club with restaurants or facilities for an exclusive membership or a commercial business, is outside this concession. Commercial use generally is outside it. A reasonably sized kitchen that only supports recreation, on a site with no commercial floor space, may still be considered.
- Use must stay exclusive to owners, residents, and bona fide visitors. Commercialising the space removes the basis for the concession.
- Capped items still need APP-151 compliance. Covered landscape and play areas under paragraph 8 are the exception to the APP-151 cap, but they still need the APP-151 pre-requisites.
- The OP is blocked if the DMC undertaking is not registered, or if the ADM-2 disclosure is incomplete.
- The BA will prosecute a breach of the exemption conditions. The AP must warn developers and purchasers.
- Leaving out the elderly toilet or fitness equipment, once the 31 August 2025 rule applies, risks refusal of the plans.
