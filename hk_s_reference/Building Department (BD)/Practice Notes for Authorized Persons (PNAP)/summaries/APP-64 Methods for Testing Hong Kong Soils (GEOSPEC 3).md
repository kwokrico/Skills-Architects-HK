# APP-64

**Official title:** Methods for Testing Hong Kong Soils (GEOSPEC 3)

**In plain words:** Soil tests for investigation, design and construction should follow Geospec 3, and only endorsed reports from laboratories accredited for those tests are accepted. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage, but poor fill tests can stop acceptance of a certificate of completion for filling works.

**Numbers that matter**
- Geospec 3, Model Specification for Soil Testing, covers 32 expanded Phase I tests and 7 Phase II tests.
- From 1 July 2004, only Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme (HOKLAS) endorsed certificates are accepted, and only from laboratories accredited to Geospec 3 for those tests.
- Phase I tests in GEO Report 36 that were done before 1 July 2004 are still acceptable if they carry a HOKLAS endorsement. Appendix C maps them to the Geospec 3 equivalents.
- The Building Authority may refuse approval or consent, in the context of Buildings Ordinance section 17(1)(6)(b), if soil tests that support plans or conditions are not to Geospec 3.

**What to submit**
- Use only a HOKLAS-accredited laboratory for the tests you specify, and submit only HOKLAS-endorsed certificates and reports.
- Ground-investigation plans in the Scheduled Areas, site-formation plans, and other plans with geotechnical content may be refused if they depart from Geospec 3.
- A certificate of completion for filling may not be accepted if relative compaction or in-situ density tests are not to Geospec 3 (the filling-works note in the PNAP 55 lineage).
- Follow the credibility checks: proper test schedules and soil-type information; supervise sampling, storage, transport and preparation; document and vet the results; do not reproduce a HOKLAS report except in full; clear any doubt with the Hong Kong Accreditation Service before you submit.

**Watch-outs**
- After the 1 July 2004 cut-off, a report that is not HOKLAS-endorsed, or not from a laboratory accredited to Geospec 3 for that test, is not accepted.
- Do not reproduce part of a HOKLAS report, and do not knowingly misstate test facts. The Building Authority can sanction that.
- Poor sample handling can make the test unrepresentative.
