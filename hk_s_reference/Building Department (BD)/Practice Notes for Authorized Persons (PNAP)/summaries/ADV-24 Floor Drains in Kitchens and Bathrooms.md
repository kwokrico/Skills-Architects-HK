# ADV-24

**Official title:** Floor Drains in Kitchens and Bathrooms

**In plain words:** Every sanitary fitment, including a floor drain, needs a water-seal trap before it joins the common drainage stack. Water in a floor-drain trap can dry out if nothing replenishes it. This note allows you to divert a little waste water into that trap so the seal stays wet. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- You may divert some waste water from a waste fitment to the U-trap of a kitchen or bathroom floor drain, so the seal stays charged.
- The design must still comply with the Building (Standards of Sanitary Fitments, Plumbing, Drainage Works and Latrines) Regulations. Discharge must flow smoothly, and waste must not flow back out of the floor drain.

**What to submit**
- If the arrangement needs a modification of those regulations, apply. It is considered on its merits.

**Watch-outs**
- A dried U-trap lets sewer gas into the room. That was a hygiene lesson of the SARS period.
- A badly designed diversion branch can make waste flow back out of the floor drain.
