# ADV-3

**Official title:** Standardization of Floor Numbering

**In plain words:** Number floors in one logical run, so emergency services, the occupation permit (OP), and the Rating and Valuation Department all use the same labels. This does not change how gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC) is counted. A plant floor, a refuge floor, a car park, and a sky garden still take a number in that run.

**Numbers that matter**
- Except the ground floor and the roof, use a logical consecutive numerical series.
- Above ground, numbers go up: for example ground floor, 1/F, 2/F, 3/F.
- Below ground, a higher basement number means a lower floor: for example ground floor, Basement 1, Basement 2, Basement 3.
- The order does not skip a floor because of its use. A shop, car park, clubhouse, communal podium, sky garden, refuge floor, plant room, or observation deck still takes the next number.
- The only omissions that are accepted are 4, 13, and numbers that end in 4. Any other omission is not allowed.
- A non-numerical name on its own is not accepted. It is acceptable together with the normal number, for example “20/F Sky Garden”. An alias, or “also known as x/F”, is not accepted. Illogical, unconventional, or non-consecutive numbers are not accepted.
- Appendix A examples include B4/F to B1/F, LG/F, UG/F, G/F, CL/F for a cockloft, M/F for a mezzanine, dual labels such as “7/F (or 7/F Mechanical Plant Floor)”, “20/F (or 20/F Sky Garden)”, and “40/F (or 40/F Refuge Floor)”, and R/F for the roof.
- Display the floor number in Chinese and English, conspicuously, at the main entrance lobby, at lift lobbies, and on the walls of protected lobbies and landings of required staircases on each floor. Fireman’s-lift floor-served notices follow the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Access for Firefighting and Rescue. The Fire Services Department checks them before the occupation permit.
- This note does not apply if a sales brochure was available to the public and a copy was deposited with the Buildings Department by recorded delivery before 1 September 2010, or at least one unit was sold before that date, or the occupation permit application was submitted before that date.
- Floor numbers on the occupation permit follow the approved building plans. The Commissioner of Rating and Valuation adopts those numbers. The Department answers floor-name enquiries from the approved plans and the occupation permit. A departure from the approved description is not normally entertained.
- Bring this note to the client and to the conveyancing solicitors.

**What to submit**
- General building plans, and other plans to the Building Authority, with each floor numbered clearly. If the Department or another department finds the sequence illogical or confusing, the Department will advise you to amend it.

**Watch-outs**
- Skipping a floor other than 4, 13, or a number ending in 4, or using a marketing alias without the approved number, leads to advice to amend. A renaming request after approval is refused.
- A plant, refuge, or sky-garden floor without its consecutive number causes problems for emergency services, and for the occupation permit and the Fire Services notices.
- Floor numbers on the plans or the occupation permit that do not match the sales brochure are a client and solicitor problem. Warn them early.
- Missing the bilingual displays in the lobby, on the stairs, and at the lifts can hold up Fire Services acceptance on the way to the occupation permit.
