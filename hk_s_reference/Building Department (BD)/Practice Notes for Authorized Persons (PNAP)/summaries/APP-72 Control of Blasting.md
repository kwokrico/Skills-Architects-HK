# APP-72

**Official title:** Control of Blasting

**In plain words:** Blasting under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) and the Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295) needs a blasting assessment, an independent supervisor, and a separate permit from the Commissioner of Mines, and it affects programme and how much rock you can take out for a basement. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- The Mines Authority is the Commissioner of Mines in the Civil Engineering and Development Department.
- The blasting assessment is prepared by a competent person with at least 4 years’ experience in the design and supervision of blasting, and it is signed by the registered geotechnical engineer, the registered structural engineer, or both, as appropriate. Submit it with the site formation, excavation and lateral support, or foundation plans. If the need is found later, submit it with revised plans.
- Appendix A contents: plans of sensitive receivers; a study of topography, geology, groundwater and constraints; a condition survey; an effects assessment showing no injury or damage; preventive measures; action limits for vibration and air-overpressure, with evidence that stakeholders agree; an outline blast design; safety management; monitoring stations and criteria; protective measures, evacuation and road closure; explosives delivery logistics; and, if needed, whether an on-site store is feasible.
- The plans must show blasting areas, geology and groundwater, sensitive receivers, control criteria, instrumentation, and precautionary measures (road closure, evacuation, restricted hours), plus reporting requirements, and a note that the plans are to be read with the blasting assessment.
- Supervision conditions under Buildings Ordinance section 17: a competent supervisor, independent of the registered contractor, assisting the registered geotechnical engineer or registered structural engineer stream as a technically competent person grade T5, with at least 4 years’ experience; or one person who is both the T5 and the competent supervisor. Send curricula vitae to the Building Authority before blasting starts. Appendix B sets specific tasks for the T5 (with the supervisor) and for grade T3.
- Demolition by blasting is exceptional. Follow the Code of Practice for Demolition and the Appendix A items, signed by the registered structural engineer and the registered geotechnical engineer.
- The registered contractor also needs a Mines blasting permit, which may impose design, control and monitoring conditions.
- Near a railway, see PNAP 77 and PNAP 279 (the legacy numbers used in the text), and the General Specification for Civil Engineering Works.

**What to submit**
- Identify the need for blasting at the geotechnical assessment stage of the general building plans.
- Submit the blasting assessment and the marked-up plans with the site formation, excavation and lateral support, or foundation submission.
- The Building Authority accepts the supervision personnel’s curricula vitae before blasting.
- The Mines permit is separate from Buildings Department approval and consent.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not blast without the assessment, and do not blast without the Mines permit.
- Do not set sensitive-receiver limits without evidence of agreement.
- Do not use demolition blasting in a dense urban area without exceptional justification and compliance with the Code of Practice for Demolition.
- Supervision by the registered contractor’s own staff alone is not enough. The competent supervisor who assists the T5 must be independent, unless the same person holds both qualifications.
