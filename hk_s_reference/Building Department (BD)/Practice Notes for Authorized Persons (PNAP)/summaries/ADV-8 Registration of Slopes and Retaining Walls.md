# ADV-8

**Official title:** Registration of Slopes and Retaining Walls

**In plain words:** The Geotechnical Engineering Office keeps a Catalogue of Slopes for sizable man-made slopes and retaining walls. Each feature has a registration number. When you form or upgrade one, you submit the record with the completion certificate, and you tell the owner the number so it can go into the deed of mutual covenant. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Appendix A types: F is a fill slope of 15 degrees or steeper, with fill at least 1 m thick, and no associated wall. C is a cut slope with no associated wall. R is a retaining wall supporting a slope or platform flatter than 15 degrees. A series of walls that are each too small to register, on an overall slope steeper than 45 degrees, are combined by height. CR and FR are cut or fill with associated walls. DT is disturbed terrain, such as a repaired landslide scar or a composite cut and fill, on a natural gradient steeper than 15 degrees, or a group of slopes that are each below the height criteria but together are high enough to register.
- Registration criteria, Appendix B: a cut slope, including associated walls, and a retaining wall, higher than 3 m; a fill slope, including associated walls, higher than 5 m; a fill, including associated walls, lower than 5 m if it poses a direct risk to life at consequence-to-life Category 1 or 2 (see the former PNAP 234); and disturbed-terrain features that meet those height criteria in total.
- Examples that are not registered: a seawall or other marine structure on the water face; a dam or water-retaining structure, though a fill embankment in front of a service reservoir should be registered; a basement or screen wall that is part of a building and has its free-air side inside; a tunnel or shaft lining, though a free-standing portal slope or wall is registered; a culvert or covered nullah, though a free-standing open-nullah sidewall that meets the criteria is registered; a bridge abutment, though a fill embankment, reinforced fill, or slope is registered; and every temporary feature still under construction. An abandoned quarry face and a completed controlled tip are registered as cut or fill. A wall designed and built monolithic with a building is not registered. If one is already in the catalogue, tell the Geotechnical Engineering Office.
- Provide a 1:1000 site plan with a location plan, the Appendix C record sheet, and photographs. Provide the basic data as a soft copy in the Appendix D structure. The slope information system database can be downloaded from hkss.ced.gov.hk. Digital photographs are preferred.
- Maintenance responsibility is recorded by the Lands Department Slope Maintenance Responsibility Information Centre and on its website. For land outside the lot, read the lease, including cutting-away clauses and green-hatched-black areas.
- The Office returns a copy of the 1:1000 plan with the registration numbers of newly formed or modified features. Advise the owner to keep those numbers for maintenance records and to put them in the deed of mutual covenant.

**What to submit**
- The Appendix C proforma, with the soft copy and photographs, together with the certificate of completion of the site formation and/or the feature upgrading works.

**Watch-outs**
- Completing site formation or feature upgrading without sending the record sheet and the soft copy to the Geotechnical Engineering Office.
- Missing a low fill wall that is consequence-to-life Category 1 or 2. The “higher than 5 m” fill rule does not exempt it.
- Treating a free-standing wall as a non-registrable basement wall, or failing to tell the Office about a monolithic wall that is wrongly still in the catalogue.
- Leaving the owner without feature numbers weakens the maintenance trail in the deed of mutual covenant.
