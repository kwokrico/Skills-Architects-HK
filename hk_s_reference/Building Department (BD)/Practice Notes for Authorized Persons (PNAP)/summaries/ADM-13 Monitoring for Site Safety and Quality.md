# ADM-13

**Official title:** Monitoring for Site Safety and Quality

**In plain words:** The Site Monitoring Section inspects construction and demolition sites, concentrating on work that is unsafe or of poor quality where the public is affected. This note sets out what they look for and what the authorized person (AP), registered structural engineer (RSE), and registered geotechnical engineer (RGE) must do on site. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- The Section looks for: work that diverges from the approved plans; defective materials; missing precautionary measures; lack of supervision by the responsible people; departure from the approved or agreed sequence; workmen or plant operators who lack experience for a specially hazardous operation; failure to follow Building Authority conditions or orders; and a breach of the Buildings Ordinance or its subsidiary legislation.
- Inspections are more frequent for demolition, deep excavation, percussion piling, steep slope formation, high retaining walls, work projecting over a public thoroughfare, and heavy powered mechanical plant.
- Officers carry a warrant. They may inspect premises, enter land, check compliance, carry out tests, make openings, and take reasonable samples for the purposes of the Ordinance.
- The AP, RSE, and RGE should give ready access; issue the approved plans, agreed sequences, and Building Authority conditions to the registered contractor to keep on site; for minor works, issue the prescribed plans, works details, and supervision plans to the prescribed registered contractor on site; explain sequences, conditions, and orders to the contractor, with a translation if needed; meet the supervision requirements for building and demolition works; provide adequate precautions, especially against falling objects; tell the Building Authority as soon as possible of any contravention arising from the works; and put unsatisfactory matters right without delay.
- Selected minor works under construction are subject to audit inspections for site safety. They are not put through the full systematic approved-plan programme. The matters of concern, remedies, cooperation, and departmental referrals still apply as the case warrants.
- A contravention outside the Buildings Ordinance is referred to the appropriate government department.
- A similar note is issued to registered contractors. Bring this note to the client and the contractor.

**What to submit**
- No new plan under this note. The Section inspects in parallel with the New Buildings Division’s approval, consent, permits, licences, occupation permit, and performance tests.

**Watch-outs**
- A material departure from the approved plans, missing precautions, or weak supervision can lead to corrective advice, an order under Buildings Ordinance section 23 or 24A, prosecution, and disciplinary action, decided case by case.
- Failing to keep the approved plans, sequences, and conditions on site, or failing to notify a contravention promptly, raises the enforcement risk.
- Hazardous stages without extra supervision attract more frequent inspections and a higher chance of sanction.
