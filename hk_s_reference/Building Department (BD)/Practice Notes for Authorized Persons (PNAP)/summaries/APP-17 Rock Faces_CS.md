# APP-17 Rock Faces
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This update December 1996 | Buildings Department (first issued June 1979; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Which rock faces are "massive" under Building (Planning) Regulation 47, and what a site formation submission for a rock face must contain. Stability methods are summarised in Tables 5.6 and 5.7 of the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes. Shear-strength guidance is in Geoguide 1.

## Regulatory Overview
For regulation 47, only a widely jointed rock face, with joint spacing over 600 mm and with no unfavourably oriented joints, other discontinuities, or other defects, is a massive rock face, and any other slope is under Building (Planning) Regulation 27. Adequate measures are required to prevent uncontrolled rockfalls during construction, particularly where work on an existing rock face affects public safety.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the site formation plans must state (Appendix, paragraphs 4–5)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Assumptions | Joint spacing, persistence, orientation, infill, roughness, and aperture are often not fully known until excavation is nearly complete. Assumptions, and their basis, must be stated clearly and fully on the plans, together with provision for local support of the final face and, as appropriate, general support |
| Blasting | Controlled to avoid damage to the final face. Pre-split or smooth-face methods for the final face in suitable geological conditions |
| Expanding agents | State the method that prevents uncontrolled flow, and assess any adverse effect on stability |
| Survey immediately after excavation | Scale the intended final face by removing loose material. A qualified geotechnical engineer or engineering geologist, for example a Registered Professional Engineer (Geotechnical) with adequate rock-face experience, surveys it, including water seepages and weak or relatively impermeable zones. An unfavourably oriented persistent layer of kaolinitic clay in altered or weathered rock is the example given, and was a principal cause of the Fei Tsui Road landslide of 13 August 1995 |
| Drawings for approval | Large-scale engineering geological drawings, to Geoguide 3 descriptions, with dimensioned location and extent of all support and drainage measures; calculations confirming support design and overall stability; photographs of the scaled faces where physically possible |

**SD takeaway:** Treat a rock face as a regulation 27 slope unless joints are spaced over 600 mm and none are unfavourably oriented, and show pre-split finishing, assumed joints, and support on the site formation plans.
