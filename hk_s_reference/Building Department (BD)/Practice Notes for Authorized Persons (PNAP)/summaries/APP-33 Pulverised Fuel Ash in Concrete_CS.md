# APP-33 Pulverised Fuel Ash in Concrete
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision September 2001 | Buildings Department (first issued December 1982; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Conditions for using pulverised fuel ash as a partial cement replacement. Early strength can be lower than an ordinary Portland cement mix, especially in cold weather, so curing has to be effective.

## Regulatory Overview
Pulverised fuel ash may be used as a partial replacement for ordinary Portland cement only on the conditions in paragraph 2. Very early strengths may be lower than equivalent ordinary Portland cement mixes, particularly in cold weather.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Limits on pulverised fuel ash content (paragraph 2)
| Rule | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Separate constituent | Only with ordinary Portland cement, and complying with BS 3892: Part 1:1982, except that the criterion for maximum water requirement may not apply |
| Blended cement | Comply with BS 6588:1985, with a nominal pulverised fuel ash content not exceeding 25%. Do not also use pulverised fuel ash as a partial replacement in addition to blended cement |
| Cementitious content | Not exceeding 35% by mass of the cementitious content (ordinary Portland cement plus pulverised fuel ash) |
| Up to 25% | Usually only in normal construction |
| Above 25% | Special applications such as marine construction and massive pours, requiring expert advice and stringent site control. The authorized person and registered structural engineer must be satisfied there will be no adverse effect from removal of formwork, creep, and long-term deflection |
| Specification | State the content and the extent of use in the structural submissions. The concrete supplier's quality control must keep the finished concrete within the specification and the statutory requirements. Deviations are reported to the authorized person and registered structural engineer |

**SD takeaway:** Cap pulverised fuel ash at 25% of the cementitious content for normal work, never combine it with a blended cement that already contains it, and do not exceed 35% even for a marine or massive pour.
