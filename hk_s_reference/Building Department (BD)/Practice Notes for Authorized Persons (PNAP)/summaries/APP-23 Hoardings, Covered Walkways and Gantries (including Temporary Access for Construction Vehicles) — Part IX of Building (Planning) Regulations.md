# APP-23

**Official title:** Hoardings, Covered Walkways and Gantries (including Temporary Access for Construction Vehicles) — Part IX of Building (Planning) Regulations

**In plain words:** This note sets permits, widths, lighting, and renewal rules for hoardings, covered walkways, and gantries before works start. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). These are temporary works to protect the public.

**Numbers that matter**
- Erect and maintain hoardings, walkways, and gantries under a permit granted under Building (Planning) Regulations 65 and 66 before works start. The application under regulation 64 is six sets of plans, or one set if you use the Electronic Submission Hub. Show temporary vehicular access, post holes, traffic signs and provisions, and planters or trees. Declare signs and lights that will be hidden or moved, and any effect on public transport, crossings, and carriageway width.
- Trees on government land need prior approval from the department that maintains them, and a preservation, protection, and monitoring proposal, before the hoarding permit. Report damage the same day.
- If the hoarding enters the carriageway, keep a lane of at least 3 m on a one-way road, and at least 5.5 m on a single carriageway used both ways. Temporary traffic arrangements follow the Highways Department code on lighting, signing, and guarding. If you cannot meet those widths on a traffic-sensitive road (a red route, a pink route, a bus corridor, or within 70 m of a signalised junction on such a road), submit a traffic impact assessment at least 2 months before the hoarding permit.
- Clear width for pedestrians: on the carriageway, at least 1.1 m. On a pavement that is 2.5 m wide or less, normally at least 1.5 m; a narrower width is allowed only if pedestrian level of service C is maintained, and never less than 1.1 m. If the existing pavement is more than 2.5 m and up to 3 m, the clear width is the pavement width minus 1 m. If the existing pavement is more than 3 m, the clear width is at least 2 m. Vertical clearance inside a covered walkway is at least 2.3 m. Trees, signs, scaffold poles, gate leaves, and similar items must not obstruct the walkway.
- Lighting: average illuminance on the floor of 35 to 50 lux. A typical layout is an 18 W or 20 W fluorescent fitting, 600 mm long, at 3 m spacing, for a walkway 2 m by 2.5 m.
- A gantry is normally not wider than 7.3 m. A wider gantry needs a swept-path check, a statement of how often it will be used, and measures for pedestrian safety. Works on a Highways Department road need an excavation permit under Cap. 28. Temporary construction access follows Highways Department standard drawings, Annex 1, and APP-144.
- The first permit can be self-certified in 30 days if Appendix A of the Highways Department and Transport Department is met, no traffic impact assessment is required, there is no contractor’s shed on top, the design is structurally safe and complies with the Buildings Ordinance, trees are not felled or pruned in a way that interferes, demolition uses the standard designs, and any slope or retaining wall is not adversely affected (certified on Appendix E). Otherwise the period is 60 days. Consent for the building works is refused if the required hoarding proposals have not been received.
- A demolition permit normally lasts 6 months. A permit for general building works lasts 3 years, with an annual safety certificate (Appendix F) submitted at least 7 days before each anniversary. An idle site, or a missing annual certificate, can lead to cancellation. Apply to renew at least 30 days before expiry, with Form BD109, an Appendix G certificate, and colour photos. A minor amendment that meets Appendix H can be self-certified: deposit the amended plans and certificate within 7 days. Do not use a walkway for advertising or storage.

**What to submit**
- Form BA19, the plans, and Appendix E where you want the streamlined 30-day route. The annual Appendix F certificate. Renewal on Appendix G with photos. A minor amendment is deposited under Appendix H.
- Deal with the Transport Department and the Highways Department on the traffic impact assessment, on suspension of parking (Annex 2), and on the excavation permit.

**Watch-outs**
- If you do not declare affected traffic facilities, the permit is delayed.
- A walkway that is too narrow, obstructed, or poorly lit is a public-safety failure, and the Building Authority may carry out repairs under regulation 67 at the owner’s cost.
- Missing hoarding proposals block consent for the building works.
- Do not use the walkway for advertising or storage.
