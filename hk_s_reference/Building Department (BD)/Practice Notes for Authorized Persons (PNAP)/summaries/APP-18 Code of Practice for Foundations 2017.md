# APP-18

**Official title:** Code of Practice for Foundations 2017

**In plain words:** Compliance with the Code of Practice for Foundations 2017 is treated as meeting the related Building (Construction) Regulations, and this note explains how that code is applied, including the 2024 edition and the September 2025 amendments. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- The code in force for this note is the 2024 edition plus Appendix A amendments of September 2025. Those 2025 amendments include: a limit on ultimate bearing capacity in granular soil of 3,000 kPa, raised to 4,500 kPa for foundations that support buildings with basements on granular soil; the overburden depth used for allowable bearing pressure (qo) is generally not more than 3 m or B, whichever is smaller, while for a basement on granular soil the effective perimeter overburden is not more than 10 m or B; the elastic modulus of a shallow foundation is about 1× the standard penetration test N-value; a raft on granitic saprolite or residual soil with N greater than 30 may use about 1.5× N. Related wording on plate-load tests and tension tests is also updated.
- A novel piling system needs a pre-submission enquiry under ADM-19, with full technical details of the system and a demonstration.
- Steel H-pile sections need manufacturing records, mill certificates, carbon equivalent value, HOKLAS mechanical and chemical tests, welding control, and quality assurance. Sections on the Buildings Department “deemed-to-satisfy” web list need not be repeated on the plans; otherwise include them.
- Where presumed capacities from Geotechnical Engineering Office Technical Guidance Notes 26 and 53 are used (marble or igneous rock), Buildings Ordinance section 17(1) item 6 conditions cover an as-built settlement analysis, a structural tolerance assessment, a post-foundation report, a settlement monitoring scheme before the pile cap or superstructure, and a final performance review before the occupation permit (OP) or BA14.
- Mass concrete fill under a shallow foundation, with a maximum depth greater than 1 m, is a structural element under the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Concrete 2013.
- Pre-drilling, post-drilling, and proof core drilling are done by a registered specialist contractor in ground investigation field works. For proof coring under regulation 20 of the Building (Construction) Regulations, declare independence from the foundation contractor.
- At a large-diameter bored pile, an optional pipe of at least 150 mm diameter may be stopped about 1 m above the founding interface. A thin sediment layer, segregation, or a weathered seam may be accepted with justification from the registered structural engineer (RSE) and registered geotechnical engineer (RGE), or with a pre-approved remedial method statement and supervision.
- Two-stage pile plans: Stage 1 gives maximum capacities, layout, and specifications, without a rigorous pile-cap analysis or load schedules. Stage 2 gives the full load path, the schedules, and the rigorous analysis. Consent may follow Stage 1. BA14 is accepted only after any Stage 1 amendments, and Stage 2, are approved.
- Site-specific ground investigation may be deferred in non-scheduled areas and in Schedule Areas 3 and 5. Approval may rest on existing ground investigation within 20 m of the boundary (from the Geotechnical Information Unit or a previous development). The site-specific investigation and the assessment that checks the assumptions run in parallel with consent. If they do not match the approved plans, revise the plans before consent, or consent is refused under Buildings Ordinance section 16(3)(ba).
- For piles within 10 m of the boundary, consent may wait until pile-wall or grout-curtain records and the assessment are in.
- Where approval and consent are sought together, Form BA8 is not submitted before the 32nd day after the plans were submitted.
- For driven H-piles, consent for working piles may be given before the trial proof-load test is finished if the dynamic test (pile driving analyzer with CAPWAP) is satisfactory. Submit proof-load reports within 14 days of completing the test.
- The first stage of excavation and lateral support may proceed with the initial consent: excavate to the bottom of the first strut layer, but not more than 1.5 m below existing ground level, and install the first waling and struts.
- Consent for the pile cap or superstructure comes only after satisfactory records, BA14, proof tests, and any imposed conditions.
- If an as-built pile length differs from the approved length by more than 5%, or lengths are significantly uneven, do a back-analysis and amend the load schedule. In Scheduled Areas 2 and 4, also follow APP-61 and clause 7.8 of the code. For a large-diameter pile, BA14 after at least 85% of the interface proof drilling may allow early selection of piles for proof coring.

**What to submit**
- Foundation plans, calculations, and reports. Geotechnical documents are signed by the RGE (APP-141). Submit BA14 with the phased records. Stage 1 and Stage 2 packs follow Annex C1. Where a technical-guidance-note presumed value is used, submit the monitoring and the final settlement performance review.

**Watch-outs**
- Consent or BA14 on the two-stage path without Stage 2, without proof tests, or without the settlement reviews required for presumed values, is not in order.
- Boundary piles without the wall or curtain records can hold consent.
- Mass concrete fill deeper than 1 m that is not treated as structure is not accepted.
- A deferred ground investigation that does not match the approved plans leads to refusal of consent.
