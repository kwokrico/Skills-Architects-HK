# ADV-36 Modular Integrated Construction
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision July 2025 | Buildings Department (first issue December 2017)

> Scope note: Design, quality control, and pre-acceptance for freestanding volumetric modules manufactured off site with finishes, fixtures, and fittings, then assembled on site. Pre-acceptance is not a prerequisite for approval of general building plans. Structural design still follows the concrete, precast, and steel codes named in Appendix A.

## Regulatory Overview
The project team engages the MiC supplier at the early design stage to settle standardisation, buildability, delivery to the particular site, supervision, and the programme of plan submissions. Fire performance, watertight joints, stability, robustness, temporary stages, movement, and access for maintenance are designed before the module is fixed.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Design locks (Appendix A)
| Topic | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Fire | Address the fire protection of the elements. A non-code-compliant design is justified as equivalent to the prescriptive standards and, where necessary, supported by a fire engineering assessment under the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings |
| Joints and gaps | Modules have more joints, including in drainage pipes and the envelope. Design them against water ingress |
| Stability | The building of modular units resists wind and other lateral loads, including temporary stability during assembly, with adequate support at every stage |
| Robustness | Tie all modular units and in-situ elements together horizontally and vertically. Each unit sustains accidental loading so that the extent of collapse of an individual unit is not disproportionate to the cause |
| Temporary stages | Fabrication, lifting, transport, assembly, and installation may produce a different load path and higher stress. Design for the temporary imposed load at each stage |
| Movement | Provide joints for cumulative creep, shrinkage, differential movement, and thermal movement, short-term and long-term, whether or not they occur together, and for fabrication and installation tolerances |
| Maintenance | Provide access points, inspection pits, or accessible recesses at the early design stage for structural connections, drainage pipes, building services, and joints |
| Separate balcony or utility platform | Do not design a balcony or utility platform as a separate module joined in situ. If one is proposed, its area is not accepted as MiC floor area for gross floor area and site coverage concessions under Joint Practice Note No. 8 |

**SD takeaway:** Tie the modules together against disproportionate collapse, detail every joint against water, and do not count a separate balcony or utility-platform module as MiC floor area.

### 2. Enquiry and in-principle acceptance (paragraphs 3–5 and Appendix C)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-submission enquiry | PNAP ADM-19. A determination on an unconventional design or on the performance of a prototype is available within 45 days |
| In-principle acceptance | For the performance items in Appendix C. If the information is complete and the system meets the Buildings Ordinance, a letter of in-principle acceptance is issued within 45 days of receipt. Comments are also issued within 45 days |
| Response | Clarify every comment within 30 days or the application is treated as abandoned. The Department then accepts or refuses within 30 days of the response. A refusal is processed as a new application, again within 45 days |
| Not a prerequisite | Pre-acceptance is not required before general building plans are approved |
| Plans with the application | General notes on the codes. Intended building height, number of storeys, and use. General building plans of all MiC floors, or of the system or prototype, at a scale not less than 1:100. Module interfaces with upper, lower, and adjacent modules and with in-situ work at a scale not less than 1:50. Structural plans at not less than 1:100. The module that represents the whole building and its height when assembled. Mirrored modules count as one type |
| On those plans | Fire-safety tables for the MiC portion (occupant capacity, exit widths, fire resistance ratings, and combustibility); prescribed-window areas without detailed calculations; drainage part plans on the general building plans, not a separate drainage submission; barrier-free access for the MiC portion; structural connections, temporary loads, ties, and hypothetical removal of a critical element; fabrication and installation tolerances; and accessible recesses for joints, bolts, welds, and drains in sunken slabs |
| Factory proof | A Full Certificate under the Modular Integrated Construction Manufacturer Accreditation Scheme, or ISO 9001 certification plus a quality assurance scheme as in Appendix B |

**SD takeaway:** If the module is unconventional, clear it by enquiry or in-principle acceptance in the 45-day track before detailed design, and draw the module interfaces at 1:50.

### 3. Factory supervision (Appendix B)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Factory | A valid Full Certificate from the Building Technology Research Institute under the Modular Integrated Construction Manufacturer Accreditation Scheme, or ISO 9001 or an equivalent quality assurance certification. Imposed as a condition under section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance when plans are approved |
| Before production | Submit the Full Certificate, or the quality assurance scheme, at least 14 days before production starts in the factory, unless it is already covered by an in-principle acceptance and is unchanged. Submit the MiC supervision plan at least 14 days before production |
| Quality assurance scheme | Quality-control tests and acceptance criteria, calibration, the production process, and the frequency of inspection and audit by in-house staff and by independent parties. An independent party is not directly employed by the factory and is a certification body under HKCAS or an inspection body under HKIAS |
| Minimum supervision | Authorized-person stream: Grade T3, weekly. Registered-structural-engineer stream: Grade T3, weekly. Registered-contractor stream: Grade T3, weekly, and Grade T1, continuous. Grades are as in the Code of Practice for Site Supervision. The authorized person’s and structural engineer’s teams cover fire-resisting construction, drainage, and structure as in PNAP APP-158 |
| Log book | Kept at the factory, with a copy at the site office, and produced to the Buildings Department when required |
| Quality audit | The authorized person, the registered structural engineer, and the authorized signatory inspect the first batch in person, then audit at least once every month. Quarterly audits are in person. The months between may be by videotelephony, assisted by the supervisory teams, and not inferior to a personal visit. Submit the endorsed audit report within 14 days after the check |

**SD takeaway:** Name a Grade T3 weekly supervisor for each stream and a Grade T1 continuously for the contractor, and submit the supervision plan at least 14 days before the factory starts.
