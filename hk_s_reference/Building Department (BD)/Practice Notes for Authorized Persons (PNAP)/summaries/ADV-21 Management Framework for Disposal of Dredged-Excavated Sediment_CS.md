# ADV-21 Management Framework for Disposal of Dredged-Excavated Sediment
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision April 2007 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009; previously PNAP 252; first issue May 2001)

> Scope note: The Dumping at Sea Ordinance (Cap. 466) framework for private projects that dispose of dredged or excavated sediment at sea, including reclamation, marinas, and a basement excavation large enough to need marine disposal. It assumes the mud stays in place unless removal is justified. The dumping permit is applied for by the contractor, not by the authorized person.

## Regulatory Overview
Disposal space at sea is not allocated until the need to remove the sediment has been demonstrated to the Secretary of the Marine Fill Committee, except for Category L sediment below 50,000 m³ and the emergency and maintenance cases in paragraph 4. The Director of Environmental Protection classifies the sediment as Category L, M, or H against the chemical exceedance levels in Appendix A, and the committee then allocates an open-sea or confined marine site.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When removal has to be justified (paragraphs 3–5)
| Case | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Default | Plan on keeping the mud in place. Allow time for consolidation, with treatment if needed. Extra time for consolidation is not a justification for dredging |
| Exempt from the rationale submission | Category L sediment below 50,000 m³. Also: emergency dredging for safety or to avert an environmental hazard; maintenance or deepening of harbour fairways, berths, anchorages, navigation channels, or approaches; maintenance, but not construction, of watercourses, rivers, stream courses, drainage channels, or outfalls |
| Scrutiny | The Marine Fill Committee looks at whether a performance specification is practical, whether the risk strategy is complete, and whether the options, including new technology, have been assessed. If cost is used, it must include the actual cost of mud disposal from the committee secretariat, plus dredging, transport, disposal management, and monitoring |
| When to ask | As early as possible, preferably at the environmental impact assessment stage if one is conducted |

**SD takeaway:** Design the basement or reclamation to leave the mud in place, and start the removal justification early if marine disposal is still required.

### 2. Categories and chemical limits (Appendix A)
| Category | Test | Disposal rule in the note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L | Every contaminant at or below the lower chemical exceedance level | Dredge, transport, and dispose so that loss of contaminants into solution or by resuspension is minimised |
| M | Any contaminant above the lower level, and none above the upper level | Dredge and transport with care, and isolate from the environment on final disposal unless biological tests show the material will not adversely affect the marine environment |
| H | Any contaminant above the upper level | Dredge and transport with great care, and isolate from the environment on final disposal |

Selected limits (lower / upper), mg/kg dry weight unless noted:

| Contaminant | Lower | Upper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cadmium | 1.5 | 4 |
| Chromium | 80 | 160 |
| Copper | 65 | 110 |
| Mercury | 0.5 | 1 |
| Lead | 75 | 110 |
| Zinc | 200 | 270 |
| Arsenic | 12 | 42 |
| Nickel | 40 | 40 |
| Silver | 1 | 2 |
| Low molecular weight PAHs | 550 µg/kg | 3,160 µg/kg |
| High molecular weight PAHs | 1,700 µg/kg | 9,600 µg/kg |
| Total PCBs | 23 µg/kg | 180 µg/kg |
| Tributyltin in interstitial water | 0.15 µg TBT/L | 0.15 µg TBT/L |

For nickel and tributyltin, the upper level is exceeded if the result is greater than the value shown.

**SD takeaway:** One result above the upper level makes the sediment Category H and rules out ordinary open-sea disposal.

### 3. Sampling, biological tests, and the report (paragraphs 7–12 and Appendix B)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Waiver of sampling | Emergency dredging, or small-scale maintenance dredging of less than 5,000 m³ in situ. Submit previous data or the known history |
| Time | Sampling, testing, and the Sediment Quality Report normally take 8 months |
| Grid | Low contamination: 200 m by 200 m, surface sample only. High: 100 m by 100 m, vertical profile. Very high, such as near an outfall or nullah: 50 m by 50 m, vertical profile |
| Vertical profile | Continuous samples. Top of sub-samples at the seabed, 0.9 m, 1.9 m, 2.9 m, then every 3 m to the bottom of the dredged layer |
| Holding | Keep samples at 4°C in the dark and do not freeze. Recommended maximum holding time is 2 weeks for a chemical test and 8 weeks for a biological test |
| Laboratories | Chemical tests by a HOKLAS-accredited laboratory, or an equivalent national accreditation for an overseas laboratory |
| Tier III | Required for Category M, and for Category H only if one or more contaminants exceed 10 times the lower chemical exceedance level. Category L does not go to Tier III |
| Category M tests | One set of three tests on each composite of up to 5 continuous samples: a 10-day burrowing amphipod test, a 20-day burrowing polychaete test, and a 48–96 hour larvae test. Failure of any one test is failure of the biological test |
| Category H dilution | If above 10 times the lower level, mix the sample with 9 portions of reference sediment before the same three tests |
| Formal report | Where biological screening is required, submit the Sediment Quality Report at least 3 months before the dredging contract is tendered, or at least 2 months before a maintenance-dredging works order. If biological screening is not required, the preliminary report may be deemed the formal report |
| Reliability | At approval, the Director specifies a reliability period of not less than 3 years from the actual start of sampling. If the lapse between the start of sampling and the start of construction exceeds 3 years, re-testing is required unless waived. The contract must state the expiry date and that extending the report is the contractor’s cost if the dumping-permit application is late |

**SD takeaway:** Allow about 8 months for the Sediment Quality Report, and keep the dredging tender inside the report’s reliability period, which is at least 3 years from the start of sampling.

### 4. Where it may be dumped (paragraphs 13–16, Appendix C, and Notes 1–6)
| Outcome | Disposal |
| --- | --- |
| Category L | Type 1, open-sea disposal |
| Category M that passes biological screening | Type 1, open-sea disposal at dedicated sites |
| Category M that fails, or Category H that is not above 10 times the lower level, or Category H above that multiple that passes the dilution test | Type 2, confined marine disposal. East Sha Chau mud pits are designated for this |
| Category H that fails the dilution test | Type 3, special treatment or disposal, agreed with the Director of Environmental Protection. Treatment so that it becomes suitable for confined marine disposal is not ruled out |
| Small Category L jobs | Allocation of disposal space for less than 50,000 m³ of Category L sediment is delegated to the Director of Environmental Protection |
| Contract | Include the disposal requirements and Notes (1) to (6). Open-sea sites are multi-user, so the contract must allow diversion from one site to another. Type 2 and Type 3 allocations must state the committee’s and the Director’s conditions |
| Charge | For a private project with marine disposal space, a charge per cubic metre measured in situ at the dredging site and certified by the authorized person, registered structural engineer, or registered geotechnical engineer |
| Permit | The contractor applies to the Director for the dumping permit under the Dumping at Sea Ordinance and must meet its conditions |
| East Sha Chau plant | A trailer suction hopper dredger must use a down-a-pipe method, the design of which is approved in advance by the Director of Civil Engineering, and the contractor provides the equipment |
| Fill database | Send fill and disposal requirements, and later updates, to the Marine Fill Committee Secretariat, copied to the Buildings Department |

**SD takeaway:** Category L can go to open sea; Category M and Category H need a confined site or special treatment, and the contractor cannot dump until the permit is issued.
