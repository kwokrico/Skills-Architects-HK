# APP-17

**Official title:** Rock Faces — Building (Planning) Regulations 27 and 47

**In plain words:** This note says when a rock face counts as “massive” under Building (Planning) Regulation 47, and how every other rock face is designed and checked under Regulation 27. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- For Regulation 47, a face is a “massive rock face” only if it is widely jointed, with joint spacing greater than 600 mm, and it has no unfavourable joints, discontinuities, or defects. Any other rock face follows Regulation 27.
- Start the design with site investigation, aerial-photo interpretation, mapping, and drillholes, plus packer tests, impression packer, closed-circuit television, and similar tools. Use Geoguides 1 to 4 and the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes (Tables 5.6 and 5.7 for analysis and stabilisation).
- Plans must state the jointing assumptions used for overall and local support. Good practice in the note: controlled blasting, or pre-split or smooth-face blasting where that suits the rock; if expanding agents are used, stop uncontrolled flow and assess the effect on stability; immediately after excavation, scale the final face and survey it by a qualified geotechnical engineer or engineering geologist (for example a geotechnical registered professional engineer with rock-face experience), including seepages and weak impermeable zones such as kaolinitic clay layers, the lesson of the Fei Tsui Road failure. Submit large-scale engineering-geological drawings, support and drainage details, calculations, and photographs for Building Authority approval.
- Prevent uncontrolled rockfalls during construction, especially on an existing face that could affect the public.

**What to submit**
- Site-formation plans stating the assumptions and the good-practice notes above. After excavation, submit the geological drawings, photographs, and calculations for approval. Use a qualified geotechnical professional for the surveys.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not call a face “massive” unless joint spacing is greater than 600 mm and the discontinuities are favourable.
- Skipping the post-excavation scaling survey can miss a weak layer of the kind found at Fei Tsui Road.
- Inadequate control of blasting or expanding agents near the public is a safety refusal risk.
