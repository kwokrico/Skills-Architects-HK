# APP-94

**Official title:** Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Ordinance, Cap. 502

**In plain words:** Cap. 502 upgrades fire safety in prescribed commercial premises and in specified commercial buildings. It does not change gross floor area or site coverage, and upgrade work that is not exempt still needs Buildings Ordinance approval.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly practice note (PNAP) 212. First issued August 1997. Last revision October 2007. This revision October 2010 (paragraph 5 amended; paragraph 12 on the advisory committee added).
- The Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Ordinance came into force on 2 May 1997. The amendment ordinance came into force on 1 June 1998.
- Prescribed commercial premises (PCP) are a building or part used, or proposed, for banking other than merchant banking, off-course betting, a jewellery or goldsmith business with a security area, a supermarket, hypermarket, or department store, or a shopping arcade, and the total floor area is more than 230 m². Floor area is measured under sections 3(3) to 3(6). Shopping-arcade examples are in Appendix A.
- A specified commercial building (SCB) is used as an office or for business, trade, or entertainment, and either the plans were first submitted for BA approval on or before 1 March 1987, or it was built on or before that date and no plans had been submitted by then.
- Construction measures, any or all: adequate means of escape; means of access for firefighting and rescue; measures to limit fire spread and to keep the structure sound. Detailed standards are in Schedules 2 (PCP) and 5 (SCB), and in the 1996 Means of Escape Code, the 1996 Fire Resisting Construction Code, and the 1995 Means of Access Code.
- Fire service installations, any or all: automatic sprinklers; automatic cut-off of mechanical ventilation; emergency lighting; manual alarms; portable extinguishers; fire hydrant and hose reel. Follow the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment 1994.
- The Director of Buildings covers planning, design, and construction. The Director of Fire Services covers installations and equipment. The Buildings Department Fire Safety Section handles the construction side.
- Directions are a fire safety direction for PCP under section 5(1), and a fire safety improvement direction for SCB under section 5(1)(A). After a conviction for non-compliance, a magistrate may make a compliance order under section 6(1).
- Where there is genuine difficulty, a fire engineering alternative that meets the ordinance’s main aims can be considered by the advisory committee (ACFSO), established on 1 June 2010. Terms of reference and membership are in APP-145 Appendix A.
- The Fire Services Department wants three sets of fire service installation plans, not two.
- There is no fee under Building (Administration) Regulations regulation 42 if the proposals are entirely for improving fire safety under those directions.

**What to submit**
- Non-exempt upgrade works still need approval under the Buildings Ordinance.
- Submit three sets of fire service installation plans to the Fire Services Department.
- If the whole proposal is only to meet a fire safety direction or a fire safety improvement direction, no fee is charged under regulation 42.
- If it is unclear whether premises are prescribed commercial premises, ask the Buildings Department Fire Safety Section.

**Watch-outs**
- Premises of 230 m² or less, or the wrong activity, are not prescribed commercial premises, and the wrong ordinance may be used.
- A specified commercial building turns on the 1 March 1987 plan-submission or construction date.
- Ignoring a direction can mean conviction and a magistrate’s compliance order.
- A fire engineering alternative needs the advisory-committee route.
- The fee waiver and the three-set plan count apply to these fire-safety submissions. Using the ordinary two-set count or charging a fee is wrong.
