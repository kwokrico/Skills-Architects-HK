# APP-124

**Official title:** Streets for Site Classification

**In plain words:** The site class, and therefore the maximum site coverage (SC) and plot ratio (PR), depends on how many specified streets at least 4.5 m wide abut the site under Building (Planning) Regulation (B(P)R) 18A. Counting something that is not a street can wrongly unlock a Class B or Class C intensity.

**Numbers that matter**
- A street used for classification must be at least 4.5 m wide.
- B(P)R 18A took effect on 31 December 2005.
- A street needs the character of a street: a passage or access, with factors such as lighting, paving, and drainage. A public amenity car park, a public open space, a nullah, and the sea are not streets.
- Whether an intervening strip still leaves the site abutting the street depends on the function, status, and ownership of that land.
- Under B(P)R 18A(3), a specified street is one of the following, or parts of them that together make the street. An intervening strip that does not qualify splits them into separate specified streets.
- (a) Vested in the Government and maintained by the Highways Department (HyD), meaning a public street.
- (b) On land held under the same government lease as the site, where the lease requires the street land to be surrendered when required.
- (c) Required to be constructed on unleased government land under the lease of the site.
- (d) Land over which the site owner has an express right of way that can be used at all times, set out in an instrument.
- (e) On land the site owner holds under the government lease, meaning a private street the owner owns, with an undertaking that it remains a street for the life of the building, for example an assignment as common area registered in the Land Registry.
- (f) Parts under (a) to (e) that together constitute the street.
- A new specified street must be completed before the occupation permit (OP), and it must remain a street for the life of the buildings that rely on it for SC and PR. Building over it, or extinguishing it, breaches the Buildings Ordinance (BO) and can lead to disapproval under section 16(1)(d). An incomplete street at OP stage can lead to refusal of the OP under BO section 21(6)(a).
- First issued October 2001. This revision November 2005.
- File reference: BD GP/BORD/80 (VI).

**What to submit**
- Prove the street with the matching document: the lease or land grant, HyD confirmation, a right-of-way deed, or a Land Registry undertaking, as the case requires.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not count a nullah, an open space, or a car park as a street.
- Informal access, without an express right-of-way instrument under 18A(3)(a)(iv), is not enough.
- A private street without a lifelong common-area undertaking under 18A(3)(a)(v) is not enough.
- Later extinguishing the street, or building over it, undermines the site classification.
