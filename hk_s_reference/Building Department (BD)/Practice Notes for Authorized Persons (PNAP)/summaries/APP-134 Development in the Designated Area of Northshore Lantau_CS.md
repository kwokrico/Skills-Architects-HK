# APP-134 Development in the Designated Area of Northshore Lantau
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision February 2006 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 283; first issue January 2004)

> Scope note: Special ground-investigation and deep-foundation submissions for the Designated Area of Northshore Lantau. The boundary plan is held in the Geotechnical Information Unit of the Civil Engineering Library; Appendix A is a small-scale location plan. Technical design guidance is in GEO Technical Guidance Note No. 12, not in this note. General piled-foundation guidance is in PNAP 66 and the Code of Practice for Foundations.

## Regulatory Overview
The Designated Area is underlain mainly by medium-grained granite with rhyolite dykes, but also by cavity-forming metasedimentary rocks, soft or loose superficial deposits, and anomalously deep or steeply inclined rockhead. Deep foundations there need staged ground investigation before the general building plans are fixed, because layout changes or abandoning part of a site can be the practical response.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Ground that affects layout (paragraphs 2–4 and 7)
| Condition | Design effect |
| --- | --- |
| Metasedimentary rocks (marble, metasandstone, metasiltstone) | Dissolution, cavities, cavity-fill deposits, and residual soils, as described in the appendix to Hong Kong Geological Survey Sheet Report No. 6 |
| Superficial deposits | Soft, loose, or weakly lithified deposits in depressions in the subcrop, most directly above or adjacent to metasedimentary rocks |
| Igneous rockhead | Anomalously deep, or steeply inclined |
| Consequence | Long piles through this ground have caused abandonment of planned residential towers at the Tung Chung New Town reclamation where cavity-fill and a deep, locally steeply dipping rockhead made the foundations too costly and difficult |
| When to investigate | Initial ground investigation before general building plans are finalised, so structure locations can be adjusted. Some deep drillholes are required. Investigation is best in stages and must be adequate to assess whether the ground will affect the development |

**SD takeaway:** Investigate the Designated Area, including deep drillholes, before locking tower positions.

### 2. Submissions with the foundation plans (paragraphs 6 and 8–10)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Ground investigation procedure | Follow the Code of Practice for Site Supervision. Seek advice from the Chief Geotechnical Engineer/Mainland West of the Geotechnical Engineering Office on the investigation proposal before the works start |
| With the foundation plans | (i) an explanatory guide; (ii) a ground-investigation report including equipment and procedures; (iii) interpretation, a schedule of geotechnical design assumptions, anticipated problems, and geotechnical requirements for design, construction, testing, inspecting, and maintenance; (iv) design calculations |
| Supervision of the foundation works | As specified in the Code of Practice for Site Supervision |
| Standards | GEO Technical Guidance Note No. 12; latest Guide to Site Investigation and other GEO guidance; GEO Publication No. 1/96 for pile design and construction practice |

**SD takeaway:** Lodge the ground-investigation interpretation, design assumptions, and foundation calculations with the foundation plans.
