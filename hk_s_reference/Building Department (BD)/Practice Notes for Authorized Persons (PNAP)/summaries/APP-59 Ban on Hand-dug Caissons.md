# APP-59

**Official title:** Ban on Hand-dug Caissons

**In plain words:** The Building Authority refuses plans that include hand-dug caissons unless the Authority is personally satisfied that a narrow exception applies, including on karst and marble sites (see also APP-61). This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- Buildings (Amendment) Ordinance 1995, dated 19 January 1995. The definition is in Buildings Ordinance section 2(1). The refusal power is section 16(1A). The ban took effect on 1 February 1996, after a 12-month grace period.
- A hand-dug caisson is any foundation or earth-retaining structure, or part of one, whose construction includes a person digging a shaft from inside the shaft, with or without machine tools.
- An exemption is possible only if the Building Authority is personally satisfied that either (a) the depth is not more than 3 m and the diameter of the largest circle that fits inside the shaft is at least 1.5 m, or (b) for that site it is the only practical construction method, or there is no other safe engineering alternative. A narrow or steep site where machines are not possible or not safe is the example given.

**What to submit**
- Plans that include hand-dug caissons face refusal under section 16(1A) unless the exceptional case is shown to the Building Authority personally.
- Where permission is given, the method statement must contain stringent safety requirements. The registered contractor must comply, follow the Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations, and use the Hong Kong Institution of Engineers guidance notes and the Occupational Safety and Health Council Code of Safe Working Practices.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not specify hand-dug caissons for convenience. The test is genuine impossibility, or that no other method is safe.
- Do not carry out exempted work without enforcing the method-statement safety requirements and labour compliance.
- The authorized person and the registered structural engineer are strongly urged to avoid hand-dug caissons even where older grace arrangements once applied. Worker safety comes first.
