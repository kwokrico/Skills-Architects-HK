# APP-163 Code of Practice on Access for External Maintenance 2021
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision September 2024 | Buildings Department (first issue February 2021)

> Scope note: Submission of maintenance-and-repair access plans under the Code of Practice on Access for External Maintenance 2021, which gives guidance on sections 27(2), 28(5), 31(3), and 34(3) of the Building (Construction) Regulation. The code superseded the Code of Practice on Design for Safety — External Maintenance 2019. Appendix A records amendments consolidated in the 2024 Edition. The detailed access rules are in the code, not restated here, except the amendments in this note.

## Regulatory Overview
External walls, curtain walls, external claddings, roofs, and their projections need adequate means of access for maintenance and repair. The requirement does not apply to building works being carried out, or whose consent had been given, as at 31 January 2021; in general, building works may refer to foundation works.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Plans, and when the new balcony anchor applies (paragraphs 2–6)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Alteration and addition | Explore suitable access in accordance with the code, and the Labour Department codes and guidance on occupational safety |
| Submission | Maintenance-and-repair access plans, with the details, information, supporting documents, and the developer or owner’s letter of undertaking set out in the code |
| Immediate effect | All Appendix A items except item 1 |
| New paragraph 3.2.4 | New general building plans, or a major revision, submitted for approval on or after 1 December 2024, and plans previously disapproved and resubmitted on or after that date |
| Ongoing projects | Authorized persons are advised to provide the anchors anyway |

**SD takeaway:** From 1 December 2024, new general building plans need an anchor on every balcony and utility platform.

### 2. Anchor on a balcony or utility platform (Appendix A, item 1)
Paragraph 3.2.3 remains: access to the outer surface of a balcony and a utility platform shall be by a power-operated elevating work platform or a suspended working platform.

New paragraph 3.2.4: at least one anchor for a personal fall-arresting system at every balcony and utility platform. Readily accessible from the adjoining floor, at a height of not less than 1.5 m and not more than 1.8 m above the finished floor of that floor, and complying with paragraph 7 of Appendix D of the code. Structural and fixing details are submitted for approval in accordance with Appendix F of the code.

**SD takeaway:** Place the balcony anchor between 1.5 m and 1.8 m above the adjoining finished floor.

### 3. Inaccessible roof edge (Appendix A, items 2 and 3)
Where stepping onto an inaccessible roof is required, guard-rails with toe-boards follow paragraph 4 of Appendix D of the code. If permanent guard-rails around the whole perimeter are impracticable, subject to paragraph 4.2.4:

| Alternative | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Collapsible guard-rail | Proprietary system designed for imposed loads and wind loads not inferior to the Building (Construction) Regulation, the Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads, and the Code of Practice on Wind Effects in Hong Kong |
| Temporary edge protection | With a suitable platform and access ladder, designed from a prior risk assessment under the Labour Department’s Overview of Work-at-Height Safety and Five Steps to Risk Assessment |
| Anchorage with those alternatives | Anchorage points or a horizontal lifeline for a personal fall-arresting system and/or a travel-restraint system, complying with the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance and the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance, at the roof access or at other locations not less than 2 m from the roof edge or fragile area, so the lanyard can be attached before the user is at risk of a fall |
| Access within 2 m of the edge, a fragile area, or opening onto a pitched roof | Permanent guard-rails at least around the roof access or landing |
| Notes on plans | State the anchorage locations, that structural details will be submitted separately, the load basis for a collapsible rail, and the risk-assessment basis for temporary edge protection |

**SD takeaway:** If the roof cannot take a permanent perimeter rail, put the fall-arrest anchorage at least 2 m from the edge, or keep a permanent rail around the access if the access itself is within 2 m of the edge.
