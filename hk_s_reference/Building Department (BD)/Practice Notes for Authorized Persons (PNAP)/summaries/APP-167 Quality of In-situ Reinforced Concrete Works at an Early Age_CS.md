# APP-167 Quality of In-situ Reinforced Concrete Works at an Early Age
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue March 2022 | Buildings Department

> Scope note: Early rebound-hammer testing where a superstructure uses different concrete grades, imposed under item 6 of section 17(1) of the Buildings Ordinance on the first approval of such plans. Wider quality-supervision items are in Appendix A of PNAP APP-158. A similar note has been issued to registered contractors. It is not a concrete-mix design code.

## Regulatory Overview
Where vertical and horizontal elements use different concrete grades, rebound hammer tests are required on the higher-grade elements between the seventh and tenth day after casting, to BS EN 12504-2:2021. If the test cannot be done in that window, for example because of a public holiday, it is done on the next working day.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is tested, and by whom (Appendix A, paragraphs 1–5)
The registered structural engineer selects the elements at random. A grade T3 technically competent person in the engineer’s stream witnesses at least 10% of the tests. Tests are carried out by a grade T1 or above person in the contractor’s stream, trained in rebound hammers, or by a laboratory accredited under the Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme. Records stay on site. Kickers are tested as well as the vertical elements.

| Element of higher grade | Minimum frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Vertical elements higher than the immediate supports at the base (pile cap, footing, raft, transfer plate, or transfer beam) | 50% |
| Vertical elements higher than adjoining beams or slabs at the top | 10% |
| Transfer beams | 50% |
| Beams higher than adjoining beams or slabs on the same floor | 10% |

**SD takeaway:** Test half of the higher-grade columns on a transfer and half of the transfer beams, between day 7 and day 10.

### 2. Alert level (Appendix A, paragraph 6)
In the absence of an engineering assessment:

| Concrete | Alert if readings are below |
| --- | --- |
| With pulverized-fuel ash or ground granulated blastfurnace slag | 55% of the minimum characteristic strength |
| Without those materials | 65% of the minimum characteristic strength |

Follow-up is to examine approved plans, order and delivery records, concreting and curing methods, and supervision records; to ascertain quality early by further hammer tests or cores; to tighten supervision of similar elements on other floors; and, if the review requires it, to report to the Buildings Department and suspend concreting until the follow-up is complete.

**SD takeaway:** A rebound reading under 55% of characteristic strength for ash or slag concrete, or under 65% without them, is the alert to check the pour and consider stopping similar casts.

### 3. Alternative (Appendix A, paragraph 7)
The maturity method on the seventh day, under clause 11.7.5.4 of the Code of Practice for Structural Use of Concrete 2013, may replace the rebound hammer test. The number of measuring points and the alert level follow paragraphs 5 and 6.

**SD takeaway:** Maturity testing on day 7 can replace the rebound hammer test if the same sample rates and alert levels are kept.
