# APP-96

**Official title:** Registration of General Building Contractors and Specialist Contractors

**In plain words:** General building works and five kinds of specialist work must be done by a contractor on the matching register under Buildings Ordinance sections 8 to 8F. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage, but the wrong category can still invalidate consent and supervision.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly practice note (PNAP) 214. First issued November 1997. Last revision September 2013. This revision September 2020 (paragraphs 4, 6, and 10 amended). Appendix A scope demarcation is revision 9/2013.
- Section 8A registers: registered general building contractor (RGBC), registered specialist contractor (RSC) on sub-registers, and minor works contractors. The registers are gazetted every year. The Buildings Department website lists the contractor, the authorized signatory, the registration number, and the expiry.
- The five specialist categories are demolition, foundation, ground investigation field works, site formation, and ventilation. Minor works contractors are covered in APP-148. Registration procedure is in PNRC 38.
- An RGBC does general building and street works other than the specialist categories, and may also do every minor-works type in Part 2 of Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation. An RSC does only its own category, plus minor works under Building (Minor Works) Regulation sections 28(1)(b) to 28(1)(ea).
- Section 8B(2) looks at the company’s management structure, the experience and qualifications of its people, access to plant and resources, and whether the authorized signatory can understand the work and the statutory requirements.
- There must be at least one authorized signatory. A corporation also needs at least one technical director with board authority over plant, technical and financial support, decisions, and supervision of the authorized signatory. If the technical director is not qualified, another officer or the authorized signatory assists. Other suitable qualified staff are also required.
- An individual applicant is the authorized signatory. In a partnership, a partner is. In a corporation, the board appoints the authorized signatory. The technical director must be a director under the Companies Ordinance. One person may be both if both tests are met. Another officer may assist only one technical director, and the authorized signatory cannot be that other officer. An authorized signatory, technical director, or other officer generally cannot also be registered with another contractor, except a holding-company and subsidiary sharing arrangement.
- The Contractors Registration Committee must recommend inclusion under section 8B(10). It may advise on adding an authorized signatory, technical director, or other officer, on renewal under section 8C(4), and on restoration under section 8D(3). Membership is as in sections 8(3) and 8(3A).
- Demolition is not specialist work only if all of these are true: not in Scheduled Area 1; height 10 m or less; clear spans 6 m or less and cantilevers 1 m or less; not prestressed; earth retaining 1.5 m or less; no building within 5 m.
- Foundation specialist scope is all foundations except those with penetration of 3 m or less.
- Site formation is specialist work unless the gradients are gentle (under 15° across the lot and within 10 m around it), there is no steep or high slope or wall (over 30° or over 1.5 m), and no wall, slope, or combination over 1.5 m is to be built, as the note details.
- Ventilation specialist scope is work under the Building (Ventilating Systems) Regulations.
- Temporary works generally sit with the RGBC, foundation RSC, site-formation RSC, or demolition RSC for their own works. Pile caps may be RGBC or foundation RSC. A basement that is not a pile cap is RGBC. A diaphragm wall with penetration over 3 m is foundation RSC. Landscape and street works are RGBC, with some site-formation RSC work. A retaining structure with a diaphragm wall, piles, or caissons is foundation RSC.
- Mini-piles of 400 mm diameter or less are foundation RSC, or RGBC if they are under 3 m deep. They are not site-formation RSC work.
- Dredging and reclamation are typically site-formation RSC where the paragraph 6 triggers apply. Trial pits are normally ground-investigation RSC, with limited RGBC exceptions.
- Fire dampers may be installed by an RGBC or a ventilation RSC. If an RGBC installs them, a ventilation RSC must inspect and certify them (Fire Safety Code clause E8.3 and APP-13).

**What to submit**
- The owner appoints the correct register category. The Authorized Person (AP), Registered Structural Engineer (RSE), or registered geotechnical engineer chooses by competence.
- A grey case is decided by the Building Authority (BA) case by case, on a proposal from the AP, RSE, or registered geotechnical engineer.
- Registration, renewal, and restoration follow PNRC 38, the practice note for registered contractors.

**Watch-outs**
- An RGBC cannot be appointed for specialist work such as piles deeper than 3 m, demolition outside the exceptions, or steep site formation.
- The same authorized signatory or technical director should not be registered with two unrelated contractors.
- Fire dampers installed by an RGBC still need certification by a ventilation RSC.
- Mini-piles are not site-formation works for a site-formation RSC.
