# APP-93 Planning and Design of Drainage Works
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision May 2014 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 211; first issue August 1997)

> Scope note: Planning of common drains and stacks in new buildings so that they can be maintained without entering a separately occupied unit. The Building Authority requires the arrangements below under section 28(1). External drainage access is also in PNAP ADV-14. If a duct or well also carries rainwater or pipes serving a non-mandatory feature or non-essential plant room, gross floor area exemption is subject to PNAP APP-151.

## Regulatory Overview
Except on car-parking floors, common underground drains run in land that is sterilized or otherwise designated as common parts. In a domestic building that is not for single occupancy, internal common soil and waste stacks are in the common parts and no pipework of one unit protrudes into a separately occupied unit on the floor below, and that last rule is encouraged, but not required, for non-domestic premises.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Pipe ducts and pipe wells (paragraph 3)
| Item | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Pipe duct | Access from the common parts. Unobstructed working space not less than 700 mm in front of the pipes. Access door or panel not less than 600 mm wide by 2,000 mm high, to Part C of the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings 2011 |
| Pipe well | Plan size not less than 1,200 mm by 1,500 mm. No opening except inspection and maintenance access from the common parts, at not more than 21 storeys apart. Cat ladder with guard rings for the full height. Grating platforms at not more than 4 storeys apart. Each access opening not less than 600 mm by 2,000 mm, to Part C. Ventilation opening at top and at bottom, each not less than one-tenth of the horizontal area of the well |
| Exemption | The area of pipe ducts and pipe wells may be exempted from gross floor area. An open pipe well may also be exempted from site coverage |
| Re-entrant or light well with external stacks | The lowest level is common parts, with access, which may be a cat ladder, for maintenance and cleansing |
| Buildings more than three storeys | Separate pipework to a manhole for the sanitary fitments on the floor that is level with the bottom of the waste or foul stack |
| Soil pipe | Nominal diameter not less than the outlet of any water-closet fitment or slop sink it serves |
| Bends | Only where a change of direction is unavoidable. At the bottom of a stack, radius not less than four times the radius of the stack measured to the centreline where practicable, and in no case less than 200 mm to the centreline |

**SD takeaway:** Put common stacks in a duct with 700 mm clear working space, or in a well at least 1,200 mm by 1,500 mm with access every 21 storeys, and do not run one flat's pipes into the flat below.

### 2. Traps, sunken slabs, and screened pipes (paragraphs 5–7)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Bathtub or shower to its trap | A modification of regulation 25(1) may allow a total length up to 750 mm at a minimum gradient of 1:40, so that pipes need not enter the unit below |
| Sunken slab | The void is not backfilled with concrete or similar material. If backfilling cannot be avoided, provide access panels or cleaning eyes extended to finished floor level so that every part of the pipework can be inspected and cleared, including by a camera |
| Pipes enclosed by an architectural feature | Provide for visual inspection of pipes, connections, and fixings from the common parts, and show that access on the drainage plans and the building plans. Pipes only partly screened, and meeting Appendix C, are not treated as enclosed |
| High-rise | Consider separate pipes in zones at design stage. See PNAP ADV-14 for maintenance of external pipes |

**SD takeaway:** Keep a bath or shower waste to 750 mm at 1:40 if it must reach a trap without entering the flat below, and leave a sunken-slab void open or give every pipe an access point at floor level.
