# ADV-35

**Official title:** Greening in Buildings

**In plain words:** Planting on a building can be building works, or it can be just soil and plants, depending on how it is fixed. This note tells you how to judge that, and how to design the loads, drainage, and maintenance access when greening is added. It grants no gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC) concession. Concessions for greenery, where they exist, come from other notes such as APP-42, APP-152, and the joint practice notes.

**Numbers that matter**
- Whether greening is building works is decided case by case, looking at the whole picture: how it is fixed, whether it is permanent, its nature and intended use, its size, the associated works and facilities, and how easily it can be assembled and removed. Example: a fixed planter box is building works. Laying soil in that box and planting in it is not building works under the Buildings Ordinance.
- Some greening installation works can use the minor-works simplified requirements. See Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation and APP-147.
- If there are no new building works, a plan submission is not necessary. The authorized person (AP) or registered structural engineer (RSE) must still assess load capacity, drainage, fire safety, and any change of use.
- If there are new works or strengthening, submit an alteration and addition proposal for approval.
- Design for every load combination: drainage, substrate, growing medium, and the plant species. Use fully saturated weights. Include the irrigation system and construction loads. Under the Code of Practice for Dead and Imposed Loads 2011, soil, waterproofing, the drainage system, and plants are dead loads, and imposed loads are added. Where people may gather, use at least the minimum imposed load for that use. Take extra care on an inaccessible roof, a long-span roof or podium, a water pond, and a cantilever slab.
- Drainage must discharge heavy rain. The surface fall is not shallower than 1:80, both for the structure and for the greenery. Adapt the system to the greenery specification. An existing building may need extra or specific drainage. Waterproof properly before greening. Inspect and maintain it, and put leakage or a blockage right immediately.
- Greening must not reduce or obstruct a refuge floor or the safety of that refuge.
- Protective barriers follow Building (Construction) Regulation 8. Provide safe access for maintenance.
- Advise owners on safe use and on regular inspection and maintenance of the affected structure and drainage.
- For an existing building, check the approved records against the proposed greening loads. APP-117 covers alteration and addition structural assessment.

**What to submit**
- First decide whether the work is building works. Then use the minor-works route or a full alteration and addition approval, as applicable.

**Watch-outs**
- Assuming that planting is outside the Buildings Ordinance without looking at how it is fixed.
- Underestimating the saturated load on a cantilever or a long-span roof, which can cause collapse or seepage.
- Greenery that obstructs a refuge.
- A drainage fall shallower than 1:80, which ponds water and overloads the structure.
