# APP-28

**Official title:** Requirements for Qualified Supervision of Site Formation Works, Excavation Works, Foundation Works on Sloping Ground, and Ground Investigation Works in the Scheduled Areas — BO Section 17

**In plain words:** For site formation, excavation, foundations on sloping ground, and ground investigation in the Scheduled Areas, the Building Authority (BA) may impose Buildings Ordinance (BO) section 17 conditions for qualified geotechnical supervision, and the supervisors must be named for agreement. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Building (Administration) Regulations (B(A)R) regulations 8(1)(bb) and 8(1)(bc) are among the provisions under which Category I inspections check design assumptions on site.
- Where the geotechnical risk is minor and the consequence of failure is insignificant, the Building Authority normally will not impose qualified geotechnical supervision.
- A lease or title may already require geotechnical supervision. Check that through a sectional CBS search.

**What to submit**
- The Building Authority imposes the section 17 conditions at plan approval or at consent. Submit the named individuals for agreement. Categories I, II, and III may be combined in one package. Geotechnical content of the plans also follows B(A)R regulation 8.
- Category I: periodic inspections by a qualified geotechnical engineer who took part in preparing the geotechnical submission, on sites where stability or ground movement is sensitive to the design assumptions. The inspector checks those assumptions on site and judges whether the design should change. Assumptions that are often wrong include the geological model, for example weathering versus how far a sheet pile penetrates, and the groundwater regime, for example a perched water table. A typical nominee is a registered professional engineer (geotechnical) (RPE(Geotechnical)), or an equivalent person, from the firm that prepared the submission. An RPE(Geotechnical) is not always required; the Building Authority decides case by case.
- Category II: periodic inspections by a senior qualified geotechnical engineer who is responsible for the geotechnical submission, on a high-sensitivity site, for example excavation next to old buildings on shallow foundations. That senior person must give the site their own attention. A typical nominee is an RPE(Geotechnical) who is a partner or director, or an equivalent person, of the firm that prepared the submission. An RPE(Geotechnical) is not always required.
- Category III: full-time supervision by a suitably experienced person, for day-to-day compliance with the specification and the procedures, for example ground anchors, compaction of a fill slope, or a complex excavation and lateral support (ELS) system. A typical nominee is a suitably experienced and qualified graduate engineer or geologist, or a technical inspector or works supervisor.

**Watch-outs**
- Nominating a Category I or Category II supervisor who was not involved through the design firm.
- A Category III supervisor who is not experienced enough for anchors, excavation and lateral support, or compaction.
- Missing a supervision duty that the lease already imposes.
- Failing to check sensitive assumptions, especially geology and groundwater, as the works go on.
