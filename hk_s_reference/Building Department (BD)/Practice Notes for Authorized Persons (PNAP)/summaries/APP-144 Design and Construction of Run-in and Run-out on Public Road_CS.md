# APP-144 Design and Construction of Run-in and Run-out on Public Road
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issue September 2006; in operation 1 January 2007 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 300)

> Scope note: Design, construction, and completion certification of a vehicular run-in and run-out where ingress and egress are allowed under the lease. Dimensions are on the Highways Department standard drawings named in Appendix A, which are drawings rather than stated dimensions in the text. A similar note was issued to registered contractors. It is not a car-park layout code.

## Regulatory Overview
The run-in and run-out match the adjoining footpath material and the Highways Department standards in Appendix A, and the details go on the general building plans for circulation to the Highways Department. A completion certificate is submitted before, or with, the occupation-permit application, and defects the Highways Department will not accept may be a ground to refuse that permit under section 21(6)(a) of the Buildings Ordinance.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Material and construction (paragraphs 3–7 and Appendix A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Concrete footpath | Concrete run-in and run-out. Highways Department drawings H1113, H1114, H1115, and H1116, and General Specification clauses 6.68, 9.44, and 16.58 to 16.62. Sections 9, 10, and 11 (Parts 5 to 7) of the General Specification for Civil Engineering Works |
| Paver-block footpath | Paver blocks, with a visual contrast and/or a change in pattern. Drawings H1103, H5101, H5102, H5114, H5115, and H5116. Colour specified by the authorized person. Laboratory test certificates or information from the registered contractor. Latest Highways Department requirements incorporated on the general building plans |
| Cutting | Saw-cut method, so adjoining pavement is not damaged |
| Damage outside the run-in | Reinstated after a separate excavation permit from the Highways Department |
| Making good | The authorized person ensures the adjoining footpath or pavement is made good |
| Utilities | Check services underneath, coordinate diversions, and follow Highways Department Technical Circular No. 3/90 or any update on minimum ground cover |

**SD takeaway:** Match the footpath material, saw-cut the edges, and put the Highways Department drawing requirements on the general building plans.

### 2. Completion and who builds it (paragraphs 8–11 and Appendix B)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Certificate | Appendix B, Certificate of Completion of Vehicular Run-in and Run-out, if the work is finished before the occupation-permit application. Early completion is encouraged |
| Take-over | Buildings Department notifies the Highways Department, which inspects. Defects are sent back for rectification. An objection on defects may refuse the occupation permit under section 21(6)(a) |
| Highways Department builds it | Submit the entrustment letter and the demand-note receipt. A temporary run-in that does not endanger the public or inconvenience traffic may allow the occupation permit to be processed before the permanent work is finished |
| Programme | Authorized persons are encouraged to build the permanent run-in with their own resources. The Highways Department may not have resources to undertake the work |

**SD takeaway:** Certify the permanent run-in before the occupation permit, because an unaccepted defect can refuse that permit.
