# ADV-38

**Official title:** Innovative Building Materials and Technologies

**In plain words:** A supplier can ask the Buildings Department to pre-assess an innovative building, drainage, or structural product that is not tied to one site, so the industry knows whether it can meet safety, health, and environmental standards. A letter of in-principle acceptance is not approval of a project, and it is not a gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC) concession. Innovative geotechnical products are not covered here. Approach the Geotechnical Engineering Office of the Civil Engineering and Development Department for those.

**Numbers that matter**
- The supplier or manufacturer appoints an authorized person (AP), a registered structural engineer (RSE), and/or a registered geotechnical engineer (RGE). An RGE is needed if there is a geotechnical matter, for example a new foundation system. Those professionals evaluate compliance with the Buildings Ordinance and submit the pre-acceptance application.
- The application contains: the Appendix A form, signed by the supplier or manufacturer and the appointed professionals; an innovation brief (the use, the design principle, job references, and what is innovative, for example a new cement replacement or aggregate, or the absence of a local or international standard); building, drainage, and structural plans that show the Ordinance standards are met, or that performance is equivalent or not inferior; technical specifications and a catalogue; technical documents; accredited laboratory tests or assessments; and a factory quality assurance scheme with ISO 9001 or an equivalent.
- The mechanism does not apply to a material, product, system, or technology already accepted in a practice note, for example modular integrated construction, or already in a code of practice, or already in the Central Data Bank.
- A determination is normally within 45 days, either an in-principle acceptance letter or comments. Reply to all comments within 30 days, or the application may be treated as abandoned. After a reply, the Department determines the case within 30 days. An incomplete reply can be refused, with a list of what is still outstanding, and a new application is then required. A discussion before you apply is encouraged.
- The acceptance letter states requirements and limitations, reminds you that the project still needs approval and consent, states whether the minor-works simplified requirements are suitable, and is published on the Buildings Department website with a short description.
- Validity is 5 years at most. Renew on the Appendix B form, with an AP, RSE, or RGE as appropriate, against the Buildings Ordinance requirements then in force.
- Pre-acceptance is not an exemption from prior approval and consent, and it is not a prerequisite for approving plans that use the product. Plan submissions should include a section 42 modification or exemption where that is appropriate. The project design must comply with the Ordinance, the approved plans, and the limitations in the acceptance letter.

**What to submit**
- Form IBMT 1 (Appendix A): 2 signed coloured sets of building, structural, and drainage plans, and 1 set of supporting documents (the innovation brief, specifications, calculations if they apply, evidence of compliance, laboratory reports, the quality assurance scheme, and a list of limitations).
- Renewal on the Appendix B form before the 5 years expire.
- On the project general building plans: apply for a Buildings Ordinance section 42 modification or exemption as needed, and follow the limitations and any minor-works suitability stated in the letter.

**Watch-outs**
- Treating the acceptance letter as project approval or consent.
- Using an acceptance that is more than 5 years old, without renewal.
- Putting modular integrated construction, or an item already in the Central Data Bank, through this track. Those are outside its scope.
- Ignoring a limitation in the letter, or failing to seek a section 42 modification for the project.
