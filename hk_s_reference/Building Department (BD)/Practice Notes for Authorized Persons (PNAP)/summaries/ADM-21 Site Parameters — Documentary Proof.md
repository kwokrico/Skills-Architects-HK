# ADM-21

**Official title:** Site Parameters — Documentary Proof

**In plain words:** Site coverage (SC) and plot ratio depend on the site area you can show you own or can realistically control, and on the boundaries and setting-out that support that area and the site class. This note says which documents the Buildings Department will accept, and which ones only get the general building plans through on a provisional basis. Superstructure consent waits for a proper lease plan or a land survey plan.

**Numbers that matter**
- Building (Administration) Regulation 8(1)(h)(i) requires a diagrammatic plan, with full dimensions and calculations, showing height, site coverage, plot ratio, and disposition under Building (Planning) Regulations 19, 20, and 21. ADV-33 Appendix A1 item 3(a)(1.1) Part C says general building plan approval needs a detailed lease plan or a land survey plan.
- The authorized person (AP) must keep the site figures in the proposal accurate, and must update them before certifying that the completed building complies with the Buildings Ordinance.
- Common proofs, from paragraph 4:
  - A District Survey Office lease plan, with lot areas, boundaries, and setting-out coordinates.
  - An authorized land surveyor’s land survey plan, with the same content.
  - Lease documents that give approximate areas and boundaries but no setting-out coordinates.
  - Deed polls of subdivided lots, with areas and boundaries, with or without coordinates.
  - An approved general building plan of an existing building that shows the site area in the site coverage and plot ratio calculations.
- A District Survey Office lease plan or an authorized land surveyor’s land survey plan is acceptable for processing the general building plans. The other three are provisional reference only. They can help plan approval, but they do not unlock superstructure consent.
- Before consent to start the superstructure, a District Survey Office lease plan or an authorized land surveyor’s land survey plan must be submitted. The Building Authority imposes that through a Building (Administration) Regulation 10 condition on the general building plan approval.
- If the lease plan or land survey plan disagrees with the other paragraph 4 sources, the Department generally follows the lease plan or the land survey plan. Note Buildings Ordinance section 14(2): approval does not give you title and does not waive the lease.
- If both a lease plan and a land survey plan exist and they disagree, the AP must explain, on the general building plans, which set is chosen. The Department must accept that explanation before superstructure consent.
- If, after approval, the lease plan or land survey plan shows figures that differ from the approved plans and that difference affects layout, site coverage, or plot ratio, submit amended general building plans before superstructure consent can be processed.
- Under the Land Survey Ordinance (Cap. 473) Code of Practice, coordinates and dimensions go to 0.001 m. Areas are rounded (5 up, 4 down) to the nearest 0.1 m² if the area is under 2,000 m², and to the nearest 1 m² if the area is 2,000 m² or more. The Department uses the same rounding on general building plans.
- When you rely on a surveyor’s plan, submit two sets. One set is for central processing and referral to the District Lands Office or District Survey Office, with form SMF-0043, the survey report, and the survey record plan, as Lands Department Practice Note 2/2013 describes. You may attach the encroachment-avoidance papers in Legal Advisory and Conveyancing Office Practice Note 4/2008.

**What to submit**
- With the general building plans, prefer a District Survey Office lease plan or an authorized land surveyor’s land survey plan. If you only have the provisional proofs, expect a regulation 10 condition requiring one of those two before superstructure consent.
- Two sets of the land survey plan, prepared to the Cap. 473 Code, when you rely on the surveyor’s plan.

**Watch-outs**
- Heading for superstructure consent on provisional proofs only blocks consent, or breaches the approval condition.
- Ignoring a clash between the lease plan and the land survey plan, or between either of them and the site area on the plans, raises a section 14(2) problem and usually needs amended plans. Building on an inflated site area is both a title risk and a density risk.
- Using approximate lease areas as the final plot-ratio base, without setting-out, leads to errors in site class and in street and lane deductions. Those items are on the ADM-19 density checklist.
- Certifying completion while the site figures are still the old ones, and do not match the surveyed title, exposes the compliance certificate.
