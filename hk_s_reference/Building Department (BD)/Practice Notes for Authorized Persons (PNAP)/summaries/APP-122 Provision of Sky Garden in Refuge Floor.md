# APP-122

**Official title:** Provision of Sky Garden in Refuge Floor

**In plain words:** A communal sky garden may be placed on a refuge floor if it meets the means-of-escape and fire-resisting-construction rules, with Fire Services Department (FSD) agreement and the other tests in Joint Practice Note (JPN) No. 1. Gross floor area (GFA) treatment stays in JPN No. 1 and must be applied separately, and greenery on a roof used as a refuge can support a wholesale conversion where the building envelope is tight.

**Numbers that matter**
- Comply with paragraph 21 of the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire and paragraph 18 of the Code of Practice for Fire Resisting Construction, except for the special cases this note states. When measuring the net refuge area and the minimum dimension, ignore space taken by planters, equipment, and furniture.
- For wholesale conversion of an existing building, a refuge, or a main roof used as a refuge, may be accepted even if it does not meet means-of-escape paragraphs 21.2 and 21.3, or fire-resisting-construction paragraph 18.2, or both, if the fire service installations are enhanced to the FSD’s satisfaction and suitable greenery is provided.
- Where the main roof is the refuge, at least 50% of the total roof area must be planted greenery. Short grass turf that people can walk on counts toward the net refuge area. Paragraphs 2(b) to 2(d) still apply.
- A lift that opens onto a refuge combined with a sky garden needs a protected lobby. Doors need a fire resistance period (FRP) of at least 1 hour. Walls need at least 2 hours. Landing doors of a lift that is not a fireman’s lift must lock automatically when the fire alarm is on.
- An example of enhanced fire service installations is a drencher flow of more than 10 litres per minute per square metre over external openings, and fast-response sprinklers where sprinklers are required.
- Previously PNAP 258. First issued December 2001. This revision September 2010.
- File reference: BD GP/LEG/6(II).

**What to submit**
- Consult the Building Authority (BA) and the FSD early if the design is special.
- Show compliance with the means-of-escape code, the fire-resisting-construction code, and JPN No. 1. Where a technical waiver is sought, show the enhanced fire service installations.
- Provide a maintenance and management manual.
- Post notices that naked-flame activities, such as barbecues, are not allowed.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not count the footprint of planters or furniture as net refuge area.
- Furniture, equipment, rubbish bins, artificial features, and finishes must be non-combustible and must not give off toxic gas. They must not block an exit route or a lift opening. Bins need self-closing lids. Furniture and equipment must be firmly fixed.
- A lift door onto the refuge without a protected lobby, or without the automatic lock, does not comply.
- Live plants are preferred. Avoid artificial plants. Treat highly flammable live plants, such as those with resin or volatile oils, with caution. Under paragraph 3, the 50% greenery is live planting. Artificial plants do not count as that greenery.
