# APP-4

**Official title:** Water Supply and Wells

**In plain words:** This note covers proof of mains water at the first plan submission, and when a well may be allowed if the mains cannot serve the site. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Send the Water Authority’s Certificate Regarding Water Supply Availability, Form WWO 1004 or an equivalent, with the first general building plan submission.
- If the Water Authority certifies that a mains supply is not available (Building (Standards of Sanitary Fitments, Plumbing, Drainage Works and Latrines) Regulation 10A(5)), permission for a well or another source may be considered.
- Flushing demand: dwellings, offices, and similar uses, 450 litres per required soil fitment per day; restaurants, 13.5 litres per seat per day; cinemas, 4.5 litres per seat per day; schools, 18 litres per head per day; hotels and boarding houses, 90 litres per room per day. Potable demand is assessed on its merits.
- Fresh or salt mains water is not for evaporative air-conditioning, except for the immediate needs of a specific industrial process.
- Wells are normally not allowed on government land. A well on private land (Building (Construction) Regulation 85) is allowed only if the yield is adequate and drawing water will not harm nearby services, buildings, structures, or land. In Yuen Long Town (Area 2) and Ma On Shan (Area 4), wells may cause excessive settlement, and flushing is usually supplied from the mains when that is possible.
- Yield test (Appendix A): only from 1 Dec to 30 Apr; 7 consecutive days; pumping not more than 12 hours a day, with at least 12 hours of recovery. On days 1 to 6, pump the proposed daily yield, averaged over the pumping period. Measure the metered quantity, not the pump’s rated output. Time the water levels. On day 7, check suspended particles at full pump discharge and maximum drawdown. Discharge to a drain, not back into the aquifer. Accept the test if the daily quantity is at least the required amount, recovery is adequate, and the water is clean. If pumping would exceed 12 hours a day, or there is concern about long-term yield, a more sophisticated test (for example BS 6316:1983) may be required. Notify the Buildings Department and the Geotechnical Engineering Office at least 1 week before the test.

**What to submit**
- Well application: plans and cross-sections of the drawdown influence; well design, screen, switching, and development method; the rate and hours per day; a yield-test proposal. If needed, a registered geotechnical engineer (RGE) site-investigation report, a geotechnical assessment with calculations, and field verification tests.
- Authorized person certificates under Building (Administration) Regulation 25A, using the models in Appendix B (permanent connection) and Appendix C (yield test under regulation 10A(7)). Send an extra copy of the yield-test report to the Geotechnical Engineering Office Information Unit. Water quality must satisfy the Building Authority. A Water Authority sample report may be required.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not design the supply without the Water Authority availability certificate.
- A well in a geologically sensitive scheduled area without a proper RGE assessment risks settlement and refusal.
- A yield test outside the December-to-April window, or without notice to the Buildings Department and the Geotechnical Engineering Office, is not acceptable.
