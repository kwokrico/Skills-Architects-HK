# ADV-27

**Official title:** Protection of Natural Streams/Rivers from Adverse Impacts Arising from Construction Works

**In plain words:** Keep natural streams as natural as you can. Concrete lining is the last option, not the first. This note follows Environment, Transport and Works Bureau Technical Circular (Works) No. 5/2005. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). It can constrain the site layout, dewatering, and landscaping beside a stream. A similar note goes to registered contractors.

**Numbers that matter**
- A natural stream or river, for this note, is a natural channel carrying natural water from higher ground, either all year or in the wet season, with a natural bed of bedrock, boulders, cobbles, gravel, sand, silt, or clay, and largely natural banks with riparian vegetation. It does not include a flood-retention pond, a fishpond, or an engineered stormwater system such as a drainage channel, nullah, or ditch. Examples of what is excluded: the Tin Shui Wai, Yuen Long, and Tuen Mun nullahs, engineered sections of the Shing Mun River, and concrete U-channels.
- Ecologically important streams and rivers are on a list the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department updates. Avoid impacts on those especially.
- Direct impacts include training, filling, culverting, narrowing, widening, damming, realignment, diversion, and shotcrete on the banks.
- Indirect impacts include site formation, landfilling, discharge of silt or polluted water, dumping debris nearby, foundation dewatering, and geotechnical or demolition work near the stream.
- Engineering options, from least impact to most, in Appendix A: natural rock bank protection; selective removal of obstructions and vegetation; enlarging one bank; enlarging both banks; deepening; realigning into a new channel; and, last, a new artificial smooth lining such as concrete.
- Extra measures for an ecologically important stream: soft but robust bank techniques, geotextile-reinforced grass, bio-engineering, stone facing, gabions; leave the bed intact where you can; translocate species, keep migratory paths, and compensate; put similar substrate back after the works; do not work across the full width if the stream is wide enough to leave a flowing portion; provide a fish ladder if a dam is on an upper breeding reach; consult the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department about rare species; preserve pools.
- Construction precautions in Appendix B: temporary isolation with sandbags or silt curtains that have a lead edge on the bottom; temporary crossings on stilts; stockpiles covered and set back; wastewater handled by minimising it, then reusing or recycling it, then treating it; hydroseeding of disturbed banks; lateral support that does not unduly block flood flow; and supervisory staff stationed on site.
- Comply with whichever of Cap. 28, Cap. 102, Cap. 131, Cap. 132BK, Cap. 208, Cap. 354, and Cap. 358 apply. Put the mitigation into the contract. Follow the recommendations of any required environmental review or study.

**What to submit**
- No standalone Buildings Department form. The measures go into the contract and into any environmental study the project needs.

**Watch-outs**
- Concrete lining as the default.
- Working in a flowing stream in the wet season, which collapses banks and sends turbid water downstream.
- Working across the whole width of an ecologically important stream, with no refuge flow left.
- Disturbing a rare species without consulting the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department.
