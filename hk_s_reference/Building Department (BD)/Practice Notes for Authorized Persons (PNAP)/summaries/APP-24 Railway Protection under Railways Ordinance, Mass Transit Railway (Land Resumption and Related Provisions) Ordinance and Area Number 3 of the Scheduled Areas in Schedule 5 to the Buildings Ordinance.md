# APP-24

**Official title:** Railway Protection under Railways Ordinance, Mass Transit Railway (Land Resumption and Related Provisions) Ordinance and Area Number 3 of the Scheduled Areas in Schedule 5 to the Buildings Ordinance

**In plain words:** This note sets extra scrutiny, clearances, and movement limits for works near a railway, including Scheduled Area Number 3. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- The protection area is about 30 m outside the outer railway structure, fence, or wall, or outside the nearest rail. If any part of a lot is inside that 30 m, the whole lot may be treated as inside the area. Stations may extend further. Some lines are Area Number 3 in Schedule 5 to the Buildings Ordinance.
- Works in a protection area are specially scrutinised, and measures under section 27 of the Railways Ordinance or section 15 of the Mass Transit Railway (Land Resumption and Related Provisions) Ordinance may apply. A monitoring plan covers site investigation, foundations, excavation and lateral support, and any work that causes movement or vibration. Tell the MTR Corporation when work starts. Send monitoring records to the MTR Corporation every two weeks, or at an agreed frequency. If an Alert or Action limit is reached, suspend the work. The Buildings Department and the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department issue a joint press statement within 48 hours, and another within 48 hours after an agreed restart.
- Ground investigation in Area 3 needs prior approval and consent (Building (Administration) Regulation 8(1)(l)). Plans follow Appendix B. Approval and consent may run together under ADM-16. Underground drainage in Area 3 is not exempt from the Buildings Ordinance, except certain items under the minor-works control system. Outside Area 3 but still in a protection area, send ground-investigation and underground-drainage proposals to the MTR Corporation before you start. Class I and Class II minor works for spread footings, excavation, or underground drainage in Area 3 are copied to the MTR Corporation, and you need their agreement before you start.
- Appendix B clearances. No pile, foundation, borehole, well, soil nail, or similar element within 3 m of an underground railway structure without prior special agreement from the MTR Corporation. No piling within 3 m of a railway fence or wall, or within 7 m of the nearest track centreline where there is no fence, without agreement. Any part of an anchor is more than 3 m from the railway structure, and the centroid of the fixed length is more than 2× that fixed length away. The change in vertical or horizontal pressure on an underground structure is not more than 20 kPa, unless an engineering approach is accepted. A borehole within 10 m in plan of an underground structure, with its tip within 3 m in depth of the highest point of that structure, uses a verticality method. A borehole within 3 m in plan uses a depth-control method. A building opening is at least 5 m from a railway ventilation opening, or 2.5 m if the exhaust is directed away. Scaffolding, signs, and projections above the tracks are not within 6 m in plan without agreement. Vertical greening is at least 5 m from a railway ventilation opening. On an unfenced section, trees are at least 2 m from the nearest rail. A crane jib arc, storage, and mobile plant within 6 m are controlled.
- Empirical movement limits in Appendix B, part C(a): angular distortion not more than 1:1000; total movement not more than 20 mm; difference in rail level not more than 5 mm; blasting peak particle velocity not more than 25 mm/s; prolonged vibration not more than 15 mm/s; overhead-line and signalling furniture, peak particle velocity not more than 10 mm/s and amplitude not more than 80 µm. Alert, alarm, and action levels are about 50%, 75%, and 100% of the movement limits, and about 60%, 80%, and 100% of the vibration limits. The table gives distortion of 1:2000 (alert), 1:1350 (alarm), and 1:1000 (action), and movements of 10 mm, 15 mm, and 20 mm, with the other rows scaled in the same way.
- An engineering approach (Appendix C) sets staged alert, alarm, and action levels from estimated movements, each stage being 10% to 50% of the total. Alert is 50% of the current stage plus the stages already passed. Alarm is 100% of the current stage plus the stages already passed, except the last stage, where alarm is the mean of alert and action. Action is 100% of the total estimated movement.

**What to submit**
- Plans are circulated to the MTR Corporation. Put the monitoring plan in the approval documents. Give commencement notice, and send records every two weeks, to the MTR Corporation. Area 3 ground investigation and drainage need full approval and consent. Copy minor-works submissions to the MTR Corporation. Use a pre-submission enquiry for an engineering approach. An independent survey team sends the initial readings to the Buildings Department and the MTR Corporation before works start. Consent may be given stage by stage as monitoring is reviewed.

**Watch-outs**
- A borehole that can punch into a tunnel, or any work within 3 m, or piling inside the fence or track buffer, without MTR Corporation agreement, is not acceptable.
- Crossing an alert, alarm, or action level without stopping the work brings a press announcement and a regulatory stop.
- A pressure change of more than 20 kPa needs an accepted engineering approach.
- Openings or greenery too close to a railway vent, or a crane or storage within 6 m without agreement, breach the clearances.
