# ADV-32

**Official title:** Provision of Babycare Rooms and Lactation Rooms in Commercial Buildings

**In plain words:** The Buildings Department encourages at least one babycare room in a commercial building, and at least one lactation room in an office building. The sizes in this note are design minima. This note does not grant a gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC) concession. Unless another instrument exempts them, these rooms count as accountable floor area.

**Numbers that matter**
- A babycare room is for breastfeeding, expressing milk, bottle-feeding, and changing nappies. Separate the nappy-changing area from the feeding area, and provide private space for breastfeeding. Recommend at least one babycare room per building, scaled to need.
- Put it in a common area, easy to reach, on the ground floor or by a convenient lift if it is upstairs. Separate it from toilets, and do not let foul air into it. Use clear directional signs. The symbol is in Appendix A. Both men and women can enter. The breastfeeding area is signed “Breastfeeding Area for Woman only / 餵哺範圍，女士專用”.
- A single-user room is at least 5 m², with space to turn a wheelchair. A multiple-user room is at least 12 m², with separate breastfeeding cubicles, one of them for a wheelchair user. Sample layouts are Figures 1 and 2 of Appendix B.
- Where a sanitary fitment such as a sink is provided, lighting and ventilation follow Building (Planning) Regulation 36. A comfortable temperature is recommended, with a thermostat if that helps. Finishes should be family-safe, floors non-slip, and edges not sharp.
- Cubicle doors, and the door of a single-user room, may lock, but must be openable from outside in an emergency. An “engaged” indicator is optional.
- Provide: comfortable chairs or benches; a nappy-changing table with a raised edge and a wipeable soft mat; a sink with a lever tap and a soap dispenser; a way to dry hands, such as paper towels; a waste bin with a hinged cover for nappies; a small side table; and an electrical outlet for a pump. Optional items include a sensor alcohol dispenser, clothing hooks, a safety mirror, a floor drain or leak sensor, and an emergency alarm. Prefer automatic sensor taps and soap. If space allows, put the nappy table perpendicular to the wall, at a height and with knee space suitable for a person with a disability.
- A lactation room is for offices. Recommend at least one per office building. Put it in a common area, with access for authorised people or employees, separated from toilets. Size is at least 7.5 m². It accommodates at least 2 users, with privacy, and a wheelchair user, in separate cubicles with doors. A sample layout is Figure 3 of Appendix B. Sign it for women only. Locks must be openable from outside in an emergency.
- Basic lactation facilities: electrical outlets, small side tables, and comfortable chairs or sofas. Optional: a refrigerator, which need not be inside the room; paper towels; a covered waste bin; a mirror; hooks; a sink and soap if feasible; and a floor drain or leak sensor where there is a sink. Expressed milk is stored in the refrigerator.
- Both rooms are free of charge during the building’s opening hours. Post the hours, who may use the room, and a management contact. Check that the room is working, regularly.

**What to submit**
- These are guidelines, not a mandatory submission. Show the rooms on the general building plans, with access, and with sanitary provisions where a sink is provided under Building (Planning) Regulation 36.

**Watch-outs**
- A babycare room next to toilets, or on a path for foul air.
- A room below the 5 m², 12 m², or 7.5 m² minima, or a multiple-user room with no wheelchair cubicle.
- A privacy lock that cannot be opened from outside in an emergency.
- These rooms are not the children’s or unisex toilets counted under ADV-28. Do not mix the two provisions up.
