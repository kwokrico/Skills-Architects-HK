# APP-128

**Official title:** Geotechnical Design Information

**In plain words:** This note is good practice for organising geotechnical plans, reports, calculations, and computer analyses so that the works, the rest of the site, and neighbouring ground stay stable during construction and afterwards. It does not directly change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC), though an early geotechnical assessment can still affect how much of the site can be built on.

**Numbers that matter**
- The stability duty lines up with Building (Construction) Regulation (B(C)R) 23 for foundations near features or slopes.
- Computer analyses should use a pre-accepted program. The practice note then numbered 79 is the reference. State the program name, version, developer, Buildings Department reference, and validity period, plus the accepted scope and limitations.
- Geotechnical feasibility at general building plan stage follows Building (Administration) Regulation (B(A)R) 8(1)(ba) and the practice note then numbered 78, where the assessment criteria are met.
- Site formation follows B(A)R 8(1)(bb). Excavation and lateral support follows B(A)R 8(1)(bc) and the related practice notes. Scheduled Areas follow the practice notes then numbered 85 and 161. Northshore Lantau follows the practice note then numbered 283.
- Design review during construction links to the performance review in the practice note then numbered 246, now PNAP APP-115.
- Distinguish proposed works from existing works under B(A)R 14.
- First issued January 2003. This revision December 2005.
- File references: BD GR/OA/71/1; GP/BORD/75.

**What to submit**
- The report needs an executive summary, and a schedule of geotechnical design assumptions and parameters with the justification from ground investigation, laboratory tests, and groundwater-level monitoring.
- Build in provisions to review the design during construction.
- For a computer analysis, the registered geotechnical engineer (RGE) completes the Appendix A proforma. Include why the model was chosen, the assumptions and parameters, input taken from the ground conditions, the salient output (factor of safety, envelopes, and displacements), and how that output was interpreted for the design.
- Where the assessment criteria are met, submit the geotechnical feasibility with the general building plans.

**Watch-outs**
- Soil and groundwater parameters need a justification. A missing executive summary or a missing assumption schedule is a known deficiency.
- Computer output is not acceptable without the validation scope, the limitations, and acceptance of the program by the Buildings Department.
- Demolishing a building that supports the ground, without provisions for the stability of adjacent ground, is not acceptable. Do not stockpile debris near a geotechnical feature.
- Avoid the other common deficiencies the note lists, including survey shortcomings, missing rainfall precautions, an unclear sequence, and discrepancies between plans and the report.
