# APP-47

**Official title:** Unauthorized Alterations and Additions — Buildings Ordinance Section 14

**In plain words:** The Building Authority (BA) has no power to approve or consent to building works after they have already been carried out, except for the minor-works and exemption routes described below. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC), and unauthorised building works (UBW) cannot be turned into accountable or concession GFA by a retrospective plan approval.

**Numbers that matter**
- Buildings Ordinance (BO) section 14(1): apart from minor works under the simplified requirements, and works exempted under section 41, no person shall commence or carry out building works without prior approval and consent from the Building Authority. Under section 42(5), the exemption powers do not apply to section 14.
- Household Minor Works Validation Scheme, section 39C, from 31 December 2010: prescribed building works listed in Schedule 3 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation (B(MW)R), completed before that date, may be inspected and certified, and in some cases altered, rectified, or reinforced, and then retained. The Building Authority shall not serve a removal order under section 24, or a warning notice under section 24C, solely because those works contravened section 14 before that date. Their legal status remains unauthorised. The Building Authority may still act if their condition becomes dangerous.
- A submission of plans for completed works, other than minor works under the simplified requirements, is rejected under section 16(1)(a), because it is not a proposal made beforehand; under section 16(1)(c), because Form BA5 and Form BA17 refer to works “to be carried out” or to a proposal “to erect”; and under section 16(1)(d) where an effective demolition order covers the works.
- The validation procedure for eligible household minor works completed before 2010 is in section 62 of the B(MW)R.
- Starting works without consent can lead to a demolition order and to prosecution under Part IV, with a fine or imprisonment.

**What to submit**
- Advise the client that approval and consent are required before the works start.
- Use the minor-works simplified requirements where they apply.
- Do not submit as-built plans of unauthorised works in the expectation of approval.
- Eligible household minor works completed before 31 December 2010 follow the validation procedure in B(MW)R section 62.

**Watch-outs**
- Submitting plans of completed illegal works in order to obtain retrospective approval.
- A client starting works without consent, which can bring a demolition order and a Part IV prosecution.
- Treating the Validation Scheme as if it changed the legal status of the works from unauthorised to authorised.
