# APP-10 Oil Storage Installations
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision August 2007 | Buildings Department (first issue May 1978; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Above-ground tanks for petroleum products that are an oil storage installation under section 2(1) of the Buildings Ordinance, and the licence under the Building (Oil Storage Installations) Regulations. An installation is deemed to satisfy those regulations if it meets the Code of Practice for Oil Storage Installations 1992. Demolition safety is also in Practice Note 71.

## Regulatory Overview
An oil storage installation is any tank of not less than 110,000 litres, or a group of tanks in the same cluster or bunded area any one of which is not less than 110,000 litres, constructed above ground for storing petroleum products. Construction must not start without approval and consent under section 14(1), and operation must not start before a licence.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Licence, inspection, and alteration (paragraphs 5–8)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| New installation | Six copies of proposed operation instructions in English and one in Chinese, plus one set of general arrangement plans, agreed before a licence. An occupation permit and a certificate of general inspection are required before the licence |
| Existing installation, new tank or other alteration | Endorsement on the licence. Return the original licence with the application. Submit amended operation instructions and plans if operation changes |
| Annual renewal | Certificates of external inspection of the tanks under Regulation 8(1)(b) and of inspection of the associated works under Regulation 8(1)(c). The associated-works certificate is for a further twelve months, or states that the works are unfit until listed repairs are completed. Copy the Director of Fire Services |
| Internal general inspection | Of all tanks, not later than the tenth year of the tank's life, then once in the fifth year after the year of the first inspection. Certificate issued immediately to the licensee, who delivers it to the Building Authority |
| Repair or alteration | Two copies of proposals signed by a registered structural engineer, for written authorization under Regulation 10. Extensive repair, or alteration that is non-exempted building works, also needs section 14(1) approval |
| During construction or repair | Precautions against typhoon damage to partially constructed tanks |

**SD takeaway:** Treat any above-ground petroleum tank of 110,000 litres or more as an oil storage installation: obtain approval and consent, a licence, and an internal inspection by year 10 and then every fifth year after that first inspection.

### 2. Demolition (paragraph 10 and Appendix D)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Method | Agreed with the Building Authority before or with the application for consent or written authorization. Minimise pollution, fire, and explosion |
| Hoarding | Enclose the work area. If only part is demolished, or demolition is in stages, erect a fire screen between the part being demolished and the part still in operation |
| Gas-free | All pipes and tanks certified gas-free by a competent person. Permit-to-work on site. Follow the Institute of Petroleum Marketing Safety Code |
| Waste | Identify sludge and chemical waste. Disposal complies with the Waste Disposal (Chemical Waste) (General) Regulations. Keep the drainage system and oil interceptors serviceable, and seaboard valves closed and locked, until tanks, pipes, and sludge are removed. Effluent discharges comply with any Water Pollution Control Ordinance licence |
| Bund | Do not demolish bund floors and walls before tanks and pipe spills are removed, unless precautions prevent petroleum products escaping |
| Storm drains | Keep contaminated water out of the storm system. A plan of the storm drains is on site at all times |
| Before work | A contamination assessment report to the Environmental Protection Department guidance note, including past activities, an inventory of chemical and hazardous substances with locations and composition, manufacturing processes, and the soil sampling plan. After above-ground structures are removed, test soil for hydrocarbons and lead as that report specifies |

**SD takeaway:** Do not start demolition until a contamination assessment and an agreed method are in place, and keep the bund and the oil interceptors intact until the tanks and sludge are gone.
