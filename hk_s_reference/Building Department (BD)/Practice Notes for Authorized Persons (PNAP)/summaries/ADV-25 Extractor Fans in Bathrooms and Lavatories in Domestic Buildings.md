# ADV-25

**Official title:** Extractor Fans in Bathrooms and Lavatories in Domestic Buildings

**In plain words:** If a domestic bathroom or lavatory has an extractor fan, keep the plumbing seals intact and size the fan so the room does not go into strong negative pressure. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). It does affect the door or wall, because you need a way for replacement air to get in.

**Numbers that matter**
- Check that plumbing seals are intact and that they operate as designed. Consider the quality and quantity of incoming air, the airflow path, and the fan capacity.
- The World Health Organization figure cited for optimum bathroom ventilation is 2 cubic feet per minute per square foot, which is 10.2 litres per second per square metre. A much larger volume adds little comfort and risks pulling the building into negative pressure.
- Provide an air-relief opening, such as a door undercut or a louvred opening in the door or wall, so negative pressure stays small. Do not let the incoming air and the exhaust short-circuit each other.

**What to submit**
- Nothing dedicated. This is a design recommendation.

**Watch-outs**
- An oversized fan in a sealed bathroom pulls the room into negative pressure, can empty the trap seals, and was identified at Amoy Gardens as a path for pathogens.
