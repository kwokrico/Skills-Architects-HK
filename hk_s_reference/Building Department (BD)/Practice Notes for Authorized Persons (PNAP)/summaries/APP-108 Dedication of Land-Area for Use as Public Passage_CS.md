# APP-108 Dedication of Land/Area for Use as Public Passage
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision June 2003 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 233; first issue November 1999)

> Scope note: Concessions the Building Authority may grant for dedicating land or area as public passage. Surrender for street widening is dealt with under Building (Planning) Regulation 22(2), not as this dedication. It is not a lease or town-plan instrument.

## Regulatory Overview
The Building Authority may exempt dedicated public-passage area from gross floor area and may allow additional plot ratio and site coverage. Bonus intensity is available only where the dedication is accepted, and the resulting intensity must stay within any statutory town-plan restriction unless the plan provides otherwise.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Two dedication types and the bonus (paragraph 2)
| Type | Concession |
| --- | --- |
| Setback at street level for public passage | Site coverage and plot ratio may be exceeded as calculated under the Building (Planning) Regulations. Maximum additional plot ratio is five times the area dedicated or 20% of the permissible plot ratio, whichever is the less. Dedicated area may also be exempted from gross floor area |
| Area within or through a building at ground or other floors | Modification of the relevant regulations. Bonus site coverage, if allowed, is normally equivalent to the extent calculated under Building (Planning) Regulation 22(1). Maximum bonus plot ratio, as additional gross floor area, is generally five times the dedicated area at ground level and two times at other floor levels. Total bonus must not exceed 20% of the permissible plot ratio, and the dedicated area is exempted from accountable gross floor area |

**SD takeaway:** A ground-level public passage can be tested at five times its area as bonus plot ratio, and an upper-floor passage at two times, both capped at 20% of permissible plot ratio.

### 2. When dedication is accepted, and when bonus is allowed (paragraphs 4–8)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Street widening on town plans or street-improvement plans | Consider under Building (Planning) Regulation 22, not as this dedication |
| Town plan | Resultant intensity shall not exceed restrictions in the statutory town plan unless the plan provides otherwise |
| Grounds for acceptance | Alleviation of vehicular or pedestrian congestion; linkage with a footbridge or subway, existing or proposed, that is or will be readily accessible and used by the public and that significantly enhances public safety or convenience; improvement of road safety or significant enhancement of public convenience; or suitable greenery compatible with the passageway |
| Justification | Genuine public need, and a dedicated area reasonable for that need |
| Exemption versus bonus | Accepted dedication may be exempted from gross floor area. Bonus plot ratio and site coverage only if government considers the dedication essential, taking account of alternative public passages nearby |
| Shared use | If the area serves both the public and building users, exempted and bonus gross floor area are assessed by the proportion of traffic from each |

**SD takeaway:** Draw a public passage only where a genuine public need is shown; count bonus plot ratio only if the dedication is essential, not merely convenient.

### 3. Ownership, notices, and the deed (paragraphs 9–10)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Maintenance | Provision, maintenance, and repair rest with the building owners, and are a condition of dedication |
| Notices | Conspicuous notices of the dedication, opening hours, and the party responsible for management, to the satisfaction of the Building Authority |
| Occupation | A temporary occupation permit or occupation permit will not be issued before the Deed of Dedication is executed and all terms and conditions are fulfilled |

**SD takeaway:** Keep the passage in owner control, show opening hours on site, and complete the Deed of Dedication before applying for an occupation permit.
