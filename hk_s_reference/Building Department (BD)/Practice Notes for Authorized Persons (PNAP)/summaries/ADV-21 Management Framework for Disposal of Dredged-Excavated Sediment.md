# ADV-21

**Official title:** Management Framework for Disposal of Dredged/Excavated Sediment

**In plain words:** If a private project must dispose of dredged or excavated sediment at sea, and the Marine Fill Committee has not yet agreed the mud proposal, this note is the sampling, testing, and disposal framework under the Dumping at Sea Ordinance (Cap. 466). It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). It does set the programme for a reclamation, a marina, or a deep basement that produces marine mud.

**Numbers that matter**
- Re-test if more than 3 years pass between the start of sampling and the start of construction, unless the Director of Environmental Protection waives that.
- Disposal space is not allocated until you have shown the Secretary of the Marine Fill Committee why the sediment has to be removed. Do that preferably at the environmental-impact-assessment stage. Category L sediment of less than 50,000 m³ is exempt from that justification.
- Dredging that does not need that justification: emergency dredging for safety or to avert an environmental hazard; maintenance or deepening of harbour fairways, berths, anchorages, navigation channels, or approaches; and maintenance, not construction, of a watercourse, river, stream, drainage channel, or outfall. Otherwise, plan on the basis that the mud stays in place. Extra time for consolidation is not accepted as a justification.
- Appendix A chemical exceedance levels. Category L: every contaminant at or below the lower chemical exceedance level. Category M: any contaminant above the lower level and at or below the upper level. Category H: any contaminant above the upper level. For nickel and tributyltin, the lower and upper levels are the same, so a result above the listed value exceeds the upper level.
- The paired figures below are lower level / upper level. Units are milligrams per kilogram dry weight unless noted. Cadmium 1.5 / 4. Chromium 80 / 160. Copper 65 / 110. Mercury 0.5 / 1. Nickel 40 / 40. Lead 75 / 110. Silver 1 / 2. Zinc 200 / 270. Arsenic 12 / 42. Low-molecular-weight polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons 550 / 3,160 micrograms per kilogram. High-molecular-weight polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons 1,700 / 9,600 micrograms per kilogram. Total polychlorinated biphenyls 23 / 180 micrograms per kilogram. Tributyltin 0.15 / 0.15 micrograms of tributyltin per litre of interstitial water.
- Tier II sampling grids. Low contamination: 200 m by 200 m, surface only. High contamination: 100 m by 100 m, with a vertical profile. Very high contamination, near an outfall or a nullah: 50 m by 50 m, with a vertical profile. Vertical sub-sample tops: the seabed, 0.9 m, 1.9 m, 2.9 m, then every 3 m down to the dredged bottom.
- Sample sizes, as a guide: 0.5 litres for metals and metalloids; 0.5 litres for organics; 6 litres for a biological response. Store at 4°C, in the dark, and do not freeze. Maximum holding time is 2 weeks for a chemical test and 8 weeks for a biological test.
- Tier III biological screening. Category M: one set of three toxicity tests on each composite of up to 5 continuous samples of the same category. The tests are a 10-day amphipod test, a 20-day polychaete test, and a 48-hour to 96-hour larvae test. Category H with any contaminant above 10 times the lower chemical exceedance level: a dilution test, the sample mixed with 9 portions of reference sediment. Purge ammonia if the overlying water is 20 mg per litre or more: 6 volume replacements in 24 hours, and the purge may be repeated once. The sample fails if it fails any one of the three tests. Table 2 criteria, at p of 0.05 or less: amphipod survival below 80% of the reference; polychaete dry weight below 90%; larvae normality below 80%.
- Sampling, testing, and the sediment quality report normally take about 8 months. A biological-screening sediment quality report is submitted at least 3 months before the dredging contract is tendered, or at least 2 months before a maintenance works order. The Director’s reliability period starts on the actual date sampling began, and is not less than 3 years.
- Disposal types, Appendix C. Type 1 is open sea. Type 1 dedicated sites are for Category M that passes the biological tests. Type 2 is confined marine disposal, for example the East Sha Chau mud pits. Type 3 is special treatment or disposal, for Category H that fails the dilution bioassay. Sampling of vessels may be required for 5% of vessels.
- The Director may waive sampling and testing for emergency dredging, or for maintenance dredging of less than 5,000 m³ in situ.
- Agree the rationale with the Committee Secretary, then consult the Director on existing data, then submit the sampling and chemical-testing proposal to the Director, copied to the Committee. A preliminary sediment quality report follows. Biological screening follows if the sediment is Category M, or certain Category H. The formal sediment quality report is at least 3 months before tender.
- The Committee allocates disposal space. The Director is delegated to allocate it for Category L below 50,000 m³. Put the Committee’s and the Director’s disposal requirements, and notes (1) to (6) of Appendix C, into the contract. Private projects are charged per cubic metre in situ, certified by the authorized person (AP), registered structural engineer (RSE), or registered geotechnical engineer (RGE).
- Fill and disposal details go on the Committee’s electronic form, copied to the Buildings Department. The contractor applies to the Director for the dumping permit under the Dumping at Sea Ordinance.
- Chemical tests are by a HOKLAS-accredited laboratory, or an equivalent. Biological tests are by an accredited laboratory.
- If the reliability of the sediment quality report expires before the contractor obtains the dumping permit, the contract must say so, and the contractor pays for any extension of reliability.
- Type 3 sediment: the AP or RSE identifies and agrees the treatment and disposal with the Director. It is site-specific and is not prescribed in this note.
- Disposal by trailer suction hopper at East Sha Chau requires the down-a-pipe method approved by the Director of Civil Engineering.

**What to submit**
- The sampling proposal, the preliminary report, and the sediment quality report to the Director of Environmental Protection, with copies to the Marine Fill Committee as the sequence above requires. The disposal allocation request, and the contract clauses that carry the disposal conditions.

**Watch-outs**
- A programme that lets the sediment quality report expire before the dumping permit.
- Ignoring the starting assumption that mud stays in place, which leads to a refusal by the Committee. A cost justification that does not include the option and risk assessments.
- Type 3 sediment with no agreed treatment.
- Close supervision of sampling matters. The category, and who is liable for the disposal, rest on the quality of the data.
