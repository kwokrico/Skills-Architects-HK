# ADV-10

**Official title:** Lift Shaft Platforms

**In plain words:** A temporary platform inside a lift shaft under construction is a safety-critical working place. The Labour Department can stop the work if the design, supervision, and records in this note are missing. This note does not change permanent gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC). During a temporary occupation permit, the fire separation between occupied areas and construction areas still has to be maintained while shaft work goes on.

**Numbers that matter**
- These platforms are temporary and wall-supported. They may shelter from rain, support formwork, protect the mid-shaft, catch debris, or act as a crash deck. They are governed by the Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations, and by the general duties in the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) and the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59).
- The Labour Department may serve a suspension notice under Cap. 509 section 10 if any of the following is missing.
  - The platform is properly designed by an authorized person or registered structural engineer employed by the contractor (the contractor’s AP or RSE), with full details and specifications.
  - Construction plans and method statements for erection, alteration, maintenance, and dismantling are prepared by an engineer who is a technically competent person of grade T4 or above (the contractor’s engineer), and are checked and signed off by the contractor’s AP or RSE.
  - Construction is under the full-time immediate supervision of a competent person of grade T1, appointed in writing by the contractor. On completion, the contractor’s engineer checks it.
  - An alteration is designed by the contractor’s AP or RSE. The method statement is prepared by the contractor’s engineer and checked and signed off by the contractor’s AP or RSE.
  - Before erection, maintenance, or dismantling, a planning team (managerial or supervisory representatives of the main contractor and the subcontractor, including at least one person of grade T4 or equivalent) carries out a risk assessment, and a competent person inspects stability.
  - The platform is maintained as the construction plans require, and accumulated material is cleared regularly.
  - Dismantling is under the full-time supervision of the same grade T1 competent person.
  - Material is not thrown, tipped, or shot onto a platform unless the contractor’s AP or RSE designed the platform for that purpose.
  - Suitable fall-arrest systems are provided and used during erection, maintenance, and dismantling.
- Keep on site, for Labour Department inspection: the design plan; the construction plan and method statement; a record of dates and of the supervising competent person for erection, maintenance, and dismantling; and the risk assessment report.
- Plan fire safety, worker safety, and occupant safety at design stage. Every temporary occupation permit condition, including a boundary plan accepted or approved by the Building Authority, every precaution such as fire separation of adequate fire rating between occupied and construction areas, and every safety feature, must be kept in place at all times, including during lift-shaft and platform work, until the occupation permit is issued. See paragraphs 9 and 12 of Volume 1 of the Construction Industry Council Guidelines on Safety of Lift Shaft Works.
- The contractor’s AP or RSE is not the same appointment as the project AP or RSE, unless that person is also employed by the contractor for this work. A similar note goes to registered contractors.

**What to submit**
- Platform design and supervision are on the contractor’s side, under the Labour Department regime. The temporary occupation permit boundary plan is accepted or approved by the Building Authority, and the permit conditions stay in force until the occupation permit.

**Watch-outs**
- A platform with no proper design, or supervision by someone who is not a technically competent person of the required grade, can draw a suspension notice. The note cites fatal and injury cases.
- Dumping debris onto a platform that was not designed as a debris deck.
- Occupied zones under a temporary occupation permit without the fire separation maintained during shaft work.
- Mixing up the project AP with the contractor’s AP or RSE. Both roles can exist. The suspension risk sits mainly with contractor non-compliance, but the temporary occupation permit safety conditions remain.
