# APP-11

**Official title:** Street Improvement Schemes — Submission of Building Plans in respect of Lots affected thereby

**In plain words:** This note says a street improvement line is only a desired layout: approval of building plans does not promise that the works will be built, that land will be resumed, or that compensation will be paid. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Street improvement schemes approved before 1982 remain valid despite Cap. 370.
- An improvement plan shows desirable works only. It is not a statement that the works will be carried out or that land will be resumed, and it is not a basis for compensation or damages.
- Buildings Ordinance approval of plans that follow the improvement plan does not mean the works will be implemented or that compensation will be paid. Approval of plans that do not follow it does not stop the Government acting under other laws, including resumption.

**What to submit**
- If a lot looks affected, ask the Highways Department what the Government intends before you submit formal general building plans, so the design is not wasted.

**Watch-outs**
- Buildings Ordinance approval does not lock in implementation of the street improvement, and it does not create a right to compensation.
- Designing to an improvement line that may never be built, without Highways Department confirmation, risks a wasted redesign.
