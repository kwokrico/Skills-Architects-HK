# ADV-15

**Official title:** Fixing of Reinforcement for Concrete Works

**In plain words:** Bad fixing of reinforcement is a known cause of corrosion, spalling, and collapse. This note sets hold points: do not use a batch until it is tested, and do not concrete until the authorized person (AP) or registered structural engineer (RSE) has inspected the steel. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC).

**Numbers that matter**
- Do not fix bar or fabric from a batch until testing of that batch is complete. See the current reinforcement-testing practice note (formerly PNAP 122).
- Remove loose rust, excessive flaky rust, and mill scale by wire brushing. Do not use steel that is badly corroded, damaged, or scaling.
- Use enough spacers, chairs, and supports to keep the reinforcement where the approved plans show it, and to keep the specified minimum cover. Maximum spacing of spacers and chairs is 1.5 m. For bars of 20 mm diameter or less, reduce the spacing so the bars do not sag.
- Spacers are concrete, or a proprietary plastic or concrete type. Make them as small as practicable. They must carry the reinforcement and the construction loads without breaking, deforming, or overturning. Their strength and durability must be at least that of the surrounding concrete.
- Tie enough intersecting bars and laps. The ends of tying wire, devices, and clips must not stick into the cover zone.
- Stop people and plant walking on completed reinforcement. Use planks or ladders that are supported independently and clear of the steel. Protect the steel from later trades, such as conduit fixing. On a cantilever, it is critical that the top reinforcement does not deflect. Use extra chairs and ties.
- Laps and joints only in the specified positions. Connectors follow the manufacturer’s recommendations and equipment.
- Brace free-standing reinforcement against wind. Support and brace a prefabricated cage before it is lifted.
- The registered contractor gives continuous supervision so the fixing matches the approved plans and this note, and so the reinforcement stays correct through placing and compaction.
- The contractor tells the AP or RSE to inspect all reinforcement after it is fixed and well before concreting. For walls and columns, inspect before the formwork is fully erected, so there is access.
- The AP and RSE provide adequate quality supervision. A similar note goes to registered contractors.

**What to submit**
- No extra form. The inspection before concreting is the hold point. Record it in the supervision system.

**Watch-outs**
- Concreting without an AP or RSE inspection of the fixed reinforcement, or inspecting a wall or column only after the shutters are closed.
- Spacers more than 1.5 m apart, or too far apart for bars of 20 mm or less. Tie-wire ends in the cover zone.
- Walking or running a wheelbarrow on the top steel of a cantilever, which moves the reinforcement. The note cites this as a contributor to collapse and casualties.
- Using steel from an untested batch, or steel that is badly corroded.
