# APP-136 Emergency Vehicular Access under Building (Planning) Regulation 41D
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision July 2006 | Buildings Department (previously PNAP 288; first issue May 2005)

> Scope note: How to show emergency vehicular access on general building plans, how to measure the major facade, and the Appendix B relaxations. Design and construction standards are in Part VI of the Code of Practice for Means of Access for Fire-fighting and Rescue. An access that meets that code complies with regulation 41D. Regulation 41D does not apply to building works being carried out, or whose consent had been given, as at 31 December 2004; in general, building works may refer to foundation works of the proposal.

## Regulatory Overview
Regulation 41D of the Building (Planning) Regulations requires emergency vehicular access to all new buildings, designed and constructed to requirements the Building Authority specifies, currently Part VI of the means-of-access code. The general building plans must include a separate access plan, and a shortfall is an exemption or modification on Form BA16.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the access plan must show (paragraphs 4–5)
| Item | On the plan |
| --- | --- |
| Alignment | Extent and alignment, marked and coloured as the access, distinct from other building works |
| Geometry | Width, inner and outer turning radii, loading capacity, and gradient for the entire access |
| Signs | Location of all types of signage and the crash gate |
| Overhead structure | A separate section showing clear headroom, incorporated into the plan |
| Facade | A block plan showing the major facade served, coloured for identification |
| Percentage | Calculation of the total length of facade served and that length as a percentage of the total length of all perimeter walls. Projections accepted by the Building Authority are disregarded, excluding balconies and utility platforms under Joint Practice Notes 1 and 2 |
| Paving | Use of paving other than hard paving, for example grass paving |
| Public street | Information may be confined to alignment, geometry, the block plan, and the percentage. Geometry may be confined to the portion serving the major facade, and loading capacity is not required |

**SD takeaway:** Put a coloured access plan on the general building plans, with width, radii, gradient, loading, headroom, and the facade-length percentage.

### 2. Measuring one-fourth of the perimeter (paragraph 6)
Paragraph 24.2(e) of the means-of-access code sets the major-facade requirement. For a simple contour (square, rectangle, circle, triangle, L-shape, cruciform, and similar), use the actual total length of the perimeter walls to calculate one-fourth. For a complicated contour, the total length of the notional building contour line in Appendix A may be used instead.

**SD takeaway:** Measure one-fourth of the real perimeter for a simple plan, and one-fourth of the notional contour for an irregular plan.

### 3. Exemption and Appendix B relaxations (paragraphs 7–9 and Appendix B)
Exemption from any or all standards in the code, under regulation 41D(3), if compliance is impracticable having regard to topography, or unwarranted because the use is a low fire risk. Apply on Form BA16 with full justification and, where appropriate, a fire-safety report as required by paragraph 27.2 of the code. The Building Authority consults the Director of Fire Services. Appendix B items generally do not need a fire-safety report, though enhanced measures may be required case by case.

| Situation | Code paragraphs | Relaxation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Dead-end access | 24.2(d), 25.2 | No turning space if fire-service appliances need not reverse more than 30 m |
| 2. Run-in and run-out | 24.2(a), 25.3(c) | Width may be relaxed to not less than 4.5 m |
| 3. Domestic building of 3 storeys or below, including a single-family building to which paragraph 5 of the means-of-escape code applies | 24.2(a), 25.3(c) | Width may be relaxed to not less than 4.5 m depending on topography |
| 4. Same domestic case | 24.2(e), 25.3(c) | Horizontal distance from the access to the facade exceeds 10 m but is within 30 m, subject to fire-service enhancement which may include a hose reel |
| 5. Same domestic case | 24.2(e), 25.3(c) | Distance exceeds 30 m, subject to enhancement which may include a sprinkler system and/or other enhancements depending on the scope, distance from the main road, and water supply |
| 6. Redevelopment on an original Class A, B, or C site, other than industrial, cinema, or theatre | 25.3(c) | Existing road or street serving as the access may be not less than 4.5 m wide. The one-fourth major-facade rule may be relaxed where topography makes compliance impracticable. For a Class A site the major facade served shall not be less than the minimum distance between the two opposite side boundaries within 10 m of the access. Class B and C sites follow paragraph 24.2(e) |

**SD takeaway:** A dead end is acceptable if appliances reverse no more than 30 m, and a constrained domestic access of three storeys or below can drop to 4.5 m wide.
