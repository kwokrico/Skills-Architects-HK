# APP-95

**Official title:** Sale Offices and Show Flats on Construction Sites

**In plain words:** A sales office or show flat in a new building on an active site needs a temporary occupation permit, and a temporary building used for that purpose also needs a temporary building permit. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage, and a temporary occupation permit is not an occupation permit.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issued August 1997. Re-issued August 2009.
- No part of a new building, whether under construction or substantially finished, may be used as a sales office or show flat without a temporary occupation permit.
- No temporary building on the site may be used for that purpose without a temporary building permit and a temporary occupation permit for that use.
- The Building Authority (BA) must be satisfied that the space is structurally suitable, separated from the construction by fire-resisting construction, has its own protected access and escape with a direct connection to a street, and has fire service installations to the satisfaction of the Director of Fire Services.
- Conditions may limit how long people may occupy it, how many people may be there at once, and whether site works must stop in part or in full during occupation.
- Breaking the requirements or the conditions can mean revocation of the temporary occupation permit and prosecution under Buildings Ordinance sections 40(1) and 40(5).

**What to submit**
- The Authorized Person (AP) applies with information showing the four criteria above, and states the period and the expected number of people.

**Watch-outs**
- A sales gallery in an unfinished tower without a temporary occupation permit is not allowed.
- Fire separation from the construction, and an escape route that is not shared with the works, are required.
- The protected escape must connect directly to a street.
- Going over the permitted number of people, or ignoring a condition that site works must stop, can be a criminal offence.
