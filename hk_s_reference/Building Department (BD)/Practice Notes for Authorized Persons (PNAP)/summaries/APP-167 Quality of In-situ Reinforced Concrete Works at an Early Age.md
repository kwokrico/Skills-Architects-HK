# APP-167

**Official title:** Quality of In-situ Reinforced Concrete Works at an Early Age

**In plain words:** Where a structure uses more than one concrete grade, the Building Authority will require early rebound hammer tests (RHT) on the higher-grade elements, as an alert system, alongside the quality-control items in APP-158 (a similar practice note went to registered contractors). This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issue March 2022.
- The condition is imposed under Buildings Ordinance section 17(1) item 6, on the first approval of the superstructure plans (Appendix A).
- Test between the 7th and the 10th day after casting. If that day is a holiday, test on the next working day. Method: BS EN 12504-2:2021.
- The registered structural engineer (RSE) picks the elements at random. The frequency must be at least:
  - 50% of vertical elements that are a higher grade than the immediate supports at the base (pile cap, footing, raft, transfer plate, or transfer beam). Include kickers if they are present.
  - 10% of vertical elements that are a higher grade than the adjoining beams or slabs at the top.
  - 50% of transfer beams.
  - 10% of beams that are a higher grade than adjoining beams or slabs on the same floor.
- The RSE’s technically competent person (TCP) at grade T3 must witness at least 10% of the RHTs.
- The RHT is carried out by the registered contractor’s (RC) TCP at grade T1 or above, trained in rebound hammers, or by a Hong Kong Laboratory Accreditation Scheme (HOKLAS) laboratory. Record the results on the Annex form and keep them on site for the RSE’s TCP.
- Alert levels are empirical, and they apply when there is no engineering assessment. With pulverised fuel ash (PFA) or ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS), follow up if the RHT equivalent is below 55% of the minimum characteristic strength. Without PFA or GGBS, follow up if it is below 65%.
- Alternative: the maturity method on the 7th day, under Concrete Code 2013 clause 11.7.5.4. Use the same measuring points and the same alert levels as the RHT.

**What to submit**
- The condition is attached at the first superstructure approval for a multi-grade structure. Keep the Annex RHT records on site.
- The RSE decides which elements to test and how often, at or above the table minima.
- On a project already under way, the RSE and the RC should advise the client to consider using the system even if it was not imposed.
- If a result is below the alert level: check the approved plans, the order and delivery records, the method and curing, and the supervision records. Do more RHTs or coring. Tighten supervision of similar elements. Keep the review. If necessary, report to the Buildings Department and suspend concrete works until the follow-up is finished.

**Watch-outs**
- Skipping the early RHT on a transfer system, or on higher-grade vertical elements, when more than one concrete grade is used.
- Missing grade T3 witnessing of at least 10% of the tests.
- Weak follow-up, or no report to the Buildings Department, when a result is below the alert level. That is a structural safety liability.
