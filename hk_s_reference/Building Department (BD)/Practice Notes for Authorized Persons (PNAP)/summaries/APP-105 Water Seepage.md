# APP-105

**Official title:** Water Seepage

**In plain words:** This note sets how to keep rain and water out of roofs, external walls, curtain walls, windows, balconies, utility platforms (UPs), bathrooms, kitchens, plumbing and drainage, car park floors, and basements, and when a pipe may pass through structure. Genuine pipe ducts that follow PNAP APP-93 may be left out of gross floor area (GFA). Site coverage (SC) stays unchanged.

**Numbers that matter**
- Performance sits in the Building (Construction) Regulations (B(C)R): section 3 (materials), section 27 (external walls impervious to water), sections 32 and 33 (damp walls and wet floors), and section 34 (weatherproof roof).
- Water-bearing pipes are generally not allowed inside columns, slabs, structural walls, beams, transfer plates, pile caps, or footings, except the piercings in Appendix A paragraph 2.
- A vertical hole through a slab or transfer plate must not exceed 150 mm diameter or the minimum bar spacing, whichever is less. It must not cut main reinforcement. Trimming bars must be at least the size of the main slab reinforcement.
- A horizontal hole through a beam must not exceed 150 mm or one-third of the beam depth, whichever is less. Place it at the neutral axis. Provide vertical and horizontal trimming of at least 16 mm on each side. Do not cut shear reinforcement.
- A horizontal hole through a structural wall must not exceed 150 mm or the minimum vertical bar spacing, whichever is less. Trimming on both sides must be at least the size of the vertical wall reinforcement.
- Prefer pipe sleeves cast into the structure, where the hole has no adverse effect and is easy to reach.
- Roof waterproofing (Appendix B) must cover the whole roof, turn up at least 300 mm above the finished level, and use angle fillets where vertical and horizontal surfaces meet. Flood or water tests are required. Annex 1 recommends waterproof concrete of at least grade C35. The structural roof slab should be at least 150 mm thick, with high-yield (HY) bars of at least 10 mm at not more than 150 mm centres, on both faces and in both directions, and steel of at least 0.25% of the concrete section.
- A bathroom needs a membrane over the whole floor, turned up the walls at least 300 mm. Bathtub and shower walls need membrane to at least 2,200 mm. Basin and sink walls need membrane to at least 1,100 mm above finished floor level, or 300 mm above the fitting, whichever is higher. A sunken slab must be monolithic with the floor and waterproofed over the whole sunken area, with access panels or inspection chambers.
- A kitchen, including an open kitchen, needs a floor membrane with a 300 mm turn-up. The wall at a sink or countertop needs membrane to at least 1,100 mm, or 300 mm above the countertop.
- At a pipe passing through a wet area, dress the waterproofing up at least 20 mm above the raised platform, down at least 50 mm into the floor outlet, and 100 mm horizontally around the pipe so that it overlaps the wet-area membrane.
- Enhanced Appendix B guidelines apply to a new general building plan (GBP), a major GBP revision, and alteration and addition (A&A) work submitted after 31 March 2025.
- Previously PNAP 230. First issued October 1999. Last revision December 2010. This revision September 2024.
- File references: BD GR/1-55/173; GP/BORD/92; GP/BREG/P/9 (III).

**What to submit**
- On structural and drainage plans, state clearly that no water-bearing pipe, other than an Appendix A paragraph 2 piercing, will be embedded in structural elements.
- If embedding is proposed, submit the route and the justification. Leaving this out can lead to disapproval under Buildings Ordinance (BO) section 16(1)(i). B(C)R section 14(4)(a) is also relevant.
- Carry out the compliance tests, including flood tests and the water-penetration tests for precast and curtain walls required by the codes.
- A GFA exclusion for a genuine pipe duct is a modification, including a modification of Building (Planning) Regulation (B(P)R) 23(3)(a). The duct needs enough access for inspection and maintenance under PNAP APP-2. Smart monitoring and access devices are encouraged.

**Watch-outs**
- Embedding a water-bearing pipe in structure without approval and justification can get the plans disapproved.
- Weak waterproofing or weak supervision can leave the authorized person (AP), registered structural engineer (RSE), and registered contractor (RC) liable for later seepage, including a health nuisance from foul water and damage to the structure.
- Tanking on the outside of a basement cannot practically be maintained after backfill. Test it before backfill.
- Car park and basement design should include falls, intercepting channels at the top and bottom of ramps, and water-stops at movement joints. A basement, excluding pile caps, thick footings, and diaphragm panels, is advised to be designed as a water-retaining structure.
- The AP, RSE, and RC must see that materials, design, workmanship, and supervision meet the BO performance rules.
