# APP-90

**Official title:** Buildings Ordinance section 18(6) — Authority to Enter Buildings

**In plain words:** If shoring inside a neighbouring building is the only precaution that will work, and the owners or occupants will not agree, the Building Authority (BA) can authorise entry under Buildings Ordinance section 18(6). This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issued June 1997. Re-issued August 2009. See also practice note (PNAP) 71 on shoring for demolition.
- Apply only after there is no other way to provide the precaution, and after the Authorized Person (AP) or Registered Structural Engineer (RSE) has taken all reasonable steps and still has no consent.
- If satisfied, the BA normally issues written authorisation within 2 weeks and posts a copy on the building.
- Sections 18(4) and 18(5) cover repair of damage and compensation for loss. Section 18A sends disputes to the Lands Tribunal.
- The written application must include: the applicant’s name; Hong Kong identity card, passport, or business registration certificate number; the full address and lot of the building to be entered; the purpose; justification that internal shoring is the only precaution; the name of any agent under section 18(6)(b); and the registration certificate number if the applicant or agent is a registered contractor.

**What to submit**
- A written application with every particular listed above. Do not enter before the authorisation is issued.
- Follow the statutory repair and compensation duties. Use PNAP 71 where the shoring is for demolition.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not ask for section 18(6) entry before voluntary consent and other methods have been exhausted.
- Entry without the posted authorisation is not authorised.
- Damage must be repaired and loss compensated. A dispute can go to the Lands Tribunal.
- An incomplete application delays the usual 2-week turnaround.
