# ADV-34

**Official title:** Building Information Modelling (BIM)

**In plain words:** The Buildings Department encourages a building information model with a plan submission, but it approves the paper plans or the two-dimensional PDF, not the model. If the model and the plans disagree, the plans win. This note does not grant a gross floor area (GFA) concession. A good model does make the area diagrams and the means-of-escape drawings in ADV-33 easier to check.

**Numbers that matter**
- Information in the model must be identical to the prescribed plans. Keep manual editing of drawings generated from the model to a minimum.
- The Department processes approval on the paper plans or the two-dimensional PDF. The model is used to check the contents. On a discrepancy, the plans prevail.
- General building plans follow ADM-19 and the “Guidelines for using BIM in General Building Plans Submission”, plus the sample drawings and models on the Department’s website.
- Other statutory plans follow the “Guidelines for using BIM in Statutory Plan Submissions (other than GBP)”, plus the joint Construction Industry Council and Buildings Department samples and templates. Council standards are at bim.cic.hk.
- You may add an optional real-time simulation, as a .wmv or .avi file that plays in Windows Media Player 11 or later, to show phasing: demolition, foundation, excavation and lateral support, site formation, substructure, superstructure, or alteration and addition.
- Appendix A examples of where a model helps: irregular building form, projections, site and street levels, area calculations, means of escape and compartmentation, whether plans match each other, and alteration-and-addition relationships; complicated drainage and the relationship of underground drains to foundations and site formation; materials and grades, fire resistance and cover, steel connections, transfer structures and load paths, basement and geotechnical features, facade and curtain-wall assembly; pile levels, bearing stratum, and top-down construction; site formation; and demolition sequence and temporary measures.
- The Department continues to expand how BIM is used.

**What to submit**
- The model together with the statutory plans, where you use one. Make the model match the plans. Use the Construction Industry Council user guides and standards.

**Watch-outs**
- A clash between the model and the plans delays processing and raises audit questions, even though the plans prevail.
- Heavy editing of drawings after they leave the model defeats the point of a consistent model.
- The model by itself is not the approved plans.
