# ADV-5 Tropical Hardwood Timber
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued July 1992 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Encourages alternatives to tropical hardwood in hoardings, formwork, and internal finishes. It does not yet amend a regulation; the note says that exercise was still under way.

## Regulatory Overview
The Buildings Ordinance Office asks authorized persons to reduce tropical hardwood because Hong Kong construction is described as a major user, much of it discarded after minimal use. While amendments to legislation and practice notes were still being prepared, the note encourages steel and other alternatives on the current project.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Where to stop using hardwood (paragraphs 6–13)
| Element | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Hoarding | Consider materials other than tropical timber. Steel is named as reusable, and the Architectural Services Department and the Housing Department already required steel hoardings on their sites |
| Covered walkway | Consider whether one is necessary. It is not an essential precaution on every site, particularly where buildings are set back from the site boundary |
| Temporary formwork | Named as the largest single consumer. Alternatives listed: profiled steel sheeting as permanent formwork and as reinforcement in the hardened concrete; precast units instead of in-situ concrete; structural steel instead of a reinforced-concrete frame; steel formwork; proprietary steel props instead of timber props |
| Internal finishes | Non-timber proprietary wall-panel systems instead of hardwood frames for non-loadbearing built-in panels; alternatives for doors, frames, skirtings, and architraves, especially where they are painted |
| Waste | Specify that hardwood waste is separated and reused rather than dumped with other rubbish |
| If no alternative | Specify hardwood from renewable sources |

**SD takeaway:** At schematic design, prefer a steel or precast frame and steel hoarding and formwork so tropical hardwood is not locked into the specification.
