# APP-41

**Official title:** Buildings to be Planned for Use by Persons with a Disability — B(P)R Regulation 72 / Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008

**In plain words:** The Building Authority (BA) must refuse new plans and alteration plans that do not provide reasonable access for persons with a disability, under the Disability Discrimination Ordinance (Cap. 487) section 84 and Building (Planning) Regulations (B(P)R) regulation 72. This note does not give an automatic gross floor area (GFA) concession; ramps, lifts, sanitary fitments, and elderly-friendly features affect the planning, and an accessible-lift extension may seek an amenity concession under practice note APP-42, paragraphs 44 to 45, where those criteria are met.

**Numbers that matter**
- The governing provisions are Building (Administration) Regulations (B(A)R) regulation 8(1)(m); B(P)R regulations 2(1) and 39(3)(e), regulation 72, and the Third Schedule; and regulation 61(3) of the Building (Standards of Sanitary Fitments, Plumbing, Drainage Works and Latrines) Regulations.
- Part 4 of the Third Schedule lists categories that require only limited barrier-free access. Except for a building exempted under B(P)R regulation 72(4), every building that is not in Part 4 must comply in full.
- The Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008, in editions through 2025, contains the statutory requirements and the recommended practice. Amendments are in Appendices C to I, covering 2012 to 2024. Appendix J is recommended elderly-friendly design in Chapter 6, from the task force on ageing in place, to be applied first by administrative practice and later by an amendment of the B(P)R.
- Appendix A describes special circumstances for varying the requirements on an alteration of an existing building that did not have access at the outset: ramp works in common parts the applicant does not control; consent of co-owners or the owners’ corporation declined or unavailable; and, where there is a ground beam, a spatial or structural constraint.

**What to submit**
- Show the barrier-free access clearly on the plans, under B(A)R regulation 8(1)(m).
- An application for exemption or modification is considered on its merits. The Building Authority may refer it to the Advisory Committee on Barrier Free Access (ACBFA).
- Follow the current Design Manual: Barrier Free Access 2008 (2025 Edition) on the Buildings Department website.
- Encourage the client to adopt the elderly-friendly recommendations in Appendix J.

**Watch-outs**
- General building plans that do not show the barrier-free provisions clearly.
- Treating a Part 4 building, which has limited requirements, as though it were fully exempt.
- Altering a building that has no access, without exploring practical access measures.
- Claiming hardship without the Appendix A evidence of no control over the common parts, a refusal by the owners’ corporation, or a structural constraint.
