# APP-100

**Official title:** Structural Plans of Glass Reinforced Polyester (GRP) Water Tanks

**In plain words:** A glass reinforced polyester (GRP) water tank is building works, and the structural submission depends on its size. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage, but the tank’s weight must still be carried by the roof or floor.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly practice note (PNAP) 222. First issued March 1998. This revision December 2010 (paragraph 7 amended; paragraph 9 on minor works added). See APP-147 and Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation.
- GRP is strong but not stiff, so stiffness controls the design. Allow for creep and for the environment so the tank does not fail early.
- Before consent, for a tank that is not small: test reports from a recognised laboratory or organisation on the fibreglass panel’s mechanical properties, and showing the panel can withstand 6 times the design hydrostatic pressure, plus the manufacturer’s quality-control details for the panels and for fabrication of the tank.
- Performance tests, filled to overflow at ambient temperature: no visible leak after standing at least 48 hours. Side deflection at most 1.0% of the tank depth, and bottom deflection at most 10 mm, after the full load has been on for at least 2 hours.
- A small tank is volume 9 m³ or less and water head 2 m or less. Paragraphs 3 to 5 of the note are normally not required.
- An indoor very small tank is volume 4 m³ or less and water head 1.2 m or less. Submit only location, dimensions, capacity, and weight, plus calculations for the floor structure that supports the tank.
- Some GRP tank work in an existing building is minor works under Schedule 1. See APP-147.

**What to submit**
- Full structural plans for a tank above the small-tank limits: design standards; material type, standards, and mechanical properties; maximum designed capacity and the main dimensions and thicknesses; how the main components are made; fixing and supports; and calculations showing the tank and its base support are adequate.
- Lodge the 6-times hydrostatic test reports and the quality-control documents before consent.
- After the tests, submit the leakage and deflection reports with a statement by the Authorized Person (AP) or Registered Structural Engineer (RSE) that the performance was met.
- A small tank (9 m³ or less and head 2 m or less) needs location, principal dimensions, capacity, weight, design standards, material specifications, fixing details, and support calculations.
- An indoor tank of 4 m³ or less and head 1.2 m or less needs location, dimensions, capacity, weight, and floor-structure calculations only.
- Existing-building work may use the minor-works path where Schedule 1 applies.

**Watch-outs**
- Designing for strength only, and ignoring stiffness, creep, and the environment, can cause early failure.
- Consent should not be sought without the 6-times hydrostatic substantiation, where that test applies.
- A failed leak or deflection test, or a missing AP or RSE confirmation, is not acceptable.
- Do not stretch the small-tank limits to avoid the full test submission.
- Support and fixing calculations that are too light can fail the floor.
