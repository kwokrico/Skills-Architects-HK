# APP-43 Licensing of Child Care Centres, Kindergartens and Restaurants
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision August 1996 | Buildings Department (first issued December 1985; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Buildings Department guidelines used when licensing plans for child care centres, kindergartens, and restaurants are referred for comment on structural safety and means of escape. Unless an application complies, an intended change in use under Buildings Ordinance section 25 will be prohibited. Child care centres and kindergartens should not be in an industrial building.

## Regulatory Overview
A child care centre or kindergarten may not be above ground floor in a building that is not purpose-built unless the location conditions in paragraph 2 are met, including the Code of Practice for Means of Escape and the height rules in the licensing regulations. A floor proposed for restaurant use must be shown to carry its self-weight, finishes, permanent partitions, other permanent construction, proposed plant, and the minimum imposed load under the Building (Construction) Regulations.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Where a centre or kindergarten above ground floor is acceptable (paragraph 2)
| Location | Extra conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Composite building with not more than two commercial floors above ground floor | Premises conform to the means-of-escape code. A child care centre is subject to the 12 m and 24 m maximum height rule under Child Care Centre Regulation 19. A kindergarten is subject to the 24 m maximum height rule under Education Regulation 7 |
| Composite building with three or more commercial floors, where the centre or kindergarten occupies one or more entire commercial floors and not more than two floors above ground floor remain commercial | Same |
| Institutional building; high-rise domestic building; wholly commercial building | Same, plus two independent means of escape for the exclusive use of the occupants |
| Composite building with three or more commercial floors above ground floor, other than the case above | Same, plus the two exclusive stairs |
| Single-staircase building, other than the two unconditional cases | Same height and code rules, plus two extra staircases to the premises to be licensed, and the single staircase serving the whole building sealed off from them |
| Single-family domestic building not exceeding three main storeys, and New Territories exempted buildings | Unconditional |

**SD takeaway:** Do not place a child care centre or kindergarten above ground floor in a non-purpose-built building unless the height limits and, where required, two exclusive stairs are provided, and never in an industrial building.

### 2. Restaurant licence (paragraphs 4–7 and Appendix A)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Discharge value | Existing restaurants established after the occupation permit must be included when calculating the discharge value of staircases and exit routes for a further restaurant |
| Unauthorized works | The licensing authority is advised to reject the application until works that risk patrons and employees are removed or rectified. Works not exempted under section 41 need approval and consent first. See PNAP 125 |
| Structural particulars | Raised screeds in kitchens, stages, and lavatories; new brick walls or solid partitions; fish tanks; large kitchen equipment and walk-in freezers; large central air-conditioning units |
| Screed | Confirm it was laid under supervision, or core-test material, type, density, and thickness |
| Plant and partitions | State type, size, thickness, and density, and attach the manufacturer's catalogue, so the specific or operating weight in the calculation is correct |

**SD takeaway:** Check a restaurant floor for the imposed load plus screeds, tanks, and kitchen plant, and recount stair discharge including every restaurant already in the building.
