# APP-78

**Official title:** Occupation of New Buildings — Buildings Ordinance section 21

**In plain words:** The Building Authority (BA) handles an occupation permit (OP) under Buildings Ordinance (BO) section 21, and must process the application within 14 days. This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage, but a refusal for unfinished fire or sanitary work can still stop occupation.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly practice note (PNAP) 188. First issued August 1996. Re-issued August 2009.
- The statutory processing time is 14 days.
- Apply on Form BA 13.
- Minor defects may be cleared inside that 14-day period by the Authorized Person’s (AP) written confirmation and, where suitable, photographs. Send them early enough for the OP to be prepared.
- After a refusal that is not for a premature application, the BA re-inspects if needed and issues the OP or refuses again within 14 days of the AP’s written notice that the items are fixed.
- See PNAP 53 and PNRC 25 (the practice note for registered contractors) for amendment plans, record plans, and schedules.

**What to submit**
- Form BA 13, with the required certificates, record plans, and schedules.
- The Development Division building surveyor arranges an early inspection with the AP. The AP arranges it with the registered general building contractor (RGBC).
- A premature application that is refused needs a fresh Form BA 13. Other refusals do not.
- A refusal is sent first by fax, then by a formal letter. The letter may add comments beyond the statutory grounds.

**Watch-outs**
- Certifying completion too early can lead to disciplinary proceedings under the Buildings Ordinance.
- Obviously unfinished controlled work, such as missing sanitary fitments or fire doors, is refused at once.
- An application with missing certificates should be avoided, even though the BA will process it as far as it can.
- Missing the 14-day window for written or photographic proof of minor repairs delays the OP.
- User plant that blocks the inspection, or building-services plant that must work on completion and is not ready, holds up the permit.
