# APP-92 Amendments to Code of Practice for Means of Escape 1996
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
First issued August 1997 | Buildings Department (re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Replaces paragraph 14(3)(b) of the Code of Practice for the Provision of Means of Escape in Case of Fire 1996 on dead-end travel. Other travel-distance limits stay in paragraph 14 of that Code.

## Regulatory Overview
Where travel from an exit door of a room to a staircase, or to a point of discharge to a street or to an open area at ground level complying with paragraph 8.2, is possible in one direction only, the dead-end limit in the substituted paragraph 14(3)(b) applies. The amendment was to be incorporated in the next reprint of the Code.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Dead-end distance (paragraph 1)
| Measurement | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| From any point in a room to an exit, or to a point from which travel in different directions to two or more exits is available | Not more than 18 m, being the sum of direct distance and travel distance |
| From that choice point to one of the exits | Not exceeding the direct-distance and travel-distance limitations in paragraph 14 |

**SD takeaway:** In a dead-end, keep every point in a room within 18 m, direct distance plus travel distance, of an exit or of a point where two directions of escape become available.
