# APP-44 Streets in relation to Site Area
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This update January 1990 | Buildings Department (first issued February 1986; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: Whether plot ratio can be moved from a park site to another site when new streets split a large development, under Building (Planning) Regulation 23(2)(a). There is no general right to transfer plot ratio.

## Regulatory Overview
New streets that enclose or isolate land split one site into several sites, and each new site is usually developed to its maximum, which fragments open space. The Buildings Ordinance has no explicit provision that permits a transfer of plot ratio potential from a park on an island site to another site.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When a higher plot ratio on another site may be considered (paragraphs 3–5)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Why a free transfer is not given | The Buildings Ordinance cannot stop the park later being built on, which would put excess building mass on the neighbourhood and overload the infrastructure |
| Extra controls that can support a request | Enforceable lease control drawings, Master Layout Plans, and conditional permissions under Town Planning Ordinance section 16 |
| What may be considered | On an application on Form 29, a higher plot ratio on another site within the large project, and consequential adjustments of other regulations, if this does not significantly impair specific planning requirements |
| Later change | A change to the control drawings, Master Layout Plans, or town planning permission that would negate the benefits of the Form 30 permission would be opposed |

**SD takeaway:** Do not assume plot ratio can move from a dedicated park onto another block unless lease drawings, a Master Layout Plan, or a section 16 permission can lock the park, and apply for that on Form 29.
