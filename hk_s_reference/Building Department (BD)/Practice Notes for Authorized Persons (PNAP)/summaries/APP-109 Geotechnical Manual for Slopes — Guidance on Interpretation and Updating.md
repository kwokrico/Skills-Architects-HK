# APP-109

**Official title:** Geotechnical Manual for Slopes — Guidance on Interpretation and Updating

**In plain words:** This note explains how to read the Geotechnical Manual for Slopes, 2nd edition, 1984, and replaces out-of-date words and sections. It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC), but it does govern slope and site-formation design, which can affect whether the site can be developed and how far the building must stand off a slope.

**Numbers that matter**
- “Risk” in the old manual is now “consequence”. Consequence-to-life categories are 1, 2, and 3, replacing high, low, and negligible. Economic consequence categories are A, B, and C. A combined label looks like 2A.
- Table 1, minimum factor of safety (FoS) for a new slope under 10-year-return rainfall: economic category A is 1.4 for life categories 1, 2, and 3. Category B is 1.4 for life category 1, and 1.2 for life categories 2 and 3. Category C is 1.4 for life category 1, 1.2 for life category 2, and greater than 1.0 for life category 3.
- Life category 1 also needs an FoS of at least 1.1 under the predicted worst groundwater.
- Table 2, for an existing slope or for remedial and preventive works, where investigation and history are rigorous, under 10-year rain: life category 1 needs FoS 1.2, category 2 needs 1.1, and category 3 needs greater than 1.0. Back-analysis may assume the existing FoS is 1.0 under the worst known loading and groundwater.
- Temporary works use the same FoS as permanent new works in Table 1, judged on the conditions while the temporary works exist. The category may be lower during construction.
- An exceptionally large platform that does not support structures may use 90% maximum dry density (MDD) in the interior, if fill at formation level is compacted to 95% for at least 1.5 m vertically and the slopes around the edge are compacted to 95% for at least 3 m.
- The standard treatment for existing loose fill is to recompact the surface fill to a depth of 3 m. That may be waived for life category 3, for a hazard judged too small, or where mature vegetation and a reasonable alternative solution exist.
- Consequence examples in Table 3: life category 1 includes occupied buildings, including a bus shelter with a cover, and dangerous-goods stores. Category 2 includes heavily used open space, a high-traffic road, an uncovered bus stop, and a petrol station. Category 3 includes a country park, a low-traffic road, and storage that is not dangerous goods.
- Many chapters of the Manual are superseded by Geoguides and other Geotechnical Engineering Office (GEO) publications, including chapters 1, 2, and 11, and chapter 7 in large part. See Appendix A section 10.
- Was PNAP 234. Re-issued August 2009. First issued June 1999.
- File reference: BD GR/GEO/11(III).

**What to submit**
- Apply Tables 1 to 4 and this interpretation in the slope assessment and design. Where the Manual has been superseded, follow the current Geoguide.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not use the old “high / low / negligible” risk labels, or chapters of the Manual that have been superseded.
- Do not use the lower Table 2 factors of safety unless the geology, groundwater, and performance history have been rigorously investigated.
- A bus shelter is an occupied building for life category 1. Do not class it as something else.
- The owner, advised by the designer, chooses the economic factor of safety. Record how cost was balanced against consequence.
