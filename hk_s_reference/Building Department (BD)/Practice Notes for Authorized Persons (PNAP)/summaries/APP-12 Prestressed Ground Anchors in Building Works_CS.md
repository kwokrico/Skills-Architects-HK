# APP-12 Prestressed Ground Anchors in Building Works
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
This revision January 1999 | Buildings Department (first issue June 1978; re-issued under the new categorization in August 2009)

> Scope note: When permanent prestressed ground anchors may be built into a permanent building. The Buildings Department treats anchors that need long-term monitoring as short-lived temporary building works, which the Building Authority may refuse to accept in a permanent building. Exceptional cases follow Appendix A and the Model Specification for Prestressed Ground Anchors (GEOSPEC 1).

## Regulatory Overview
Permanent prestressed ground anchors are accepted only in exceptional circumstances, and then only if provision and installation follow Appendix A. The Geotechnical Engineering Office prior-approval list does not remove the duties of the authorized person and the registered structural engineer, and a system outside that list may still be submitted in the usual way.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What the submission must show (Appendix A, paragraphs 3–7)
| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| With the approval submission | Plan of location, inclination, and length; Anchor Class as defined in GEOSPEC 1, design working load, and design free length, with geotechnical justification and calculations; which anchors have Suitability Tests and Extended Acceptance Tests; compressive movement to be allowed between the anchor pad and the fixed length during stressing; anchor pad details; long-term monitoring schedule, access, and the maintenance party's agreement to monitor and maintain |
| Also | Plans and sections of the anchored structure with site boundaries and geotechnical information, and the registered structural engineer's own calculations. Plans of adjacent buried and surface public utilities and structures, demonstrating that the anchors will not cause damage |
| Before consent | Name of the Approved Anchor System and Approved Anchor Types; specification of materials that may need replacement; schedule, procedures, and interpretation guidelines for future monitoring; name and experience of the installation contractor, who must be on the list of Nominated Anchor Contractors in the Prior Approval Documents. Section 17(1) conditions may be imposed. Consent may be refused if the information is unsatisfactory or conflicts with the Prior Approval System |
| On site | A certified true copy of the full Prior Approval Documents before the anchor works start |
| As-built | Full length and details of each anchor; system and type; materials that may need replacement; design load; total length, bond length, free length, inclination, strata, grout quantity and pressures; dates of installation, testing, and monitoring; stressing and test records including trial anchors; monitoring to date; future monitoring schedule and interpretation guidelines |

**SD takeaway:** Do not use permanent prestressed ground anchors unless the maintenance party has agreed in writing to the GEOSPEC 1 monitoring, and show inclination, free length, working load, and tests on the submission.

### 2. Anchors outside the site (Appendix A, paragraphs 8–11)
| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Monitoring life | Requirements are in GEOSPEC 1, including testing of existing grease in the anchor heads for chloride, nitrate, and sulphide, using Corrigenda 1/98 and 2/98 |
| Outside the boundary | Approval for anchors into a public or private street, government land, or adjacent private land is given only in exceptional circumstances, because they can obstruct later works and utilities and can inhibit development of the land |
| Government land | Apply to the Lands Department at an early design stage |
| Adjacent private land | The Form BA5 for the site formation works includes the lot number of that land, to indicate the adjoining owner's acquiescence |

**SD takeaway:** Keep anchors inside the site boundary unless Lands permission, or the adjoining lot number on Form BA5, is already in hand.
