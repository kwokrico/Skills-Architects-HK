# APP-16

**Official title:** Cladding

**In plain words:** This note, under Building (Construction) Regulations 3 and 28, sets strength, fire, durability, and testing rules for cladding fixed to a building. It does not itself change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC); the thickness limits used in that measurement are in APP-2 (not more than 90 mm overall, or not more than 75 mm on a non-structural prefabricated external wall).

**Numbers that matter**
- Cladding is a facing or architectural finish added to the structure (metal, stone, glass-reinforced concrete, and similar). It must perform in materials, fixings, strength, durability, non-combustibility, and access for maintenance. Provide flexible horizontal and vertical joints for differential movement. Anchors, dowels, and fixings are stainless or otherwise corrosion-resistant. Sand-cement bedding, epoxy, or both together, are not suitable as the only permanent fixing for stone.
- Non-combustibility follows Fire Safety Code Part E section 5. External cladding may use sections E10.1 and E12.1 as deem-to-satisfy. Internal cladding may use section E13.1.
- Structural plans are required when the cladding is more than 6 m above the adjoining ground or floor. Show thickness, strength, durability, type, fixings, and the support layout; framing; elevations with expansion joints; anchors and supports; loads and codes; and specifications for materials and workmanship (welding, galvanising, bimetallic contact), plus calculations (check of the parent structure, stability, element design, and deflection).
- Metal cladding may be folded into the superstructure plans (the location is still shown on the general building plans), with reference to ADV-33 Appendices B6 and B9.
- If a separate registered structural engineer (RSE) designs the cladding: appoint them on BA4 and BA5; they certify completion under Building (Administration) Regulation 25(3); they submit an assessment report and a statement that the project RSE is aware of the effect on the parent structure. The separate RSE supervises construction, including cast-in anchors, unless the project RSE has already installed those anchors under the superstructure plans.
- Anchor tests, where the cladding is more than 6 m up: a representative pull-out to at least 1.5× the manufacturer’s recommended tensile load, with no separation, plastic deformation, or other harmful effect.
- Stone tests: flexural strength to ASTM C880 or BS EN 12372, and anchorage to ASTM C1354. At least 5 random specimens of each. An independent testing agency. The authorized person or RSE endorses the results and submits them before the occupation permit. Limestone: after 50 thermal cycles and 50 wet-dry cycles (Appendix B), the aged residual flexural strength and anchorage strength are at least 80% of the standard values. Acceptance: characteristic flexural strength greater than 3× the design allowable; average flexural strength greater than the allowable times the flexural safety factor; characteristic anchorage strength greater than 4.2× the allowable; average anchorage strength greater than the allowable times the anchorage safety factor. Characteristic strength is the average minus K times the standard deviation, with K = 3.41 when there are at least 5 specimens. The anchorage safety factor equals the flexural safety factor times 1.4. Default examples: granite 4.5 and 6.3; limestone 7.2 and 10.08; marble 6.3 and 8.82.
- Some erection, repair, replacement, and removal of cladding can be minor works under APP-147 and Schedule 1 of the Building (Minor Works) Regulation.

**What to submit**
- Show cladding location and material on the general building plans. Submit the structural plans described above. If a separate RSE is used, submit BA4 and BA5. Submit test reports before the occupation permit. Use the minor-works path where it applies.

**Watch-outs**
- Stone fixed only by bedding or epoxy, or missing structural details where the cladding is more than 6 m up, delays or refuses approval and consent.
- Limestone without the aged test, or stone that fails the flexural or anchorage safety factors, is not accepted.
- Make clear which RSE is responsible for cast-in anchors: the project RSE or the cladding RSE.
