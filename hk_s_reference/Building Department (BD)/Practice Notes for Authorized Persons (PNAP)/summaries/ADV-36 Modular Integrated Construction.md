# ADV-36

**Official title:** Modular Integrated Construction

**In plain words:** Modular integrated construction (MiC) means freestanding volumetric modules, with finishes, fixtures, and fittings, made in a factory and assembled on site. This note streamlines design, factory quality control, and supervision under the Buildings Ordinance. Gross floor area (GFA) and site coverage (SC) concessions are not calculated in this note. They are in Joint Practice Note No. 8. A balcony or utility platform made as its own module does not count as MiC floor area for that concession. Do not invent a percentage bonus from this note alone. Read Joint Practice Note No. 8 and APP-161 for the figures. The APP-151 10% cap on other concessions is a separate track, documented through ADV-33.

**Numbers that matter**
- Fire performance of MiC elements that are not covered by the code needs equivalent performance, with or without fire engineering, under the Code of Practice for Fire Safety in Buildings. Joints and gaps, especially in drainage and the envelope, are prone to seepage. Design them against water ingress.
- The structural codes for concrete, precast concrete, and steel apply. Address stability under wind and other lateral loads, and temporary support during assembly. Tie modules to each other and to the in-situ work, horizontally and vertically, so the building is robust and does not collapse disproportionately. Temporary stages, fabrication, lifting, transport, and assembly, can produce higher stresses than the finished building. Allow for cumulative movement from creep, shrinkage, differential movement, and temperature, and for fabrication and installation tolerances. Design access and recesses early, so structural connections, drains, services, and joints can be inspected and repaired.
- A separate balcony module or utility-platform module should be avoided, because the in-situ joints are a long-term maintenance burden. If one is proposed, its area will not be accepted as MiC floor area under Joint Practice Note No. 8, issued jointly by the Buildings Department, the Lands Department, and the Planning Department, for the GFA and site coverage concessions.
- The factory must hold a valid Full Certificate under the Building Technology Research Institute Modular Integrated Construction Manufacturer Accreditation Scheme, or ISO 9001 or an equivalent. That is imposed as item 6 of a Buildings Ordinance section 17(1) condition on plan approval. A copy of the quality assurance scheme, or of the Full Certificate, is submitted at least 14 days before factory production starts, under Building (Administration) Regulation 10, unless an unchanged in-principle acceptance already covers it. The scheme covers quality-control tests, calibration, the production process, and how often independent parties inspect and audit. An independent party is a certification body accredited by HKCAS, or an inspection body accredited by HKIAS.
- Supervision, Table 1. Authorized-person stream: grade T3, weekly. Registered-structural-engineer stream: grade T3, weekly. Registered-contractor stream: grade T3, weekly, and grade T1 continuously. The MiC supervision plan is submitted at least 14 days before production. Log books are kept at the factory, with a copy in the site office.
- Quality audit. The authorized person, the registered structural engineer, and the authorized signatory of the registered contractor visit the factory in person for the first batch. After that, audits are at least monthly. Every quarter the audit is in person. In the months between, it may be in person or by videotelephony of at least 480p colour, recorded on a non-rewritable DVD-ROM. Each person endorses their own audit report. Reports go to the Buildings Department within 14 days.
- Alternative for the authorized person and the registered structural engineer, after that first factory visit: audit on site after delivery, instead of further factory audits. Notify the Building Authority in writing 1 month before production starts. Sample at least 1% of each module type. Appendix B annex checklist covers storey height, projections, prescribed windows and APP-116, barrier-free access, drainage, fire resisting construction including open-up of dry walls, and waterproofing. The registered structural engineer opens reinforcement at 3 locations of 100 mm by 300 mm, uses a cover meter on 6 locations of 450 mm by 450 mm, carries out non-destructive tests, and takes tensile tests at 1 specimen per 40 tonnes of the same cast and thickness. Results are within 60 days of delivery.
- In-principle acceptance, Appendix C. Determination in 45 days. Reply to comments within 30 days or the application is abandoned. A further determination is in 30 days. In-principle acceptance is not a prerequisite for general building plan approval. The checklist covers general building plans, structural plans, and drainage for the MiC portions, means of escape, fire resisting construction, barrier-free access, permanent and temporary structural connections, the quality assurance scheme, the installation sequence, and waterproofing of joints. Sample drawings are Annexes I to III.
- Engage the MiC supplier early. An unconventional prototype can go through the ADM-19 pre-submission enquiry, which is determined within 45 days. In-principle acceptance is optional, on the 45-day, 30-day, and 30-day cycle above. It is not a prerequisite for general building plan approval.
- Quality control supervision of fire-resisting construction, drainage, and structure also follows APP-158.

**What to submit**
- Plan-approval conditions will require the accredited manufacturer or ISO 9001, and qualified supervision, under section 17(1) item 6. Under Building (Administration) Regulation 10, submit the quality assurance scheme or certificate 14 days before production, the MiC supervision plan, and the audit reports.
- On the general building plans, if you claim the exemption, follow ADV-33 Appendix A2 item 27: a plan note and a diagram of module types and MiC floor area, citing APP-161.

**Watch-outs**
- Claiming the Joint Practice Note No. 8 concession on a separate balcony module or utility-platform module.
- Starting production without the quality assurance scheme or the accredited-manufacturer certificate on file.
- Missing the first-batch factory visit, the monthly audits, or the DVD record.
- Using the on-site alternative without 1 month’s prior notice to the Building Authority, or sampling below 1% of each module type.
- Alteration and addition after the occupation permit, without approval. The ADV-33 MiC note still applies.
- Seepage at joints, or a disproportionate collapse, from modules that are not adequately tied.
