# APP-49

**Official title:** Site Investigation and Ground Investigation

**In plain words:** Site investigation and ground investigation supporting a Buildings Ordinance submission follow the stated guides and the foundation code, and the field works are carried out by a registered specialist contractor in that category. This note does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage (SC); the results can still limit the bulk excavation limit and the foundation choice, and therefore the buildable bulk, especially in the Mid-levels under practice note APP-30.

**Numbers that matter**
- Site investigation, under Building (Construction) Regulations (B(C)R) section 2, means the physical characteristics of the site, including documentary studies, surveys, and ground investigation. Ground investigation means boreholes, trial pits, and tests carried out on site and in the laboratory.
- Follow the list in Geotechnical Engineering Office Technical Guidance Note No. 1, which points to Geoguide 2, Geoguide 3, and related documents, and follow the Code of Practice for Foundations 2017 (2024 Edition).
- Ground investigation field works, meaning all ground-investigation operations on site except laboratory testing and field density tests, are a category of specialised works. They must be carried out by a registered specialist contractor (ground investigation field works) (RSC(GIFW)). Registration and scope are in practice note APP-96. Laboratory tests and field density tests are carried out by a laboratory accredited for the particular test, under practice note APP-64.
- The RSC(GIFW) is responsible for the ground-investigation reports supporting foundation plans, site formation plans, and excavation and lateral support (ELS) plans, under Building (Administration) Regulations (B(A)R) regulations 8(1) and 9, and for a geotechnical assessment lodged with the general building plans under B(A)R regulation 8(1)(ba). That assessment includes a plan showing the scope and extent of the ground investigation. The specialist contractor remains responsible for correctness even where another party carries out a geophysical survey. Cores and samples are kept, except those that have been tested, until acknowledgement of the related site formation, foundation, or works.
- Quality supervision follows the Technical Memorandum for Supervision Plans 2009 and the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009 (2024 Edition).
- Digital ground-investigation reports and laboratory-test reports are required, as PDF plus the AGS data format in ASCII text, not split across files, including the local additions of the Geotechnical Engineering Office (GEO). Each ground-investigation station is a separate PDF, with photographs, and with a unique file name, for example SSB-BH1.

**What to submit**
- Engage an RSC(GIFW).
- Submit the ground-investigation and laboratory-test package as PDF and as AGS data.
- Use the investigation for the foundation, site formation, excavation and lateral support, and geotechnical assessment submissions.
- Retain the cores for inspection by the Buildings Department and the Geotechnical Engineering Office until the related works are acknowledged.

**Watch-outs**
- Ground investigation field works carried out by a contractor who is not an RSC(GIFW).
- A paper-only ground-investigation submission, with no AGS data.
- A laboratory that is not accredited for the test it reports.
- Discarding cores before the related works have been acknowledged.
- A geotechnical assessment with no plan of the ground-investigation scope.
