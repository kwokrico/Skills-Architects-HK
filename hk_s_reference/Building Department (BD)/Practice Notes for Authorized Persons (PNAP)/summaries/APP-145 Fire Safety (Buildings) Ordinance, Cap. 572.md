# APP-145

**Official title:** Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance, Cap. 572

**In plain words:** This note explains upgrading of older composite and domestic buildings under the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572). It does not change gross floor area (GFA) or site coverage, and works done only to comply with a fire safety direction pay no fee under Building (Administration) Regulation 42.

**Numbers that matter**
- Formerly PNAP 302. The Ordinance came into force on 1 July 2007. PNAP revision October 2010 (Advisory Committee). The Buildings Department enforces fire safety construction. The Fire Services Department enforces fire service installations and equipment.
- Target buildings: composite buildings, or domestic buildings (more than 3 storeys if the building is wholly domestic), whose plans were first submitted on or before 1 March 1987, or which were built on or before that date without plans. Cap. 121 New Territories buildings are excluded.
- For the “domestic purposes” definition, domestic use excludes a hotel, guesthouse, elderly home, child care centre, or nursery. A composite building’s non-domestic part excludes a building that is wholly a factory, industrial building, godown, warehouse, or bulk storage.
- Fire safety construction is in Schedule 1 (non-domestic parts of a composite building) and Schedule 2 (domestic buildings and domestic parts). Codes used for this upgrading: Means of Escape 1996, Fire Resisting Construction 1996, and Means of Access 1995.
- Fire service installation examples: sprinklers; automatic cut-off of mechanical ventilation; emergency lighting; manual alarms; fire hydrant and hose reel; Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations 1994.
- Fire safety directions under sections 5(1) and 5(2). Fire safety compliance orders after conviction under section 6. The Advisory Committee on Fire Safety Orders may advise on alternatives under section 5(10).
- No Building (Administration) Regulation 42 fee if the improvement works are entirely for compliance with a fire safety direction.
- Voluntary works, or works under a fire safety direction, follow the usual Buildings Ordinance approval. Submit three sets of fire service installation plans to the Fire Services Department, instead of the usual two.

**What to submit**
- Buildings Ordinance approval for alteration and addition, whether voluntary or under a fire safety direction.
- Three sets of fire service installation plans for the Fire Services Department.
- Where structural integrity or technology makes a Schedule item unreasonable, use the Advisory Committee path, including fire-engineering alternatives aimed at the primary objectives.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not apply Cap. 572 codes to Cap. 121 village houses.
- Do not ignore the Advisory Committee path where structural or technical limits make Schedule items unreasonable.
- Do not send too few fire service installation plan sets to the Fire Services Department.
