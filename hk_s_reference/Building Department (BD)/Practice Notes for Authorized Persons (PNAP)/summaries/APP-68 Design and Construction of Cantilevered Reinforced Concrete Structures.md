# APP-68

**Official title:** Design and Construction of Cantilevered Reinforced Concrete Structures

**In plain words:** Exposed cantilevered reinforced-concrete slabs and beams, such as canopies and balconies, need minimum thickness, reinforcement, falls and waterproofing, and the longer exposed slabs also need a construction report. This note is not itself a gross floor area concession and does not change site coverage; projections over a street still follow PNAP APP-19 and Building (Planning) Regulations regulations 7 and 10.

**Numbers that matter**
- A submission that follows this note is taken to meet Building (Construction) Regulations regulation 4. Drainage and the plan items in paragraph 4 of Appendix A are tied to Building (Planning) Regulations regulations 4(b) and 10(3).
- A cantilever under a permanent solid cover, not more than one storey high and at least as large in plan as the cantilever, may be treated as not exposed.
- Prefer a beam-and-slab cantilever where the clear span is more than 1000 mm, rather than a pure slab cantilever. Set the span-to-effective-depth ratio to clause 7.3.4.2 of the Code of Practice for the Structural Use of Concrete 2004 (2nd edition).
- Cast the cantilever monolithically with its supports. Do not put a construction joint along the external edge of a support unless a previously approved alternative is of equal strength and is watertight. Supervise to Table 9.1 of the Code of Practice for Site Supervision 2009.
- Exposed work: provide effective waterproofing. Protect the membrane with 1:3 cement-sand mortar laid to a fall of at least 1 in 75. Provide a drip or groove at the bottom edge. Every canopy needs surface drainage into the building surface-water system. Where the span cannot be reached for cleaning, space drainage outlets not more than 5 m apart. Avoid embedding pipes. If a pipe must be embedded, follow PNAP APP-105.
- Cantilever beams: overall depth at the support at least 300 mm, high-yield stirrups, and exposure condition 2 or worse if the beam is external.
- Cantilever slab thickness: span 500 mm or less, at least 110 mm; span over 500 mm up to 750 mm, at least 125 mm; span over 750 mm, at least 150 mm. High-yield steel in both faces and both directions. Main bars at least 10 mm in diameter, spaced not more than 150 mm apart, with steel area at least 0.25 percent of the gross concrete area. Main bars that drop into the support are not more than 16 mm in diameter. Limit the crack width to 0.1 mm at the serviceability limit state, or limit the working stress in deformed high-yield bars to 100 N/mm². Cover for exposure condition 2 or worse.
- An external cantilever slab with a span over 750 mm that is exposed: waterproof concrete with cube strength at least 35 MPa; main bars hot-dip galvanized to BS EN ISO 1461; waterproof membrane protected by 1:3 cement-sand mortar with a maximum water/cement ratio of 0.65, or an equivalent.

**What to submit**
- Show on the general building plans how water is stopped from accumulating. Show surface drainage on the drainage plans.
- The Building Authority may set supervision, workmanship and construction-report requirements as conditions of approval and consent.
- For an exposed cantilever slab with a span over 750 mm, submit Form BA14 and a construction report. The report covers as-built dimensions, falls, outlets, screed, waterproofing, construction joints, concrete grades, bar diameter and spacing, the date of casting, and covers measured with a covermeter. Include photos of the bars before concreting, and photos after concreting and before finishes.
- On completion, the authorized person should arrange inspection documents for the incorporated owners or the management.

**Watch-outs**
- Do not use a pure slab cantilever on a long span, and do not put a construction joint at the face of the support without approval.
- Missing falls, drips or canopy drainage leads to ponding and to the structure breaking down.
- An exposed span over 750 mm without galvanizing, waterproof concrete, a construction report and Form BA14 does not follow this note.
- Clogged drains and air-conditioner loads are a long-term maintenance risk. Assess finishes, parapet and waterproofing dead load, and maintenance and ponding imposed load, accurately. Detail the cantilever so it can be demolished or replaced without harming the main structure, especially over a street.
