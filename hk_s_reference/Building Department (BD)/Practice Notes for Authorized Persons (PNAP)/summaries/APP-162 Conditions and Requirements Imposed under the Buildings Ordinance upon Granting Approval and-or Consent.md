# APP-162

**Official title:** Conditions and Requirements Imposed under the Buildings Ordinance upon Granting Approval and/or Consent

**In plain words:** The Building Authority may impose case-specific conditions under Buildings Ordinance section 17 on quality supervision, qualified supervision, quality assurance and quality control, material testing, instrumentation, and records, to be met before consent, during the works, or when completion is certified (see also APP-158; a similar practice note went to registered contractors). This note does not change gross floor area or site coverage.

**Numbers that matter**
- First issue September 2020. This revision March 2025. Typical conditions are listed in Appendix A. Common conditions for particular materials and works are also on the Buildings Department website under “Codes and references”.
- From 1 July 2025, where the estimated total cost of the building works is over $30 million, section 17(1) item 6(e) will require both a Mobile Plant Alert System and a Tower Crane Alert System (together called 4S). The conditions are imposed on the first approval, or a major revision, and/or the first consent, for demolition, site formation, excavation and lateral support (ELS), foundation, pile cap, and superstructure that uses mobile plant and/or tower cranes. The same applies to alterations and additions (A&A) with structural works above that cost.
- For superstructure, circular letter 28 March 2024 already required the alert systems from 1 July 2024, on the first superstructure approval or major revision and on the consent that follows.
- Qualified supervision, typical arrangement: the registered structural engineer (RSE) assigns a quality control supervisor (QCS) for inspection at least weekly, minimum grade T3 on the RSE stream. The registered general building contractor (RGBC) or registered specialist contractor (RSC) assigns a quality control coordinator (QCC), full-time on site, minimum grade T1 on the contractor stream. A log book stays on site. For Type 2 mechanical couplers, the QCC grade rises from T1 to T3.
- Connecting Type 2 couplers needs a full-time registered contractor technically competent person (TCP) while that task is under way. Bar fixing may be inspected at hold points if it can still be checked.
- Materials follow Building (Construction) Regulations section 3: nature and quality, mixing, installation, recognised tests, and the manufacturer’s recommendations. If material is used before the test results, full traceability is required.
- The authorized person (AP) declares the cost under Building (Administration) Regulations regulation 10. A missing declaration can lead to refusal under Buildings Ordinance section 16(3)(b).

**What to submit**
- At the first consent application, whichever type of works comes first, the AP declares whether the estimated total cost of the building works exceeds $30 million. Count demolition, site formation, ELS, foundation, pile cap, superstructure, and A&A. Later consents need no further declaration. If the declared cost is over $30 million, the Building Authority imposes the 4S conditions on the first consent for every works type in paragraph 4.
- Typical regulation 10 documents: quality supervision plan; manufacturer’s quality assurance scheme; pre-construction surveys and monitoring; material certificates; strength and performance tests; RSE and authorized signatory (AS) audit reports; RSE confirmation statement; record plans and reports; and the $30 million cost declaration.
- Keep the approval and consent letters on site. Brief the supervision team on the conditions imposed.
- For a named material or method, quote the Buildings Department’s common conditions on its website. A method statement already accepted elsewhere, such as APP-169, can be used that way.
- Monitoring conditions can cover vibration, tilting, and movement of adjacent buildings, land, streets, and services, and groundwater where that is appropriate.

**Watch-outs**
- Missing the $30 million declaration at the first consent can lead to refusal of consent.
- Understating the estimated cost to avoid the 4S conditions is an AP liability.
- Using materials before the test results, with no traceability.
- Ignoring the QCS and QCC log books.
- Treating “continuous supervision” of critical coupler work as occasional visits.
