# Waterworks Regulations (Cap. 102A)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Consolidation: contents last updated date 15.1.2026 | Cap. 102 sub. leg. A | Water Authority means the Director of Water Supplies (Cap. 102, section 2)

> Scope note: These regulations govern permission to construct, install, alter, or remove a fire service or inside service, inspection before pipes are covered, restrictions on gardens, hosepipes, fresh-water flushing, salt-water flushing, and air-conditioning, and the Part 3 and Schedule 2 rules for pipes, valves, cold-water cisterns, hot-water apparatus, flushing fittings, baths, basins, and sinks, together with meter installation and siting. They are not the Ordinance’s definition of a fire service, inside service, or connexion, and this summary omits fishing, charge concessions, and plumber-licence administration. Schedule 2 pages in the extract print last updated date 8.12.2017.

## Regulatory Overview

Where the Ordinance requires permission, an application for a fire service or an inside service must include the plans, specifications, and other information the Water Authority requires, and no pipe or fitting forming part of that service may be used or covered up until the Water Authority has inspected and approved it (regulations 3, 5, and 6). Schedule 2 then fixes pipe materials and minimum diameters, cistern construction and overflows, flushing volumes, and the level of discharge into a bath, basin, or sink, for pipes and fittings installed or intended to be installed in a fire service or inside service (regulation 19 and Schedule 2).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Permission, inspection, and the licensed plumber (regulations 3, 5, and 6)

| Size and position | 5(4) | The Water Authority decides the size and position of the connection to the main |

| Cover-up | 6(3) | No pipe or fitting forming part of a fire service or inside service shall be used or covered up until inspected and approved by the Water Authority |

**SD takeaway:** Submit plumbing proposals, and take the connection size and position from the Water Authority, before treating the boundary connection as fixed; keep every new pipe and fitting uncovered until inspection and approval (regulations 3(4), 5(4), and 6(3)).

### 2. Use of the supply (regulations 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 15)

| Use | Regulation | Rule |
|---|---|---|

| Garden, lawn, playground, garage, car park | 10 | Except with written permission, no draw-off point and no drawing of fresh water for a garden, lawn, playground of any kind, garage or car park, or other place where the supply is not used for a domestic purpose or another purpose the Water Authority has approved; the same permission is required to extend or alter an inside service for those purposes |
| Hosepipe | 11(1) | No person shall draw fresh water from an inside service by a hosepipe or similar apparatus |
| Hosepipe exceptions | 11(2) | Regulation 11(1) does not apply where fresh water is drawn from a cold water cistern approved by the Water Authority for that purpose, or for use in a domestic appliance or apparatus approved by the Water Authority |
| Fresh water flushing | 12(1) | If fresh water from the waterworks is used, without written permission, to flush water-closets, latrines, or urinals, the occupier and the owner of the premises commit an offence |
| Air-conditioning and pools | 13 | Except with written permission, no person shall use water from the waterworks for any heating, cooling, or humidification plant, or any swimming pool |
| Salt water flushing | 15(1) | The Water Authority may require salt water for flushing water-closets, latrines, and urinals, and may supply salt water for that purpose |

| Existing fittings | 15(3) | Where salt water is required for an existing water-closet, latrine, or urinal that used other water, the owner replaces pipes and fittings if necessary so that they comply with regulation 15(2) |

Schedule 2, Part 2, paragraph 11 is the fitting-level rule that matches regulation 11: except with the written permission of the Water Authority, a fitting with a threaded outlet, or any device facilitating connection of a rubber hose or another flexible hose, must not be used.

**SD takeaway:** Fresh water for a garden, lawn, playground, garage, car park, swimming pool, heating, cooling, or humidification plant, or for flushing a water-closet, latrine, or urinal, and any water other than water from the waterworks in an inside service, each require the written permission of the Water Authority (regulations 9, 10, 12, and 13).

### 3. Pipes, fittings, protection, booster pumps, and relaxation (regulations 19–25 and Schedule 2, Parts 1 and 2)

Regulation 19(1): subject to the savings in regulations 19(2) to (6) and 19A, Part 3 and Schedule 2 apply to any pipe or fitting installed or intended to be installed in any fire service or inside service. Regulations 19(2) to (6) and 19A provide that pipes and fittings already installed are not required to be altered or renewed merely because a later amendment introduced a new requirement, unless the Water Authority considers the pipe or fitting so defective, or in such condition, as to cause or be likely to cause waste, undue consumption, or pollution. For a water heater, regulation 19(6) is limited to the Schedule 2 amendments made by the Waterworks (Amendment) Regulation 1999 (L.N. 106 of 1999).

### 4. Cold water cisterns (Schedule 2, Part 3)

| Paragraph | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | No cistern for storage of cold water shall be installed or used except with written permission. The Water Authority specifies the maximum permitted capacity |
| 2 | Watertight, of adequate strength, properly supported, and made of concrete, stainless steel, or fibre glass |
| 4 | Located to minimize the risk of contamination of stored water, with a suitable close-fitting lockable cover that is not airtight, positioned to facilitate inspection and cleaning |
| 5 | If a non-potable cistern adjoins a potable cistern, a physical break is required so that the walls and slabs are separated. Tie beams linking the cisterns for structural requirements may be fitted only if constructed so that cross contamination via the tie beams is not possible |
| 6 | Inlet of a single cistern fed by gravity: a ball float valve and a stop valve |
| 7 | Inlet of a single cistern fed by a pumped supply: an automatic control switch, and no stop valve |
| 7A | Each inlet of a twin cistern fed by a pumped supply: an automatic control switch and a stop valve for temporary isolation |
| 7B | A ball float valve or automatic control switch must shut off the supply when the water level is 25 mm below the invert of the overflow pipe or warning pipe. The invert of the inlet pipe, or the outlet of the ball float valve, must be not less than 25 mm above the top of the overflow pipe |
| 7C | All overflow pipes and warning pipes of a potable water cistern must be of a corrosion-resisting material |
| 7D | An overflow pipe one commercial size larger than the inlet pipe, and in no case less than 25 mm nominal diameter, fitted and extended to terminate in a conspicuous position. It must not be connected to a drain or sewer, or to the overflow pipe from another cistern |
| 7E | A stop valve at the outlet. A drain-off pipe so the cistern can be emptied |
| 8 | Without written permission, a cistern for fresh water from the waterworks shall not be connected so that it can store any water other than water supplied from the waterworks |
| 9 | Installed so that it is easily accessible for cleaning or repair. Where headroom is limited and clearance from the ceiling or underside of the roof is limited, a quickly detachable fitting must allow the cistern to be removed for cleansing and repair |
| 10 | Safe access by a secure permanent ladder or a readily available portable ladder |

**SD takeaway:** Obtain written permission and a Water Authority maximum capacity before placing a cold-water cistern; if potable and non-potable cisterns adjoin, separate their walls and slabs, and discharge the overflow to a conspicuous position with no connection to a drain, a sewer, or another cistern’s overflow (paragraphs 1, 5, and 7D).

### 5. Hot water inside services (Schedule 2, Part 4)

Paragraph 1A limits this Part to a water heater used for heating water not intended for human consumption, and to a pipe or fitting that may come into contact with water heated by that heater. Hot fresh water inside-service pipe materials remain those in Part 1, paragraphs 3 and 8.

| Paragraph | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | Subject to paragraph 2, a water heater must be supplied from a cold water cistern |
| 2 | With written permission, a water heater may be connected direct to a main if it has been tested satisfactorily at the factory to a pressure of at least 1.5 times its maximum static working pressure, and it is a non-pressure type in which no restriction of flow can be effected beyond the inlet control valve, a cistern type, an instantaneous water heater, or an unvented thermal storage type electric water heater that complies with the safety requirements under the Electrical Products (Safety) Regulation (Cap. 406 sub. leg. G) |
| 3 | If connected direct to a main, every draw-off point must be not less than 15 mm above the lowest part of the top edge of the receptacle supplied, and a gas water heater must be constructed so that no leakage of gas into water can occur |
| 4 | Where mixing valves, water blenders, or another combination of fittings are used, the cold water to those fittings must be drawn from the same source that supplies the water heater |
| 5 | A thermal storage type water heater, other than the unvented electric type in paragraph 2(b)(iv), must have an individual expansion pipe at its highest point, rising continuously without obstruction until it discharges to atmosphere above the cistern at a height sufficient to prevent a constant outflow of hot water via that pipe |
| 6 | Taps or other fittings for drawing off water, other than a screwed plug with a removable key for emptying the system, must not be connected to any part of the hot water system below the top of the hot water cylinder in such a way that the water level in the cylinder can be lowered |
| 7 | A tap for hot water must not be fixed further, measured along the axis of the supply pipe, from a hot water fitting or from a flow and return system than the distance in the table below |
| 8 | A loose jumper type valve with a loose valve plate that stops backflow, or a similar device, at the inlet if the heater has no non-return valve. This does not apply to unvented thermal storage type electric water heaters |
| 9 | Pipes conveying hot water must be steel internally lined with chlorinated polyvinyl chloride, copper, or a corrosion-resisting material |
| 10 | A hot water cylinder or tank of not less than 100 L: if mild steel, the requirements for cylinders or tanks in BS 417-2:1987; if copper, the relevant requirements in BS 1566-1:2002+A1:2011 and BS 1566-2:1984 |
| 11 | An unvented thermal storage type electric water heater system must have a pipe branching from the supply pipe above the top of the heater, or another device, to prevent drain-down if the source supply fails; an anti-vacuum valve complying with the relevant requirements in BS EN 13959:2004 and BS EN 14451:2005, or another device, to prevent heated water being syphoned back; and a vessel to accommodate expansion of heated water where a non-return valve or similar device at the inlet constrains that expansion |

| Largest nominal diameter of pipe | Distance in metres |
|---|---|

**SD takeaway:** For a heater within paragraph 1A, supply it from a cold-water cistern unless written permission and the paragraph 2 tests allow a direct main connection, and keep the hot draw-off within the paragraph 7 pipe-length limits.

### 6. Flushing apparatus, baths, basins, and sinks (Schedule 2, Parts 5 and 6)

| Paragraph | Flushing rule |
|---|---|

| Paragraph | Bath, basin, and sink rule |
|---|---|
| Part 6, para. 1 | Every inlet to a bath, lavatory basin, or sink shall be distinct from, and unconnected with, any outlet. Every outlet for emptying shall have a well-fitting and easily accessible watertight plug or some other equally suitable apparatus |
