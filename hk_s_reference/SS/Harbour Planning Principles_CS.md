# Harbour Planning Principles
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Revised version endorsed by the Harbour-front Enhancement Committee on 27 April 2006

> Scope note: Eight principles for Victoria Harbour and its harbour-front. They are guidelines for planning, preservation, development and management. They are not an outline zoning plan and they do not print a promenade width or a building height.

## Regulatory Overview
The vision is to make Victoria Harbour and its harbour-front areas an attractive, vibrant, accessible and sustainable world-class asset: a harbour for the people, a harbour of life. The mission is to realise that vision through balanced use of land and marine resources, with open public engagement.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. The eight principles
| Principle | What the text requires |
|---|---|
| 1 Preserving Victoria Harbour | Protect the harbour for people and visitors as a public asset, a natural and cultural heritage asset, and a source of economic and social value |
| 2 Stakeholder engagement | Engage all sectors early and continuously |
| 3 Sustainable development | Balance economic, social and environmental needs of the present generation without compromising future generations |
| 4 Integrated planning | Plan infrastructure, land and marine uses, and water quality together |
| 5 Proactive harbour enhancement | Treat the harbour-front as a symbol of urban design excellence |
| 6 Vibrant harbour | Balance the maritime and logistics role with cultural and leisure use |
| 7 Accessible harbour | Give pedestrians ample unrestricted and convenient visual and physical access, preferably at grade, to and along the harbour |
| 8 Public enjoyment | Maximise public enjoyment. Minimise land and impact from infrastructure, utility installations and uses incompatible with these principles |

**SD takeaway:** On a harbour-front site, test pedestrian access at grade and public enjoyment before adding a frontage that the principles treat as incompatible.
