# Transport Planning and Design Manual, Volumes 2 and 6
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Public PDFs dated August 2026 | Transport Department

> Scope note: Only the schematic rules that Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines Chapter 8 refers onward and does not itself print: footway crossfall, at-grade crossing type and width, and junction type. Carriageway widths, the three-zone footpath, parking ratios, signs, signals, accidents, surveillance and tunnels are not repeated here. The manual is written first for Transport Department staff. Its criteria are guidelines unless a clause says otherwise.

## Regulatory Overview
Volume 2 is highway design. Volume 6 Chapter 10 is the pedestrian planning framework. Chapter 8 of the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines already adopts the three-zone footpath and the carriageway widths. Use this note only for the geometry that chapter leaves to the manual.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Footway crossfall (Volume 6, clause 10.5.2.2)
The Through Zone should have a crossfall between 2% and 3.3%, for drainage without a walking hazard.

**SD takeaway:** Set the Through Zone fall inside 2% to 3.3%. Do not use a steeper fall to shed water across the walking line.

### 2. At-grade crossings (Volume 2, section 3.7)
| Rule | Figure or test |
|---|---|
| Where | Not generally on a trunk road or a primary distributor, where pedestrians should be segregated. A primary distributor that also serves as a district distributor may use a signal crossing and no other at-grade form |
| Width | Generally not less than 2.5 m and not greater than 9 m. The normal range is 4 m to 6 m. A width of 2.5 m may suit an uncontrolled cautionary crossing |
| Kerb | Dropped kerbs for the full width of the crossing, with the ramps outside that width. Do not continue a dropped kerb around a corner radius |
| Grade separation | Complete segregation by footbridge or subway is described as the safest form, and land may be reserved for it. A grade-separated crossing beside an at-grade crossing is likely to be under-used and should be avoided without an exceptional justification |
| Heavy flow | At or above 1,200 pedestrians per metre width per hour, an at-grade crossing is generally difficult and an alternative should be considered |

**SD takeaway:** Size a normal at-grade crossing at 4 m to 6 m, and do not place one on a trunk road or a primary distributor unless the manual's district-distributor exception applies.

### 3. Junction type (Volume 2, Chapter 4 contents)
The manual provides priority junctions, traffic signals, roundabouts and grade-separated junctions. Urban roads with at-grade junctions should preferably not be superelevated above 4% to 5% (clause 3.3.3.4). Junction spacing for the road hierarchy remains the table in Chapter 8 of the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines.

**SD takeaway:** Name the junction type. Do not take a spacing figure from this note.
