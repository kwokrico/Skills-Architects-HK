# Public Open Space in Private Developments Design and Management Guidelines
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Development Bureau. The Bureau's notice states that the guidelines took effect on 14 February 2011.

> Scope note: Design and management of public open space in a private development, on private land or on adjoining government land, where the public is entitled to enter. The design guidelines are a framework. The management guidelines are advisory and do not override the lease, a deed of dedication, or a statute.

## Regulatory Overview
Design is organised by connectivity, appropriateness and quality. The guidelines sort the space into five types: public green, plaza, courtyard, pocket space and promenade. Design guidelines apply to future cases, with flexibility for the merits of the case, and existing cases are advised to follow them on a reasonable basis.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Public access
| Rule | What the text prints |
|---|---|
| Hours | Keep the space open for the hours in the lease or deed of dedication, or as the Government otherwise agrees |
| Key route | A public open space that is a key passage in the local pedestrian system, or a waterfront promenade, should be open 24 hours unless the lease or deed grants an exemption for security or night-time public safety |
| Minimum | Subject to the lease, public access should not be less than 13 hours |
| Notice | Write the opening hours and the boundary on a plate at each main entrance |

**SD takeaway:** Read the lease hours first. A promenade or a key pedestrian link is a 24-hour space unless the lease says otherwise.

### 2. Commercial use of a passageway
A commercial use must not block a major access, entrance or walkway. The guidelines state a 10% ceiling for commercial activities and a 10% minimum area without bonus gross floor area. Lands Department considers a waiver in its landlord capacity. This is not a licence to put shops across the public route.

**SD takeaway:** Keep the public route clear, and treat 10% as the commercial ceiling printed for that passageway case, not as a site-coverage rule for the whole development.
