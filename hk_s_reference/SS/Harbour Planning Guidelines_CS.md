# Harbour Planning Guidelines
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Draft paper for the Harbour-front Enhancement Committee agenda of 14 June 2007. The Urban Design Study for the New Central Harbourfront records that the guidelines were prepared in June 2007.

> Scope note: A checklist that elaborates the Harbour Planning Principles. The archived file is the draft annex, not a later consolidated edition. It does not print a promenade width, a setback depth, or a building height in metres.

## Regulatory Overview
The guidelines group the work under nine aspects: public engagement, land use planning, urban design, landscaping, physical linkage, land formation, harbour-front management, sustainable development, and temporary land uses. A comprehensive public engagement programme is described for a large-scale public harbour-front project of 20 hectares or above, for government-initiated reclamation, and for a project of territorial significance.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Promenade, setback and access
| Point | What the draft prints |
|---|---|
| Promenade | A continuous promenade of adequate width along the harbour-front, as far as practicable, for strolling, jogging, circulation, furniture, trees and utilities. "Adequate" is not given a width. A cycle track is encouraged where the width and the local circumstances allow |
| Setback | New development, redevelopment, or a temporary use along the harbour-front should where possible be set back for that promenade. Where a marine operation cannot set back, provide a convenient passageway around the site |
| Access | Continuous pedestrian access at grade is encouraged. Where that is impossible, provide an alternative passageway. Prefer an at-grade crossing when traffic permits. Elevated links are the alternative, not the first choice |
| Hours | Waterfront promenades, sitting-out areas and open spaces should be open free of charge by day and at night and, if 24 hours is not practicable, should close as late as possible |

**SD takeaway:** Draw the promenade and the at-grade link before fixing the building line. Do not invent the promenade width from this draft.

### 2. Building form on the harbour-front
| Point | What the draft prints |
|---|---|
| Height | A gradation of height, descending towards the harbour, so the building does not dominate the harbour and permeability to the water is kept |
| Mass | Diversity of mass and varying heights along the harbour-front |
| Frontage | Active street frontage, such as shops, bars and cafes, integrated with the promenade. Avoid mechanical plant rooms and blank walls on these frontages |
| Views | Preserve views between ridgelines or peaks and major vantage points along the harbour as far as possible |

**SD takeaway:** Step the height down toward the water and keep plant off the promenade frontage. Take any metre from the outline zoning plan, the lease, or PNAP APP-152, not from this draft.
