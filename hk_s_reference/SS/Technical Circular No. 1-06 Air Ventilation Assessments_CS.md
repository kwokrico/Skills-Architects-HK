# Technical Circular No. 1/06 Air Ventilation Assessments
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
19 July 2006 | Joint circular of the Housing, Planning and Lands Bureau and the Environment, Transport and Works Bureau

> Scope note: How major government projects should compare external air ventilation. It is not a pass/fail wind code, and it does not print a Lawson comfort criterion, a podium height, or a gap as a percentage of site frontage. Building separation, setback and greenery percentages are in PNAP APP-152.

## Regulatory Overview
The circular asks government proponents to consider air ventilation when a major project may affect the macro wind environment. Quasi-government organisations and the private sector are encouraged to apply it voluntarily. The stated purpose is to compare design options and find problem areas. The circular says the framework does not provide an absolute benchmark that makes an impact acceptable or unacceptable.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When a government proponent should test the need
| Category | Trigger printed in paragraph 7 |
|---|---|
| (a) | Planning study for a new development area |
| (b) | Comprehensive land-use restructuring, including closing and building over existing streets |
| (c) | Area-wide plot ratio and height control review |
| (d) | Site over 2 hectares with an overall plot ratio of 5 or above |
| (e) | Total gross floor area exceeding 100,000 square metres |
| (f) | Podium coverage extending over one hectare |
| (g) | Development above a public transport terminus |
| (h) | Building height exceeding 15 metres within a public open space or a breezeway designated on a layout plan, an outline development plan, an outline zoning plan, or proposed by a planning study |
| (i) | Waterfront site with a lot frontage exceeding 100 metres |
| (j) | Elevated structure at least 3.5 metres wide that abuts or partly covers a pedestrian corridor along a street block developed or developable at plot ratio 5 or above on both sides, or that covers 30% of a public open space |

**SD takeaway:** Use this list to decide whether an air ventilation study is in scope. A private project is encouraged, not compelled, by this circular. A planning condition can still require the study.

### 2. What is compared
Report the wind velocity ratio at each test point. A higher velocity ratio means the proposal is less likely to have reduced the wind. The site spatial average velocity ratio summarises the immediate vicinity. The local spatial average velocity ratio summarises the local area and takes precedence over the site ratio. For an exposed site, also consider wind gust. No comfort scale and no minimum ratio are printed.

**SD takeaway:** Compare options by velocity ratio. Do not mark a scheme acceptable because it meets a comfort criterion or a frontage-gap percentage that this circular does not print.
