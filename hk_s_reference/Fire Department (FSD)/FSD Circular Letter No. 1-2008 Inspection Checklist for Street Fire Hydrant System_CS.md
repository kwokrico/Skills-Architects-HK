# FSD Circular Letter No. 1-2008 Inspection Checklist for Street Fire Hydrant System
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
23 January 2008 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: This circular adopts the compliance-inspection checklist for pedestal street fire hydrant systems. Design requirements sit in Section 5.25 of the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment, and test acceptance criteria in Section 2.25 of the inspection code. It is not a substitute for those sections.

## Regulatory Overview
The checklist is for formal compliance inspection of street fire hydrant systems and is for use with immediate effect from this circular. Supply may be direct from the town main, from a gravity tank, from pumps and a tank, or from sea-water pumps, and the installed layout must tally with the approved building plans.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Hydrant siting, colour and valves (checklist 4.1; Annex I)

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Fresh water | Hydrant body painted red |
| Sea water | Hydrant body painted yellow, with a white band when fed directly from a government trunk main |
| Out of service | Cap of the 100 mm outlet painted blue |
| Valves | A control valve on each hydrant. An isolating valve where the system is fed directly or indirectly from a government main |
| Underground valve spindle | 250 mm to 500 mm below the valve-pit cover |
| Control-valve pit cover | Not greater than 300 mm × 300 mm, with "FH" engraved. Isolating-valve pit covers follow the WSD standard |
| Control-valve pit | Between 1.5 m and 3 m from the street hydrant, and outside the designated emergency vehicular access |
| Arrow on hydrant top | V-shaped arrow head 100 mm high and 50 mm wide, pointing to the control valve. Yellow arrow on a red hydrant; red arrow on a yellow hydrant |
| Hydrant number | Not less than 75 mm, on the body facing the roadway. Yellow on red; red on yellow |
| Clearance | No obstruction within 1.5 m in front and on the two sides |

**SD takeaway:** Keep 1.5 m clear in front and both sides of each street hydrant, and put the control-valve pit 1.5–3 m away, outside the emergency vehicular access.

### 2. Pumps and tank (checklist 4.2, 4.3, 5; Annex I Figure C)

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Pumps | Duplicate duty and standby. Permanently primed. Non-return valve where a tank is provided, to stop backflow into the tank |
| Power | Electricity with a secondary supply, or a diesel standby pump if there is no secondary supply. Diesel starting instructions displayed in the pump room. Diesel starting-pressure switch unaffected by mains failure |
| Rating | Motor or engine rated to give 20% more power in addition to the hydraulic power required for the rated flow |
| Stop | No automatic stop other than switching off at the pump room. Once started, only a manual stop at the pump room, other than the jockey pump |
| Changeover | Standby pump energised within 15 seconds of duty-pump failure. Duplicate electric pumps selected through a selector switch |
| Start | Pump starts automatically when any hydrant outlet is opened |
| Room | Solely for fire-service pumps or equipment. Clear of any exit or normal communication route. Marked in English and Chinese. Locked against unauthorised tampering |
| Status | "Power Supply On", "Pump Running" and "Pump Failed" at the pump room, repeated to the fire control centre or to a status panel at the main entrance |
| Tank | Water tank and its capacity marked in English and Chinese. Refilling in working order |
| High tank | Where H, from tank bottom to the centre line of the outlet coupling of the lowest street hydrant, is greater than 20 m, provide a bypass pipe of the same size as the pump suction at the pump suction and discharge (Annex I Figure C) |

**SD takeaway:** If the street-hydrant tank bottom sits more than 20 m above the lowest hydrant outlet, add a full-size bypass around the pump set, and keep the pump room off exit routes.

### 3. Flow test acceptance (checklist 6; Annex II Figures A and B)

Annex II gives two pedestal-hydrant test arrangements. Each figure shows a 100 mm outlet and 65 mm outlets, with a φ70 mm hose discharged to a drainage point or channel and a flow-measurement device on one leg. Figure B adds a dividing breeching.

| Figure | Acceptance marked on the drawing |
|---|---|
| A and B | F ≧ 2,000 L/min |
| A and B | P ≧ 170 kPa |

Checklist item 6.2 records flow at one 65 mm outlet and the running pressure while two 65 mm outlets discharge simultaneously. The checklist says the test follows the chosen Annex II figure. The letter places the underlying acceptance criteria in Section 2.25 of the inspection code, and the system requirements in Section 5.25 of the minimum-installations code.

**SD takeaway:** Size the street hydrant supply so one 65 mm outlet can deliver at least 2,000 L/min at not less than 170 kPa while a second 65 mm outlet is also open.
