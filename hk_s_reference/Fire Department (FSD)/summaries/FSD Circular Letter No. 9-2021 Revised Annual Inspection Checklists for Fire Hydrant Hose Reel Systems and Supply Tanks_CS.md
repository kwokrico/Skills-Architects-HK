# FSD Circular Letter No. 9-2021 Revised Annual Inspection Checklists for Fire Hydrant Hose Reel Systems and Supply Tanks
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
25 November 2021 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: Replaces the annual inspection checklists for fire hydrant/hose reel systems (Annex A, Rev. 01) and supply tanks (Annex B, Rev. 01), effective 1 February 2022. The checklists state minimum inspection items against the applicable minimum-FSI Code and do not themselves revise that Code.

## Regulatory Overview
The annexed checklists supersede those attached to FSD Circular Letter No. 4/2019 (English) and No. 2/2020 (Chinese), and they are read with the completion and responsibility principles in Circular Letter No. 4/2019 of 13 December 2019. Registered contractors apply them to fire hydrant/hose reel systems and supply tanks regardless of when the building plans were submitted for approval.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Water supply type (Annex A, item 1)
| Supply | Where the water-supply portion is inspected |
|---|---|
| Direct town main connection, no pump and no tank | Appendix I |
| F.S. tank refilled directly from the town main | Appendix I; tank on the Supply Tanks checklist |
| F.S. tank refilled from the town main through a transfer pumping installation | Annual Inspection Checklist for Water Supplies; tank on the Supply Tanks checklist |
| Internal fire main serving more than one building and/or system | Appendix I; any tank on the Supply Tanks checklist |

**SD takeaway:** Choose one of these four supply arrangements on the schematic, because a shared internal fire main or a transfer-pump refill changes the plant and the checklist.

### 2. Hydrant and hose reel geometry (Annex A, items 4 and 5)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Fire hydrant | Intact, including pressure-reducing facility where applicable; clear of obstruction and freely usable; cabinet, where provided, labelled, with a door that opens without any tool |
| Rising main | Automatic air vent valve at the appropriate position (required under both hydrants and hose reels) |
| Hose reel drum | Painted red |
| Nozzle cabinet glass | Size and design allow free use of the reel; glass easily frangible and 1.5 mm thick or less |
| Fixed hose reel | Equipped with a hose guide |
| Recessed hose reel | Control valve and nozzle, when recessed, within 500 mm of the front wall surface |
| Hose reel cabinet label | "FIRE HOSE REEL" and the Chinese characters, lettering at least 50 mm high |
| Cabinet door | Opens without a key, except a cabinet fitted with an easily frangible glass panel |
| Jet | Hose reel capable of a jet 6 m long |
| Instruction | Legible standard operation notice on the wall adjacent to the reel, or on the cabinet door if the reel is inside a cabinet |
| Manual call point | Hose reel and its manual call point clear of obstruction |

**SD takeaway:** Recess hose-reel valves and nozzles no more than 500 mm behind the wall face, use nozzle glass of 1.5 mm or less, and set cabinet letters at least 50 mm high with a 6 m jet as the performance check.

### 3. Fire service inlet (Annex A, item 6)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Inlet | Intact, including built-in non-return valves; fitted with a drain cock for pressure relief, tested in working order |
| Built-in stop valve | If installed, or last inspected in detail, more than 4 years ago, the stop valve with bonnet, stem, disc and disc seat is removed for detailed inspection; the installation date or last detailed-inspection date is displayed on the inlet |
| Cabinet label | "FS INLET" and the Chinese characters, lettering at least 50 mm high |
| Identification plate | Metal plate, raised or engraved, English and Chinese characters at least 50 mm high |
| More than one FH/HR system | Where the inlets are not interconnected, each inlet is labelled with the block or area served |
| Intermediate booster | Start/stop buttons and the audio and/or visual alarm are adjacent to the corresponding inlet, labelled and in working order |
| Clearance | Inlet clear of obstruction and freely usable |

**SD takeaway:** Put each inlet, its 50 mm bilingual plate, its drain cock and, where an intermediate booster is used, the start/stop buttons and alarm on a clear wall, and label the block served if inlets are not cross-connected.

### 4. Pumps, pressure reducing valves and takeover time (Appendices II, III and IV; Table I)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Jockey pump | Zero-flow pressure test only |
| Other water supply sources | Zero-flow pressure and pressure at rated flow |
| Priming | Pump sets kept fully primed; a pump under negative suction is equipped with a priming tank |
| Duplicate fixed fire pumps | Standby pump starts automatically and takes over within 15 seconds of electrical failure of the duty pump, and within 15 seconds of mechanical failure of the duty pump |
| No-flow indicator | Where provided beside each hose reel, turns on if the standby fixed fire pump fails to operate when required |
| Running test | Not less than 10 minutes without abnormal noise, excessive vibration, undue leakage or overheating; circulation pipe or relief valve, or a hose-reel discharge, used for cooling |
| Intermediate booster | Starts from the start button at the corresponding F.S. inlet and stops only from the stop button at the same inlet; pipes between the inlet and the pumps kept fully primed |
| Duplicate intermediate boosters | Standby starts automatically and takes over within 15 seconds of failure of the duty pump |
| Pressure reducing valve | Labelled with pressure setting and usage; full-flow test confirms downstream pressure; where full flow is difficult, a flow similar to discharging two hose reels may be used |
| Pump room | Shelters pumps from tampering and weather, or a roof pump space protects electrics from water; door locked; direct access kept; lighting; underground drainage pump where provided; fire seals or fire stops at openings through a required fire barrier; as-built schematic displayed where provided |

**SD takeaway:** Specify duplicate fire pumps and intermediate boosters to take over within 15 seconds, and provide a priming tank wherever pump suction is negative.

### 5. Supply tanks (Annex B)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Structure | Whole tank, exterior and interior, including any cat ladder, intact and free from leakage |
| Label | English and Chinese, stating usage and capacity |
| Roof | Properly roofed, with the hatch cover securely fastened closed |
| Priming tank | Located and piped so the pump casing and suction pipes can be fully primed; supports intact |
| Stored water | Level not less than 90% of the required storage capacity, and below the overflow |
| Refill | When the level drops by not more than 10% of the required storage capacity, the ball float valve or the transfer pump starts to refill |
| Low-level alarm | Where provided, operates when the level cannot be held above 90% of the required storage, at the pump control panel and/or the F.S. control and indicating panel |
| Priming-tank start | Where applicable, the served pump starts automatically when the priming-tank level cannot be held at more than two-third of the required storage capacity |
| Combined domestic and fire-fighting storage | Maximum potential draw-off by domestic services (potable or flushing) does not reduce the fire-fighting supply below the required reserve |
| Outlet fittings | Vortex inhibitor or filter, where provided, intact and not blocked; foot valve, where provided, operates and is free from leakage and blockage |
| Switches | Level switches and stop-valve monitoring switches in an enclosure of an appropriate IP rating; where more than one level switch is fitted, float cables or strings are kept from swirling together |
| Table I record | Location, premises served, quantity, capacity in litres. Usage: S system supply, J jockey pump only, P priming, T transfer. Type: RC reinforced concrete, GRP glass-reinforced polyester/fibre-glass, or M metal |

**SD takeaway:** Keep the fire-water level at or above 90% of the required reserve, start refill once storage has fallen by 10%, and set any shared domestic outlet so it cannot draw the fire-fighting reserve down.

### 6. Sprinklers extended from the hose-reel system (Annex A item 9; Appendix V)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Scope | Sprinkler installation extended from the FH/HR system to protect refuse chutes and similar risks, where provided |
| Sprinklers | Correct type for the application, correct orientation, not covered by foreign material |
| Stop valves | Labelled "Normally Open" or "Normally Closed" |
| Flow switch | Tested through a test/drain valve where one is provided; if there is no test/drain valve, the circuit to the F.S. control and indicating panel, or other control panel, is tested |

**SD takeaway:** Where refuse chutes are sprinklered from the hose-reel system, show that branch, its labelled stop valve and its flow switch on the FH/HR schematic.
