# FSD Circular Letter No. 1/2001 — Application of Ceiling, Flush, Recessed and Concealed Sprinklers
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
1 February 2001 | Fire Services Department — Fire Safety Command

> Resolves LPC Rules ambiguity on non-pendant sprinklers. Read with FSI CoP ¶4.18–4.19 (amendments pending at issue) and **FRC Code 1996** for curtain-wall buildings.

---

## Regulatory Overview

This circular clarifies that FSD-accepted **ceiling and flush sprinklers** (installed per manufacturer's instructions) are generally accepted in **light and ordinary hazards up to and including OH Group III**, and FSD-accepted **recessed and concealed sprinklers with fast-response heat sensing elements** are likewise accepted to the same hazard limit. The LPC statement that such heads are "not suitable for life safety applications" is **not applied locally** for these conditions.

---

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Sprinkler head types now acceptable (SD schedule)

| Head type | Acceptance |
|---|---|
| Ceiling / flush (FSD-approved type) | Light + ordinary hazard **≤ OH Group III** |
| Recessed / concealed with **fast-response** element (FSD-approved) | Same limit |

**SD takeaway:** Pendant-only ceiling rules no longer apply for approved aesthetic heads — but only to stated hazard groups and with **FSD-listed products + manufacturer installation rules**.

### 2. Curtain-wall buildings — "one grade in excess" rule

| Condition | Sprinkler grade |
|---|---|
| Curtain-wall building **without** FRC Code 1996 standard | Normally **one grade above** accepted risk category |
| Curtain-wall building **built to FRC Code 1996** | **One-grade-in-excess dispensed with** |

**SD takeaway:** Confirm **FRC compliance on building plans** early — it directly affects sprinkler hazard grading and cost on full-glazed towers.

### 3. AP action

Authorized Persons should **confirm FRC on plans** so FSD can determine appropriate sprinkler grade during plan processing.
