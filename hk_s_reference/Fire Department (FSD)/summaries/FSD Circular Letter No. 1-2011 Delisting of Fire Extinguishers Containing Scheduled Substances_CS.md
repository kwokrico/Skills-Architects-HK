# FSD Circular Letter No. 1-2011 Delisting of Fire Extinguishers Containing Scheduled Substances
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
10 October 2011 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: This circular delists, from 1 April 2012, the previously type-approved fire extinguishers that contain scheduled substances under Cap. 403, and it replaces pages of Part VIII of FSD Circular Letter No. 4/96 on clean-agent and halon installations. It is not a product catalogue of extinguishers that remain approved. Symbol drawings in Appendices A to E are cited and are not included in this PDF.

## Regulatory Overview
With effect from 1 April 2012, fire extinguishers containing a scheduled substance listed in Part 1, 2, 3, 8 or 9 of the Schedule to the Ozone Layer Protection Ordinance, Cap. 403, are delisted from the Government Gazette and their sale or supply will not be approved. Existing extinguishers and fixed sprayer units containing those substances may stay in use if they are in efficient working order and are maintained by a registered fire service installation contractor.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Delisting and replacement (letter; Appendix)

| Item | Lock |
|---|---|
| Delisting date | 1 April 2012 |
| Units covered | The types and models in the Appendix, being the previously type-approved scheduled-substance extinguishers referred to in Circular Letter No. 3/2009 |
| After delisting | Sale or supply is not approved |
| Continued use | Allowed if efficient and maintained by an RFSIC. Early replacement is advised |
| Replacement submission | Follow "Certification of Alteration & Addition to FSI" on pages VI.2 to VI.3 of Part VI of Circular Letter No. 4/96 |
| Sizing | Take account of the aggregate volume of the premises to be protected and the maximum protected volume of the new substitute |
| Approved substitutes | Government Gazette or the FSD accepted-equipment list |
| Agents during maintenance | Recycle HCFCs, halons and similar agents |
| Disposal | Unwanted extinguishers containing scheduled substances are chemical waste under the Waste Disposal (Chemical Waste) (General) Regulation, Cap. 354C, and go to a licensed facility |
| Circular Letter No. 9/97 | Obsolete from 1 April 2012 |

**SD takeaway:** From 1 April 2012 specify only a still-approved substitute, and check its maximum protected volume against the aggregate volume of the space.

### 2. Halon systems left in existing buildings (replacement page VIII.5, section 7)

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Halon 1301 modular system | NFPA 12A (7.1.1) |
| Halon 1211 modular system | NFPA 12B (7.1.2) |
| Approval | Tested and approved by an organisation recognised by the Department. Ancillary equipment of an approved type accepted by the Department (7.2) |
| Containers | Preferably outside the protected area. Location inside is allowed only where fire exposure is unlikely to impair system performance (7.3.1) |
| Manual control | Where containers are inside the protected area, or are not readily accessible in a fire, provide manual control, preferably mechanical, in a readily accessible place outside the protected area (7.3.2) |
| Entrance mark for CO2, BCF or BTM | Premises protected by CO2, BCF or BTM fixed installations or sprayer units are marked with the symbol on or near the entrance door, to the design, colour and size of the diagram at Appendix A (6.2) |

**SD takeaway:** On an existing halon installation, keep cylinders outside the protected room where possible, and put a manual release outside if the cylinders stay inside.

### 3. Clean agents after the 2011 revision (replacement pages VIII.5 section 8 and VIII.6)

| Agent | Status on the revised page |
|---|---|
| FM200 (heptafluoropropane) | Listed. Acceptable for automatic total flooding (8.1) |
| Inergen (nitrogen, argon and carbon dioxide) | Listed. Acceptable for automatic total flooding (8.1) |
| CEA-410 (perfluorobutane) | Listed. Acceptable for automatic total flooding (8.1) |
| HFC-23 (trifluoromethane) | Listed. Acceptable for automatic total flooding (8.1) |
| NAFS-III (HCFC Blend A) | Import banned since 1 January 2010 |
| HCFC-123 (dichlorotrifluoroethane) | Import banned since 1 January 2010 |
| HCFC-124 (chlorotetrafluoroethane) | Import banned since 1 January 2010 |

| Installation rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Automatic total flooding | FM200, Inergen, CEA-410 and HFC-23 only, designed to NFPA 2001 (8.1) |
| Clean-agent modular systems | NFPA 2001. Approvals and container location are the same as halon section 7 (8.5) |
| Fixed sprayer units | Acceptable for small Category 5 dangerous-goods stores, certain electrical installations, and in certain circumstances as an alternative to automatic CO2 and BTM where the aggregate volume is less than or equal to 42.5 m³ (8.2) |
| Sprayer volumes | Maximum protection volume of each approved unit is on the Gazette or the FSD accepted-equipment list, not restated in this circular (8.2) |
| Plans | Layout plans for total flooding systems and for fixed sprayer units are submitted before installation (8.3) |
| Entrance mark | Premises protected by FM200, NAFS-III, Inergen, CEA-410 or HFC-23 fixed installations, or by NAFS-III or HCFC-124 fixed sprayer units, are marked on or near the entrance door to the diagrams at Appendices B to E (8.4) |

**SD takeaway:** For a new automatic total-flooding system specify FM200, Inergen, CEA-410 or HFC-23 to NFPA 2001, and use a fixed sprayer as the CO2 or BTM alternative only where the aggregate volume is 42.5 m³ or less.
