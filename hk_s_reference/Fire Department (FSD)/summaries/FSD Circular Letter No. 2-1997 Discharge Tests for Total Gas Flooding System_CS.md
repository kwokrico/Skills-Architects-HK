# FSD Circular Letter No. 2-1997 Discharge Tests for Total Gas Flooding System
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
12 March 1997 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: This circular and its attached guide cover installation fixing and testing of low-pressure fixed gas flooding systems not more than 25 bar, including systems such as FM200 and NAFSIII. It does not cover gas systems above 25 bar, and it cancels only the discharge-test details in Circular Letter No. 3/90 for 25-bar fixed halon systems.

## Regulatory Overview
From 12 March 1997, a practical discharge test of a fixed gas flooding system at not more than 25 bar may be omitted only if the pipework is fixed and pneumatically tested to the attached guide and the system is designed on an approved engineered programme, or if the system is modular or pre-engineered and installed to the manufacturer’s specification. In both of those cases a puff test is still required at FSI inspection; if neither case is met, a practical discharge is required in the same manner as a CO2/halon system.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When the practical discharge test may be omitted (letter)

Halon imports were banned from January 1994. The omission applies to all types of low-pressure fixed gas flooding system. It takes effect immediately and cancels the discharge-test details in Circular Letter No. 3/90 dated 23 February 1990.

| Route | Conditions, all of which apply to that route |
|---|---|
| (a) | (i) Pipework fixed and pneumatically tested to the attached “Guide for Pipe Fixing & Pneumatic Test of 25-bar Fixed Gas Extinguishing System”, and (ii) designed to an engineered computer programme approved by a recognized approving organization listed in Part III of FSD Circular Letters |
| (b) | Modular or pre-engineered, installed in accordance with the manufacturer’s specifications |
| If (a) and (b) are not met | Practical discharge in the same manner as a CO2/halon system, as prescribed in Appendix I to the Code of Practice for Inspection and Testing of Installations and Equipment |
| Both (a) and (b) | Puff test of the installed pipework, as detailed in the guide, at the time of FSI inspection |

**SD takeaway:** A clean-agent layout can omit the practical discharge only if it is either an approved engineered design with guide-compliant pipework, or a modular system installed to the manufacturer; otherwise detail a full discharge test.

### 2. Pipe supports (guide sections 2 and 3, Tables 1 and 2)

The guide is for supports, hangers, bracings, and the pneumatic test of gas-flooding pipework not more than 25 bar. Other supports recommended by the system manufacturer may be used if they satisfy the system pressure and temperature requirement.

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Guides and clips | Mild steel. Must hold the pipe under all conditions of operation and service, allow expansion and contraction, and prevent excessive stress from transmitted weight in connected equipment |
| Anchor | To the building structure: beams, columns, concrete walls, or slabs |
| Risers | Where practical, fixed independent of the connected horizontal piping, to prevent longitudinal or lateral movement or sway |
| Other services | No gas piping hung or fixed from other piping, for example water or air pipes |
| Every section | Fixed to restrict both vertical and lateral movement. Generally no section without a hanger, support, or bracing |
| Nozzles and changes of direction | A support or hanger within 1 m of every discharge nozzle, and of any downstream section where flow direction changes |
| Short offsets | Fixing may be omitted on a horizontal drop or rise below 300 mm long |
| Double-baffle nozzles | One fixing within 1 m of the nozzles is acceptable |

Table 1 — maximum spacing between pipe supports:

| Pipe size up to | Maximum spacing |
|---|---|
| 15 mm | 1.6 m |
| 20 mm | 1.8 m |
| 25 mm | 2.1 m |
| 32 mm | 2.7 m |
| 40 mm | 2.7 m |
| 50 mm | 3.0 m |
| 65 mm | 3.3 m |
| 80 mm | 3.6 m |
| 100 mm | 4.2 m |
| 150 mm | 5.2 m |

Table 2 — fixing steel angles:

| Pipe size as printed | Angle |
|---|---|
| 15 mm to 32 mm inclusive | 25 × 25 × 3 mm |
| 40 mm and 80 mm inclusive | 32 × 32 × 3 mm |
| 100 mm and 150 mm inclusive | 50 × 50 × 6 mm |

**SD takeaway:** Gas-flooding pipework needs its own structure anchors, a support within 1 m of every nozzle and change of direction, and support spacing within Table 1. Do not hang it from water or air pipes.

### 3. Drawing 1 typical hangers and Drawing 2 welds

Drawing 1 is titled “Typical Pipe Hangers”. Illustrated drops are dimensioned 1 200 mm maximum. Details show 3 mm at the clip. Bolts and nuts are to be hex head.

| Band on Drawing 1 | Steel and anchorage as drawn |
|---|---|
| Pipe hanger for 15 mm–32 mm | 25 mm × 25 mm × 3 mm steel angle; 25 mm × 3 mm mild-steel plate; 1 × 12 mm diameter hole with 10 mm anchor bolt and washer |
| Pipe hanger for 40 mm–80 mm | 32 mm × 32 mm × 3 mm steel angle; fillet weld typical; 2 × 12 mm diameter holes with 10 mm anchor bolt and washer |
| Pipe hanger for 100 mm–150 mm | 50 mm × 50 mm × 6 mm steel angle; fillet weld typical; 2 × 20 mm diameter holes with 16 mm anchor bolt and washer |

Clip schedule printed on Drawing 1:

| Clip size | Clip bolt | Diameter of hole drilled | G |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 mm–32 mm | 6 mm | 8 mm | 10 mm |
| 40 mm–90 mm | 8 mm | 10 mm | 15 mm |
| 100 mm–150 mm | 10 mm | 12 mm | 15 mm |

The 40 mm–80 mm hanger title and Table 2 (“40 mm and 80 mm inclusive”) do not use the same upper size as the clip row, which reads 40 mm–90 mm.

Drawing 2, “Pipe Support Welding”: fillet welds joining two members at right angles are considered to be 45°, but the weld face may be mitred or flat, convex, and concave. In no case can L be less than the thickness of the material.

**SD takeaway:** Cast hanger anchors to the bolt pattern on Drawing 1, and check the 40 mm band against both Table 2 (to 80 mm) and the Drawing 1 clip row (to 90 mm) before the support schedule is issued.

### 4. Pneumatic test and puff test (guide sections 5 and 6)

| Test | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Pneumatic test | Closed circuit for 10 minutes at not less than 10 bar. At 10 minutes, pressure drop shall not exceed 10 percent of the test pressure. Pressure increased in 3.5 bar increments |
| When the pneumatic test may be omitted | Total piping contains no more than one change-in-direction fitting between the storage container and the discharge nozzle, and all piping is physically checked for tightness |
| Certificate | Registered FSI contractor carries out the test after installation and submits a certificate to FSD for record |
| Puff test | At FSD inspection, in accordance with clause 4.7.2.2.13 of NFPA 2001. Short flow test to show that flow is continuous, check valves are properly oriented, and piping and nozzles are unobstructed |
| Puff-test medium | Gaseous nitrogen or an inert gas, at a pressure not exceeding the normal operating pressure of the system |
| Where introduced | At the clean-agent cylinder connection. Quantity sufficient to verify that each and every nozzle is unobstructed |
| Proof at nozzles | Visual indicators at each and every nozzle |

**SD takeaway:** Even where the practical discharge is omitted, keep a puff-test connection at the cylinder and a straight-through route to every nozzle; a pneumatic-test waiver exists only for a single change of direction between container and nozzle.
