# FSD Circular Letter No. 4-1997 FSD Circular Letters 1996 Chinese Version
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
13 March 1997 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: Circular Letter No. 4/97 encloses the Chinese text of the consolidated FSD Circular Letters 1996, issued on 22 October 1996. That compilation governs plan content, sprinklers, smoke control, detection, services in protected exits, extinguishing agents, FSI power, transformers, street hydrants, petrol filling, emergency vehicular access, mixed use, site fire-fighting water, and mechanical ventilation. It is not an English edition, and it is not a product catalogue: Part III names accepted test bodies and listed products without adding design dimensions. Most sheets are dated 22 October 1996. The clean-agent sheets (Part VIII, paragraphs 8) are stamped revised 5 November 1997. Page X.2 (transformers) is stamped revised 16 December 1998. Diagram appendices in this PDF (EVA Appendix A, sign samples, and power sketches) have no extractable geometry.

## Regulatory Overview
The enclosed compilation is the working set of FSD circular requirements for fire service installations and related building provisions as issued on 22 October 1996, in eleven parts. Part II sprinkler rules apply only to existing installations, or to new buildings whose plans were submitted before 1 April 1995; plans submitted on or after that date follow the Loss Prevention Council Rules under FSD Circular Letter No. 2/1994 of 16 September 1994.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which sprinkler rules apply (Part II, opening note)

| Submission | Governing rules |

**SD takeaway:** Confirm the first plan-submission date before using the Part II water-supply rules; work submitted on or after 1 April 1995 is an LPC Rules job under Circular Letter No. 2/1994.

### 2. Sprinkler water supplies under the 29th Edition (Part II, paragraphs 2 to 5)

Direct connection to a town main is generally not accepted, because Hong Kong supply is sometimes restricted. A Water Supplies Department 24-hour supply is the exception. Plans for a town-main supply are submitted with the Water Authority’s comments. Before work starts, the applicant sends the Water Authority a true copy, certified by the authorized person and stamped by FSD, with Form G.

| Mode | Acceptance in this Part |
|---|---|
| Town main, both ends fed, not a 24-hour supply (¶2.2.1(a), rule 2221) | Minimum stored water for 30 minutes. The town main must discharge into the tank. Water Supplies Department will not allow one pipe to serve both the town main and the tank (see ¶3.2). Provide a fire-service inlet and a sprinkler alarm on a direct line to the Fire Services Communication Centre |
| Town main, both ends fed, one 24-hour source (¶2.2.1(b)) | Acceptable for any category except extra-high hazard. Extra-high hazard follows rules 2210.3 and 2210.31, Grade III supply |
| Automatic pump, town main feeding one end of the suction tank (¶2.2.2, rule 2223) | Acceptable if the suction tank holds at least two-thirds of the capacity required for the hazard category, and the sprinkler alarm is on a direct line to the Fire Services Communication Centre |
| Duplicate town mains (¶2.2.3, rule 2231(1)) | One main on a Water Supplies Department-approved 24-hour supply, connected direct; the other connected to the distribution pipe |
| Automatic pump with pressure tank, gravity tank, or elevated private reservoir (¶2.2.4, rules 2231(v) and (vi)) | A pump fed from a pressure tank is acceptable for extra-light and ordinary hazard |
| Elevated private reservoir, gravity tank, pressure tank, two such reservoirs or gravity tanks, elevated reservoir plus pressure tank, pump from a river or canal, pump from two limited reservoirs (¶3.1) | Acceptable if installed to the Fire Offices’ Committee rules for the hazard category. Drawing references are rules 2222, 2224, and 2231(vii), (ix), and (viii) |

| Town main feeding any system (¶4.1) | No branch for any other purpose, including hose reels |
| Direct from the city main with no tank (¶5.2) | Anti-pollution stop valve, to the Water Authority’s specification, between the town-main connection and the fire-service inlet |

Pressure-loss calculation summaries required by subsidiary rule 1412.1(17) need not be submitted. A summary is kept for FSD to check if required. For plan approval, FSD accepts an undertaking that the completed system’s pressure and flow will comply with rules 2300 to 2337. Final approval depends on that undertaking. The registered FSI contractor is responsible for it (¶1.1 and ¶1.2).

A supply that meets the fire-service rules does not by itself earn an insurance premium discount (¶8).

**SD takeaway:** Do not draw a sprinkler system straight off a town main unless a 24-hour supply is confirmed; otherwise store at least 30 minutes and feed the tank from the main, with an inlet and a direct-line alarm.

### 5. Smoke extraction (Part IV, read with the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment, clause 5.23)

Paragraph numbers below that start with 5.23 are clauses of that code. The designer under clause 5.23(i) A1 is to hold M.C.I.B.S.E., M.H.K.I.E., or an equivalent qualification, with suitable mechanical-ventilation and air-handling experience.

Design aims include a clear layer about 2 m high for escape. “Smoke-free” means visibility is not seriously reduced, not that smoke is eliminated.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Equipment that handles smoke (¶1.4, clause 5.23(i) B20) | Able to operate at 250°C for not less than 1 hour. Evidence may be a test certificate or a temperature calculation for that system. A silencer inside the system need not meet this if it does not obstruct flow in operation and the owner accepts that it may be damaged and replaced |
| Basement fans (¶1.5) | Two extract fans in each compartment, each able to handle 50% of the required extract rate. Supply or makeup air may be by two supply fans. A supply fan may serve 4 compartments and must be able to provide 50% of the supply or makeup for any 2 compartments. Size the supply on the two largest compartments served. If one supply fan serves more than one compartment, FSD agreement is required so that each compartment is pressurized correctly |
| Extract outlets (¶1.9, clause 5.23(i) B7) | Distributed evenly. In general, one outlet at the centre of each 500 m² of floor |
| Automatic start where smoke detectors are provided only to start the system (¶1.10) | At least one smoke detector per 250 m² of usable floor area, evenly distributed, and at least two smoke detectors in the starting circuit. If the area or floor has sprinklers and other detection or protection, the smoke system is also started by those systems |
| Static smoke barrier, flexible material (¶2.2, clause 5.23(ii) A(c)) | A 1000 mm barrier needs at least 150 mm of overlap. A deeper barrier needs a greater overlap |
| Static system, detectors provided only to start it (¶2.4, clauses 5.23(ii) B(g) and B(h)) | Horizontal distance from any point to the nearest detector not more than 7.5 m. This follows Fire Offices’ Committee automatic-fire-alarm rules clause 3.3.1 for a flat ceiling, and is applied here regardless of beam depth or other fittings |
| Quick opening (¶2.5, clause 5.23(ii) B(k)) | A winch with crank and gearing is not a quick-opening device |
| Static-system electrics (¶2.8) | The system fails safe on loss of power. No extra electrical requirements are set |

A smoke zone that covers several smoke reservoirs will open the barriers and vents in all of them on one real or false signal (¶2.6).

**SD takeaway:** Size basement smoke plant as two extract fans at 50% each, and place an extract outlet in the centre of every 500 m²; equipment in the smoke path is specified for 250°C for one hour.

### 7. Services in protected exits (Part VII, and Part XI paragraph 5)

Protected areas in Part VII are staircases, smoke lobbies, refuge balconies, and similar spaces. Their purpose is rapid escape. Risers, switchgear, and building services in those spaces are to be avoided. The developer provides structural ducts for cable trays, distribution boards, telephone lines, ventilation, water supply, and drainage.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Part VII ¶4 | A duct or shaft (a fire compartment) has the same fire resistance as the surrounding structure, in non-combustible fire-resisting material. Access doors not less than half an hour, self-closing, and fastened by lock and key or a similar method. A 100 mm single-leaf brick wall is stated as the preferred construction |
| Part VII ¶5 | A non-standard enclosure, such as a duct inside a stair, may be accepted if it works and is shown on the plans, and only as a temporary measure |
| Part XI ¶5.1 | Ducts, drains, chilled-water pipes, gas pipes, and cables or distribution equipment are not installed in a protected area. If found, they are removed, or enclosed in a fire-resisting casing equal to the fire resistance of the protected structure. The casing must not reduce the usable width so that the exit is obstructed. The arrangement is accepted by the Buildings Department |
| Part XI ¶5.2 | Ventilation or air-conditioning, other than staircase pressurization, may be provided if every supply or exhaust opening, except one opening direct to outside, is protected by a fire-and-smoke damper started by smoke detectors in the protected area and in the adjoining compartment that shares the air side. Those dampers meet UL 555S and UL 555, or an equivalent national or international standard (¶5.2.2, which refers to the dampers in the printed cross-reference ¶5.5.1) |
| Part XI ¶5.2.3 | A fan-coil unit used only for the protected area and wholly inside a fire-resisting structure: scroll, fan wheel, and casing non-combustible to BS 476: Part 4; wiring in metal conduit or casing; insulation to Part XI paragraph 4 |
| Part XI ¶5.3 | A room opening direct onto a stair, a corridor leading to a stair, or a fireman’s-lift lobby: if it has a fire door of suitable fire resistance, follow ¶5.2; if it does not, follow ¶5.1 |

**SD takeaway:** Keep risers and building services out of stairs and smoke lobbies; if a duct must pass, enclose it to the same fire resistance as the protected structure and do not steal exit width.

**SD takeaway:** A ground-floor filling station in an industrial or car-park building needs 4-hour separation, a non-hazardous buffer floor, sprinklers throughout, and either a blank façade for three floors plus a canopy, or a drencher on the canopy.

### 12. Emergency vehicular access (Part X, paragraph 5)

Paragraph 5.1 is new buildings on newly developed land. Paragraph 5.2 is redevelopment, whether or not the lease conditions change.

Measurement (¶5.3.4 and ¶5.3.8): from the first step that leads to the ultimate place of safety, up to the floor of the highest storey. High-rise is more than 30 m. Low-rise is not more than 30 m. An ultimate place of safety (¶5.3.9) is a street or an unobstructed open area at least as wide as the total width of the exit routes that discharge to it. A private area or private street is accepted only if it can be shown that it meets the Building Authority’s requirements and will not be built over or obstructed.

| New development (¶5.1) | Access |
|---|---|
| Industrial lot | The minimum standard for vehicular access on industrial land in the Hong Kong Planning Standards and Guidelines (Appendix A of this Part). That diagram has no extractable dimensions in this PDF |
| Uses with their own access law, such as cinemas and theatres | That law |
| Domestic, three storeys or fewer | Usually no emergency vehicular access. If the building is part of a large development, pump access to within 30 m of any building on the lot |
| Other uses | Access so that a fire appliance reaches one principal face of every building. Remote buildings of very low life or fire risk, such as a microwave station or a beach hut, may be specially considered |
| Width | Normally at least 7.3 m, the minimum street width. Where there is no formal road, emergency vehicular access not less than 6 m wide. Headroom under an overhead structure not less than 4.5 m |
| Design appliance (¶5.1.6) | 50 m turntable ladder: weight 20 tonnes; turning-circle diameter 22 m; length 11.35 m |
| Gradient (¶5.1.7) | Not steeper than 1 in 10, and never steeper than 1 in 10 where the appliance leaves level ground. After it has left level ground, the gradient may increase to a maximum of 1 in 6 |

From 1 January 1997, signs mark the designated emergency vehicular access (¶5.1.8). Carriageway access: an entrance route plan; marker signs at not more than 100 m; “no parking” signs to the Road Traffic (Parking on Private Roads) Regulations and the private-road code at 50 m intervals, except on a designated parking area. Access that is not a carriageway: the entrance plan, plus either the 100 m markers or a route mark on a kerb, planter, or similar object at not more than 100 m. Sign samples in the appendices are not extractable from this PDF.

Redevelopment (¶5.2). Where the rule is “principal face”, the access is at least 6 m wide so that an appliance can operate.

| Use | Access |
|---|---|
| Low-rise or high-rise commercial | Appliance to a principal face |
| Domestic of more than three storeys, one staircase | Appliance to a principal face. Temporary housing, and a dwelling for one household only, are excepted |
| Domestic of more than three storeys, two or more staircases | Pump to within 30 m of the fire-service inlet. The approving officer may vary this reasonably for the building |
| High-rise domestic | Pump to within 30 m of the fire-service inlet, with the same power to vary |
| Low-rise or high-rise hotel | Appliance to a principal face |
| Low-rise or high-rise institutional | Same as hotel |
| Place of public entertainment in a low-rise or high-rise building | Cinema or theatre: access to Cap. 172, Places of Public Entertainment Ordinance. Other places of public resort: same as hotel |
| Dockyard, site area over 2300 m² | Case by case |
| Composite | The most onerous of the rules for the uses present. A building with two or more staircases, lower floors used only as residents’ car parking, and upper floors residential only, is treated as high-rise domestic under ¶5.2.3 |
| Low-rise or high-rise industrial-office, industrial, and godown | To the diagrams in Appendix A. Those sheets have no extractable dimensions in this PDF |
| Gradient | Same as ¶5.1.7: not steeper than 1 in 10 on leaving the level, then up to 1 in 6 |
| Signs | Same as ¶5.1.8 |

Use definitions that drive the table are in ¶5.3: commercial (bank, commercial garage or car park that does not repair vehicles, office, restaurant, shop); composite (two or more of domestic, commercial, institutional, hotel, and place of public assembly, and not an industrial building, godown, or other special hazard); domestic (dwelling only, with residents’ car parking or garages permitted on the lower floors); hotel; industrial (manufacture or the listed ancillary processes); institutional (club, educational institution, hostel, hospital including mental hospital and clinic, prison and similar institution).

**SD takeaway:** On a new site, keep a 6 m emergency lane where there is no street, 7.3 m where it is a road, 4.5 m headroom, a 22 m turning circle, and a gradient no steeper than 1 in 10 off the level; a redeveloped commercial, hotel, or single-stair domestic block needs a 6 m way to one principal face.

### 13. Uses that cannot share a building (Part X, paragraph 6)

Fire resistance follows Part XV of the Building (Construction) Regulations, Cap. 123. Dangerous-goods licensing and Fire Protection Command policy cover licensed storage and manufacture. Petrol filling at ground level of an industrial or car-park building is paragraph 4.

| Building | Uses that cannot be in it |
|---|---|
| Industrial, as defined in section 2 of the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance, Cap. 59 | Every use other than: offices that do not assemble people who would be unaware or unprepared (the elderly, children, and people whose work is unrelated to the industrial process; staff who normally work in those offices are not in that group); car parking for staff or for commercial use; a bank at ground or first floor; a staff canteen; a fast-food counter; an electrical shop; a retail kiosk; a shop supplying manufactured goods, building materials, or motor-vehicle parts; a showroom related to the principal use |
| Composite industrial-office | Same list. The lower portion may have a wider commercial use if it is fully separated from the industrial and office parts by a buffer of a non-hazardous trade, such as a car-park floor. The kind and extent of that commercial use are not limited |
| Domestic, as defined in section 2 of the Buildings Ordinance, Cap. 123 | Industrial use, other than building services; a use listed in regulation 49 of the Building (Planning) Regulations, Cap. 123; a building treated as a single-staircase building under paragraph 6(2)(b) of the Code of Practice on Fire Escape and Related Requirements (火警走火通道及有關規定的守則) |
| Composite, as defined in section 2 of the Buildings Ordinance | The same three exclusions as domestic |
| Domestic or composite building that contains a cinema or theatre, and meets the places-of-public-entertainment regulations | Industrial use other than building services; regulation 49 uses; a school; a child-care centre |
| A building used only for an institutional purpose, such as a school, hospital, or church | Every use other than a use ancillary to that institution |

Notes on the permitted industrial exceptions: the bank is fully separated from the factory by construction of appropriate fire resistance; the fast-food counter is at ground floor only and has a food-factory licence; an electrical shop sells electrical accessories and, in most cases, also repairs them; a retail kiosk sells cigarettes, drinks, canned food, and other convenience goods.

**SD takeaway:** Keep industrial processes (other than building services) and Building (Planning) Regulations regulation 49 uses out of domestic and composite buildings; where those buildings also contain a cinema or theatre, a school and a child-care centre are excluded as well, and any bank in an industrial building stays fully fire-separated on the ground or first floor.

### 14. Construction sites, floors over 30 m (Part X, paragraph 7)

For a building under construction, provide a water supply to floors more than 30 m high, at not less than 900 litres per minute, by one of the following:

| Option | Requirement |
|---|---|
| (a) | Temporary use of the hydrant system required under section 16(1)(b)(ii) of the Buildings Ordinance, extended as the works rise |
| (b) | Electric pumps on floors agreed after a joint test by the contractor and FSD. Standard 65 mm instantaneous inlet and outlet couplings, each pump able to deliver 900 litres per minute. Number and output are at the contractor’s discretion within that |
| (c) | Portable pumps, same coupling and 900 litres per minute, on floors agreed after the joint test, with a fuel tank for at least one hour of running |

Where option (b) is used: a fixed pump feeds floors over 30 m; the pump is near a stair and clear of the exit; each 65 mm inlet and outlet is to BS 336, each controlled by its own wheel-operated screw valve, opened anti-clockwise, with the open direction engraved in Chinese and English; an air-release valve; flow at least 900 litres per minute with the hydrant outlet pressure between 350 kPa and 850 kPa; a plan at a conspicuous position at the site entrance; direction signs at the ground-floor stair entry and at suitable intervals; each pump on the site main supply; the supply able to run every fixed pump in each building under construction at the same time; duplicate cabling, or cable in metal conduit or trunking. One leg of a duplicate supply comes from the main supply and the other from the upstream side of the main switch, through an automatic changeover switch beside each pump.

**SD takeaway:** Before upper floors pass 30 m, plan either a rising temporary hydrant or a fixed pump at 900 L/min with outlet pressure held between 350 kPa and 850 kPa.

### 15. Mechanical ventilation: heaters, dampers, plenums, and flexible ducts (Part XI, paragraphs 1 to 4 and 7)

Duct heaters (¶1):

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Elements | Evenly across the duct. Sheathed, black-heat, with cold tails, fixed to supports of fire-resisting material. An element longer than 800 mm has an end support |
| Clearance | No acoustic or thermal insulation in the duct within 1 m of the heater. An access door. A hole about 150 mm from the elements for a test thermometer |
| Interlock | Heater cannot energise before the fan. Fail-safe sail or differential-pressure switch cuts the heater when there is no airflow. Fail-safe manual-reset duct thermostat within 600 mm of the elements, cutting the heater when average duct temperature exceeds 50°C ±10%, and within 90 seconds of reaching that temperature. On normal fan stop, the heater drops at once and the fan runs on for at least 3 minutes. A mushroom-head manual-reset emergency stop next to the fan motor, which also defeats the run-on. Control-circuit voltage not over 220 V |
| Fan-coil not over 2 kW of electric heat | Exempt from the airflow switch, the 3-minute run-on, the emergency stop, and the summer-mode run-on. Sequence interlock and the overheat thermostat still apply |
| Proprietary duct heaters | Submitted before installation and listed as acceptable by FSD |

Fire dampers in a mechanical ventilating or air-conditioning system controlled by the Building (Ventilating Systems) Regulations, or by the ventilating-system bylaws for scheduled premises (¶2).

Locally made blade dampers:

| Situation | Blade |
|---|---|
| Duct through a floor or ceiling | 6 mm mild steel, casing to the same standard |
| Duct through a wall of less than 2 hours fire resistance | 3 mm mild steel |
| Duct through a wall of 2 hours or more | 6 mm mild steel |
| Side clearance, blade to casing | Not more than 1.5 mm |
| Multi-blade overlap when closed | At least 5 mm |
| Closing | By blade weight only. No spring and no added weight |
| Blade length | Not more than 600 mm. A wider opening uses ganged dampers. A small excess is considered only if FSD is shown that operation is not affected |
| Bearings | Brass, or similar |

Proprietary dampers: tested and installed to a standard accepted by an approved laboratory, with fire resistance equal to the structure they protect. Accepted standards include BS 476: Part 20, integrity of the damper only (insulation and load-bearing are not required), and UL 555.

All dampers: fusible link placed so that it does not obstruct closing, and not rated above 69°C; airflow must not hold the damper open; damper fixed in the plane of the fire-resisting element so that it stays if the duct falls; an access door large enough for maintenance; no internal lining within 1000 mm; casing, when the damper is open, at least 30 mm longer than the blade, 15 mm at each end. An opening in a fire-resisting wall, floor, or ceiling is protected by a damper to the same standards.

Ceiling and raised-floor plenums used as ducts, other than a computer-room installation (¶3). A naturally ventilated space is exempt if compartmentation is maintained. A purpose-made non-combustible suspended, ventilated, or raised floor may be used as an air path only if all of the following are met: materials to BS 476: Part 4, or an equivalent, approved by an accepted laboratory; fire-and-smoke dampers operated by a probe-type smoke-detection system, so that compartmentation holds; power and control cables in heavy-gauge screwed metal trunking or conduit to the relevant British Standard and the current IEE Regulations; pipes in the space of metal, and insulation to paragraph 4; pneumatic control tube of copper, with flexible tube only on the last connection to the terminal, not longer than 300 mm, of an accepted flame-spread type; the void contains only services that principally and solely serve that area; access openings for cleaning and inspection.

Insulation (¶4):

| Position | Standard |
|---|---|
| Internal lining of a duct, including fasteners, adhesives, and tapes | BS 476: Part 6, overall index I not over 12, and initial index i1 not over 6, or an equivalent |
| External insulation of ducts and pipes | BS 476: Part 7, or an equivalent, or treatment with an accepted fire-retardant product |
| Accepted treatment for that external insulation | Aluminium-foil vapour barrier, limited to mineral-wool and glass-fibre insulation; or at least 12 mm plaster; or a metal cladding (aluminium, galvanised steel, and similar) |
| Where a duct passes through structure and has a fire damper | External insulation stops at the fire stop fixed to the damper casing and the structure |
| Where a pipe passes through structure | Insulation stops at a fire stop fixed to the pipe and the structure |
| Fire stop | Fire resistance equal to the element passed through. Fire resistance is regulated by the Buildings Department |

Flexible ducts (¶7): either UL 181 Class 1 (each layer of a multi-layer composite permanently bonded, or each layer tested separately to Class 1, and the lining passes the puncture test) or BS 476: Part 6 with I not over 12 and i1 not over 6, plus the puncture test. Puncture test: 10 mm steel rod, 15 mm flat-faced hammer, 1 kg, drop of not less than 500 mm, two fully extended 600 mm samples, three points 120 degrees apart, six points in all; any full penetration is a failure. Each flexible length from a rigid duct to a terminal, VAV box, or final connection is not more than 4 m, unless FSD agrees a small excess before installation. Flexible duct is not used as a main air distributor and does not pass through a fire wall, fire floor, fire ceiling, or fire compartment.

Air filters (¶6): the filter and frame meet one of BS 476: Part 4, BS 476: Part 6 with I not over 12 and i1 not over 6, UL 900 Class 1 or Class 2, or DIN 53438: Part 3 Class F1. Frames for single and multi-bank filters are non-combustible to BS 476: Part 4 and as rigid as 1 mm galvanised sheet. Gaskets match the filter. An all-metal mesh or grille is outside these rules. Steel wool is prohibited under regulation 4(1)(g) of the Building (Ventilating Systems) Regulations. A metal filter carrying a flammable coating is a special filter. Activated-carbon and similar special filters need an application to the Director. Filters other than all-metal meshes are marked at source with manufacturer, place of origin, type and model, and the standard tested to.

**SD takeaway:** Carry fire dampers in every duct that passes a fire-resisting floor or wall, keep flexible ducts to 4 m and out of fire separations, and do not use a ceiling void as a duct unless it is non-combustible to BS 476: Part 4 and dampered on a smoke-probe system.

### 16. Battery rooms and Category 5 dangerous-goods ventilation (Part XI, paragraphs 8 and 9)

The mechanical ventilation keeps the average hydrogen concentration at or below the safety limit of 1% of the air content. Design follows the guidance in BS 6133. FSD may ask for the calculations.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Fan | Extract to outside. Motor totally enclosed, fan-cooled |
| System | Not connected to any other system in the building |
| Air path | Extract at high level, supply at low level, arranged for cross-flow |
| Interlock | Extract-fan motor electrically interlocked with the charger so the charger cannot start while the fan is stopped. Fan on a circuit independent of the charger. Interlock relay separately fused |
| If the battery duty cannot meet that interlock | Another safety measure, agreed by FSD before installation |
| Materials | Corrosion-resistant |
| Open batteries, any size | A separate battery room meeting paragraphs 8.1, 8.2, and 8.3 |
| Sealed batteries to BS 6133, over 400 ampere-hours | Separate battery room to those paragraphs |
| Sealed batteries to BS 6133, not over 400 ampere-hours, meeting the BS 6133 safety standard | Separate battery room not required |
| Valve-regulated sealed batteries to BS 6290: Part 4, any size | Separate battery room not required |

Category 5 dangerous-goods premises are places that manufacture, store, and/or convey Category 5 flammable liquids. A sealed Category 5 premises has mechanical ventilation of at least 5 litres per second per square metre of floor area, arranged for cross-flow so that every part is ventilated. Exhaust and inlet are placed where the vapour collects; if the vapour is heavier than air, the exhaust is at low level. Electrical equipment is explosion-protected to BS 5345.

**SD takeaway:** Give open batteries, and sealed batteries over 400 Ah, a dedicated extract room held to 1% hydrogen with the charger interlocked to the fan; a sealed Category 5 store needs 5 L/s per m² and explosion-protected electrics to BS 5345.
