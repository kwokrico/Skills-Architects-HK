# Codes of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
March 1994 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: This is the March 1994 Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment, together with the inspection and testing code bound in the same PDF. It is the schedule behind the Director of Fire Services certificate on building plans. It is **not** the September 2022 code, and it is **not** the Buildings Department code for means of escape. The 1994 PDF does not itself say that this edition remains the reference under the Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Ordinance (Cap. 502) or the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572).

## Regulatory Overview

The code classifies premises and then lists the systems and coverage for each class, with a high-rise line at 30 m from staircase discharge to the floor of the uppermost storey and a basement split at 230 m² of usable floor area.

Where a premises type is not set out, the Director of Fire Services determines the requirements, and the Director may add, substitute or relax any requirement so that the building still has the minimum installations for its stated purpose.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Certificate, occupation and standards (paras 1.3, 1.4, 1.6; inspection-code preliminary note)

| Rule | Constraint |
|---|---|
| para. 1.4 | The Building Authority may refuse plans under section 16(1)(b) of the Buildings Ordinance unless the Director certifies that no fire service installation is necessary, or that the plans show the minimum installations for the purpose stated in the certificate, in accordance with the code the Director publishes |
| para. 1.3 | For a particular building the Director may require extra installations, substitute them, or relax a requirement, so long as the result is the minimum necessary for the intended purpose |
| para. 1.6 | Standards quoted in the code are the current version at building-plans submission |
| Part IV, General | Where requirements are not detailed for particular premises, the Director determines them |
| Inspection code, preliminary note | After plans are certified, the Building Authority may under section 21(6)(d) of the Buildings Ordinance refuse a temporary occupation permit or an occupation permit unless the Director certifies that the installations shown on the plans have been provided and are in efficient working order. Until that permit, the building may be occupied only by not more than two caretakers |
| Same note | The inspection code is published for information. It does not lay down hard-and-fast rules. The Director may require extra tests |

Where a premises paragraph states the additional lining rules, acoustic and thermal linings in ductings and concealed locations, and acoustic, thermal and decorative linings in protected means of escape, are Class 1 or 2 rate of surface spread of flame to British Standard 476: Part 7, or are brought to that standard by an approved fire-retardant product. Intended storage or use of dangerous goods under Chapter 295 is to be notified to the Director.

**SD takeaway:** Freeze the purpose on the section 16(1)(b) certificate before the plate is frozen, and do not treat occupation as available until the Director has certified that those installations are actually in working order.

### 2. Classification locks (paras 1.2, 3.1, 3.2)

| Term | Threshold in this code |
|---|---|
| High-rise building | Floor of the uppermost storey **exceeds 30 m** above the point of staircase discharge at ground floor |
| Low-rise building | That floor **does not exceed 30 m** above the same point |
| Basement storey | A storey below the ground storey from which all required exit routes are upward to the ground storey |
| Cubical extent | Space within the external surfaces of the walls and roof and the upper surface of the floor of the lowest storey, excluding a roof enclosure used only for a water tank, lift gear or like apparatus. An unenclosed side is deemed to extend downwards from the outer edge of the roof |
| Fire load | (Calorific value of contents in MJ/kg × weight of contents in kg) / floor area in square metres |
| Sleeping risk | Increased life risk during hours of darkness, when occupants are resting and need extra help because of age, infirmity or childhood, or because they are transient in a strange building and cannot identify the means of escape |
| Commercial | A building, or part, for business, trade or entertainment |
| Domestic | Constructed, used or intended for habitation |
| Hotel | Used wholly or in part primarily for accommodation on a commercial basis |
| Composite | Any two or more of domestic, commercial, institutional and hotel. A separate section of this code applies to each purpose |
| Industrial | Making, altering, repairing, ornamenting, finishing, cleaning, washing, breaking up, demolition, or adapting for sale, by way of trade or for gain |
| Godown | A warehouse, or any building used wholly or in part for storage of goods or raw material |
| Institutional | Club premises; educational establishments; hostels; hospitals, including mental institutions and clinics; prisons and similar corrective institutions; sanatoria |
| Car port | Covered parking open for its entire length or width on at least two sides |
| Garage | Covered parking enclosed by walls, with or without windows, on more than two sides |
| Cold storage | A unit entirely given over to storage in an atmosphere of less than 10 °C above zero |
| Protected means of escape | Protected corridors, protected lobbies (including lobbies protecting fireman’s lifts) and protected staircases as defined in the Code of Practice for Means of Escape published by the Buildings and Lands Department |
| Natural venting of staircases | An alternative to staircase pressurization, by venting smoke from the staircase in accordance with Section 2 of BS 5588: Part 5: 1991, in particular the paras. 8.4 cited in para. 1.2, and the latest version of that standard |
| Fire service installation or equipment | Equipment or an installation manufactured, used or designed to be used to (a) extinguish, attack, prevent or limit a fire; (b) give warning of a fire; or (c) provide access for those purposes. The definition printed in para. 1.2 stops at (c) |

Group I is a designated area of special hazard normally inside a building, including audio/visual production, battery rooms and electrical charging, boiler rooms, bowling alleys, cold storage, dangerous-goods stores, electrical equipment incorporating transformers, switchgear above 1 kilovolt, generators or alternators that need a separate installation, kitchens, lift motor rooms, and telephone, computer and similar installations. Group II is a building or complex of special hazard, including aircraft maintenance, buildings devoted to audio/visual production or cold storage, bulk fuel, chemical plants, container terminals, curtain-walled buildings, a range of dangerous-goods stores, explosives, a mechanical plant room, open sites of public assembly, petro-chemical complexes, railway marshalling yards, road tunnels and shipyards. Audio/visual production, cold storage and dangerous-goods stores are in both groups.

**SD takeaway:** Measure the 30 m high-rise line to the floor of the uppermost storey at staircase discharge; a building whose top floor is exactly 30 m above that point is still low-rise.

### 3. Fireman’s lift is required, and is not a fire service installation (paras 1.5, 2.2)

| Rule | Text |
|---|---|
| para. 1.5 | Fireman’s-lift requirements in this code are formulated in accordance with regulation 41B of the Building (Planning) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 1992, and are **not** fire service installations within para. 1.2 |
| para. 2.2 | A fireman’s lift is a designated lift designed to allow Fire Services personnel safe access to at least all alternate floors |
| Where a premises paragraph requires it | The usual extent is “lift or lifts as designated” |

A fire control centre, where required, is defined as a compartment at street level with direct access to open air and vehicular approach, containing the annunciator boards, controls and terminals of the fire protection and life safety systems (para. 2.2).

**SD takeaway:** Provide the fireman’s lift under regulation 41B as a building item, not as part of the fire-service-installation plant schedule, and give it access to at least every other floor.

### 4. Hydrant, hose and alarm geometry (paras 4.3, 4.14, 5.11, 5.14)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Coverage | Sufficient hydrants and hose reels so that every part of the building, floor or basement, as the premises paragraph states, can be reached by not more than **30 m** of Fire Services hose **or** hose reel tubing. Strong rooms and safe deposit vaults are excepted in the basement paragraphs |
| Hydrant position | Prominently sited in an approach lobby to a staircase **or in the staircase enclosure**. Must not obstruct a door opening or the required width of an exit route, and must not be concealed by an adjacent door leaf when that door is open |
| Hydrant height | Outlet not less than **800 mm** nor more than **1 200 mm** above finished floor. Outlet is male round-thread or female instantaneous to British Standard 336 |
| Hose reel | Tubing not exceeding **30 m**. Internal bore not less than **19 mm**. Nozzle orifice **4.5 mm**. Jet not less than **6 m**. Control valve and nozzle adjacent, not more than **1 350 mm** above finished floor. If recessed, not more than **500 mm** from the wall face. Rising main for hose reels not less than **40 mm** nominal bore; branch to one reel not less than **25 mm**. Doors to hose-reel recesses must not be locked |
| Glass over a hydrant or hose reel | Optional lockable frangible glass, not exceeding **3 mm** thick, and not obstructing use. May be linked to a security alarm |
| Manual call point | Near the hose reels, at a level of not more than **1 200 mm** above finished floor. One actuating point and one audio warning device at each hose reel point, including fire-pump start, unless the premises paragraph says otherwise. Any call point in the building starts the fixed fire pump, regardless of zone |
| Small units | Hose reels may sit in the common area immediately outside the units, still meeting the Part IV coverage |

**SD takeaway:** Set hose coverage at 30 m of Fire Services hose or hose-reel tubing, and put the hydrant in the stair approach lobby or in the stair enclosure without cutting the exit width.

### 5. Main building packages (paras 4.14, 4.15, 4.22–4.24, 4.30–4.33)

Audio/visual advisory systems, where listed, apply only where one single occupancy on one floor exceeds **2 000 m²** and the occupants are transient shoppers, audience or guests (institutional: guests or visitors) who need the extra advice. Fire detection, where the paragraph says so, covers areas not covered by automatic fixed installations. Automatic installations other than water go where water is undesirable for the occupancy or trade.

| Package | Sprinklers | Fire control centre | Emergency generator | Other locks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic, up to and including 3 storeys (para. 4.22) | Not in this paragraph | Not in this paragraph | Not in this paragraph | Portable hand-operated approved appliance: **one per floor**, plus additional for car ports |
| Domestic, low-rise over 3 storeys (para. 4.23) | Not in this paragraph | Not in this paragraph | Not in this paragraph | Manual fire alarm and hydrant/hose reel. Hose reels so that every part of **each floor** is within 30 m |
| Domestic, high-rise (para. 4.24) | Not in this paragraph | Not in this paragraph | Yes, for the fire protection and life safety systems required in the building | Emergency lighting to all staircases, passages and public areas, **including lift lobbies on all floors and refuge areas**. Exit signs from public areas to staircases. Fireman’s lift as designated |
| Commercial, low-rise (para. 4.14) | Total floor area **exceeding 230 m²**, all parts **including staircases and common corridors** | Not in the requirements list | Yes, for the essential services it must provide | Emergency lighting throughout the building and all exit routes to ground level. Fireman’s lift as designated. No pressurization item |
| Commercial, high-rise (para. 4.15) | **All parts**, including staircases and common corridors | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Pressurization and smoke extraction: topic 9. Fireman’s lift as designated |
| Industrial/godown, low-rise (para. 4.30) | Total floor area **exceeding 230 m²**, all parts including staircases and common corridors | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Fireman’s lift as designated. Smoke extraction: topic 9 |
| Industrial/godown, high-rise (para. 4.31) | **All parts**, including staircases and common corridors | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Pressurization: topic 9. One rising main per staircase (para. 5.14) |
| Institutional, low-rise (para. 4.32) | All parts including staircases and common corridors, where total floor area **exceeds 230 m²** | Not in the requirements list | Listed for **hospitals and prisons only** | Sleeping-floor smoke detection: topic 6. Emergency lighting throughout and on all exit routes to ground level |
| Institutional, high-rise (para. 4.33) | **All parts**, including staircases and common corridors | Minimum of one; more if the building is complex | Yes | Pressurization **for hospitals** only: topic 9. No smoke-extraction item in the requirements list |

Hospital lifts designated for evacuation shall satisfy every condition for a fireman’s lift except the internal floor area of the car and the minimum rated load (para. 4.33, additional requirements).

**SD takeaway:** A domestic block does not pick up sprinklers or a fire control centre from these paragraphs; a commercial or industrial building over 230 m² total floor area does pick up sprinklers through the stairs and common corridors, and an industrial building picks up a fire control centre even when it is low-rise.

### 6. Hotels and sleeping floors (paras 4.28, 4.29, 4.32, 4.33)

Low-rise and high-rise hotels carry the same core list: audio/visual advisory system on the 2 000 m² test, emergency generator, emergency lighting throughout and on all exit routes to ground level, manual alarm, fire detection, hydrant/hose reel, fireman’s lift **if a lift is installed**, a fire control centre (minimum of one), staircase pressurization where topic 9 is met, sprinklers, static or dynamic smoke extraction, and ventilation/air-conditioning control.

| Hotel rule | Constraint |
|---|---|
| Sprinklers | **All parts of the hotels, including staircases and common corridors** |
| Sleeping floor | If any part of a floor is used for sleeping accommodation, a smoke detection system for the **entire floor, including the lift lobbies** |
| Guest-room route | Static or dynamic smoke extraction for **all internal means of escape serving all guest rooms**, irrespective of cubical extent or compartment volume. That route runs from outside the guest rooms to a pressurized or naturally ventilated staircase, a protected lobby, or open air, unless the route itself has openable windows to the open air whose aggregate area **exceeds 6.25%** of the floor area of that route |
| Other smoke extraction | An atrium compartment exceeding **28 000 m³**, or a basement level or floor of that compartment exceeding **7 000 m³**; and any fire compartment exceeding **7 000 m³** whose openable windows do not exceed **6.25%** of its floor area and whose designed fire load is likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²** |
| Pressurization | Natural venting is not provided, **and** openable windows of the rooms/units do not exceed **6.25%** of their floor area, floor by floor. The 28 000 m³ and 1 135 MJ/m² tests are not in the hotel pressurization paragraph |

The same “entire floor, including the lift lobbies” smoke-detection sentence applies to an institutional floor of which any part is sleeping accommodation (paras 4.32 and 4.33).

**SD takeaway:** On a hotel, sprinkler the stairs and common corridors, put smoke detection over the whole sleeping floor including the lift lobbies, and extract the guest-room corridor unless that corridor’s own openable windows exceed 6.25% of its floor area.

### 7. Basements (paras 4.3, 4.4)

The split is **usable** floor area, not the “total floor area” used for the commercial sprinkler test.

| Item | Usable floor area not exceeding 230 m² | Usable floor area exceeding 230 m² |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinklers | Not in the requirements list | **All parts**, except strong rooms and safe deposit vaults and except areas where water is undesirable |
| Detection | Entire basement, except car-parking areas, strong rooms and safe deposit vaults | Same exceptions |
| Hydrant/hose reel | 30 m coverage, except strong rooms and safe deposit vaults | Same |
| Emergency lighting and exit signs | Entire basement and all exit routes to ground level | Same |
| Staircase pressurization | Not in the requirements list | Basements of **three or more levels** where there is no open-air access route for firemen, cubical extent **exceeds 7 000 m³**, and designed fire load is likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²**. Number from the basement band in topic 9, and not more than the stair count in the means-of-escape code |
| Smoke extraction | Not in the requirements list | Any fire compartment exceeding **7 000 m³** whose designed fire load is likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²** |
| Audio/visual advisory | Not in the requirements list | The 2 000 m² single-occupancy transient test |
| Automatic installation other than water | Areas of special risk | Areas where water is undesirable |

**SD takeaway:** A basement is tested on usable floor area; once that area exceeds 230 m², sprinklers cover the basement except strong rooms, vaults and places where water is undesirable.

### 8. Composite buildings, curtain walls, stores, garages and kitchens (paras 4.11, 4.12, 4.16, 4.18–4.21, 4.25, 4.27, 4.34, 4.35)

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Composite (para. 4.16) | Each use follows the relevant section of this code |
| Curtain wall below six storeys (para. 4.18) | Normal requirements according to occupancy |
| Curtain wall of and above six storeys (para. 4.19) | Normal requirements according to occupancy. Where sprinklers are required, the system is **one grade above** the accepted risk category. The printed example is extra light hazard becoming ordinary hazard group 1, and ordinary hazard group 3 (special) becoming extra high hazard |
| Dangerous-goods store under 42 m³ (para. 4.20) | Fixed automatically operated approved appliance, and a portable appliance, each as required by the risk |
| Dangerous-goods store of 42 m³ and above (para. 4.21) | Automatic actuating devices, an automatic installation other than water where water is undesirable, exit signs, a manual alarm at each exit from the store, fixed automatic appliances, fixed foam, gas detection in all areas of risk, portable appliances, and special equipment, as the paragraph assigns them to the Director or to the risk |
| Cold store under 140 m³ (para. 4.11) | Portable appliance |
| Cold store of and over 140 m³ (para. 4.12) | A dry-pipe system in the cold room, as defined by the Fire Offices’ Committee Rules. Extend the building fire alarm into the cold store |
| Garage (para. 4.27) | Sprinklers where total floor area **exceeds 230 m²**, covering all parts **including the staircases leading to the garages**. Emergency lighting throughout the premises and all exit routes. Hydrant/hose reel to the 30 m rule |
| Oil-filled transformer room (para. 4.25) | Automatic fixed installation other than water where capacity is **in excess of 1 500 kVA**. The paragraph applies to transformers and switchgear above 1 kilovolt, and to generators or alternators needing a separate installation |
| Kitchen other than a domestic kitchen (para. 4.34) | Incorporate the fire protection and life safety systems of the building, plus any special equipment the Director requires |
| Lift motor room (para. 4.35) | Fire detection in **all** lift motor rooms. Portable appliance as required by occupancy |
| Explosives (para. 4.26) | Enquiries to the Commissioner of Mines and the Commissioner of Police, with the Building Authority |
| Passenger terminal (para. 4.38) | Individual consideration. The paragraph says requirements are generally those for similar uses |

Railway marshalling yards and shipyards in the contents list are those encompassing a site area of more than 2 300 m².

**SD takeaway:** Treat a curtain-walled building of six storeys or more as one sprinkler grade above its ordinary category, and keep a dangerous-goods store under 42 m³ on fixed and portable appliances rather than the full store package.

### 9. Pressurization and smoke extraction (Part II definition; paras 4.4, 4.14, 4.15, 4.28–4.31, 4.33, 5.21, 5.23)

Number of pressurized staircases once a paragraph requires them. The number shall not exceed the staircases required by the Code of Practice for Means of Escape.

| Cubical extent | Basements of three or more levels | Buildings other than hotels and hospitals | Hotels and hospitals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exceeding 7 000 m³ but not exceeding 56 000 m³ | 1 | — | — |
| Not exceeding 56 000 m³ | — | — | 1 |
| Exceeding 28 000 m³ but not exceeding 56 000 m³ | — | 1 | — |
| Exceeding 56 000 m³ but not exceeding 112 000 m³ | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Exceeding 112 000 m³ but not exceeding 168 000 m³ | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Exceeding 168 000 m³ | 4 | 4 | 4 |

| Premises | Pressurization is required only where |
|---|---|
| Commercial high-rise; industrial/godown high-rise | Natural venting is not provided; **and** openable windows of the rooms/units do not exceed **6.25%** of their floor area, floor by floor; **and** cubical extent **exceeds 28 000 m³**; **and** designed fire load is likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²** |
| Hotels, low-rise and high-rise | Natural venting is not provided, **and** the same 6.25% window test. No cubical-extent or fire-load test in that paragraph |
| Institutional high-rise | **Hospitals** only, and only where natural venting is not provided and the 6.25% window test is met as a failure (windows do not exceed 6.25%) |

Pressurization design (para. 5.21) is based on BS 5588: Part 4, with these stated changes. With all doors closed and pressure relief operating, the minimum level is **50 Pa**. The force to open any door, including the closer, shall not exceed **133 N (13.5 kgf)** at the handle or push plate. With any three single-leaf entry doors open (one on each of three levels) and the largest exit door open, plus leakage, maintain pressurization and/or a minimum average egress velocity of **0.75 m/s**. Overall height under **30 m**: single or multiple injection. Overall height **30 m or more**: multiple injection only, with air supplies not more than **12 m** apart. With refuge floors, measure that height from the lower refuge floor to the next. Plant enclosure fire resistance is that of the staircase served. Generally no fire or smoke dampers in the ductwork or shafts. Flat oval ducts wider than 1 m are not used. Aluminium sheet is not used.

Static smoke extraction may replace dynamic extraction only if **all three** of these are true (para. 2.2): each smoke reservoir is not more than **500 m²** and is formed by fixed or automatic smoke screens to Part V; the horizontal distance from the reservoir perimeter to the external wall where the outlets sit does not exceed **30 m** and one side of the reservoir abuts that wall; and the aggregate area of windows, panels or louvres used as outlets is not less than **2%** of the floor area served, with at least half of those outlets opened by automatic actuating devices.

Dynamic extraction (para. 5.23): smoke travel not more than **30 m** to the nearest extract inlet, and at least one extract point in each **500 m²** (atria and tunnels agreed per project; hotels follow the internal-means-of-escape rules). Make-up air at least **80%** of extract. Velocities: non-mechanical make-up **3 m/s**, mechanical make-up **6 m/s**, extract **6 m/s**. Basements and commercial compartments: not less than **8 air changes per hour**. Hotel internal means of escape: not less than **10 air changes per hour**, with supply and extract along the corridor not more than **10 m** apart. One hotel corridor system may serve up to ten above-ground compartments but must be able to run any **two** at once. A commercial above-ground system may serve up to ten compartments. Fire-service-installation rooms are not connected into another compartment’s extract.

Above-ground smoke extraction for commercial buildings is required for an atrium compartment exceeding **28 000 m³**, or a basement level or floor of that compartment exceeding **7 000 m³**, and for a fire compartment exceeding **7 000 m³** with the 6.25% window test and a designed fire load likely to exceed **1 135 MJ/m²** (paras 4.14 and 4.15). Industrial/godown paragraphs state the 7 000 m³ compartment test with the same window and fire-load limits.

**SD takeaway:** A sealed high-rise commercial or industrial building over 28 000 m³ with a fire load above 1 135 MJ/m² needs pressurized stairs if it has no natural venting; a hotel needs pressurization from the window test alone, and its guest corridors are extracted at 10 air changes per hour.

### 10. Tanks, pumps, inlets, generator and fire control centre (paras 2.2, 5.8, 5.12, 5.14, 5.25, 5.28)

Hydrant and hose-reel tank, by gross floor area of the largest floor (para. 5.25). A wet system in a building is to have a supply tank of adequate capacity. The tank may also store domestic water, for example flushing water, only if the maximum domestic draw-off cannot reduce the fire-fighting supply below the required reserve.

| Largest floor, gross area | Minimum quantity |
|---|---|
| Not exceeding 230 m² | 9 000 litres (9 m³) |
| Over 230 m² but not exceeding 460 m² | 18 000 litres (18 m³) |
| Over 460 m² but not exceeding 920 m² | 27 000 litres (27 m³) |
| Over 920 m² | 36 000 litres (36 m³) |

| Hydraulic item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Fixed fire pump, industrial/godown | Any **3** hydrant outlets together, each **450 litres/min** at not less than **350 kPa**, aggregate not less than **1 350 litres/min** |
| Fixed fire pump, other buildings | Any **2** outlets, same 450 litres/min and 350 kPa, aggregate not less than **900 litres/min** |
| Pressure cap | Not exceeding **850 kPa** at any outlet |
| Pumps | Duty and standby. Standby within **15 seconds**. Driver rated **20%** above the hydraulic power. Enclosure used solely for the pump, clear of any exit or normal communication route |
| Intermediate booster | Where the height between the topmost hydrant and the lowest fire-service inlet **exceeds 60 m**. Fire engines boost at **800 kPa** upstream of the inlet. Bypass the booster if it fails. Industrial/godown: not less than 1 350 litres/min on one rising main, or 2 700 litres/min if there are two or more mains, with not more than 3 outlets in one main. Domestic: not less than 900 litres/min. Other buildings: 900 litres/min on one main, or 1 800 litres/min on two or more mains, with not more than 2 outlets in one main |
| Rising main | Industrial/godown: nominal bore not less than **100 mm**, **two** hydrant outlets per floor, **one rising main for each staircase**. Other buildings: not less than **80 mm**, **one** hydrant outlet per floor. Header, if used, not less than 150 mm bore for industrial/godown and 100 mm for other buildings |
| Fire-service inlet | Prominent position on the exterior, accessible to Fire Services personnel. Couplings not less than **600 mm** nor more than **1 000 mm** above ground. Each rising main has its own inlet; where there are several, they are interconnected |
| Water for fixed water systems (para. 5.28) | At least **two** supplies acceptable to the Director and the Water Authority. Fire Offices’ Committee supply types are acceptable **except** a town main with a pressure tank, gravity tank or elevated private reservoir, and town mains with an automatic pump. Unless the town main is on 24-hour supply, provide **30 minutes** stored supply, with the town main feeding the tank |
| Emergency generator (para. 5.8) | Cold start to full rated essential load in not more than **15 seconds**. Continuous rating not less than all connected fire service installation devices running together. Fuel for not less than **6 hours** at full load. Petrol, kerosene and similar highly volatile fuels are not acceptable. Not less than **600 mm** all round for maintenance. Combustion and cooling air direct to outside, with **no** fire, smoke or regulating dampers. Starts if a phase fails, or if voltage falls below **70%** of normal for more than **1 second** |
| Fire control centre (paras 2.2 and 5.12) | Street level, with direct access to open air and vehicular approach. Normally at ground floor on the main face, preferably adjacent to the main entrance. Walls of minimum fire-resisting period **one hour**. Essential supply, both normal and emergency. **Continuously manned** by trained personnel. May be used as the Fire Services Department command unit |

**SD takeaway:** Size the hydrant tank from the largest floor, allow a shared domestic tank only if domestic draw-off cannot eat the fire reserve, and put a one-hour fire control centre on the ground-floor main face where the premises paragraph requires one.

| Rule | Text |

### 12. What the inspection code adds (inspection code, preliminary note and Part I)

Inspections are by a Fire Services inspecting officer, arranged with the authorized person and the fire service installation contractor. The application is signed by both. It is submitted only when the installation is complete and the contractor has certified it in efficient working order. The authorized person attends. A further form records the result and is signed on site by the inspecting officer; the authorized person and the contractor also sign to confirm the results were brought to their attention.

**SD takeaway:** Leave the installation able to be certified in working order before occupation; the inspection code does not add a second coverage schedule.
