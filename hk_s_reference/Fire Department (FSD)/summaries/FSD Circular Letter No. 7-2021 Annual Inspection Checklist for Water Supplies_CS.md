# FSD Circular Letter No. 7-2021 Annual Inspection Checklist for Water Supplies
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
12 August 2021 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: Introduces the annual inspection checklist for water supplies that refill fire service installation tanks, effective 1 November 2021. It is the minimum inspection for transfer pumping installations and does not replace the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment.

## Regulatory Overview
The checklist covers transfer pumping installations used to refill fire service installation water tanks and is read with the completion and responsibility principles in FSD Circular Letter No. 4/2019, issued 13 December 2019. Supplies taken direct from the town main, and tanks refilled direct from the town main, are recorded on the checklist for the system served or on the Checklist for Supply Tanks, as the Annex remarks state.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which supply this checklist covers (letter; Annex remarks)
| Arrangement | Where the inspection is recorded |
|---|---|
| Transfer pumping installation used to refill F.S. installation water tank(s) | This checklist |
| System with direct town main(s) | Checklist for the corresponding system, for the water supply portion |
| Water tank refilled directly from town main(s) | Checklist for Supply Tanks, for the water supply portion |
| Transfer tank(s) | Annual Inspection Checklist for Supply Tanks |
| Conformity | Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment, the version relevant to the building or premises |
| Commencement | 1 November 2021; the arrangement is to be reviewed after 12 months |

**SD takeaway:** At schematic stage, record whether each fire tank is filled by a transfer pump, direct from the town main, or by a direct town-main connection with no tank, because each route is inspected on a different checklist.

### 2. Pump room, roof space and fire barrier (items 2.1, 2.2, 2.7 and 4)
| Location | Rule checked |
|---|---|
| Pump room or enclosure | Shelters the pumps from tampering and inclement weather; labelled for usage; entrance door kept locked; kept clear of storage and waste; artificial lighting, where provided, operating |
| Pump space on spreaders or a flat roof | Labelled for usage; electrical equipment, pump control panels and cable connections protected against ingress of water; direct access kept available |
| Underground pump room | Submersible drainage pumping installation, where provided, in working order |
| As-built diagram | Legible schematic, where provided, displayed conspicuously at the pump room, enclosure or space |
| Required fire barrier | Every opening for pipes, cables and cable containments protected with an appropriate fire stop so the barrier keeps its required fire resistance |

Items under part 4, Other Observations, are not entered on FS 251. Owners remain responsible for rectifying irregularities noted there.

**SD takeaway:** House transfer pumps in a labelled, lockable, lit room, or in a labelled roof space with electrics protected from water, and fire-stop every pipe and cable passing a required barrier.

### 3. Transfer pump performance and reduced-capacity tanks (items 2.4, 2.5 and 2.8)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Air vent | Provided at an appropriate position on the pump casing where the pump can trap air |
| Stop valves | Padlocked and labelled "Normally Open" or "Normally Closed", where applicable; electrical monitoring switches, where provided, tested |
| Level control | Duty pump starts on a low water level signal from any tank it serves, and stops only after that low-level signal, or the signal from every tank when more than one tank is served, has cleared |
| Standby | Where a second pump is the standby, it starts automatically when the duty pump fails to operate properly when required |
| Running test | After not less than 10 minutes, operation free from abnormal noise, excessive vibration, undue leakage, overheating and other malfunction, with a steady flow for cooling |
| Reduced-capacity tank (tank dependent on inflow) | Pump flow sufficient to serve that tank and any other tanks so the infill rate and the water volume in the reduced-capacity tank meet the full-capacity tank requirements |
| Table I | Pump number, floor and location, tanks served, floor and location of those tanks, pump running pressure in bar |

**SD takeaway:** If a fire tank is reduced capacity and depends on inflow, size the transfer pump so infill rate plus stored volume still meets a full-capacity tank, and provide automatic standby start.

### 4. Town main connection (item 3)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Pipework | Stop valves, check valves and backflow preventers, where applicable, securely supported and free from leakage and undue corrosion |
| Stop valves | Operate from fully open to fully closed and left in the correct position; padlocked and labelled "Normally Open" or "Normally Closed" where applicable |
| Backflow preventer | Tested in working order where provided |
| Monitoring | Electrical monitoring switches and their cables, where provided, intact and tested |

**SD takeaway:** Show a backflow preventer and padlocked, labelled stop valves on the town-main feed into the transfer installation.
