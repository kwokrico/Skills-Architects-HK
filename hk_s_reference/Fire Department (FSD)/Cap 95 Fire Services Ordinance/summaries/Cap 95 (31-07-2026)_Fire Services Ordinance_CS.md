# Cap. 95 Fire Services Ordinance
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Last updated date on the consolidated PDF: 31 July 2026 | Cap. 95

> Scope note: Cap. 95 is the parent ordinance for the Fire Services Department: fire-hazard abatement, entry and fire-ground powers, hydrant location plates, and the power to regulate fire service installations and registered fire engineers. It is **not** the installation schedule. System choice, coverage and plant sizes are in the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment and in regulations under section 25, including the Fire Service (Installations and Equipment) Regulations (Cap. 95 sub. leg. B).

## Regulatory Overview

Cap. 95 makes removal of a certified fire service installation, an installation that is not in efficient working order, an inadequate means of egress to street level, or any circumstance that materially increases fire danger or hampers the Fire Services Department, a fire hazard the Director can require to be abated.

The ordinance applies to premises, which includes any place and excludes any vessel, and it does not itself state which pumps, tanks, sprinklers or detectors a building must contain.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Fire hazard — what must still be true after the plans are certified (s. 2)

| Paragraph | A fire hazard includes |
|---|---|
| (c) | Removal from a building of any fire service installation or equipment that was provided in accordance with plans certified by the Director for section 16 of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) |
| (d) | Any fire service installation or equipment in a building that, from lack of proper maintenance or for any other reason, is not in efficient working order |
| (e) | Inadequate means of egress from any premises in a building to street level outside the building, or use of that means of egress for a purpose that gives rise to a paragraph (f) circumstance |
| (f) | Any other matter or circumstance that materially increases the likelihood of fire or other calamity, or the danger to life or property from fire or calamity, or that would materially hamper the Fire Services Department in a fire or other calamity |

**SD takeaway:** Freeze the certified fire service installation layout and a clear means of egress from every premises down to street level; later removal or blockage of either is a fire hazard under section 2.

### 2. What counts as a fire service installation (s. 2)

| Purpose | Covered |
|---|---|
| (a) | Extinguishing, attacking, preventing or limiting a fire |
| (b) | Giving warning of a fire |
| (c) | Providing access to any premises or place for extinguishing, attacking, preventing or limiting a fire |
| (d) | Facilitating evacuation from any premises in case of fire |
| (e) | Stand-by power to an installation or equipment whose purposes are (a) to (d), on loss of normal power |

| Term | Who it catches |
|---|---|
| Owner of premises | Meaning in section 2 of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) |
| Owner of a fire service installation or equipment | Also includes the occupier or the owner of the premises in or on which it is installed or kept |
| Occupier | In a domestic building, a person resident there; in any other building, a person carrying on an occupation full-time in that building |
| Premises | Any place, excluding any vessel |

**SD takeaway:** Stand-by power, warning, evacuation aids and firefighting access are fire service installations in law, so they stay inside the section 16 certificate and cannot be treated as optional landlord fit-out.

### 3. Abatement, closure and means-of-escape obstruction (s. 9, s. 25(1)(hb), s. 25(1)(hd))

| Power | What the Director or a court order can require |
|---|---|
| s. 9(a) | A prescribed notice to do specified things to abate a fire hazard within the period in the notice |
| s. 9(b) | Works to premises, with expenses recoverable, under the prescribed circumstances |
| s. 9(c) | Removal and disposal of any article or thing, under the prescribed circumstances |
| s. 9(f)(i)–(ii) | Closure of premises, or prohibition of a particular use, where a person has been convicted, as the regulations provide |
| s. 9(f)(iv) | An order requiring a person to remove any article or thing that obstructs or may obstruct, or locks or may lock, the means of escape |
| s. 25(1)(hd) | Regulations for prevention of obstruction to, and locking of, the means of escape |
| s. 25(1)(hb)(vi) | Regulations for an order that terminates the tenancy |

**SD takeaway:** Do not plan storage, gates, shutters or management locks on the means of egress; section 9(f)(iv) is an order to remove anything that obstructs or locks that route.

### 4. Entry for water, access and hazard information (s. 8)

| Purpose of entry at all reasonable hours | Design implication |
|---|---|
| s. 8(1)(b) | The Director may enter to obtain fire-fighting information on the character of the premises, the available water supplies, the means of access, and other material circumstances |
| s. 8(1)(c) | Entry to ascertain whether a fire hazard exists |
| s. 8(1)(d) | Entry for any enactment under which the Director or the Department has powers or duties |
| Notice limit | For premises that are not a place of public entertainment or public assembly, factory, workshop or workplace, and are not otherwise used for business, 24 hours’ written notice to the occupier is required before this right of entry is used |
| Warrant | A magistrate may authorize entry by force where admission is refused, urgency applies, or notice would defeat the object (s. 8(2)) |

**SD takeaway:** Keep firefighting water and the means of access legible and reachable; section 8(1)(b) exists so the Department can record them, and a schematic that hides either will fail that inspection.

### 5. Powers on a fire, including any convenient water (s. 10, s. 21)

| Power of the member in charge | Effect on the site |
|---|---|
| s. 10(a) | Such measures as appear necessary or expedient to protect life and property |
| s. 10(c) | Enter, break into or through, take possession of, or demolish premises, a vessel or a thing to end the fire or minimize the calamity |
| s. 10(d) | Close any street near the site |
| s. 10(e) | Use any convenient supply of water |
| s. 21 | Damage done by the Department in the discharge of its duty on a fire, or on an incident that may pose an immediate danger of fire, is taken to be damage by fire within a fire insurance policy (subject to the pre-2004 policy carve-out in s. 21(2)) |

**SD takeaway:** Do not rely on a private tank or street main being unavailable to firefighters; section 10(e) allows use of any convenient supply of water.

### 6. Hydrant and emergency-water location plates (s. 11)

| Rule | Constraint |
|---|---|
| Notice | 7 days’ written notice to the owner of property near a fire hydrant or emergency water supply |
| Fixing | The Director may fix a plate indicating that location on such part of the property as, in the Director’s opinion, best indicates the location |
| Offence | Refusing the fixing, obstructing it, or removing or defacing the plate after it is fixed |

**SD takeaway:** Reserve a readable face on the building or boundary for a hydrant location plate; the Director chooses the spot after 7 days’ notice, and the plate may not be removed.

### 7. Regulations, codes and registered fire engineers — where the design rules actually sit (s. 25)

| Regulation-making head | What it can cover |
|---|---|
| s. 25(1)(e) | Reports and certificates on fires, fire damage, and fire risks or fire precautions in premises, and fees |
| s. 25(1)(f), (h) | Registration of fire service installation contractors, and control of sale, supply, installation, repair, maintenance and inspection of fire service installations or equipment |
| s. 25(1)(ga), (ge) | Registration of registered fire engineers, and their duties of fire safety risk assessment and certification of compliance with fire safety requirements for premises prescribed by those regulations |
| s. 25(1)(gj) | Codes of practice on fire safety of premises, and guidance on conduct and administration of registered fire engineers |
| Classes named in s. 2 | Registered fire engineer (fire service installation); registered fire engineer (risk assessment); registered fire engineer (ventilating system) |
| s. 25(3) | A regulation may make contravention an offence punishable by a fine not exceeding $300,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 1 year, and a daily penalty not exceeding $30,000 |
| s. 25(4) | Regulations under (ga), (gc), (gd), (ge), (gf), (gg), (gh), (gi) or (gj) require Legislative Council approval |

Section 7 states the Department’s duties, including extinguishing fires, protecting life and property, and giving advice on fire protection measures and fire hazards. Sections 3 to 6, Part III, Part IV and the Schedules are constitution, discipline and the welfare fund, and they do not set building geometry.

**SD takeaway:** Use Cap. 95 to hold the certificate, the means of egress and hydrant access; take every system, tank and coverage dimension from the Code of Practice and from Cap. 95B, not from this Ordinance.
