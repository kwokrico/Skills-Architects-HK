# FSD Circular Letter No. 1-2021 Shutdown of Fire Service Installations for Maintenance Inspection Modification or Repair
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
15 January 2021 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: Replaces the FSI shutdown notification procedure for maintenance, inspection, modification or repair. It is a contractor notification and contingency procedure, in force from 1 March 2021, and it does not set new installation dimensions or equipment ratings.

## Regulatory Overview
This circular supersedes FSD Circular Letter No. 3/2008 and Appendix 9 of the Code of Practice for Inspection, Testing and Maintenance of Installations and Equipment (April 2012 Edition). It requires notification when any of nine named systems is to be shut down overnight or for more than 24 hours continuously, and it takes effect on 1 March 2021.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Systems that must be notified (paragraph A)
Notification is required when the work is expected to run overnight or for more than 24 hours continuously. The Registered Fire Service Installation Contractor notifies FSD of the defect as soon as possible, within 24 hours after the defect is identified.

| | System |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fire hydrant / hose reel system |
| 2 | Sprinkler system |
| 3 | Fire alarm system |
| 4 | Street fire hydrant system |
| 5 | Water spray system |
| 6 | Smoke extraction system |
| 7 | Staircase pressurization system |
| 8 | Dry riser system |
| 9 | Fire detection system of premises with sleeping risk |

Only shutdown of these nine systems is required to be notified. Shutting down the sprinkler system at the same time as either the fire hydrant / hose reel system or the fire detection system in premises with sleeping risk should be avoided.

**SD takeaway:** In sleeping-risk premises, keep sprinkler isolation independent of both the hose-reel system and the fire detection system so one shutdown does not take those systems offline together.

### 2. Extent, duration and sectional isolation (paragraphs B and E; guidelines)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| When written notification is required | Defects which may, in a fire, put the entire building, or a licensed/registered premises listed in paragraph C, in jeopardy |
| Examples for fire hydrant / hose reel, sprinkler and street fire hydrant | No water supply (empty or leaking tank, or piping to the tank disconnected); whole set of duty and standby pumps defective; inlet or stop valve at a tank connection defective |
| Method | Shut down by sections; if the affected portion cannot be reinstated by the end of a working shift, isolate that portion and reinstate the remainder |
| Duration of each submission | Not more than 14 days, except fire safety improvement works under a Fire Safety Direction or Fire Safety Improvement Direction under Cap. 502, Cap. 572 or Cap. 636 |
| Pumps and tanks | Whenever practicable, keep either the duty or the standby pump of the sprinkler system and of the fire hydrant / hose reel system functional, and top up water tanks of the affected and unaffected systems before work starts |
| Extension | Written extension before the previously scheduled completion date; a change in the extent of the affected system needs a fresh notification |

**SD takeaway:** Provide sectional isolation and separate duty and standby pumps so a repair can leave one pump and the unaffected zones in service, and treat a shutdown longer than 14 days as limited to improvement works under Cap. 502, Cap. 572 or Cap. 636.

### 3. Premises, temporary measures and notices (paragraph C; special attention; Appendices III and IV)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Licensed/registered premises to identify if likely affected | Residential care home for the elderly or for persons with disabilities; child care centres; places of public entertainment (cinemas, theatres, theme parks and similar premises with a large licensed area and a large number of participants); hotels and guesthouse accommodations; dangerous goods stores |
| Ordinances for improvement-works flag | Cap. 502, Cap. 572, Cap. 636 |
| Stand-by means during shutdown | Provided by the FSI owners according to the fire risk and the area affected; examples given are fire extinguishers and stand-alone smoke detectors |
| Appendix III advice to owners, occupiers and property management (not exhaustive; subject to the fire station's on-site assessment) | Emergency plan; patrol of affected areas (example: 24-hour manning, or patrol at 15-minute intervals recorded in a book); named personnel; additional extinguishers, standalone smoke detectors or a temporary firefighting water supply; no obstruction or impairment of FSI that remains available; notices; reduced stock of highly combustible goods; suspension of work that generates sparks or hot molten droplets |
| Notice | Simple standardised graphical notice, not smaller than A4, at prominent locations; Appendix IV marks the sample "Not less than 21 cm" and "Not less than 29 cm", lists the nine systems with floor or area, contractor name and emergency contact, and states that in case of fire the reader should call 999 and inform property management |
| Hot work | Suspend unnecessary activities that generate sparks or hot molten droplets in the affected areas |

**SD takeaway:** Reserve prominent wall space for an A4 shutdown notice, and allow temporary extinguishers and standalone detectors in sleeping-risk, care, child-care, hotel, place-of-public-entertainment and dangerous-goods areas while an FSI is off.
