# FSD Circular Letter No. 2-1998 Recall of Omega Sprinklers
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
19 November 1998 | Fire Services Department

> Scope note: This circular withdraws previous FSD approval for the sale and use of the listed Omega-series sprinklers made by Central Sprinkler Corporation and Central Sprinkler Company. It is a recall of those heads, not a change to sprinkler design standards for other makes.

## Regulatory Overview
With immediate effect from this letter, FSD approvals previously given for the sale and use of the Omega-series sprinklers on the attached list are withdrawn, and installations that have them are to be checked and the heads replaced. The enclosed CPSC and recall sheets identify the heads by model mark on the deflector, and by three stacked metal disks on most models.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Approval withdrawn (letter, and enclosed UL note)

| Item | Statement in the papers |
|---|---|
| Maker | Central Sprinkler Corporation and Central Sprinkler Company, USA |
| FSD action | Approvals on the sale and use of the Omega-series sprinklers on the attached list, previously approved by FSD, are withdrawn |
| Effect | Immediate, letter dated 19 November 1998 |
| Existing installations | Check sprinkler installations for these heads and arrange replacement |
| Enclosed UL note | UL and ULC listings for all Omega-brand sprinklers manufactured by Central Sprinkler were withdrawn when new UL 199 requirements took effect on 8 October 1998. Of more than 4,000 samples, 27 percent did not operate at 5 psi |
| Enclosed CPSC release, 14 October 1998 | Recall of Omega-brand sprinklers manufactured since 1982. CPSC states that, on average, between 30 and 40 percent of Omegas removed for testing failed to activate, and that in some buildings all Omegas tested failed to activate |

**SD takeaway:** Do not specify Omega-series heads from Central Sprinkler, and treat any already installed on the list below as requiring replacement before that installation is relied on.

### 3. How to identify a head (enclosed CPSC release and identification sheets)

| Check | What the enclosure says |
|---|---|
| Deflector | Read the writing on the deflector. The model is circled on the identification diagrams. If the type or model is in doubt, note the nomenclature or markings and do not guess |
| Most models | Three flat round metal disks stacked one above the other with a small space between each disk |
| Do not | Unscrew the sprinkler, or shut down the sprinkler system, in order to see whether the head is an Omega |
| Concealed | Many concealed sprinklers look alike with the cover plates on, so the plate should be removed and the deflector markings viewed |

**SD takeaway:** On an existing ceiling, identify Omega heads from the deflector mark or the three stacked disks, and remove the cover plate of a concealed head before treating it as a different model.
