# Cap 499 — Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
Contents page last updated **11 April 2025** (printed 11.4.2025) | Cap. 499 | Director of Environmental Protection; Secretary for Environment and Ecology

> Scope note: Cap. 499 is the statute that designates projects, requires an environmental impact assessment (EIA) study brief or a permission to apply directly for an environmental permit (EP), and prohibits construction, operation, or decommissioning of a designated project without an EP or contrary to its conditions. It is not the Technical Memorandum on Environmental Impact Assessment Process (the criteria document issued under s. 16), and it does not print air-quality objective concentrations. Appeal-board procedure (Part VI) and offence penalties are omitted here except the s. 9 prohibition.

## Regulatory Overview

Schedules 2 and 3 list designated projects: Part I of Schedule 2 is the construction and operation list, Part II of Schedule 2 is the decommissioning list, and Schedule 3 lists major designated projects that require an EIA report. A person planning a designated project applies to the Director of Environmental Protection for an EIA study brief or, in the cases in s. 5(9), (10) or (11), for permission to apply directly for an EP, and s. 9 then prohibits carrying out the project without that permit or contrary to its conditions.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Designated projects and anti-splitting (s. 4, Sch. 1)

| Rule | What the Ordinance prints |
|---|---|
| Lists | Projects in Schedules 2 and 3 are designated projects (s. 4(1)) |
| Gazette amendments | The Secretary may add or remove projects. Addition does not affect an existing project that has already been approved, permitted, authorized, or consented to under an Ordinance the Secretary specifies in the amending order, or whose construction commences within **6 months** of the addition (s. 4(2)–(3)) |
| Contiguous projects | After consulting the Director, the Secretary may specify in writing as one designated project contiguous projects that, taken individually, do not meet the Schedule 2 or 3 levels, proposed by the same person or associated persons, if satisfied the separation is to avoid the purposes of the Ordinance (s. 4(4)) |
| Confirmation | A person may apply for confirmation whether contiguous projects are to be treated as designated projects. The Director must advise within **14 days** that the projects are to be treated as a designated project (s. 4(5), as printed) |
| Binds Government | The Ordinance binds the Government. Sections 26 and 27 do not impose criminal liability on the Government or on a public officer acting in the course of duty (s. 3) |

**Definitions that change whether a scheme is inside the Ordinance (Sch. 1):**

| Term | Printed meaning |
|---|---|
| Designated project | A project listed in Schedule 2 or 3, or specified by the Secretary under s. 4(4) |
| Decommissioning | Ceasing production and demolishing an existing plant for the development or redevelopment of the site |
| Environmental impact | An on-site or off-site change the project may cause in the environment; an effect of that change on the well-being of people, flora, fauna and ecosystems, on physical and cultural heritage, or on a structure, site or thing of historical or archaeological significance; an on-site or off-site effect from activities carried on for the project; and a change to the project that the environment may cause. The change or effect may be within or outside the site |
| Material change | A physical addition or alteration to a designated project which results in an adverse environmental impact as defined in the technical memorandum |
| Mitigation | Elimination, reduction or control of the adverse environmental impact, including restitution by replacement, restoration, compensation or other means |
| Environmental permit | An EP issued under s. 10(5) |
| Project profile | The description of the project that complies with the technical memorandum |
| Air quality objective | The air quality objectives prescribed in Schedule 5 to the Air Pollution Control Ordinance (Cap. 311) |
| Education institution | A place providing kindergarten, primary, secondary or post-secondary education, or any other educational course, for **10 or more** persons during any one day, whether or not at the same time |
| Health care institution | Hospitals, polyclinics and clinics |
| Industrial estate | Any industrial estate referred to in Schedule 1 to the Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks Corporation Ordinance (Cap. 565) |
| Heavy / light railway | Heavy: freight, or passenger transit with capacity **exceeding 40 000** passengers per hour per direction. Light: passenger transit **not exceeding** that figure |
| Site of cultural heritage | An antiquity or monument as defined in the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance (Cap. 53), and any place, building, site, structure or relic identified by the Antiquities and Monuments Office as of archaeological, historical or palaeontological significance |
| Site of special scientific interest | An area of land or water of special scientific interest by reason of its flora, fauna or geographical features, identified by the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department and listed in the register maintained by the Planning Department |

**SD takeaway:** Screen the brief against Schedules 2 and 3 before massing; splitting one scheme into contiguous pieces that each sit under a threshold can still be specified as a single designated project.

### 2. Study brief or permission to apply directly (s. 5)

A person planning a designated project shall apply to the Director either for an EIA study brief, or, if s. 5(9), (10) or (11) is relevant, for approval to apply directly for an EP.

| Step | Printed rule |
|---|---|
| Application | Approved form, a project profile that complies with the technical memorandum, the prescribed fee, and an advertisement of the profile’s availability on the day following lodging, in a Chinese-language daily newspaper and an English-language daily newspaper, each circulating generally in Hong Kong |
| Advisory Council on the Environment | The Director informs the Advisory Council on the Environment (ACE) and forwards the profile. ACE and any person may comment on environmental issues covered by the technical memorandum within **14 days** of advertisement. The Director considers those comments in drawing up the study brief |
| Further information | The Director may, within **14 days** of receiving the application, request further information or notify defects, and may require the additional information to be advertised |
| Decision time | Within **45 days** of receiving the application or the further information, the Director shall issue a study brief and notify ACE, or by written notice permit the applicant to apply directly for an EP |
| Deemed permission | If the Director has not given written notice refusing permission within those **45 days**, the Director is taken to have consented to a direct EP application under s. 5(7)(c) |

**Three routes to apply directly for an EP:**

| Route | Test, having regard to the project profile |
|---|---|
| s. 5(9) | The environmental impact is adequately assessed in an EIA report already in the register, and the information and findings are still relevant |
| s. 5(10) | Material change to an exempted project: the impact of the material change and the mitigation measures described in the profile meet the technical memorandum |
| s. 5(11) | With the consent of the Secretary: the environmental impact is unlikely to be adverse, and the mitigation measures in the profile meet the technical memorandum |

The Director may impose conditions on a permission to apply directly, including requirements for the issue and holding of the EP, but shall be guided by the relevant technical memorandum (s. 5(12)).

**SD takeaway:** Freeze a technical-memorandum project profile, with layout and mitigation stated, before assuming a direct EP path; the 45-day clock and the 14-day public comment window sit on that profile.

### 3. EIA report and approval (ss. 6–8)

| Stage | Printed rule |
|---|---|
| How the report is prepared | In accordance with the EIA study brief and the technical memorandum applicable to the assessment (s. 6(1)) |
| Delivery | Delivered to the Director with the prescribed fee. The Director may require sufficient copies to circulate to relevant parties as defined in the technical memorandum |
| Meets the brief | Within **60 days** the Director decides whether the assessment meets the study brief and the technical memorandum. If no written notice that it does not meet them is given within 60 days, the Director is taken to have decided that it does, and the applicant submits the number of copies set out in the brief |
| If it meets | The Director advises when the report must be exhibited, whether the advertisement must contain specific material, and whether a submission to ACE or its subcommittee is required |
| If it does not | The Director advises the reasons the report is unacceptable |
| Public inspection | As soon as reasonably practicable after notification under s. 6(3)(a): available free of charge at approved locations for **30 days**; advertised once every **10 days** of that period in a Chinese-language and an English-language newspaper each circulating generally in Hong Kong. The advertisement states the nature of the project and the site, the inspection period, places and hours, that written comments may be given to the Director before the period expires, the address for comments, and any other information the Director reasonably requires |
| Failed exhibition | The Director shall require readvertisement or extension of public inspection for up to another **30 days** unless satisfied the failure is not significant |
| ACE | Notified when a report is suitable for public inspection. ACE may comment within **60 days** of receiving a copy |
| Further information for approval | Within **14 days** of the later of expiry of public inspection or receipt of ACE comments, the Director may ask for information needed to decide approval, and supplies one set of written public and ACE comments free of charge where comments were received. No such request where no comments were submitted |
| Decision | Within **30 days** of the later of expiry of public inspection, receipt of ACE comments, or receipt of that further information: approve, approve with conditions, or reject. If no written notice of rejection or approval with conditions is given in that time, the report is taken to be approved without conditions |
| Register | An approved EIA report is placed on the register. Rejection is given with reasons |

**SD takeaway:** The study brief and the technical memorandum are the content specification for the report; schematic layout, construction method and phasing have to be stable enough to survive a 30-day public inspection and a 30-day approval decision.

### 4. Prohibition on carrying out a designated project (s. 9)

Section 9(1), verbs as printed:

> A person shall not **construct** or **operate** a designated project listed in Part I of Schedule 2 or **decommission** a designated project listed in Part II of Schedule 2 — (a) without an environmental permit for the project; or (b) contrary to the conditions, if any, set out in the permit.

| Related rule | Printed effect |
|---|---|
| Who applies (s. 10(1)) | A person who wishes to **have constructed**, **construct** or **operate** a Part I project, or to **decommission** a Part II project, applies in the approved form, pays the fee, and either refers to an EIA report on the register, submits an EIA report prepared under s. 6, or refers to a s. 5(9), (10) or (11) permission and the s. 5(12) conditions |
| Matters the Director regards (s. 10(2)) | The approved EIA report on the register; attainment and maintenance of an acceptable environmental quality; whether the impact caused or experienced by the project is or is likely to be prejudicial to the health or well-being of people, flora, fauna or ecosystems; any relevant technical memorandum; any approved EIA report or conditions in an approval; comments submitted under s. 7 |
| Time (s. 10(3)–(4)) | Grant or refusal advised to the applicant and, where consulted, ACE, within **30 days** of the later of receipt of the application, expiry of public inspection, receipt of ACE comments, or receipt of information under s. 8(1). If no written rejection or approval with conditions in that time, the EP is taken to have been granted without conditions |
| Conditions (s. 10(5)–(7)) | The Director may issue an EP subject to conditions. Without limiting that power, conditions may relate to Schedule 4, but the Director shall be guided by the relevant technical memorandum. A condition may be qualified by location, time or period |
| No double regulation (s. 10(8)) | The Director shall not specify conditions that might be included in an approval under another pollution-control Ordinance unless the conditions are necessary to meet the technical memorandum or the study brief, and the approved EIA report, or the conditions on which direct application was allowed, specified expressly that the EP may include those conditions |
| Schedule 3 is not an escape (s. 9(5)) | Inclusion of a class of projects in Schedule 3 does not exempt construction, operation or decommissioning of the whole or any part from the need for an EP |

**SD takeaway:** Do not start construction, operation, or decommissioning of a Schedule 2 designated project without an EP, and do not depart from permit conditions on layout, method, or mitigation; Schedule 4 is the menu of conditions the permit can lock.

### 5. Further permit and variation (ss. 12–13, Sch. 5)

| Event | Printed rule |
|---|---|
| Change of responsibility (s. 12) | The person who assumes responsibility, while the EP is still in force, shall before assuming that responsibility apply for and obtain an EP for the whole or a part of the project |
| No new EIA report | Not required if the applicant satisfies the Director there has been no material change since the previous EP. The Director issues the further EP within **30 days** if there has been no material change and the EIA information and findings, if any, are still relevant and adequate. Silence for 30 days is taken as issue on the same conditions |
| Dropping out of the permit regime (s. 12(6)–(7)) | The Director may exempt a project from being further subject to an EP if an EP has been issued and its terms complied with, and the project is not subject to any ongoing conditions that require monitoring or other compliance. A material change to a project so exempted requires an EP unless subsequently exempted |
| Variation (s. 13(1)–(3)) | The holder, or a person who assumes responsibility, may apply to vary the conditions. Within **30 days** the Director notifies whether an EIA report is required for the variations. Silence is taken as not requiring one |
| If a report is required (s. 13(4)) | Sections 5, 6, 7 and 8 apply |
| Amendment without a new report (s. 13(5)) | The Director may amend the EP without calling for an EIA report if satisfied there is no material change to the environmental impact with the mitigation measures in place, and the project complies with the technical memorandum |
| Air-quality criterion on a variation (s. 13(5A), Sch. 5) | Schedule 5 applies to the air-quality criterion under the technical memorandum for a report required under s. 13(4), or for the s. 13(5)(b) requirement |

**Schedule 5 (added by the Air Pollution Control (Amendment) Ordinance 2025), as printed:**

| Item | Printed text |
|---|---|
| Commencement date | The date on which section 13 of that 2025 Ordinance comes into operation. Editorial note in the extract: **11 April 2025** |
| Transitional period | **36 months** beginning on that commencement date |
| Which applications | A s. 13(1) variation application where the EP was issued before the commencement date and the application is made before the transitional period expires |
| Which objectives apply | The pre-amended air quality objectives continue to have effect as a criterion for evaluating air quality impact under section 1.1(a) of Annex 4 to the technical memorandum, for sections 5, 6, 7 and 8 as applied by s. 13(4), and for s. 13(5)(b). The amended air quality objectives do not have effect as that criterion for those purposes |
| What this extract does not print | The numerical concentrations of either set of objectives |

The Chief Executive in Council may suspend, vary or cancel an EP if satisfied that continuation of the designated project is, or is likely to be, more prejudicial to the health and well-being of people, flora, fauna or ecosystems than expected when the EP was issued (s. 14(3)). In that section, “vary” includes to delete, modify or add to the conditions.

**SD takeaway:** A layout, alignment, throughput or mitigation change after the EP is a variation problem: if it is a material change, expect a fresh EIA path; during the 36-month Schedule 5 window, a qualifying variation still uses the pre-amended air quality objectives as the Annex 4 criterion.

### 6. Technical memorandum (s. 16)

The Secretary may issue technical memorandums setting out principles, procedures, guidelines, requirements and criteria for: the technical content of a project profile; the technical content of an EIA study brief or EIA report; deciding whether a designated project is environmentally acceptable; deciding whether an EIA report meets the study brief; deciding whether the Director will permit a direct EP application under s. 5(9), (10) or (11); resolving conflicts on the content of the study brief and the EIA report; taking advice from other authorities; deciding what is a material change, addition or alteration to an environmental impact or to a designated project; the issue of EPs; and the imposition of environmental monitoring and audit requirements as EP conditions (s. 16(1)).

The Director shall be guided by all applicable technical memorandums when deciding on matters under sections 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13 and 14 (s. 16(4)). A technical memorandum is published in the Gazette, laid on the table of the Legislative Council, and comes into operation on the expiry of the period for debate in the Legislative Council (s. 16(5), (10)). It is not subsidiary legislation (s. 16(12)).

**SD takeaway:** Cap. 499 creates the permit gate; the technical memorandum is what the Director must follow on whether the design is environmentally acceptable and on what monitoring conditions enter the EP.

### 7. Exemptions (s. 9(2)–(4), s. 30)

| Exemption | Printed limit |
|---|---|
| Part I projects already authorized before commencement (s. 9(2)) | A Part I project that, before the Ordinance comes into operation, had planning permission from the Town Planning Board under s. 16 of the Town Planning Ordinance (Cap. 131); consent to commence building works from the Building Authority under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123); reclamation authorized under the Foreshore and Sea-bed (Reclamations) Ordinance (Cap. 127); road works authorized under the Roads (Works, Use and Compensation) Ordinance (Cap. 370); approval as a new development under the Country Parks Ordinance (Cap. 208) or the Marine Parks Ordinance (Cap. 476); authorization under the Water Pollution Control (Sewerage) Regulation (Cap. 358 sub. leg. AL); or had commenced construction or been in operation — is exempt so far as construction and operation are concerned. Editorial note: date of coming into operation **1 April 1998** |
| Part II decommissioning (s. 9(3)) | A Part II project whose demolition plan was approved by the Building Authority under Cap. 123 before the Ordinance came into operation is exempt so far as decommissioning is concerned |
| Material change to an exempted project (s. 9(4)) | Subject to the Ordinance and requires an EP unless subsequently exempted |
| Public-interest order (s. 30) | The Chief Executive in Council may, in the public interest, exempt a project by an order published in the Gazette. The order may be subject to conditions and limitations, have effect for a period of time, and be partial as to the project or as to specified provisions. Breach of a condition or requirement of an exemption order is deemed a breach of an EP |

**SD takeaway:** A pre-commencement consent or an existing operation is not a permanent exit; a material change brings the project back under the Ordinance unless a later exemption covers that change.

| (f) | Footpaths or sitting out areas, or their associated facilities |
| (j) | Trench excavation with a trench width of not more than **1.5 m** for underground water or sewage pipes, including associated fittings or valves |
| (k) | Works that do not involve trench excavation, for underground water or sewage pipes, or for above-ground water or sewage pipes with a diameter of **450 mm** or less, including associated fittings or valves |
| (l) | Works for the provision of water tanks |
| (n) | Works carried out for village supply schemes, including electricity or gas supply, telecommunications, public lightings and their cabling, water supply and sewerage |

**SD takeaway:** The design locks that most often catch a building or site brief are the road class in A.1, the 100 m depot and cavern setbacks, the 5 ha / 1 ha reclamation and the dredging distances, sewage capacity and the 200 m sewage setback, the 400 kV substation, industrial-estate and batching-plant thresholds, the 20 ha theme park and the 10 000-person outdoor venues, Deep Bay Buffer Zone residential or recreational development under the P.1 wording as printed, and any earthworks or building works inside a country park, marine park, conservation area, site of cultural heritage, or site of special scientific interest unless a Q.1 exception fits.

### 9. Schedule 2 Part II — decommissioning projects

Decommissioning of the following is a designated project. Several items repeat the Part I capacity figures.

| Item | As printed |
|---|---|
| 1 | Airports, including fueling and fuel storage, the aircraft maintenance and repair facilities |
| 2 | A petroleum refinery with production capacity more than **500 tonnes per day** |
| 3 | A municipal, chemical or clinical waste incinerator with installed capacity more than **500 tonnes per day** |
| 4 | An electricity power plant running on fossil fuel with production capacity more than **100 megawatts** |
| 5 | A town-gas generation plant with production capacity more than **10 million m3 per day** |
| 6 | Repealed |
| 7 | An installation for storage or disposal of radioactive waste |
| 8 | A waste disposal facility for pulverized fuel ash, furnace bottom ash or gypsum |
| 9 | A metallurgical works with production capacity more than **200 000 tonnes per annum** (expressed as metal) |
| 10 | A petro-chemical plant with production capacity more than **70 000 tonnes per annum** |
| 11 | A depot for the storage of, or a manufacturing plant for the manufacture of, explosives as defined in Cap. 295 |
| 12 | A bulk chemical storage facility with storage capacity more than **80 000 tonnes** |
| 13–16 | A store for liquefied petroleum gas, or for liquefied natural gas or hydrogen, or for coal and ore, each more than **200 tonnes**; a store for petroleum more than **1 000 tonnes** |
| 17 | A ship building or ship repairing yard, including associated facilities, more than **1 ha** or with lifting capacity more than **20 000 tonnes** |

**SD takeaway:** Redevelopment that ceases production and demolishes one of these plants is itself a designated project and needs an EP for decommissioning, unless the s. 9(3) pre-commencement demolition-plan exemption applies and there is no material change.

### 11. Schedule 4 — design matters that may be specified in an EP (s. 10)

Schedule 4 pages in the extract are marked last updated **8.4.2021**. Design-relevant matters the Director may specify:

| Item | What the permit may lock |
|---|---|
| 1 | Design, alignment, plan, layout or visual appearance |
| 2 | Physical scale, scope or extent |
| 3 | Methods of carrying out the project, or the timing, phasing or order of stages |
| 4 | Amount, rate or quality of a discharge, emission or deposit of pollutants or wastes, including colour, temperature, or amount or concentration of a contaminant |
| 5 | Limits on the strength, severity or level of the environmental impact |
| 6 | Mitigation and its timing, phasing or order, including pollution-control equipment; waste and wastewater prevention, reduction, reuse, recovery and recycling; waste storage, treatment or disposal; acoustic barriers and enclosures, noise insulation, and noise control; air-pollution control; and processes for conservation, preservation or protection of flora, fauna, ecological habitats, sites of special scientific interest, or sites of cultural heritage importance, or resources |
| 7 | Pollution control, environmental protection or other mitigation **outside** the site |
| 8 | Compensation or restoration for flora, fauna, ecological habitat or other ecological resources, including replanting, relocation, re-establishment or rehabilitation |
| 9 | Landscaping measures |
| 10 | Programmes for monitoring the impact or the effectiveness of mitigation, within or outside the site, and review and audit of that data, including parameters, frequency, equipment, standards for evaluating data, action plans, and reporting |
| 11 | Equipment, methods, processes, systems, procedures or practices for construction, operation, use, implementation or decommissioning |
| 17 | Implementation and completion of mitigation measures checked and certified by qualified personnel, and submission of certified status reports |

Items 12 to 16 also allow the permit to cover training and qualifications, management plans and manuals, environmental studies during the works, public release of monitoring reports, and monitoring by accredited laboratories or audit by qualified personnel.

**SD takeaway:** Treat the EP as a design document: alignment, appearance, phasing, off-site mitigation, acoustic treatment, landscaping and certified completion of mitigation can all be conditions, guided by the technical memorandum.
