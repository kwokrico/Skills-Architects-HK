# Furnaces, ovens and chimneys: installation and alteration
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
Last updated date printed on the pages: 17 November 2022. The regulations were made under section 43 of the Air Pollution Control Ordinance (Cap. 311) and commenced on 15 December 1972. Editorial note: they were made under the repealed Clean Air Ordinance and, by section 36(1) of the Interpretation and General Clauses Ordinance (Cap. 1), continue to have effect as if made under Cap. 311. Format changes are recorded as E.R. 5 of 2022. | Environmental Protection Department

> Scope note: Cap. 311A is the prior-approval regime for installing, altering or modifying a furnace, oven, chimney or flue. It is not the Air Pollution Control Ordinance itself, and it is not the technical memorandum on air pollution abatement notices. The Authority is the air-pollution authority under the Ordinance. Plans are prepared by an authorized person under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) or by a qualified engineer, as the regulation specifies.

## Regulatory Overview

Regulation 4 requires the occupier to submit elevations, plan views and specifications to the Authority for approval not less than 28 days before work starts on the installation of a furnace, oven, chimney or flue, or on the alteration or modification of an existing furnace, oven, chimney, or of a flue between a furnace or oven and a chimney. Regulation 11 prohibits the occupier from carrying out, or causing or permitting, that work unless approval of all the plans and specifications has been obtained under these regulations.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is outside the regulations (reg. 3)

The regulations do not apply to premises where the intended work relates to any of the following.

| Case | Threshold or condition |
| --- | --- |
| Furnace or oven, or a chimney or flue connected to it, consuming conventional liquid fuel | Not more than 25 litres per hour |
| Same, conventional solid fuel | Not more than 35 kilograms per hour |
| Same, any gaseous fuel | Not more than 1 150 megajoules per hour |
| Furnace or oven operated solely by electricity | The whole installation |
| Furnace, oven, chimney or flue used for a specified process | The process is licensed under the Ordinance, or the owner is exempt from section 13 by section 20 in relation to that process |

Conventional liquid fuel means kerosene, diesel oil, petroleum and any equivalent liquid fuel. Conventional solid fuel means coal, coke, charcoal, wood and any equivalent solid fuel. Regulation 3(1A) is repealed (L.N. 423 of 1990).

**SD takeaway:** A small boiler at or below 25 litres of diesel an hour, 35 kilograms of solid fuel an hour, or 1 150 megajoules of gas an hour, and any furnace that burns only electricity, does not need this approval; a larger fuel-fired furnace, oven or chimney does.

### 2. Who signs the drawing, and at what scale (regs. 2, 5 and 6)

An authorized person is a person on the authorized persons’ register under section 3(1) of the Buildings Ordinance as an architect, an engineer or a surveyor. A qualified engineer is a registered professional engineer in the building services, gas, chemical, marine or mechanical engineering discipline under the Engineers Registration Ordinance (Cap. 409).

| Drawing | Prepared by | Scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Block plan of surrounding buildings, if the Authority requires one | Authorized person | Not less than 1:500 |
| Elevations or plan views | Authorized person or qualified engineer | Not less than 1:100 |

The Authority may set the period for the extra block plan. Plans and specifications already submitted under section 14, 16, 18 or 23 of the Ordinance count as submitted under regulation 4(1) as well, if they include the furnace, oven, chimney or flue (reg. 4(2)).

**SD takeaway:** Chimney elevations and plans are drawn at 1:100 or larger by an authorized person or a qualified engineer, and a block plan of surrounding buildings, if called for, is 1:500 or larger and is signed by an authorized person.

### 3. What the specification must contain (reg. 7)

The specification includes the type, grade and quantity of fuel, including the estimated maximum and average quantity according to the manufacturer’s published ratings, both for the furnace or oven being installed or altered and for any other furnace or oven on the same common chimney or flue; the hours of operation in each 24 hours; automatic or semi-automatic controls; controls on fuel and on air admitted; the manufacturer’s published rating if the furnace or oven is attached to a boiler that raises steam or heats water; structural details of any chimney, new or existing, through which the products of combustion may pass, including the height, the cross-sectional area at the top, and the material; full details of any mechanical stoking device if the fuel is solid; and full details of the burner if the fuel is liquid or gaseous, including burners on existing furnaces or ovens on the same common chimney or flue.

Items (a), (b), (c), (d), (e), (g) and (h) are prepared by a qualified engineer. Item (f), the structural details of the chimney, is prepared by an authorized person. The Authority may require further details and may set the time for them.

**SD takeaway:** The chimney drawing has to state height, outlet area and material, and the fuel note has to cover every furnace on that same flue, not only the new piece of plant.

### 4. Refusal, deemed approval, and the certificate (regs. 8–10 and the Schedule)

The Authority may refuse approval if not satisfied that the furnace, oven, chimney or flue can be operated without contravening the Ordinance, without causing or contributing to air pollution that exists or is imminent, or without emitting air pollutant because of unsuitable design, defective construction or maintenance, excessive wear and tear, unsuitable fuel or other material, or improper operation. A refusal is a written notice to the occupier giving reasons.

If plans and specifications are submitted under regulation 4, 5 or 7, approval is deemed given unless refusal is signified within 28 days from submission. That deemed-approval rule does not apply to an application that is only deemed to have been submitted because it was lodged under section 14, 16, 18 or 23 of the Ordinance. Where approval is given, the Authority issues a certificate of approval in the Schedule form. The note to the form says the certificate may be required for inspection and should be kept in a safe place at the premises.

**SD takeaway:** Silence for 28 days after a regulation 4 submission is deemed approval, but a chimney package that rides inside a specified-process application under sections 14, 16, 18 or 23 does not get that deemed approval.

### 5. Offences if work goes ahead without approval (reg. 12)

An occupier who contravenes regulation 11 is liable to a fine at level 5 and an additional fine of $500 for each day the offence continues. The court may also, or instead, prohibit operation from a stated date, require dismantling or removal within a stated period, or require the regulation 4 plans and specifications to be submitted within 28 days after the order. Failure to obey a prohibition order, or a dismantling order within the period or any extension, is a further offence: a fine at level 3 and $500 for each day it continues. If the late plans are submitted, regulations 5 to 10 apply as if the work had not yet been carried out. If those plans are not submitted in time, or are refused, the court may then make a prohibition or dismantling order.

**SD takeaway:** Installing or altering a chimney that is inside this regime, without the approval, is a level 5 offence plus $500 a day, and the court can stop operation or order the chimney to be taken down.
