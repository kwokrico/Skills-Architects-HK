# Noise from construction work in designated areas
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
Originally published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette No. 22 Vol. 5 on 1 June 2001; came into operation on 5 July 2001. It replaces the memorandum published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette No. 6 Vol. CXXXVIII on 9 February 1996. | Environmental Protection Department

> Scope note: This memorandum covers construction work inside a designated area that uses specified powered mechanical equipment (SPME) other than percussive piling, or that carries out prescribed construction work (PCW), during restricted hours. The procedures are those the Authority should generally adopt for construction work to be conducted on or after 1 November 1996. Outside a designated area, or where the work uses neither SPME nor PCW, the permit procedure is the memorandum on noise from construction work other than percussive piling. Designated areas themselves are set by the Noise Control (Construction Work Designated Areas) Notice under section 8A(1), not by this memorandum.

## Regulatory Overview

Section 2 requires a valid Construction Noise Permit for any SPME listed in Table A.3, and for any PCW listed in Annex B, inside a designated area during restricted hours, which are 1900 to 0700 hours and any time on a general holiday, including Sunday. A permit for SPME is not generally granted unless the corrected noise level of all the SPME does not exceed the acceptable noise level from Annex A, and a permit for PCW is not generally granted unless the application is a special case under section 5.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. What is controlled inside a designated area (ss. 2–4, Annexes B and C)

SPME is only the plant in Table A.3. PCW, prescribed for section 6(2) of the Noise Control Ordinance by the Noise Control (Construction Work) Regulation, is listed in Annex B.

| Code | Prescribed construction work |
| --- | --- |
| PCW 001 | Erection or dismantling of formwork or scaffolding |
| PCW 002 | Loading, unloading or handling of rubble, wooden boards, steel bars, wood or scaffolding material |
| PCW 003 | Hammering |

The only quiet method printed in Annex C is QPCW 001, disposal of rubble through plastic chutes. A PCW permit may also be granted if the work is screened by solid barriers, such as purpose-designed acoustic screens, buildings or topography, that effectively protect the affected receivers. The Authority may refuse an equipment permit if it believes the equipment will be used with PCW that this memorandum does not permit. Each permitted SPME item must be labelled with its Table A.3 identification code. A permit may not be issued for work on or within an NSR that is in use, or directly adjoining such a building, where the noise would travel primarily through the structure and would be likely to cause an adverse impact.

**SD takeaway:** In a designated area, erecting or striking formwork, moving rubble or reinforcement, and hammering are themselves controlled at night and on holidays, even when no breaker is running, and the only quiet method printed for rubble is a plastic chute.

### 2. Area rating and basic noise levels (Annex A, Tables A.1 and A.2)

The NSR list, the 100 m and 500 m area radii, and the influencing-factor tests (industrial area, major road above 30,000 vehicles a day, and Hong Kong International Airport) match the construction-work memorandum. A major road with fewer than 300 vehicles per hour at the time under consideration is not an influencing factor then. Table A.1 uses the same A/B/C pattern: rural A/B/B, low-density residential A/B/C, urban B/C/C, and other areas B/B/C.

| Time period | ASR A | ASR B | ASR C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All days during the evening (1900 to 2300 hours), and general holidays (including Sundays) during the day-time and evening (0700 to 2300 hours) | 45 | 50 | 55 |
| All days during the night-time (2300 to 0700 hours) | 30 | 35 | 40 |

All figures are dB(A). A permit lasting 14 days or less, including renewals that start within 21 days of an earlier permit for associated work on substantially the same site, receives +3 dB(A) on the basic noise level. More than one permit materially affecting the NSR may attract a further correction on standard acoustical principles. The permit may be issued only if the corrected noise level is equal to or less than the acceptable noise level, unless section 5 applies.

**SD takeaway:** Designated-area night limits are 30, 35 and 40 dB(A), which is 15 dB(A) stricter than the same rows in the general construction memorandum, so the same plant list will not carry over from a non-designated site.

### 3. The specified plant list (Table A.3)

| Code | Item | Sound power level, dB(A) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CNP 023 | Breaker, hand-held, mass ≤ 10 kg | 108 |
| CNP 024 | Breaker, hand-held, mass > 10 kg and < 20 kg | 108 |
| CNP 025 | Breaker, hand-held, mass ≥ 20 kg and ≤ 35 kg | 111 |
| CNP 026 | Breaker, hand-held, mass > 35 kg | 114 |
| CNP 030 | Bulldozer | 115 |
| CNP 044 | Concrete lorry mixer | 109 |
| CNP 067 | Dump truck | 117 |
| CNP 170 | Poker, vibratory, hand-held | 113 |

The notional source, the 50 m cap, the 5:1 linear-site rule, the 300 m limit of the distance table, and the barrier corrections are the same structure as the general construction memorandum: −10 dB(A) for a substantial total screen, −5 dB(A) where only quiet items (more than 15 dB(A) below the total sound power level) remain unscreened, −5 dB(A) for a directly adjacent building from which no SPME is visible, +3 dB(A) because the receiver is a building, and up to +3 dB(A) more in a confined or reverberant locality. Site fences and hoardings are not substantial barriers.

**SD takeaway:** The designated-area night list is short and loud: every named item is 108 dB(A) or above, so a hand-held breaker, a poker, a lorry mixer or a dump truck at the notional source will not meet 30 to 40 dB(A) without distance and a real screen.

### 4. Special cases that can still open a night window (s. 5)

Section 5.1 allows a permit where restricted-hour working would cause less public annoyance than daytime working, including serious interruption of road, rail or other transport, or of water, gas or electricity, and work governed by tides. The quietest practicable methods must be used. Noisy road-breaking and other particularly noisy work should not be permitted between 2100 and 0600 hours whenever possible, and loading spoil into trucks should not take place between 2300 and 0600 hours. Only in very exceptional circumstances is such work permitted after 2300 hours, or spoil loading after midnight. Section 5.2 sends work of important social implications to the Secretary, whose advice binds the Authority. Section 5.3 allows a demonstrated quiet method or special factor for SPME, in a report by a competent person. Section 5.4 allows PCW if Annex C quiet methods are used, or if solid barriers effectively protect the receivers.

**SD takeaway:** Even a special-case night permit in a designated area is expected to keep road-breaking out of 2100 to 0600 hours and spoil loading out of 2300 to 0600 hours unless the case is exceptional.

### 5. Compliance measurement (s. 6, Annex D)

Where a maximum noise level is a permit condition, measurement generally follows Annex D. Meters must comply with IEC 651:1979 (Type 1) and IEC 804:1985 (Type 1). A calibrator check immediately before and after each measurement must agree within 1.0 dB, or the measurement is not accepted. Results are rounded to the nearest whole dB(A), with 0.5 and above rounded upwards.

**SD takeaway:** A permit noise limit is checked with a Type 1 meter and a calibrator drift of not more than 1.0 dB, so the site method has to be able to hold that number, not a nominal catalogue level.
