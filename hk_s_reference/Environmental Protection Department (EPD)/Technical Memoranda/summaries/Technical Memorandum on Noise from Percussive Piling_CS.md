# Noise from percussive piling
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
Originally published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette No. 20 Vol. CXXXIX on 16 May 1997; came into operation on 19 June 1997. It replaces the memorandum published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette Extraordinary No. 38 Vol. CXXX on 7 November 1988, which came into operation on 7 December 1988. | Environmental Protection Department

> Scope note: This memorandum sets the permitted hours of percussive piling by comparing a corrected noise level at the most affected noise sensitive receiver with an acceptable noise level that depends on how that receiver is ventilated. It does not use the evening and night basic-noise-level table of the other construction memoranda. Non-percussive piling is assessed under the memorandum on noise from construction work other than percussive piling. The hours in Table 5B stage (v) are those printed for piling on or after 1 October 1999.

## Regulatory Overview

Sections 1.4 and 2 require the Authority to locate the most affected noise sensitive receiver, take the acceptable noise level from Table 1, calculate the corrected noise level from the piling method, distance, barriers and reflections, and then read the permitted hours from Table 5A or Table 5B. A Construction Noise Permit states those hours, and percussive piling is not a restricted-hours evening or night activity under this table: the hours are periods within a day that is not a general holiday.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Acceptable noise level by window and ventilation (s. 2.2, Table 1)

If Table 1 does not fit the receiver, the Authority may use such acceptable noise level as it considers appropriate. If the receiver is materially affected by more than one permit, the Authority may correct the acceptable noise level on standard acoustical principles.

| NSR window type or means of ventilation | ANL, dB(A) |
| --- | --- |
| (i) NSR, or part of an NSR, with no windows or other openings | 100 |
| (ii) NSR with central air conditioning system | 90 |
| (iii) NSR with windows or other openings but without central air conditioning system | 85 |

Subtract 10 dB(A) for hospitals, medical clinics, educational institutions, courts of law, or other receivers the Authority considers particularly sensitive to noise.

**SD takeaway:** An openable window without central air conditioning is an 85 dB(A) test, and a hospital, clinic, school or court is 75 dB(A) on that same row; a sealed, centrally air-conditioned facade is the 90 dB(A) case.

### 2. Sound power levels of the piling methods (Table 2)

| Piling method and pile type | Sound power level, dB(A) |
| --- | --- |
| Diesel hammer driving pre-stressed concrete pile | 128 |
| Diesel hammer driving steel pile | 132 |
| Diesel hammer driving steel sheet pile | 132 |
| Drop hammer driving concrete pile | 116 |
| Drop hammer driving steel pile | 126 |
| Drop hammer driving steel sheet pile | 129 |
| Hydraulic hammer (double acting) driving pre-stressed concrete pile | 126 |
| Hydraulic hammer (double acting) driving steel pile | 129 |
| Hydraulic hammer (double acting) driving steel sheet pile | 129 |
| Hydraulic hammer (single acting) driving pre-stressed concrete pile | 122 |
| Hydraulic hammer (single acting) driving steel pile | 126 |
| Hydraulic hammer (single acting) driving steel sheet pile | 126 |
| Internal drop hammer | 113 |
| Pneumatic or steam hammer (double acting) driving steel sheet pile | 135 |
| Pneumatic or steam hammer (single acting) driving steel pile | 130 |

Levels are summed with Table 3. The distance correction in Table 4 is valid only up to 300 m. Blank facades are ignored. A total screen by a substantial barrier, so that no plant is visible from any opening, is −10 dB(A). Screening of all items other than quiet items (more than 15 dB(A) below the total sound power level) is −5 dB(A). A directly adjacent building from which nothing is visible is partial screening at −5 dB(A). A building receiver adds +3 dB(A), and a confined or reverberant locality may add up to +3 dB(A) more. Site fences and hoardings are not substantial barriers.

**SD takeaway:** Diesel, pneumatic and steam hammers sit between 128 and 135 dB(A), so even the 100 dB(A) sealed-facade test fails at short range unless a real screen or a change of method removes about 30 dB(A) or more before the reflection correction is added.

### 3. Permitted hours on or after 1 October 1999 (s. 2.9, Tables 5A and 5B)

Hours below are on any day that is not a general holiday. “Nil” means no percussive piling is permitted on that test.

Table 5A applies to piling that does not use diesel, pneumatic and/or steam hammers. It also applies to diesel hammers that section 3.3 allows in a designated geologically difficult area.

| Amount by which CNL exceeds ANL | Permitted hours |
| --- | --- |
| More than 10 dB(A) | 0800 to 0900, and 1230 to 1330, and 1700 to 1800 |
| More than 0 dB(A) and up to 10 dB(A) | 0800 to 0930, and 1200 to 1400, and 1630 to 1800 |
| 0 dB(A) or below | 0700 to 1900 |

Table 5B (v) is the row printed as effective for percussive piling on or after 1 October 1999 (Stage 4), for work that does use diesel, pneumatic and/or steam hammers.

| Amount by which CNL exceeds ANL | Permitted hours |
| --- | --- |
| Greater than −10 dB(A) | Nil |
| −10 dB(A) or below | 0700 to 1900 |

Earlier stages in Table 5B (i) to (iv) are printed for periods ending on 31 March 1998, 30 September 1998, 31 March 1999 and 30 September 1999. They are not the current stage.

**SD takeaway:** From 1 October 1999 a diesel, pneumatic or steam hammer is allowed only from 0700 to 1900 on a non-holiday, and only if the corrected level is at least 10 dB(A) below the Table 1 acceptable level; any smaller margin is nil hours.

### 4. Geologically difficult areas, special cases, and measurement (ss. 3–4, Annex)

Section 3.3 requires the Authority to consider an application to use diesel hammers if the site is in Scheduled Areas No. 2 and 4 in the Fifth Schedule of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), which the memorandum describes as geologically difficult areas designated as Geologically Difficult Areas. Hours for a permit issued under that provision are taken from Table 5A, not from the diesel row of Table 5B. Quiet methods or other special factors may be demonstrated by a competent report (s. 3.1). Piling with important social implications is referred to the Secretary, whose advice binds the Authority (s. 3.2). Compliance measurement of a maximum noise level follows the Annex: IEC 651:1979 (Type 1) and IEC 804:1985 (Type 1), with before-and-after calibration agreeing within 1.0 dB, and rounding to the nearest whole dB(A).

**SD takeaway:** A diesel hammer in Scheduled Areas No. 2 or 4 of the Buildings Ordinance can be tested on the hydraulic-hammer hour bands in Table 5A; elsewhere that hammer is on the Stage 4 “10 dB(A) below, or nil” rule.
