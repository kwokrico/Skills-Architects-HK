# Noise from places other than domestic premises, public places or construction sites
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
Originally published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette No. 20 Vol. CXXXIX on 16 May 1997; came into operation on 19 June 1997. It replaces the memorandum published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette Extraordinary No. 38 Vol. CXXX on 7 November 1988, which came into operation on 7 December 1988. | Environmental Protection Department

> Scope note: This memorandum is the measurement and assessment method for noise from places other than domestic premises, public places or construction sites, and the test for issuing a Noise Abatement Notice under section 13(1)(c) of the Noise Control Ordinance. It is the fixed-plant and commercial-premises test (chillers, pumps, kitchens, workshops, loading docks and the like). It is not the construction-permit memoranda and it is not the air-pollution abatement-notice memorandum.

## Regulatory Overview

Sections 1.2 and 1.4 require the Authority, when investigating a complaint, to set the acceptable noise level for the noise sensitive receiver, measure a corrected noise level, and compare the two. A Noise Abatement Notice may be issued when the corrected noise level is greater than the acceptable noise level, and compliance is tested by measuring again at the point the notice specifies.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Area sensitivity rating, including outline zoning plan zones (s. 2.3, Table 1)

The noise sensitive receiver list is domestic premises, hotel, hostel, temporary housing accommodation, hospital, medical clinic, educational institution, place of public worship, library, court of law or performing arts centre, plus other premises of similar sensitivity that are not industrial or commercial, and only while in use. The area examined is typically 100 m radius in urban areas and 500 m or more in rural districts. The premises under investigation are not generally counted when typing the area, unless by size or character they play a major role in that typing.

An influencing factor is an industrial area, a major road with an annual average daily traffic flow in excess of 30,000, or the area within the boundary of Hong Kong International Airport. A major road below 300 vehicles per hour at the time under consideration is not an influencing factor then. Industrial areas and the Airport count at all times. An industrial area here includes a number of factories or industrial establishments, or an establishment whose industrial operation is of a significant scale.

| Type of area containing the NSR | Not affected | Indirectly affected | Directly affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rural area, including country parks or village-type developments | A | B | B |
| Low-density residential area of low-rise or isolated high-rise developments | A | B | C |
| Urban area | B | C | C |
| Area other than those above | B | B | C |

Irrespective of Table 1, an NSR within 100 m of a zone designated Industrial or Industrial Estate on a statutory outline zoning plan is assigned ASR C, and an NSR between 100 m and 250 m from such a zone is assigned ASR B, except where Table 1 already indicates C.

**SD takeaway:** A chiller or kitchen exhaust facing housing within 100 m of an Industrial or Industrial Estate zone on the outline zoning plan is assessed at ASR C even if the street itself would have scored lower.

### 2. Acceptable noise levels (s. 2.4, Table 2)

Day and evening share one set of figures. Night is lower. Where noise is received inside a building from a source on or in the same or an adjoining building and is transmitted primarily through the structure, the acceptable noise level is 10 dB(A) less than Table 2. The same 10 dB(A) reduction applies if the assessment point is an internal location of the building in which the receiver is located.

| Time period | ASR A | ASR B | ASR C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day (0700 to 1900 hours) and evening (1900 to 2300 hours) | 60 | 65 | 70 |
| Night (2300 to 0700 hours) | 50 | 55 | 60 |

All figures are dB(A).

**SD takeaway:** Fixed plant that can be heard at an open window is tested at 60, 65 or 70 dB(A) by day and evening and at 50, 55 or 60 dB(A) at night, and structure-borne noise into the flat next door is 10 dB(A) stricter.

### 3. Tonal, impulsive and intermittent corrections (s. 3.3, Tables 3 and 4)

The measured noise level is an A-weighted equivalent continuous sound pressure level (Leq) over any 30-minute period, or a shorter period if the Authority is satisfied the noise is essentially steady over 30 minutes. The corrected noise level is:

CNL = MNL + ctone + cimp + cint, in dB(A).

Tonality is tested between 31.5 Hz and 16 kHz on one-third octave bands. A band, or a pair of adjacent bands, must not be more than 15.0 dB below the highest band, must stand more than 1.0 dB above each adjacent band, and must have a tonality factor, ftone, of 3.0 dB or more. The correction ctone is then:

| ftone | Below 250 Hz | 250 Hz or higher |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.0 dB or more, and less than 6.0 dB | 0 | 3 |
| 6.0 dB or more, and less than 9.0 dB | 3 | 6 |
| 9.0 dB or more | 6 | 6 |

The ctone figures are dB(A). An impulsive character may add not more than 3 dB(A). Intermittency applies only at night (2300 to 0700 hours), when the level changes rapidly by 5.0 dB(A) or more with a degree of regularity. The intermittency factor fint then adds 3 dB(A) if it is 5.0 or more and less than 10.0, and 6 dB(A) if it is 10.0 or more.

The corrected noise level is rounded to the nearest whole dB(A), with 0.5 and above upwards. Other measured or calculated levels are taken to the nearest 0.1 dB(A) or dB, with 0.05 and above upwards.

**SD takeaway:** A tonal fan or a transformer hum at or above 250 Hz can add 3 or 6 dB(A) before it is compared with Table 2, and a plant that cycles by 5 dB(A) or more at night can add another 3 or 6 dB(A).

### 4. Where the meter goes (Annex)

Meters must comply with IEC 651:1979 (Type 1) and IEC 804:1985 (Type 1). Calibration immediately before and after must agree within 1.0 dB. At a building, the assessment point is normally 1 m from the exterior of the facade, but may be another point the Authority considers appropriate. At a place other than a building it is 1.2 m above the ground. If the noise arrives mainly through the structure, through openings other than facade openings, or through glazing specially provided to reduce noise, the point is an internal location appropriate to normal occupancy.

**SD takeaway:** The number that can trigger a notice is normally 1 m outside the facade, so a roof plant screen has to work at that point, not only at the property boundary.
