## Regulatory Overview

Section 1.2 sets the principles, procedures and standards for assessing and measuring air pollution from stationary polluting processes when the Authority issues an air pollution abatement notice under section 10. A stationary polluting process includes an activity, process, or the operation of a chimney, relevant plant, machinery or equipment that evolves any air pollutant, other than livestock farming and the operation of any motor vehicle, ship or aircraft.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When a notice can require reduction, and when it can require the emission to stop (s. 2)

| Test | What the Authority may require |
| --- | --- |
| A nuisance is established under section 4 | An abatement notice requiring the owner of the premises, or the person carrying out the activity, to reduce the emissions |
| Emission determined under section 5 exceeds a health protection concentration level by more than 100% | A notice requiring the emission, or the operation of the relevant process, to cease |
| Emission determined under section 6 exceeds a health risk level | A notice requiring the emission, or the operation, to cease |
| Emission determined under section 7 imperils, or is likely to imperil, the safety of aircraft, or otherwise interferes with normal aircraft operation | A notice requiring the emission, or the operation, to cease |

In any of these cases the notice may also require any other steps to abate the emissions (s. 2.5).

**SD takeaway:** A boiler, kitchen, cremator or process stack that is not a licensed specified process can be ordered to reduce emissions on a nuisance finding, and to stop if the one-hour health protection level is exceeded by more than 100%.

### 2. Nuisance, and who counts as a sensitive receptor (ss. 4 and 5.1)

For a nuisance, the Authority may have regard to the relative location of the source and the place affected, the locality, the time, duration and frequency, and effects that include deposit of dust, grit or particles; objectionable odour; staining, corrosion or damage of a building, plant, equipment or materials; irritation of the eye, nose or skin, or any sensory discomfort; disturbance of normal activities by the colour or opacity of the emission; an effect which in the opinion of the Authority or an authorized officer may affect public safety; or any other effect which in that opinion is unreasonable for a member of the public to suffer.

For the health protection levels, a sensitive receptor is any domestic premises, hotel, hostel, hospital, clinic, nursery, temporary housing accommodation, school, educational institution, office, factory, shop, shopping centre, place of public worship, library, court of law, sports stadium or performing arts centre, and any other premises or place of similar sensitivity by duration or by the number of people affected.

**SD takeaway:** Offices, shops and factories are sensitive receptors for this memorandum, so a flue discharge is tested at neighbouring workplaces as well as at homes, schools and clinics.

### 3. One-hour health protection concentration levels (Table 1)

Levels are one-hour averages in microgrammes per cubic metre, at 298 K (25°C) and 101.325 kPa. Chlorinated dioxins and dibenzofurans are expressed as 2,3,7,8-TCDD. The total TCDD-equivalent concentration is the sum of each PCDD and PCDF concentration multiplied by the factor in Table 2. Table 2 prints 1 for 2,3,7,8-TCDD, 0.5 for 1,2,3,7,8-PeCDD, 0.1 for the listed HxCDD and HxCDF groups, 0.01 for 1,2,3,4,6,7,8-HpCDD and the listed HpCDF congeners, 0.001 for OCDD and OCDF, 0.1 for 2,3,7,8-TCDF, 0.5 for 2,3,4,7,8-PeCDF and 0.05 for 1,2,3,7,8-PeCDF.

Pollutants most often met on a building services or chimney drawing:

| Item | Compound | One-hour HPCL, µg/m³ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Benzene | 185 |
| 12 | Carbon monoxide | 30 000 |
| 29 | Formaldehyde | 98.1 |
| 32 | Nitrogen dioxide | 300 |
| 35 | Sulphur dioxide | 800 |

The table also prints, among others, acrylamide 75.0, arsenic 0.30, benzo(a)pyrene 0.387, chromium VI compounds 8.5 × 10⁻³, chlorinated dioxins and dibenzofurans as 2,3,7,8-TCDD 3.36 × 10⁻⁵, and vinyl chloride 472. Items 1 to 38 are the full list in the PDF.

Compliance uses the receptor concentration level from the Annex calculation, or from a measurement. A continuous-readout measurement is compared as the highest level averaged over any one hour. A measurement that is not continuous is converted to a reference receptor concentration level before it is compared with the health protection level; the averaging time used for that conversion is not shorter than one hour. The conversion formula on the scan did not survive as a clean equation and is not restated here. An air pollutant that is not in Table 1 is judged under section 6 against a health risk level taken from research and publications or from the advice of a medical practitioner.

**SD takeaway:** The one-hour figures that sit on a flue calculation in this memorandum are nitrogen dioxide at 300 µg/m³, sulphur dioxide at 800 µg/m³, carbon monoxide at 30 000 µg/m³ and formaldehyde at 98.1 µg/m³, and a result more than twice the relevant figure is the cease-emission test.

### 4. What the Authority may use to estimate the emission (s. 5.2)

The Authority shall have regard to the maximum design capacity of any furnace, chimney, relevant plant or process and the type and quantity of fuel, raw material or substance used; descriptions and specifications in manuals or documents from the owner, manufacturer or operator; emission factors from relevant studies or publications, including the USEPA Emission Factor Database and Compilation of Air Pollutant Emission Factors, AP-42, or any other appropriate publication; or measurements by the Authority using methods in US CFR 40 or another method the Authority considers appropriate.

**SD takeaway:** The chimney submission’s fuel type, fuel rate and manufacturer rating are the inputs this memorandum allows the Authority to use when there is not yet a stack test.

### 5. Screening meteorology and aircraft (Annex and s. 7)

The Annex fixes the screening case at neutral atmospheric stability, ambient temperature 298 K, wind speed 2 m/s, and gravitational acceleration 9.81 m/s². Source inputs are mass discharge rate in grammes per second, discharge height in metres, discharge velocity in metres per second, discharge temperature in kelvin, and equivalent diameter in metres. The receptor concentration level from the calculation is a one-hour average in microgrammes per cubic metre. Where more than one source contributes, steps 1 to 6 are repeated and the results are added. For aircraft, section 7 requires regard to the height, colour, opacity, temperature, volume or any other characteristic of the emission and the distance from any airport or normal flight path, or to the advice of the Director of Civil Aviation.

**SD takeaway:** A stack near a flight path is judged on plume height, opacity and temperature and on the advice of the Director of Civil Aviation, not only on the Table 1 concentrations.
