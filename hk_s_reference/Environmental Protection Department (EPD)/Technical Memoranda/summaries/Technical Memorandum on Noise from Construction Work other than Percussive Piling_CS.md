# Noise from construction work other than percussive piling
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**  
Originally published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette No. 2 Vol. CXXXVIII on 12 January 1996; came into operation on 14 February 1996. It replaces the memorandum published in Special Supplement No. 5 to Gazette Extraordinary No. 38 Vol. CXXX on 7 November 1988, which came into operation on 7 December 1988. | Environmental Protection Department

> Scope note: This memorandum is the Construction Noise Permit (CNP) method for powered mechanical equipment (PME) on construction work other than percussive piling, including non-percussive piling. It applies during restricted hours (1900 to 0700 hours, and any time on a general holiday, including Sunday). Inside a designated area, specified powered mechanical equipment and prescribed construction work are assessed under the separate designated-areas memorandum. Percussive piling is assessed under the percussive-piling memorandum.

## Regulatory Overview

Sections 1.2 and 1.4 set the procedure the Authority uses to assess noise from construction work other than percussive piling, to issue a CNP under section 8 of the Noise Control Ordinance, and to test whether a permit is being complied with: locate the most affected noise sensitive receiver (NSR), set the Area Sensitivity Rating (ASR) and the Acceptable Noise Level (ANL), calculate the Corrected Noise Level (CNL), and issue a permit when the CNL is equal to or less than the ANL. A permit is not generally issued when the CNL exceeds the ANL, and it may not be issued for work on or within a building that is an NSR in use, or that directly adjoins such a building, where the noise would be transmitted primarily through the structure and would be likely to cause an adverse impact.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Who is protected, and which area rating applies (ss. 1.3, 2.1–2.2, Table 1)

An NSR is any domestic premises, hotel, hostel, temporary housing accommodation, hospital, medical clinic, educational institution, place of public worship, library, court of law or performing arts centre, and any other premises of similar sensitivity that are not industrial or commercial, but only while in use for their intended purpose.

The ASR uses an area of about 100 m radius in urban areas and about 500 m in rural districts. An influencing factor (IF) is an industrial area, a major road, or the area within the boundary of Hong Kong International Airport. A major road is one with an annual average daily traffic flow in excess of 30,000; it is not an IF at a time when the flow is less than 300 vehicles per hour. Industrial areas and the Airport are IFs at all times. If more than one IF affects the NSR equally, only one is counted.

| Type of area containing the NSR | Not affected | Indirectly affected | Directly affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rural area, including country parks or village-type developments | A | B | B |
| Low-density residential area of low-rise or isolated high-rise developments | A | B | C |
| Urban area | B | C | C |
| Area other than those above | B | B | C |

**SD takeaway:** Night-time plant next to an occupied residential, hotel, school or clinic facade is an NSR problem, and a road above 30,000 vehicles a day can lift the ASR by one band.

### 2. Basic noise levels and the permit test (ss. 2.3–2.6, 2.13, Table 2)

The ANL is the Basic Noise Level (BNL) plus a duration correction and any multiple-permit correction. If the initial permit, or the combined duration of an initial permit and renewals for associated work on substantially the same site, is 14 days or less, add 3 dB(A). A later permit is a renewal if it starts 21 days or less after another permit for associated work on substantially the same site expires. If more than one CNP will materially affect the NSR, the Authority may correct the BNL on standard acoustical principles. Issue the permit if the CNL is equal to or less than the ANL.

| Time period | ASR A | ASR B | ASR C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All days during the evening (1900 to 2300 hours), and general holidays (including Sundays) during the day-time and evening (0700 to 2300 hours) | 60 | 65 | 70 |
| All days during the night-time (2300 to 0700 hours) | 45 | 50 | 55 |

All figures are dB(A).

**SD takeaway:** The night gate on a non-holiday is 45, 50 or 55 dB(A) at the NSR; a permit of 14 days or less is allowed 3 dB(A) more, and a Sunday or public-holiday daytime uses the evening row, not a weekday daytime row.

### 3. Source position, distance, barriers and reflections (ss. 2.7–2.12)

PME is grouped at a notional source mid-way between the geographical centre of the site and the boundary nearest the NSR. On a linear site with a length-to-width ratio exceeding 5:1, only the dominant portion closest to the NSR, itself at a 5:1 ratio, is used, and a linear part of a larger site may need its own permit. If the notional point would be more than 50 m from the boundary point nearest the NSR, the point used is 50 m from that boundary point. An item that will stay in one specified position may be assessed there instead. Sound power levels are summed with Table 4 and converted to a predicted noise level with Table 5. Table 5 is valid only up to 300 m; beyond that the Authority calculates the correction. Blank facades are ignored. Distance is to the nearest facade with windows, doors or other openings, using plan distance or slant distance, rounded to the nearest metre (0.5 and above upwards).

| Screening or reflection | Correction to the predicted noise level |
| --- | --- |
| All PME totally screened by a substantial barrier so that none is visible from any window, door or other opening | −10 dB(A); a larger reduction only on standard acoustical principles |
| All PME other than “quiet” items totally screened. A quiet item is more than 15 dB(A) below the total sound power level | −5 dB(A) |
| NSR is a building directly adjacent to the site and no PME is visible from any opening | −5 dB(A), treated as partial screening |
| NSR is a building | +3 dB(A) |
| Confined or reverberant immediate locality | up to +3 dB(A) more |

Site fences and hoardings are not substantial barriers. Purpose-built acoustic barriers are considered under the quiet-method provision in section 3.1, and the applicant must demonstrate the reduction in a report by a competent person.

**SD takeaway:** A site hoarding does not buy the 10 dB(A) screen, and a facade reflection of +3 dB(A) is added whenever the receiver is a building, so the night margin has to be found in plant choice and real screening, not in the hoarding line.

### 4. Plant sound power levels that set the night list (Table 3)

Table 3 assigns an identification code and a sound power level to each PME item. Examples that usually decide a night permit:

| Code | Item | Sound power level, dB(A) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CNP 021 | Bar bender and cutter (electric) | 90 |
| CNP 049 | Crane, tower (electric) | 95 |
| CNP 103 | Generator, super silenced, 70 dB(A) at 7 m | 95 |
| CNP 102 | Generator, silenced, 75 dB(A) at 7 m | 100 |
| CNP 001 | Air compressor, air flow ≤ 10 m³/min | 100 |
| CNP 065 | Drill/grinder, hand-held (electric) | 98 |
| CNP 023 | Breaker, hand-held, mass ≤ 10 kg | 108 |
| CNP 026 | Breaker, hand-held, mass > 35 kg | 114 |
| CNP 044 | Concrete lorry mixer | 109 |
| CNP 047 | Concrete pump, stationary or lorry mounted | 109 |
| CNP 081 | Excavator/loader, wheeled or tracked | 112 |
| CNP 067 | Dump truck | 117 |
| CNP 027 / CNP 028 | Breaker, excavator mounted (pneumatic or hydraulic) | 122 |

The remainder of Table 3, including dredgers, cranes, compactors and conveyors, is in the PDF. Levels are summed pairwise with Table 4 and the total rounded to the nearest whole dB(A), with 0.5 and above rounded upwards.

**SD takeaway:** An excavator-mounted breaker at 122 dB(A) and a dump truck at 117 dB(A) will fail a night ANL of 45 to 55 dB(A) unless distance or a real screen closes the gap; a tower crane at 95 dB(A) and a super-silenced generator at 95 dB(A) are the items that can stay on a short night list.

### 5. Special cases and compliance measurement (ss. 3–4, Annex)

A permit may still be considered for quiet working methods or other special factors demonstrated by a competent report, for unavoidable constraints such as serious interruption of road, rail or utilities, tidal work, or work that would cause less public annoyance in restricted hours than outside them, and for work of important social implications, which the Authority refers to the Secretary. Where a maximum noise level is a permit condition, measurement follows the Annex: sound level meters to IEC 651:1979 (Type 1) and IEC 804:1985 (Type 1), and a before-and-after calibrator check agreeing within 1.0 dB.

**SD takeaway:** A night concrete pour or a road opening that cannot wait for daytime is a special-case argument with a method statement, not an assumption that the Table 2 night level can be ignored.
