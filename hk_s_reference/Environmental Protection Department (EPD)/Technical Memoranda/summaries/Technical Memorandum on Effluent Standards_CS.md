# Standards for effluents discharged into drainage and sewerage systems, inland and coastal waters

> Scope note: This memorandum is the guide the Director of Environmental Protection uses when fixing licence conditions for effluent under sections 15, 16 and 20 of the Ordinance. He will not normally impose conditions more stringent than the memorandum without good reason. It does not apply to livestock waste controlled by the Waste Disposal (Livestock Waste) Regulations (Cap. 354 sub. leg. A), or to dredging, dumping for land formation, or solid waste disposal. Standards differ by receiving system and by flow rate. Figures below are the lowest flow band of each table, in mg/L unless noted, and are upper limits unless the table marks them otherwise. Higher flow bands are printed in Tables 1 to 10b and are more stringent where the numbers change. Flows outside the printed bands are set case by case, and flows above the highest band are more stringent than the tables.

## Regulatory Overview

Paragraphs 2.1 and 2.2 make the memorandum the Authority’s guide to limits that make effluent acceptable in foul sewers, storm water drains, inland waters and coastal waters, covering physical, chemical and microbial quality, and the Authority will consult it when writing licence conditions. Paragraphs 4.1 to 4.3 tie those conditions to the water quality objectives of the relevant water control zone and to protection of the drainage or sewerage system, the treatment process, and the health and safety of workers in them.

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Which table applies (paras. 3.2, 6–9)

Inshore waters are coastal waters where the depth is less than 6 m at mean low tide, or that lie within 200 m of the mean low water mark, whichever position is further from the shore. Marine waters are all other coastal waters. Flow rate is the measured volume per unit time from the premises, averaged over the period of operation in any day accepted by the Authority, or a method the Authority approves if the flow cannot be measured.

Inland standards apply to groundwater as to other inland waters. Storm water drains are not normally accepted for effluent; if they are, the standard is that of the downstream inland or inshore waters, and the Director of Drainage Services is consulted on drain capacity. Where foul sewerage is inadequate and storm drains act as combined sewers, the Authority may, after that consultation, declare them foul sewers until the flow can be diverted. New effluent is not allowed to rivers, streams or storm drains within 100 m of a gazetted bathing beach that flow through the beach area.

**SD takeaway:** A basement discharge is licensed against the sewer or the water it actually reaches, and a storm-drain connection is exceptional and takes the standard of the water downstream, not a relaxed site standard.

### 3. What may not be discharged at all (paras. 6.2–6.4, 8.4–8.6, 9.1–9.3)

Dilution is not allowed as a way to meet the standards, for foul sewers, inland waters or coastal waters. The licence may specify an instantaneous peak flow. Mixing different effluent streams inside the premises is not necessarily prohibited. Unpolluted water is not allowed into foul sewers. Sludge is not normally allowed to sewers. Household septic tanks that discharge to the ground are not controlled by these tables; the Authority controls them by design and maintenance standards, and the general prohibitions still apply.

Prohibited in foul sewers: polychlorinated biphenyls; polyaromatic hydrocarbons; fumigant or pesticide; radioactive substances; chlorinated hydrocarbons; flammable or toxic solvents; petroleum oil or tar; calcium carbide; wastes liable to form scum or deposits in any part of the public sewer; and any substance of a nature and quantity likely to damage the sewer or to interfere with any treatment process.

Prohibited in inland waters: the same list, with fumigant, pesticide or toxicant; wastes liable to form scum, deposits or discoloration; sludge or solid refuse of any kind; and detergents in Group A inland waters only.

Prohibited in coastal waters: the same chemical list; wastes liable to form scum, deposits or discoloration; and sludge, floatable substances or solids larger than 10 mm.

No new effluent will be allowed within 100 m of the boundaries of a gazetted beach in any direction, including rivers, streams and storm water drains; within 200 m of the seaward boundaries of a marine fish culture zone or a site of special scientific interest, and within 100 m of the landward boundaries; in any typhoon shelter; in any marina; or within 100 m of a seawater intake point.

**SD takeaway:** A cooling-water or pool blowdown point is not a dilution device, and a new outfall is barred within 100 m of a gazetted beach or a seawater intake and within 200 m seaward of a fish-culture zone or a site of special scientific interest.

### 4. How samples are judged (para. 2.8, Annex 1)

At the date of the memorandum text, the Government Chemist is the only analyst designated by the Ordinance and is the person who certifies an effluent sample in a prosecution. The standards refer to his methods, which Annex 1 prints for reference. Examples that survived in the annex include pH by APHA 17th edition 4500-H+B, colour by Lovibond Tintometer with a 25 mm cell, and E. coli methods referenced to DoE (1983) and HMSO (1983).

**SD takeaway:** A commissioning sample is aimed at the Government Chemist’s methods in Annex 1, not at a contractor’s in-house kit, if the result has to stand as the licensed quality.
