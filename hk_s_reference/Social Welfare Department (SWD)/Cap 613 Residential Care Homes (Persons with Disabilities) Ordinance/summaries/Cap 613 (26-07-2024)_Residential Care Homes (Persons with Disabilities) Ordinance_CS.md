# Residential Care Homes (Persons with Disabilities) Ordinance (Cap. 613)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 26 July 2024 | Director means the Director of Social Welfare (section 2)

> Scope note: This Ordinance requires a licence before a residential care home for persons with disabilities is operated, with a certificate of exemption only for an existing home. It lets the Director refuse a licence if the premises are unfit or do not comply with stated design, fire, health, and safety instruments. It does not print room sizes or means-of-escape widths. Those sit in a Code of Practice under section 23, in regulations under section 24, and in the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) instruments named in section 7(3)(c).

## Regulatory Overview

A residential care home for persons with disabilities means any premises at which more than 5 persons with disabilities, who have attained the age of 6 years, are habitually received for the purpose of residential accommodation with the provision of care (section 2). Subject to sections 5, 6, and 6A, a person must not operate, keep, manage, or in any other way have control of such a home otherwise than under a licence for the time being in force (section 4).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. Who is counted, and when a licence is not required (sections 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 6A)

A person with disabilities means a person who suffers from one or more of the following: total or partial loss of the bodily or mental functions; total or partial loss of a part of the body; the malfunction, malformation, or disfigurement of a part of the body; or a disorder, illness, or disease that affects the thought processes, perception of reality, emotions, or judgement or that results in disturbed behaviour (section 2).

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Not this Ordinance | Premises used or intended for use solely for medical treatment; a boarding school within the meaning of the Education Regulations (Cap. 279 sub. leg. A); a treatment centre within the meaning of the Drug Dependent Persons Treatment and Rehabilitation Centres (Licensing) Ordinance (Cap. 566); or a home excluded by the Director by Gazette order (section 3) |
| Existing home | An existing home is a residential care home for persons with disabilities that exists immediately before 18 November 2011. Section 4 does not apply if a certificate of exemption is for the time being in force (sections 2 and 5) |
| Also an elderly home | Section 4 does not apply if the home is also a residential care home for the elderly and a licence issued under Cap. 459 is in force (section 6) |
| Scheduled nursing home | Section 4 does not apply if the home is also a scheduled nursing home as defined by section 124 of the Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 633) and an exemption under section 128 of that Ordinance is in force (section 6A) |
| Not a defence | It is not a defence that the person did not know that no licence was in force, or, for an existing home, that no certificate of exemption was in force, or, where the home is also a residential care home for the elderly, that no Cap. 459 licence was in force (section 4(2)) |

Contravention: a fine of $1,000,000 and imprisonment for 2 years, and a further fine of $10,000 for each day during which a continuing offence continues (section 4(3)).

**SD takeaway:** More than 5 persons with disabilities aged 6 or over, habitually received for residential accommodation with care, needs a Cap. 613 licence unless an existing home holds a certificate of exemption or a Cap. 459 licence or a Cap. 633 exemption already covers that home.

### 2. Licence refusal and the 36-month cap (section 7)

The application must be accompanied by the information, particulars, and plans required by the Director. The Director may refuse a licence if it appears that the premises are not fit for reasons connected with the situation, means of ingress and egress, design, construction, size, type of building, staffing, or equipment, or that the premises do not comply with any requirements relating to design, construction, fire precautions, health, sanitation, and safety set out in:

| Instrument | Section 7(3)(c) |
|---|---|
| Buildings regulations | Any regulation made by the Secretary for Development under section 38 of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) |
| Fire code under Cap. 123 | Any Code of Practice published by the Director of Fire Services and referred to in section 16(1)(b) of Cap. 123 |
| This Ordinance’s code | Any Code of Practice issued by the Director under section 23 |
| This Ordinance’s regulations | Any regulation made by the Secretary for Labour and Welfare under section 24 |

The licence must specify a validity period of not more than 36 months (section 7(4)). If a Cap. 459 licence is in force, the applicant must surrender it on the issue of the Cap. 613 licence (section 7(5)).

**SD takeaway:** Ingress, egress, and compliance with Cap. 123 design, construction, fire, health, sanitation, and safety requirements are grounds to refuse the licence, and the licence cannot run longer than 36 months.

### 3. Certificate of exemption is only for an existing home (section 11)

An application for a certificate of exemption must be in respect of an existing home and accompanied by the information, particulars, and plans required by the Director. The certificate must specify a validity period of not more than 36 months. The Director may revoke a certificate of exemption.

**SD takeaway:** A new home cannot use a certificate of exemption. That route is only for a home that existed immediately before 18 November 2011, and even then the certificate is for not more than 36 months.

### 4. Directions and an order to stop use (sections 18 and 19)

The Director may by written notice direct what is required so that the home is operated and managed satisfactorily, the welfare of residents is promoted, it is provided with adequate apparatus and equipment to safeguard it against fire or other hazards likely to endanger the lives or health of residents, and this Ordinance is complied with. The notice must indicate a period for compliance (section 18).

The Director may by written order direct that the premises cease to be used as a residential care home for persons with disabilities for the period the Director thinks fit, or until further notice, if it appears there is any danger or risk of danger to the residents, or if a section 18 direction is not complied with within the indicated period (section 19).

**SD takeaway:** Danger to residents, or failure to comply with a direction to provide fire or other hazard safeguards, can stop the use of the premises as the home.
