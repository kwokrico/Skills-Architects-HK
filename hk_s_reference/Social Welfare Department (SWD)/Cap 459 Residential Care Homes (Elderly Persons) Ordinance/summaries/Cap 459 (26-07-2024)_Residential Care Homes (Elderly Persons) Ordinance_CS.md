# Residential Care Homes (Elderly Persons) Ordinance (Cap. 459)
**Architect critical summary for schematic design**
Contents last updated 26 July 2024 | Director means the Director of Social Welfare (section 2)

> Scope note: This Ordinance requires a licence before a residential care home for elderly persons is operated, and it lets the Director refuse a licence if the premises are unfit or do not comply with stated design, fire, health, and safety instruments. It does not print room sizes, corridor widths, or staffing ratios. Those sit in a Code of Practice under section 22, in regulations under section 23, in regulations under section 38 of the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), and in a Code of Practice published by the Director of Fire Services under section 16(1)(b) of Cap. 123.

## Regulatory Overview

A residential care home means any premises at which more than 5 persons who have attained the age of 60 years are habitually received for the purposes of care while resident therein, and premises include any building, enclosure, ground, or open air space (section 2). Any person who operates, keeps, manages, or otherwise has control of a residential care home while no licence is in force commits an offence, and it is not a defence that the person did not know that no licence was in force (section 6).

## Critical main topics and subtopics

### 1. When this Ordinance does not apply, and when another licence is enough (sections 3, 6A, and 6B)

| Case | Rule |
|---|---|
| Not this Ordinance | A residential care home maintained and controlled by the Government or the Housing Authority; one used or intended for use solely for medical treatment of persons requiring medical treatment; a treatment centre as defined by section 2 of the Drug Dependent Persons Treatment and Rehabilitation Centres (Licensing) Ordinance (Cap. 566); or a home excluded by the Director by order in the Gazette (section 3) |
| Cap. 613 licence or certificate of exemption | Section 6 does not apply to a residential care home that is also a residential care home for persons with disabilities if a licence or certificate of exemption issued under Cap. 613 is in force (section 6A) |
| Scheduled nursing home | Section 6 does not apply to a residential care home that is also a scheduled nursing home as defined by section 124 of the Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 633) if an exemption under section 128 of that Ordinance is in force (section 6B) |

**SD takeaway:** More than 5 residents aged 60 or over habitually received for care needs a Cap. 459 licence, unless a Cap. 613 licence or certificate of exemption, or a Cap. 633 scheduled-nursing-home exemption, is already in force, or the home is in section 3.

### 2. What can stop the licence (section 8)

The application must be accompanied by such information, particulars, and plans as the Director may require. The Director may refuse to issue a licence if it appears that:

| Ground | Section 8(3) |
|---|---|
| Premises not fit | For reasons connected with the situation, means of ingress and egress, design, construction, size, type of building, staffing, or equipment, the premises are not fit to be used for the purposes of a residential care home |
| Non-compliance | The premises do not comply with any requirements relating to design, construction, fire precautions, health, sanitation, and safety set out in a regulation under section 38 of Cap. 123; a Code of Practice published by the Director of Fire Services under section 16(1)(b) of Cap. 123; a Code of Practice issued by the Director under section 22; or a regulation under section 23 |

A licence authorizes a residential care home of a type prescribed for the purposes of this section, for 36 months or such lesser period as may be indicated (section 8(4)). If a Cap. 613 licence is in force, the applicant must surrender it on the issue of the Cap. 459 licence (section 8(4A)).

**SD takeaway:** Freeze ingress, egress, and the fire and health layout against Cap. 123 and the Director’s Code of Practice before schematic sign-off. This Ordinance makes non-compliance a ground to refuse the licence, and the licence itself is for not more than 36 months.

### 3. Remedial directions and an order to stop use (sections 19 and 20)

The Director may by written notice direct what is required so that the home is operated and managed satisfactorily, the welfare of residents is promoted, adequate apparatus and equipment required as safeguards against fire or other hazard likely to endanger life or health are provided, and this Ordinance is complied with. The notice must indicate a period for compliance (section 19).

The Director may by written order direct that the premises cease to be used as a residential care home for such period as the Director thinks fit, or until further notice, if it appears there is any danger or risk of danger to persons in residence, or if a section 19 direction is not complied with within the indicated period. The order takes effect from the date of service. Service includes posting a copy in a conspicuous place upon or in the premises (section 20).

**SD takeaway:** A fire or other hazard that endangers residents can be met first by a direction to provide apparatus and equipment, and then by an order, effective on service, that the premises cease to be used as a residential care home.

### 4. Where the design rules are made (sections 22 and 23)

The Director may issue Codes of Practice setting out principles, procedures, guidelines, and standards for the operation, keeping, management, or other control of residential care homes. A copy is available for public inspection free of charge.

The Secretary for Labour and Welfare may by regulation provide for the design, construction, hygiene, and sanitation of residential care homes; precautions against fire or other peril likely to endanger the lives or health of residents; the provision of and control of entrances and exits; and the classes or description of homes, including admission having regard to age. The Director may by written notice waive wholly, partly, or conditionally the requirements of any regulation in respect of a particular home.

**SD takeaway:** Room, hygiene, fire-precaution, and entrance standards are for the section 22 code and the section 23 regulations, not for this Ordinance text.
